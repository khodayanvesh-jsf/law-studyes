# 5. Revocation of Offer and Acceptance through various modes including electronic medium
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/revocation-of-offer-and-acceptance-through-various-modes-including-electronic-medium/

## In One Minute
Revocation means taking back an offer or an acceptance before it binds. Section 5 sets the deadline: a proposal can be revoked at any time before the communication of its acceptance is complete as against the proposer (in the post, until the acceptance is posted), but not afterwards; an acceptance can be revoked at any time before the communication of the acceptance is complete as against the acceptor (in the post, until it reaches the proposer), but not afterwards. Section 6 lists the modes by which a proposal comes to an end: (1) notice of revocation , (2) lapse of time , (3) failure of a condition precedent , and (4) death or insanity of the proposer, if the acceptor learns of it before accepting. A rejection or counter-offer also ends an offer. A revocation must itself be communicated (Section 4). Revocation by email or other electronic means is valid (IT Act, Section 10A), and the IT Act fixes when an electronic revocation is sent and received (Section 13). In exams, marks come from Sections 5 and 6 quoted with the illustration, the timeline of when each party can revoke, cases such as Byrne v. Van Tienhoven , Dickinson v. Dodds and Shuey , and the electronic rules. In real life, it tells you how long you can change your mind.

## Legal Provision
- Section 5. Revocation of proposals and acceptances. A proposal may be revoked at any time before the communication of its acceptance is complete as against the proposer, but not afterwards. An acceptance may be revoked at any time before the communication of the acceptance is complete as against the acceptor, but not afterwards. Illustration A proposes, by a letter sent by post, to sell his house to B. B accepts the proposal by a letter sent by post. A may revoke his proposal at any time before or at the moment when B posts his letter of acceptance, but not afterwards. B may revoke his acceptance at any time before or at the moment when the letter communicating it reaches A, but not afterwards.
- Section 5, Uttar Pradesh amendment (U.P. Act 57 of 1976). At the end of the first paragraph, the following Explanation is inserted: "Explanation—Where an invitation to a proposal contains a condition that any proposal made in response to such invitation shall be kept open for a specified time and a proposal is thereupon made accepting such condition, such proposal may not be revoked within such time."
- Section 6. Revocation how made. A proposal is revoked— (1) by the communication of notice of revocation by the proposer to the other party; (2) by the lapse of the time prescribed in such proposal for its acceptance, or, if no time is so prescribed, by the lapse of a reasonable time, without communication of the acceptance; (3) by the failure of the acceptor to fulfil a condition precedent to acceptance; or (4) by the death or insanity of the proposer, if the fact of his death or insanity comes to the knowledge of the acceptor before acceptance.
- Section 4 (third paragraph and illustration (c)). The communication of a revocation is complete,— as against the person who makes it, when it is put into a course of transmission to the person to whom it is made, so as to be out of the power of the person who makes it; as against the person to whom it is made, when it comes to his knowledge. (c) A revokes his proposal by telegram. The revocation is complete as against A when the telegram is despatched. It is complete as against B when B receives it. B revokes his acceptance by telegram. B's revocation is complete as against B when the telegram is despatched, and as against A when it reaches him.
- Section 3 (relevant part). … the revocation of proposals and acceptances, respectively, are deemed to be made by any act or omission of the party … revoking by which he intends to communicate such … revocation, or which has the effect of communicating it.
- Information Technology Act, 2000
- Section 10A. Validity of contracts formed through electronic means. Where in a contract formation, the communication of proposals, the acceptance of proposals, the revocation of proposals and acceptances, as the case may be, are expressed in electronic form or by means of an electronic records, such contract shall not be deemed to be unenforceable solely on the ground that such electronic form or means was used for that purpose.
- Section 13(1). Save as otherwise agreed to between the originator and the addressee, the despatch of an electronic record occurs when it enters a computer resource outside the control of the originator.
- Section 13(2) to (5) (time and place of receipt), Section 11 (attribution), Section 12 (acknowledgment) and the First Schedule are explained in point 9 below.
- In simple words
- Section 5: the proposer can take back his offer until the acceptance is sent beyond the acceptor's control . The acceptor can take back his acceptance until it reaches the proposer . UP amendment: in Uttar Pradesh, if a tender notice says offers must stay open for a fixed time, and you make an offer on that condition, you cannot withdraw it within that time. Section 6: an offer ends by (1) notice of revocation, (2) time running out , (3) the acceptor not fulfilling a condition that had to be met before accepting, or (4) the proposer's death or insanity , if the acceptor knew of it before accepting. Section 4: a revocation is complete against the person revoking when he sends it; against the other side when it reaches his knowledge . IT Act, Section 10A: a revocation by email or electronic means is as good as one on paper. IT Act, Section 13(1): an email is sent when it leaves the sender's control.

## Meaning and Definition
What revocation means

Revocation is the withdrawal of a proposal or an acceptance by the person who made it, before it becomes binding. It is different from:

rejection , which is the offeree saying "no" to the offer; and lapse , where the offer ends without anyone acting, because time runs out.

Why the law allows revocation

An offer is only a proposal. Until it is accepted, there is no promise, so the proposer is free to withdraw. An acceptance by post binds the proposer when posted, but the acceptor is bound only when it arrives. The Act lets the acceptor withdraw during that gap, so that both sides have some protection.

Running example

Meena (Hyderabad) offers by post to sell her sewing machine, model XL-200, to Ravi (Vijayawada) for ₹8,000 , "open till Friday".

Monday: Meena posts the offer. Wednesday: Ravi reads it. Thursday, 10 a.m.: Ravi posts his letter of acceptance. Saturday, 11 a.m.: Meena receives Ravi's letter.

## Key Points to Remember
- Proposer may revoke until the acceptance is complete as against him (in the post: until it is posted ) (Section 5). Acceptor may revoke until the acceptance is complete as against him (in the post: until it reaches the proposer) (Section 5). Revocation must be communicated (Section 4; Byrne ). Modes: notice, lapse, failure of condition precedent, death or insanity known before acceptance (Section 6). A promise to keep an offer open is not binding without consideration ( Routledge v. Grant ), except under the UP amendment for tender-type proposals. A general offer is revoked by the same publicity ( Shuey ). Electronic revocation is valid (IT Act, Section 10A); its despatch and receipt are fixed by Section 13. The IT Act does not apply to certain negotiable instruments, powers of attorney, trusts and wills (First Schedule); the immovable-property entry was omitted in 2022.
