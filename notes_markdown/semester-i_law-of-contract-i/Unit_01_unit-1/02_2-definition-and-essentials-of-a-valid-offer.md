# 2. Definition and essentials of a valid Offer

## In One Minute
Every contract starts with an offer . The Act calls it a proposal . Under Section 2(a), a person makes a proposal when he signifies to another his willingness to do or to abstain from doing anything, with a view to obtaining the assent of that other . A valid offer must be definite , must be made with the intention of being bound on acceptance , and must be communicated to the person who is to accept it. It must be different from an invitation to offer , such as a price list, a shop display, an advertisement for an auction, or a call for tenders. In exams, marks come from the definition, the list of essentials with a case for each, and the offer versus invitation-to-offer distinction ( Harvey v. Facey , MacPherson v. Appanna ). In real life, this decides whether you can say "I accept" and hold the other person to a bargain.

## Legal Provision
- Section 2(a). When one person signifies to another his willingness to do or to abstain from doing anything, with a view to obtaining the assent of that other to such act or abstinence, he is said to make a proposal;
- Section 2(b). When the person to whom the proposal is made signifies his assent thereto, the proposal is said to be accepted. A proposal, when accepted, becomes a promise;
- Section 2(c). The person making the proposal is called the "promisor", and the person accepting the proposal is called the "promisee";
- Section 3. Communication, acceptance and revocation of proposals. The communication of proposals, the acceptance of proposals, and the revocation of proposals and acceptances, respectively, are deemed to be made by any act or omission of the party proposing, accepting or revoking by which he intends to communicate such proposal, acceptance or revocation, or which has the effect of communicating it.
- Section 4 (first paragraph). The communication of a proposal is complete when it comes to the knowledge of the person to whom it is made. Illustration (a): A proposes, by letter, to sell a house to B at a certain price. The communication of the proposal is complete when B receives the letter.
- Section 8. Acceptance by performing conditions, or receiving consideration. Performance of the conditions of a proposal, or the acceptance of any consideration for a reciprocal promise which may be offered with a proposal, is an acceptance of the proposal.
- Section 9. Promises, express and implied. In so far as the proposal or acceptance of any promise is made in words, the promise is said to be express. In so far as such proposal or acceptance is made otherwise than in words, the promise is said to be implied.
- Section 29. Agreements void for uncertainty. Agreements, the meaning of which is not certain, or capable of being made certain, are void.
- In simple words
- Section 2(a): an offer is a willingness to do or not do something, shown to another person, in order to get his "yes" . Section 2(b), (c): when that "yes" comes, the offer becomes a promise . The offeror is the promisor ; the acceptor is the promisee . Section 3: an offer can be made by words or conduct (any act or omission meant to communicate it). Section 4: an offer is communicated only when it comes to the knowledge of the offeree. Section 8: some offers are accepted by doing what they ask (useful for offers to the public). Section 9: an offer in words is express ; one made by conduct is implied . Section 29: an offer so vague that no certain agreement can come out of it produces a void agreement.

## Meaning and Definition
Offer and proposal mean the same thing

The Act uses the word proposal . English law and most textbooks use offer . They mean the same thing. The person who makes it is the offeror (proposer, promisor). The person to whom it is made is the offeree (promisee once he accepts).

Why the law needs a clear offer

A contract is formed the moment an offer is accepted. So the law must be able to point to one definite offer whose acceptance closes the deal. If every price list, advertisement or enquiry were an offer, a shopkeeper could be bound to sell to a thousand people the same item. That is why the law separates a true offer from a mere invitation to offer .

Running example

## Key Points to Remember
- Offer = proposal (Section 2(a)): willingness to do or abstain, shown to another, to obtain his assent . It must be made with an intention to be bound on acceptance. It must be definite (Section 29). It must be communicated ; communication is complete when it comes to the knowledge of the offeree (Section 4). An invitation to offer (display, price list, auction notice, tender notice, quotation) is not an offer. A general offer can be accepted by anyone who knows of it and performs its conditions (Section 8; Carlill ). Offers may be express or implied (Section 9). Silence cannot be made acceptance ( Felthouse v. Bindley ).
