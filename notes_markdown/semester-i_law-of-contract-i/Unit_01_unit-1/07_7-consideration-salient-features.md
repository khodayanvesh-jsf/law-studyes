# 7. Consideration - salient features
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/consideration-salient-features/

## In One Minute
A promise is enforced by law only when something is given in return for it. That "something in return" is consideration . It is the price of the promise . Under Section 2(d), consideration is an act, abstinence or promise , done at the desire of the promisor , by the promisee or any other person . It may be past, present or future . It need not be adequate , but it must be real and lawful . Without it, an agreement is void (Section 25), except in a few cases. Indian law is wider than English law on two points: who may give consideration, and past consideration. This is a favourite long-answer topic. In real life, it explains why a gift promise cannot be enforced but a sale can.

## Legal Provision
- Section 2(d). When, at the desire of the promisor, the promisee or any other person has done or abstained from doing, or does or abstains from doing, or promises to do or to abstain from doing, something, such act or abstinence or promise is called a consideration for the promise.
- Section 2(f). Promises which form the consideration or part of the consideration for each other are called reciprocal promises.
- Section 10 (relevant part). All agreements are contracts if they are made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for a lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and are not hereby expressly declared to be void.
- Section 23 (opening words). The consideration or object of an agreement is lawful, unless it is forbidden by law; or is of such a nature that, if permitted, it would defeat the provisions of any law; or is fraudulent; or involves or implies injury to the person or property of another; or the Court regards it as immoral, or opposed to public policy.
- Section 25 (opening words). An agreement made without consideration is void, unless … (the three exceptions are in the note Exception to consideration ).
- Section 25, Explanation 2. An agreement to which the consent of the promisor is freely given is not void merely because the consideration is inadequate; but the inadequacy of the consideration may be taken into account by the Court in determining the question whether the consent of the promisor was freely given.
- In simple words
- Section 2(d) says what consideration is. Section 2(f) says that two promises given for each other are reciprocal promises . Section 10 says a valid contract needs lawful consideration . Section 23 says when consideration is unlawful . Section 25 says an agreement without consideration is void , except in a few cases. Its Explanation 2 says a small consideration is still consideration.

## Meaning and Definition
The idea: something for something

Consideration is what each side gives or promises in exchange for the other's promise. The Latin phrase is quid pro quo , "something for something".

The law enforces bargains , not bare promises. A bare promise without consideration is a nudum pactum (a naked promise). The maxim ex nudo pacto non oritur actio means no action arises from a bare promise .

Why the law asks for consideration. It separates a bargain from a gift. In a bargain, each side has given or risked something, so it is fair to enforce it. A bare promise to give a gift has nothing behind it.

The classic English definition

In Currie v. Misa (1875), consideration was described as some right, interest, profit or benefit to one party, or some forbearance, detriment, loss or responsibility given, suffered or undertaken by the other. Put simply, it is either a benefit to the promisor or a detriment (loss or burden) to the promisee.

The Indian definition, broken into parts

Read Section 2(d) as five questions.

Part of Section 2(d) Question to ask Answer in simple words "at the desire of the promisor" Who asked for it? The promisor must have wanted it "the promisee or any other person" Who gave it? The promisee, or even a third person "has done … does … promises to do" When was it given? Past, present or future "done or abstained from doing" What kind of thing? An act, an abstinence, or a promise "something" How much ? Anything of some value in law, even if small

Running example

Ravi promises to pay Sita ₹15,000 for her used scooter. Sita promises to hand over the scooter next Sunday.

Ravi is the promisor of the promise to pay. Sita is the promisee . Sita's promise to hand over the scooter is the consideration for Ravi's promise. Ravi's promise to pay ₹15,000 is the consideration for Sita's promise.

Each promise is the price of the other. These are reciprocal promises (Section 2(f)).

## Key Points to Remember
- Consideration is the price of the promise : something for something. Section 2(d): an act, abstinence or promise , done at the desire of the promisor , by the promisee or any other person . It may be past, present or future . It need not be adequate (Section 25, Explanation 2), but inadequacy may show that consent was not free. It must be real (not impossible, uncertain, or an existing duty) and lawful (Sections 10, 23). Promises given for each other are reciprocal promises (Section 2(f)). Consideration need not be money. Motive is not consideration. Indian law is wider than English law on third-party and past consideration. An agreement without consideration is void , except in the Section 25 cases.
