# 1. Definition and essentials of a valid Contract
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/definition-and-essentials-of-a-valid-contract/

## In One Minute
A contract is an agreement that the law will enforce (Section 2(h)). An agreement is born when one person makes a proposal and the other accepts it (Sections 2(a), 2(b), 2(e)). Not every agreement is a contract. Section 10 says an agreement becomes a contract only when it is made with free consent , by competent parties , for a lawful consideration , with a lawful object , and is not expressly declared void . Courts add one more: the parties must intend to create legal relations ( Balfour v. Balfour ). So remember the line: all contracts are agreements, but all agreements are not contracts. In exams, marks come from listing every essential, giving the section for each, and adding one case or example for each. In real life, this checklist tells you whether a promise can be taken to court.

## Legal Provision
- Section 2. In this Act the following words and expressions are used in the following senses, unless a contrary intention appears from the context:— (a) When one person signifies to another his willingness to do or to abstain from doing anything, with a view to obtaining the assent of that other to such act or abstinence, he is said to make a proposal; (b) When the person to whom the proposal is made signifies his assent thereto, the proposal is said to be accepted. A proposal, when accepted, becomes a promise; (c) The person making the proposal is called the "promisor", and the person accepting the proposal is called the "promisee"; (d) When, at the desire of the promisor, the promisee or any other person has done or abstained from doing, or does or abstains from doing, or promises to do or to abstain from doing, something, such act or abstinence or promise is called a consideration for the promise; (e) Every promise and every set of promises, forming the consideration for each other, is an agreement; (f) Promises which form the consideration or part of the consideration for each other are called reciprocal promises; (g) An agreement not enforceable by law is said to be void; (h) An agreement enforceable by law is a contract; (i) An agreement which is enforceable by law at the option of one or more of the parties thereto, but not at the option of the other or others, is a voidable contract; (j) A contract which ceases to be enforceable by law becomes void when it ceases to be enforceable.
- Section 10. What agreements are contracts. All agreements are contracts if they are made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for a lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and are not hereby expressly declared to be void. Nothing herein contained shall affect any law in force in India and not hereby expressly repealed by which any contract is required to be made in writing or in the presence of witnesses, or any law relating to the registration of documents.
- Section 11. Who are competent to contract. Every person is competent to contract who is of the age of majority according to the law to which he is subject, and who is of sound mind, and is not disqualified from contracting by any law to which he is subject.
- Section 13. "Consent" defined. Two or more persons are said to consent when they agree upon the same thing in the same sense.
- Section 14. "Free consent" defined. Consent is said to be free when it is not caused by— (1) coercion, as defined in section 15, or (2) undue influence, as defined in section 16, or (3) fraud, as defined in section 17, or (4) misrepresentation, as defined in section 18, or (5) mistake, subject to the provisions of sections 20, 21 and 22.
- In simple words
- Section 2(a)–(b): a proposal (offer) is made; when it is accepted , it becomes a promise . Section 2(c): the one who proposes is the promisor ; the one who accepts is the promisee . Section 2(e): a promise, or set of promises, given for each other is an agreement . Section 2(h): an agreement the law will enforce is a contract . Section 2(g), (i), (j): some agreements are void from the start; some contracts are voidable at one party's option; a valid contract can later become void . Section 10: lists what turns an agreement into a contract. Sections 11, 13, 14: explain "competent", "consent" and "free consent".

## Meaning and Definition
The two-step formula

The Act builds the definition in two steps.

Agreement = offer + acceptance . A proposal, when accepted, becomes a promise (Section 2(b)). Every promise forming the consideration for each other is an agreement (Section 2(e)). Contract = agreement + enforceability by law. An agreement enforceable by law is a contract (Section 2(h)).

Definitions by writers

Writers on contract law give similar definitions. Two are often quoted in exams:

Pollock: "Every agreement and promise enforceable at law is a contract." Anson: a contract is a legally binding agreement between two or more persons by which rights are acquired by one or more to acts or forbearances on the part of the other or others.

Both say the same thing as Section 2(h): agreement + legal enforceability .

"All contracts are agreements, but all agreements are not contracts"

A contract is always an agreement. But an agreement is a contract only if it meets the Section 10 conditions. A social or domestic promise (to go to a film, to give pocket money) is an agreement but usually not a contract, because the parties did not mean it to go to court.

Why the law has these essentials

The law enforces a promise only when it is fair and sensible to do so. Each essential protects someone:

Free consent protects a person who was forced or cheated. Capacity protects minors and persons of unsound mind. Consideration separates a bargain from a bare promise. Lawful object protects society from illegal deals. Intention to create legal relations keeps family and social promises out of court.

Running example

Anil offers to sell his used laptop to Bhavana for ₹30,000 . Bhavana accepts. She pays an advance of ₹5,000 today and promises to pay the balance ₹25,000 (₹30,000 − ₹5,000) on delivery next Monday.

## Key Points to Remember
- Contract = agreement + enforceability by law (Section 2(h)). Agreement = offer + acceptance (Sections 2(a), 2(b), 2(e)). Section 10: free consent, competent parties, lawful consideration, lawful object, not expressly declared void. Intention to create legal relations is added by courts ( Balfour v. Balfour ). Competence: majority, sound mind, not disqualified (Section 11); a minor's agreement is void ( Mohori Bibee ). Consent without freedom makes a contract voidable ; mutual mistake of fact makes an agreement void . Uncertain (Section 29) and impossible (Section 56) agreements are void. Writing is needed only where a law requires it (Section 10, second paragraph).
