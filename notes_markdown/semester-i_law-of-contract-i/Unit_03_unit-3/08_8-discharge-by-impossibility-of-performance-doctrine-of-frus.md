# 8. Discharge by impossibility of performance (Doctrine of Frustration)
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/discharge-by-impossibility/

## In One Minute
If an act is impossible from the start, the agreement is void . If it becomes impossible or unlawful after the contract is made, through no fault of the promisor, the contract becomes void at that moment. This is the doctrine of frustration , found in Section 56. The doctrine is narrow. A rise in cost, a delay or a bad bargain is not enough. In exams, the grounds, the limits and the cases decide the marks. In real life, it decides who bears the loss when a fire, a war, a new law or a pandemic upsets a contract.

## Legal Provision
- Section 56. Agreement to do impossible act. An agreement to do an act impossible in itself is void. Contract to do an act afterwards becoming impossible or unlawful. —A contract to do an act which, after the contract is made, becomes impossible, or, by reason of some event which the promisor could not prevent, unlawful, becomes void when the act becomes impossible or unlawful. Compensation for loss through non-performance of act known to be impossible or unlawful. —Where one person has promised to do something which he knew, or, with reasonable diligence, might have known, and which the promisee did not know, to be impossible or unlawful, such promisor must make compensation to such promisee for any loss which such promisee sustains through the non-performance of the promise. Illustrations. (a) A agrees with B to discover treasure by magic. The agreement is void. (b) A and B contract to marry each other. Before the time fixed for the marriage, A goes mad. The contract becomes void. (c) A contracts to marry B, being already married to C, and being forbidden by the law to which he is subject to practise polygamy, A must make compensation to B for the loss caused to her by the non-performance of his promise. (d) A contracts to take in cargo for B at a foreign port. A's Government afterwards declares war against the country in which the port is situated. The contract becomes void when war is declared. (e) A contracts to act at a theatre for six months in consideration of a sum paid in advance by B. On several occasions A is too ill to act. The contract to act on those occasions becomes void.
- Related provisions
- Section 32. Enforcement of contracts contingent on an event happening. Contingent contracts to do or not to do anything if an uncertain future event happens cannot be enforced by law unless and until that event has happened. If the event becomes impossible, such contracts become void.
- Section 65. Obligation of person who has received advantage under void agreement, or contract that becomes void. When an agreement is discovered to be void, or when a contract becomes void, any person who has received any advantage under such agreement or contract is bound to restore it, or to make compensation for it to the person from whom he received it. Illustration (d). A contracts to sing for B at a concert for 1,000 rupees, which are paid in advance. A is too ill to sing. A is not bound to make compensation to B for the loss of the profits which B would have made if A had been able to sing, but must refund to B the 1,000 rupees paid in advance.
- Sale of Goods Act, 1930, Section 7. Goods perishing before making of contract. Where there is a contract for the sale of specific goods, the contract is void if the goods without the knowledge of the seller have, at the time when the contract was made, perished or become so damaged as no longer to answer to their description in the contract.
- Sale of Goods Act, 1930, Section 8. Goods perishing before sale but after agreement to sell. Where there is an agreement to sell specific goods, and subsequently the goods without any fault on the part of the seller or buyer perish or become so damaged as no longer to answer to their description in the agreement before the risk passes to the buyer, the agreement is thereby avoided.
- In simple words
- First paragraph. An agreement to do something impossible in itself is void from the start. Second paragraph. If the act later becomes impossible, or unlawful by an event the promisor could not prevent, the contract becomes void at that moment. Third paragraph. A promisor who knew, or should have known, of the impossibility must compensate a promisee who did not know. Section 65. Whoever received an advantage must give it back or pay for it.

## Meaning and Definition
Frustration is the discharge of a contract because a later event makes performance impossible or unlawful, or destroys the purpose the parties had in view. Section 56 does not use the word "frustration". It speaks of impossibility. In India the two words describe the same doctrine.

Why the law excuses the parties. The law does not compel what is impossible. The maxim lex non cogit ad impossibilia says the law does not require the impossible. It would be unjust to make a promisor pay damages for failing to do what no one could do.

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 56: an act impossible in itself makes the agreement void. A later impossibility or illegality makes the contract void when it happens. Frustration is automatic. No one chooses. Satyabrata Ghose : Section 56 is exhaustive in India, and "impossible" includes impracticable and useless performance. Grounds: destruction of the subject matter, failure of the foundation event, death or incapacity, change of law, war. Not frustration: higher cost, delay, a temporary hindrance, self-induced impossibility, a risk taken on by a party, risk allocated by the contract. Effect: both parties are discharged and advantages are restored (Section 65). The third paragraph gives compensation to a promisee who did not know of the impossibility. Sale of Goods Act: Section 7 (goods perished before the contract) and Section 8 (goods perish after the agreement, before risk passes). A term of the contract that discharges performance falls under Section 32, not Section 56 ( Satyabrata Ghose ). Covid-19 is not an automatic ground. Courts look at the contract and the real cause.
