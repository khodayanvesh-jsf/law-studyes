# 10. Anticipatory Breach
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/anticipatory-breach/

## In One Minute
An anticipatory breach happens when a party refuses to perform, or makes himself unable to perform, before the time for performance arrives. The other party need not wait for the due date. He has two choices: He may accept the refusal, end the contract and sue at once. Or he may keep the contract alive and wait. The choice has risks. In exams, the meaning, the two choices and the cases earn the marks. In real life, it decides whether you can act the day a supplier says he will not deliver.

## Legal Provision
- Section 39. Effect of refusal of party to perform promise wholly. When a party to a contract has refused to perform, or disabled himself from performing, his promise in its entirety, the promisee may put an end to the contract, unless he has signified, by words or conduct, his acquiescence in its continuance. Illustrations. (a) A, a singer, enters into a contract with B, the manager of a theatre, to sing at his theatre two nights in every week during the next two months, and B engages to pay her 100 rupees for each night's performance. On the sixth night A wilfully absents herself from the theatre. B is at liberty to put an end to the contract. (b) A, a singer, enters into a contract with B, the manager of a theatre, to sing at his theatre two night's in every week during the next two months, and B engages to pay her at the rate of 100 rupees for each night. On the sixth night, A wilfully absents herself. With the assent of B, A sings on the seventh night. B has signified his acquiescence in the continuance of the contract, and cannot now put an end to it, but is entitled to compensation for the damage sustained by him through A's failure to sing on the sixth night.
- Related provisions
- Section 73, Illustration (c). A contracts to buy of B, at a stated price, 50 maunds of rice, no time being fixed for delivery. A afterwards informs B that he will not accept the rice if tendered to him. B is entitled to receive from A, by way of compensation, the amount, if any, by which the contract price exceeds that which B can obtain for the rice at the time when A informs B that he will not accept it.
- Section 75. Party rightfully rescinding contract, entitled to compensation. A person who rightfully rescinds a contract is entitled to compensation for any damage which he has sustained through the non-fulfilment of the contract.
- Specific Relief Act, 1963, Section 16(c). Specific performance of a contract cannot be enforced in favour of a person— ... (c) who fails to prove that he has performed or has always been ready and willing to perform the essential terms of the contract which are to be performed by him, other than terms the performance of which has been prevented or waived by the defendant. Explanation. —For the purposes of clause (c),— ... (ii) the plaintiff must prove performance of, or readiness and willingness to perform, the contract according to its true construction.
- In simple words
- Section 39 covers both a refusal and a disabling act, if it goes to the whole promise. The promisee "may" end the contract. He loses that right if he shows, by words or conduct, that he accepts the contract's continuance. Section 73, Illustration (c) measures the loss at the time the defaulter announces his refusal.

## Meaning and Definition
Anticipatory breach is a breach committed before the time fixed for performance. It takes two forms:

Form What happens Example Express repudiation The party says he will not perform A seller writes, "I will not deliver" Implied repudiation His conduct makes performance impossible He sells to another buyer the very goods he promised

The words of the Act. The Act does not use the words "anticipatory breach". Section 39 covers it in the words "refused to perform, or disabled himself from performing". The English case Hochster v. De La Tour gave the doctrine its shape.

Why the law lets the innocent party act early. Waiting helps no one. He can look for another supplier at once and so reduce his loss.

Before the due date, one party refuses to perform, or disables himself from performing, his whole promise (Section 39). The promisee then chooses. If he accepts the repudiation, the contract ends, he may sue at once for damages under Section 73 and must mitigate his loss, but he cannot later ask for specific performance. If he affirms and waits, the contract stays binding on both sides and the other party may withdraw his refusal; at the due date the promisee must be ready and willing to perform, and an event in between may defeat his claim (Avery v. Bowden).

## Key Points to Remember
- Anticipatory breach is a refusal or disabling act before the due date (Section 39). The refusal must be clear and cover the whole promise. The promisee may accept it, end the contract and sue at once ( Hochster ). Or he may affirm the contract and wait, staying ready and willing. If he waits, an intervening event may defeat his claim ( Avery v. Bowden ). After accepting the repudiation he can claim damages but not specific performance ( Jawahar Lal Wadhwa ). Section 39 covers refusal and also disabling oneself from performing. Specific performance needs proof of readiness and willingness (Specific Relief Act, Section 16(c)).
