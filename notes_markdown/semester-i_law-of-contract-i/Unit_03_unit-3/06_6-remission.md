# 6. Remission
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/remission/

## In One Minute
Remission means the promisee gives up, wholly or in part, his right to have a promise performed. Under Section 63 he may also extend the time for performance, or accept some other satisfaction instead. The promisor is then freed to that extent. The right belongs to the promisee, so he may give it up. In exams, the four powers in Section 63 and the five illustrations earn the marks. In real life, it is the law behind a creditor settling a debt for less.

## Legal Provision
- Section 63. Promisee may dispense with or remit performance of promisee. Every promisee may dispense with or remit, wholly or in part, the performance of the promisee made to him, or may extend the time for such performance, or may accept instead of it any satisfaction which he thinks fit. Illustrations. (a) A promises to paint a picture for B. B afterwards forbids him to do so. A is no longer bound to perform the promise. (b) A owes B 5,000 rupees. A pays to B, and B accepts, in satisfaction of the whole debt, 2,000 rupees paid at the time and place at which the 5,000 rupees were payable. The whole debt is discharged. (c) A owes B 5,000 rupees. C pays to B 1,000 rupees, and B accepts them, in satisfaction of his claim on A. This payment is a discharge of the whole claim. (d) A owes B, under a contract, a sum of money, the amount of which has not been ascertained. A, without ascertaining the amount, gives to B, and B, in satisfaction thereof, accepts, the sum of 2,000 rupees. This is a discharge of the whole debt, whatever may be its amount. (e) A owes B 2,000 rupees, and is also indebted to other creditors. A makes an arrangement with his creditors, including B, to pay them a [composition] of eight annas in the rupee upon their respective demands. Payment to B of 1,000 rupees is a discharge of B's demand.
- Related provisions
- Section 41. Effect of accepting performance from third person. When a promisee accepts performance of the promise from a third person, he cannot afterwards enforce it against the promisor.
- Section 44. Effect of release of one joint promisor. Where two or more persons have made a joint promise, a release of one of such joint promisors by the promisee does not discharge the other joint promisor or joint promisors; neither does it free the joint promisors so released from responsibility to the other joint promisor or joint promisors.
- Section 55 (third paragraph). If, in case of a contract voidable on account of the promisor's failure to perform his promise at the time agreed, the promisee accepts performance of such promise at any time other than that agreed, the promisee cannot claim compensation for any loss occasioned by the non-performance of the promise at the time agreed, unless, at the time of such acceptance, he gives notice to the promisor of his intention to do so.
- Section 135. A contract between the creditor and the principal debtor, by which the creditor makes a composition with, or promises to give time to, or not to sue, the principal debtor, discharges the surety, unless the surety assents to such contract.
- In simple words
- A promisee may do four things under Section 63:
- Dispense with performance: tell the promisor not to perform at all. Remit performance, wholly or in part: give up all or part of what is owed. Extend the time for performance. Accept any satisfaction he thinks fit in place of the performance.

## Meaning and Definition
Remission is the promisee's act of giving up, wholly or in part, the performance of a promise made to him. The Act uses the verbs "dispense with" and "remit". It does not define remission as a separate term.

Why the law allows it. The right to performance is the promisee's, so he may give it up.

It also removes a harsh English rule. In English law, a promise to accept a smaller sum for a larger debt was not binding without fresh consideration ( Foakes v. Beer ). Section 63 does not follow that rule.

## Key Points to Remember
- Remission is the promisee's giving up of performance, wholly or in part (Section 63). The promisee may also extend time or accept any satisfaction he thinks fit. Acceptance of a lesser sum in satisfaction discharges the whole debt. Section 63 does not follow the English rule in Foakes v. Beer . An extension of time rests on agreement. The promisee cannot extend time alone. Remission creates no new contract. Novation does. Accepting late performance without notice bars compensation for the delay (Section 55). Accepting performance from a third person bars enforcement against the promisor (Section 41). Release of one joint promisor does not free the others (Section 44).
