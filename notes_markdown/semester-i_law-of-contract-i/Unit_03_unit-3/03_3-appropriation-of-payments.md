# 3. Appropriation of payments
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/appropriation-of-payments/

## In One Minute
A debtor may owe a creditor several distinct debts and pay less than the total. Appropriation decides which debt that payment clears. The order is fixed by law: The debtor chooses first (Section 59). If he does not, the creditor chooses (Section 60). If neither chooses, the law decides : the earliest debt is cleared first (Section 61). In exams, this three-step order earns the marks. In real life, it settles which loan or invoice a part payment has cleared.

## Legal Provision
- Section 59. Application of payment where debt to be discharged is indicated. Where a debtor, owing several distinct debts to one person, makes a payment to him, either with express intimation, or under circumstances implying, that the payment is to be applied to the discharge of some particular debt, the payment, if accepted, must be applied accordingly. Illustration (a): A owes B, among other debts, 1,000 rupees upon a promissory note which falls due on the first June. He owes B no other debt of that amount. On the first June, A pays to B 1,000 rupees. The payment is to be applied to the discharge of the promissory note. Illustration (b): A owes to B, among other debts, the sum of 567 rupees. B writes to A and demands payment of this sum. A sends to B 567 rupees. This payment is to be applied to the discharge of the debt of which B had demanded payment.
- Section 60. Application of payment where debt to be discharged is not indicated. Where the debtor has omitted to intimate and there are no other circumstances indicating to which debt the payment is to be applied, the creditor may apply it at his discretion to any lawful debt actually due and payable to him from the debtor, whether its recovery is or is not barred by the law in force for the time being as to the limitation of suits.
- Section 61. Application of payment where neither party appropriates. Where neither party makes any appropriation, the payment shall be applied in discharge of the debts in order of time, whether they are or are not barred by the law in force for the time being as to the limitation of suits. If the debts are of equal standing, the payment shall be applied in discharge of each proportionably.
- In simple words
- Section 59: the debtor says, or the facts show, which debt the money is for. If the creditor accepts the money, he must apply it to that debt. Section 60: the debtor says nothing and the facts show nothing. The creditor may apply it to any lawful debt that is actually due and payable , even a time-barred debt . Section 61: nobody chooses. The payment clears the debts in order of time , earliest first, even time-barred ones. Debts of equal standing share it in proportion .

## Meaning and Definition
Appropriation of payments means applying a payment to a particular debt out of several debts.

The question arises only when all of these are present:

One debtor. One creditor. Several distinct debts owed by that debtor to that creditor. A payment that is not enough to clear all of them.

If there is only one debt, there is nothing to choose, so Sections 59 to 61 do not apply.

Why the choice matters. Debts are not all the same. One may carry high interest. One may be secured by a mortgage or a surety . One may be close to becoming time-barred. So which debt is cleared changes the rights of both parties.

Why this order. The money belongs to the debtor, so he gets the first say. If he is silent, the creditor gets the next say. If both are silent, the law applies a fixed rule so that no dispute is left open.

## Key Points to Remember
- Appropriation arises only with one debtor, one creditor, several distinct debts and an insufficient payment. Order: debtor (Section 59) → creditor (Section 60) → law (Section 61). Section 59: direction may be express or implied; the creditor must follow it if he accepts the payment. Section 60: creditor may choose any lawful debt actually due and payable, even a time-barred one. Section 61: earliest debt first, even if time-barred; equal debts proportionately. An exact match of amount is only a clue to intention. Appropriation to a time-barred debt does not revive it (Limitation Act, Section 19). One debt with interest: interest and costs first, then principal ( Smithaben H. Patel ; Meghraj ).
