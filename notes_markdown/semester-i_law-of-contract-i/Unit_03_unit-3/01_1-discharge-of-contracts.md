# 1. Discharge of Contracts
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/discharge-of-contract/

## In One Minute
A contract is discharged when the duties it created come to an end. After discharge, neither party can ask the other to perform. Most contracts end by performance. Others end by agreement, by a later event that makes performance impossible, by breach, by operation of law, or by lapse of time. The reason for the ending matters. It decides who can still sue, who must refund, and who must pay damages . A contract that ends by performance leaves nothing to claim. A contract that ends by breach leaves a claim for compensation. In exams, this is a list question: name each mode, give its section and add one example. Section 37 is the starting point, because every mode of discharge is an answer to one question: when is a party freed from the duty to perform?

## Legal Provision
- Section 37. Obligation of parties to contracts. The parties to a contract must either perform, or offer to perform, their respective promises, unless such performance is dispensed with or excused under the provisions of this Act, or of any other law. Promises bind the representatives of the promisors in case of the death of such promisors before performance, unless a contrary intention appears from the contract. Illustrations. (a) A promises to deliver goods to B on a certain day on payment of Rs. 1,000. A dies before that day. A's representatives are bound to deliver the goods to B, and B is bound to pay the Rs. 1,000 to A's representatives. (b) A promises to paint a picture for B by a certain day, at a certain price. A dies before the day. The contract cannot be enforced either by A's representatives or by B.
- Section 38. Effect of refusal to accept offer of performance. Where a promisor has made an offer of performance to the promisee, and the offer has not been accepted, the promisor is not responsible for non-performance, nor does he thereby lose his rights under the contract.
- Section 39. Effect of refusal of party to perform promise wholly. When a party to a contract has refused to perform, or disabled himself from performing, his promise in its entirety, the promisee may put an end to the contract, unless he has signified, by words or conduct, his acquiescence in its continuance.
- Section 56 (second paragraph). A contract to do an act which, after the contract is made, becomes impossible, or, by reason of some event which the promisor could not prevent, unlawful, becomes void when the act becomes impossible or unlawful.
- Section 62. Effect of novation, rescission, and alteration of contract. If the parties to a contract agree to substitute a new contract for it, or to rescind or alter it, the original contract, need not be performed.
- Section 63. Promisee may dispense with or remit performance of promisee. Every promisee may dispense with or remit, wholly or in part, the performance of the promisee made to him, or may extend the time for such performance, or may accept instead of it any satisfaction which he thinks fit.
- Section 65. Obligation of person who has received advantage under void agreement, or contract that becomes void. When an agreement is discovered to be void, or when a contract becomes void, any person who has received any advantage under such agreement or contract is bound to restore it, or to make compensation for it to the person from whom he received it.
- Section 2(j). A contract which ceases to be enforceable by law becomes void when it ceases to be enforceable.
- Other sections used in this note. Sections 38 to 55 and 67 say how, when, where and by whom performance is made, and what follows if it is refused or delayed. Section 53 (a party prevented from performing may avoid the contract ) and Section 55 (failure to perform on time when time is essential) make the contract voidable. Section 64 deals with rescinding a voidable contract. These are quoted in full in the notes on By performance and Discharge by Breach .
- In simple words
- Section 37: promises must be kept, or at least offered. The only escape is a ground the law itself allows. Every other section in this unit names one such ground: performance, agreement, impossibility, breach. Some grounds come from outside the Contract Act: insolvency law, the Limitation Act and the Negotiable Instruments Act.

## Meaning and Definition
The Act does not define "discharge". In practice, a contract is discharged when the rights and duties it created are ended. After discharge, neither party can ask the other to perform.

The idea behind it. Section 37 starts from the rule that promises must be kept. Discharge is the list of settled situations in which the law says a party is now free:

the parties have done what they promised (performance); they have agreed to let each other go (agreement); the law excuses them, because performance has become impossible (impossibility); one party's breach lets the other walk away (breach); the law itself ends the contract (operation of law); the time to sue has run out (lapse of time).

Why the law has these rules. A contract cannot bind for ever. The law needs to say clearly when the duty ends, so that each party knows whether it may still sue, must refund money, or must pay compensation.

## Key Points to Remember
- Discharge means the end of the duties under a contract. Performance is only one route. Section 37 is the starting rule: perform, or offer to perform. Agreement: Sections 62 and 63. Impossibility: Section 56. Breach: Section 39. Impossibility makes the contract void by itself. Breach gives the innocent party a choice. Operation of law covers death in personal contracts, insolvency, merger and material alteration. Lapse of time bars the remedy but does not erase the debt (Sections 25(3), 60, 61). Money claims may survive discharge: refund under Section 65, damages under Section 73.
