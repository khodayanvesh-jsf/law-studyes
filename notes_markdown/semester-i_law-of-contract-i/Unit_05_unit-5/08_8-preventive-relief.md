# 8. Preventive Relief
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/preventive-relief/

## In One Minute
Most remedies answer a wrong that has already happened. Preventive relief answers a wrong that is about to happen, or that is still going on. The court orders the wrongdoer to stop, or to put right what he has done, before the harm cannot be repaired. The Specific Relief Act, 1963 gives preventive relief in one form: the injunction . It is granted at the court's discretion (freedom to decide), never as of right. In exams, the meaning, the two kinds and the scheme of Sections 36 to 42 are asked as a long question on injunctions. In real life, it is how a flat owner stops a neighbour's digging before the wall cracks, or a trader stops a customer list from being leaked.

## Legal Provision
- Section 36. Preventive relief how granted. Preventive relief is granted at the discretion of the court by injunction, temporary or perpetual.
- Section 37. Temporary and perpetual injunctions. (1) Temporary injunctions are such as are to continue until a specific time, or until the further order of the court, and they may be granted at any stage of a suit, and are regulated by the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (5 of 1908). (2) A perpetual injunction can only be granted by the decree made at the hearing and upon the merits of the suit; the defendant is thereby perpetually enjoined from the assertion of a right, or from the commission of an act, which would be contrary to the rights of the plaintiff.
- Section 4. Specific relief to be granted only for enforcing individual civil rights and not for enforcing penal laws. Specific relief can be granted only for the purpose of enforcing individual civil rights and not for the mere purpose of enforcing a penal law.
- The rest of Part III, in short (each section has its own topic note)
- Section 38: a perpetual injunction may be granted to prevent the breach of an obligation existing in the plaintiff's favour, express or implied. Where the obligation arises from contract , the court is guided by Chapter II ( specific performance ). Where the defendant invades or threatens to invade the plaintiff's right to, or enjoyment of, property, it may be granted in four cases: (a) the defendant is trustee of the property for the plaintiff; (b) there is no standard for ascertaining the actual damage; (c) money compensation would not afford adequate relief; (d) it is necessary to prevent a multiplicity of judicial proceedings. Section 39: a mandatory injunction may be granted to prevent the breach and also to compel performance of the requisite acts which the court is capable of enforcing. Section 40: damages may be claimed in addition to, or in substitution for, a perpetual or mandatory injunction . They must be claimed in the plaint , but the court shall allow an amendment at any stage. Dismissal of a suit to prevent the breach bars a later suit for damages for that breach. Section 41: an injunction cannot be granted in the cases listed in clauses (a) to (j) and clause (ha). Section 42: notwithstanding Section 41(e), a negative agreement may be enforced by injunction even if the affirmative agreement cannot be specifically enforced, provided the plaintiff has not failed to perform the contract so far as it binds him. Section 20A(1): no injunction shall be granted in a suit under the Act involving a contract relating to an infrastructure project specified in the Schedule, where it would cause impediment or delay in the progress or completion of the project.
- In simple words
- Section 36: the only way the Act prevents a wrong is by an injunction. The injunction is either temporary or perpetual, and the court always has discretion. Section 37(1): a temporary injunction lasts until a fixed time or until the court orders otherwise. It may be granted at any stage of a suit. The Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 governs it. Section 37(2): a perpetual injunction comes only with the decree , after the hearing, on the merits. It stops the defendant for good from asserting a right, or doing an act, against the plaintiff's rights. Section 4: specific relief , including an injunction, protects individual civil rights. It is not granted merely to enforce a penal (criminal) law.

## Meaning and Definition
Preventive relief is relief that prevents a wrong or stops its continuance. The Act has one tool for it, the injunction : a court order that stops (restrains) a person from an act, or compels him to do one.

The Act places it in Part III: Chapter VII, "Injunctions generally" (Sections 36 and 37), and Chapter VIII, "Perpetual injunctions" (Sections 38 to 42). Sections 43 and 44 were repealed in 1974.

The question this part of the Act answers. When, and in what form, will the court stop a wrong before it happens or before it goes on any longer?

Why the law has this rule. Damages repair a wrong afterwards. Some harm cannot be repaired by money, or is hard to measure. The law therefore lets the court act first.

How the court acts. An injunction is an order to a person. If he disobeys, he is personally answerable to the court. Lawyers say equity acts in personam (on the person, not on the property). The court asks a series of questions. See Diagram 1 .

## Key Points to Remember
- Preventive relief prevents a wrong. In the Act it comes only by injunction (Part III, Sections 36 to 42). It is always discretionary and guided by equity: clean hands, no delay. Temporary injunctions: any stage, three tests. Perpetual injunctions: only by decree on the merits. Section 41 lists eleven bars, including clause (ha) of 2018. Section 42 is the exception for a negative agreement. Section 4: only individual civil rights are enforced; Section 20A: no injunction that would impede or delay a Schedule infrastructure project. Indian courts follow the three tests of Dalpat Kumar , not the English serious-question test.
