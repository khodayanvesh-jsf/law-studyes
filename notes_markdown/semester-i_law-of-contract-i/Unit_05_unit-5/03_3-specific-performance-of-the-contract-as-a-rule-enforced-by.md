# 3. Specific performance of the contract – As a rule enforced by court
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/specific-performance-of-the-contract/

## In One Minute
When a party breaks a contract, the injured party can ask the court to make the other side keep its promise. That order is called specific performance . It is the most important remedy in the Specific Relief Act, 1963. Since 1 October 2018, the court shall enforce a contract as a rule, unless a section bars it. Three provisions can bar it: the kind of contract (Sections 11(2) and 14) and the plaintiff's own conduct (Section 16). Substituted performance under Section 20 works through Sections 14(a) and 16(a). In exams, you must know all of these, with the main cases. In real life, this is the remedy a buyer uses when a seller backs out of a land or flat sale because prices have gone up.

## Legal Provision
- Section 9. Defences respecting suits for relief based on contract. Except as otherwise provided herein where any relief is claimed under this Chapter in respect of a contract, the person against whom the relief is claimed may plead by way of defence any ground which is available to him under any law relating to contracts.
- Section 10. Specific performance in respect of contracts. The specific performance of a contract shall be enforced by the court subject to the provisions contained in sub-section (2) of section 11, section 14 and section 16.
- (Section 10 was substituted by Act 18 of 2018, with effect from 1 October 2018.)
- Section 11. Cases in which specific performance of contracts connected with trusts enforceable. (1) Except as otherwise provided in this Act, specific performance of a contract shall, be enforced when the act agreed to be done is in the performance wholly or partly of a trust. (2) A contract made by a trustee in excess of his powers or in breach of trust cannot be specifically enforced.
- (In Section 11(1), " contract shall" replaced "contract may, in the discretion of the court" from 1 October 2018.)
- Section 14. Contracts not specifically enforceable. The following contracts cannot be specifically enforced, namely:— (a) where a party to the contract has obtained substituted performance of contract in accordance with the provisions of section 20; (b) a contract, the performance of which involves the performance of a continuous duty which the court cannot supervise; (c) a contract which is so dependent on the personal qualifications of the parties that the court cannot enforce specific performance of its material terms; and (d) a contract which is in its nature determinable.
- (Section 14 was substituted by Act 18 of 2018, with effect from 1 October 2018.)
- Section 16. Personal bars to relief. Specific performance of a contract cannot be enforced in favour of a person— (a) who has obtained substituted performance of contract under section 20; or (b) who has become incapable of performing, or violates any essential term of, the contract that on his part remains to be performed, or acts in fraud of the contract, or wilfully acts at variance with, or in subversion of, the relation intended to be established by the contract; or (c) who fails to prove that he has performed or has always been ready and willing to perform the essential terms of the contract which are to be performed by him, other than terms the performance of which has been prevented or waived by the defendant. Explanation. —For the purposes of clause (c),— (i) where a contract involves the payment of money, it is not essential for the plaintiff to actually tender to the defendant or to deposit in court any money except when so directed by the court; (ii) the plaintiff must prove performance of, or readiness and willingness to perform, the contract according to its true construction.
- (In 2018, clause (a) was substituted, "who fails to aver and prove" became "who fails to prove", and "must aver" in Explanation (ii) became "must prove".)
- Section 20. Substituted performance of contract. (1) Without prejudice to the generality of the provisions contained in the Indian Contract Act, 1872 (9 of 1872), and, except as otherwise agreed upon by the parties, where the contract is broken due to non-performance of promise by any party, the party who suffers by such breach shall have the option of substituted performance through a third party or by his own agency, and, recover the expenses and other costs actually incurred, spent or suffered by him, from the party committing such breach. (2) No substituted performance of contract under sub-section (1) shall be undertaken unless the party who suffers such breach has given a notice in writing, of not less than thirty days, to the party in breach calling upon him to perform the contract within such time as specified in the notice, and on his refusal or failure to do so, he may get the same performed by a third party or by his own agency: Provided that the party who suffers such breach shall not be entitled to recover the expenses and costs under sub-section (1) unless he has got the contract performed through a third party or by his own agency. (3) Where the party suffering breach of contract has got the contract performed through a third party or by his own agency after giving notice under sub-section (1), he shall not be entitled to claim relief of specific performance against the party in breach. (4) Nothing in this section shall prevent the party who has suffered breach of contract from claiming compensation from the party in breach.
- Related provision ( Limitation Act, 1963 , Schedule)
- Article 54. For specific performance of a contract. – Three years. – The date fixed for the performance, or, if no such date is fixed, when the plaintiff has notice that performance is refused.
- In simple words
- Section 9: the defendant may raise any defence that the law of contract allows. Section 10: specific performance shall be enforced, subject to Section 11(2), Section 14 and Section 16. Sections 11 to 13: contracts connected with trusts, part of a contract, and rights against a vendor with a defective title. Section 14: four kinds of contract that cannot be specifically enforced. Section 14A: the court may engage experts. Sections 15 to 19: who may sue and against whom, personal bars, no title, enforcement only with a variation. Section 20: substituted performance. Sections 20A to 20C: infrastructure projects, Special Courts, and a 12-month time limit for suits. Sections 21 to 25: compensation, possession and refund of earnest money , a sum named for breach, bar of a later suit, and awards. Article 54: the suit must be filed within three years.

## Meaning and Definition
Specific performance is a court order that a party must actually do what he promised in the contract. It differs from damages , which give money. It is the natural remedy where the subject matter is one of a kind, such as land, because no sum of money buys exactly the same plot.

The question this part of the Act answers. When will the court make a party actually perform his contract, instead of paying money?

The answer since 2018. The Act does not say that the court has no freedom left. It says the court shall enforce the contract unless a named bar applies. A plaintiff must clear a series of tests.

Is there a valid, subsisting contract, with no defence under the law of contract? If not, there is nothing to enforce (Section 9). Is the suit filed in time, within three years of the date fixed for performance or of refusal? If not, the suit is barred by Article 54 of the Limitation Act, 1963. Is the contract one that the Act allows to be enforced? If it is barred by Section 11(2) or Section 14, damages are the remedy. Is the plaintiff free of the personal bars? If he took substituted performance, cannot perform, or cannot prove readiness and willingness , he fails (Section 16). If all the tests are met, the court shall enforce the contract (Section 10). It may add compensation and possession (Sections 21 and 22).

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 10 (after 2018): specific performance shall be enforced as a rule, subject to Sections 11(2), 14 and 16. Before 1 October 2018 it was "may", in the court's discretion. Katta Sujatha Reddy (2022): the amendment is prospective. The 2024 review recalled that judgment without deciding the point again. Section 14 has four classes: substituted performance obtained, continuous duty, personal qualifications, determinable contracts. Section 16(c): the plaintiff must prove continuous readiness and willingness. Readiness is financial capacity and willingness is conduct. Filing within limitation (three years, Article 54) is not enough. Delay and price rise can defeat relief. Section 20: substituted performance after 30 days' written notice. It bars a later claim for specific performance but not compensation. Sections 21 and 22: compensation, possession and refund of earnest money must be claimed in the plaint, but the court shall allow amendment. Sections 20A to 20C: no injunction that delays a listed infrastructure project; Special Courts; suits disposed of within 12 months (plus six).
