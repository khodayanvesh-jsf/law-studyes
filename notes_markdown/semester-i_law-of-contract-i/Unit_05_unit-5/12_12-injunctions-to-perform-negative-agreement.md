# 12. Injunctions to perform negative agreement
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/injunctions-to-perform-negative-agreement/

## In One Minute
Many contracts have two halves. One half says "I will work only for you". The other half, expressed or implied, says "I will not work for anyone else". A court cannot force a person to work. It can stop him from working for a rival. Section 42 of the Specific Relief Act, 1963 allows this. The court may enforce the negative promise by injunction even where it cannot enforce the positive one. In exams, you must explain Section 42, its proviso, and the limit set by Section 27 of the Contract Act. In real life, it is how a company keeps a trained employee from joining a rival in the middle of his contract, or a brand keeps its bottler from bottling a competitor's drink.

## Legal Provision
- Section 42. Injunction to perform negative agreement. Notwithstanding anything contained in clause (e) of section 41, where a contract comprises an affirmative agreement to do a certain act, coupled with a negative agreement, express or implied, not to do a certain act, the circumstance that the court is unable to compel specific performance of the affirmative agreement shall not preclude it from granting an injunction to perform the negative agreement: Provided that the plaintiff has not failed to perform the contract so far as it is binding on him.
- Section 41. Injunction when refused. An injunction cannot be granted— (e) to prevent the breach of a contract the performance of which would not be specifically enforced;
- Section 14. Contracts not specifically enforceable. The following contracts cannot be specifically enforced, namely:— . . . (c) a contract which is so dependent on the personal qualifications of the parties that the court cannot enforce specific performance of its material terms; and (d) a contract which is in its nature determinable.
- Indian Contract Act, 1872, Section 27. Agreement in restraint of trade, void. Every agreement by which any one is restrained from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business of any kind, is to that extent void. Exception 1. —Saving of agreement not to carry on business of which good-will is sold.—One who sells the good-will of a business may agree with the buyer to refrain from carrying on a similar business, within specified local limits, so long as the buyer, or any person deriving title to the good-will from him, carries on a like business therein, provided that such limits appear to the Court reasonable, regard being had to the nature of the business.
- In simple words
- Section 42: notwithstanding clause (e) of Section 41, a contract may contain an affirmative agreement to do an act, together with a negative agreement, express or implied, not to do an act. The court may be unable to compel specific performance of the affirmative agreement. That does not stop it from granting an injunction to perform the negative agreement. Proviso to Section 42: the plaintiff must not have failed to perform the contract so far as it is binding on him. Section 41(e): an injunction cannot be granted to prevent the breach of a contract whose performance would not be specifically enforced. Section 14(c) and (d): a contract that depends so much on the personal qualifications of the parties that the court cannot enforce its material terms, or that is in its nature determinable (can be ended at will), cannot be specifically enforced. Section 27, Contract Act: every agreement by which anyone is restrained from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business is void to that extent ( void : having no legal effect).

## Meaning and Definition
A negative covenant is a formal promise not to do a certain act. An affirmative (positive) agreement is a promise to do one.

Section 41(e) says that no injunction lies where the contract itself could not be specifically enforced. Section 42 carves out an exception for the negative half.

The question this section answers. If the court cannot make a person keep his positive promise, can it at least hold him to his promise not to do something?

Why the law has this rule. Without Section 42, a person who promised to serve one employer only could walk away to a rival mid-contract, and the employer would get only damages . Section 42 lets the court enforce the part it can enforce, without forcing anyone to work.

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 42: a negative agreement, express or implied, may be enforced by injunction even if the affirmative one cannot be. Proviso: the plaintiff must not have failed to perform his own part. A covenant during the term is generally valid. A covenant after the term is void under Section 27. Authorities: Lumley v. Wagner ; Golikari ; Gujarat Bottling ; Krishan Murgai ; Percept D'Mark . The other bars in Section 41 still apply.
