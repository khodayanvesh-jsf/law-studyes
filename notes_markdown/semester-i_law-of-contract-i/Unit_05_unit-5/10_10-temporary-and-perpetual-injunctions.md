# 10. Temporary and Perpetual injunctions
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/temporary-and-perpetual-injunctions/

## In One Minute
An injunction can come early in a suit or at its end. A temporary injunction protects the plaintiff while the case is going on. A perpetual injunction is the final order that ends the case in the plaintiff's favour. Section 37 of the Specific Relief Act, 1963 draws the line. Temporary injunctions are governed by the Code of Civil Procedure. Perpetual injunctions are granted only by a decree on the merits. In exams, the six-point comparison and the three tests are the core. In real life, the temporary order is what stops a wall or a sale today; the perpetual order is what the plaintiff takes home after the trial.

## Legal Provision
- Section 36. Preventive relief how granted. Preventive relief is granted at the discretion of the court by injunction, temporary or perpetual.
- Section 37. Temporary and perpetual injunctions. (1) Temporary injunctions are such as are to continue until a specific time, or until the further order of the court, and they may be granted at any stage of a suit, and are regulated by the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (5 of 1908). (2) A perpetual injunction can only be granted by the decree made at the hearing and upon the merits of the suit; the defendant is thereby perpetually enjoined from the assertion of a right, or from the commission of an act, which would be contrary to the rights of the plaintiff.
- Section 38. Perpetual injunction when granted. (1) Subject to the other provisions contained in or referred to by this Chapter, a perpetual injunction may be granted to the plaintiff to prevent the breach of an obligation existing in his favour, whether expressly or by implication. (2) When any such obligation arises from contract, the court shall be guided by the rules and provisions contained in Chapter II. (3) When the defendant invades or threatens to invade the plaintiff’s right to, or enjoyment of, property, the court may grant a perpetual injunction in the following cases, namely:— (a) where the defendant is trustee of the property for the plaintiff; (b) where there exists no standard for ascertaining the actual damage caused, or likely to be caused, by the invasion; (c) where the invasion is such that compensation in money would not afford adequate relief; (d) where the injunction is necessary to prevent a multiplicity of judicial proceedings.
- Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, Order XXXIX (" Temporary Injunctions and Interlocutory Orders") – stated closely, not quoted
- Rule 1: where it is proved by affidavit or otherwise that (a) any property in dispute in a suit is in danger of being wasted, damaged or alienated by any party to the suit; (b) the defendant threatens, or intends, to remove or dispose of his property with a view to defrauding his creditors; or (c) the defendant threatens to dispossess the plaintiff or otherwise cause injury to him in relation to any property in dispute, the court may grant a temporary injunction , until the disposal of the suit or until further orders. Rule 2: in a suit to restrain a breach of contract or other injury, the plaintiff may, at any time after the suit begins and before or after judgment, apply for a temporary injunction to restrain the breach or injury complained of, on such terms as the court thinks fit. Rule 2A: on disobedience or breach of an injunction, the court may order the property of the person guilty to be attached, and may order him to be detained in civil prison for a term not exceeding three months. An attachment cannot remain in force for more than one year; after that, the property may be sold and compensation paid to the injured party. Rule 3: before granting an injunction, the court shall direct notice to the opposite party, except where the object of granting the injunction would be defeated by the delay. If it grants one without notice, it must record its reasons, and the applicant must at once send the other side copies of the application, the affidavit, the plaint and the documents relied on, and file an affidavit that he has done so. Rule 3A: where an injunction is granted without notice, the court shall make an endeavour to finally dispose of the application within thirty days; if it cannot, it must record its reasons. Rule 4: any order for an injunction may be discharged, varied or set aside by the court on the application of a party dissatisfied with it.
- In simple words
- Section 36: preventive relief is given by injunction, temporary or perpetual, at the court's discretion . Section 37(1): a temporary injunction lasts until a specific time, or until the court's further order. It may be granted at any stage of a suit. The Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 regulates it. Section 37(2): a perpetual injunction can be granted only by the decree made at the hearing and upon the merits. The defendant is perpetually enjoined (ordered to refrain) from asserting a right, or committing an act, contrary to the plaintiff's rights. Section 38: a perpetual injunction may be granted to prevent the breach of an obligation in the plaintiff's favour, and in four cases of invasion of property rights. Order XXXIX: Rules 1 and 2 give the grounds for a temporary injunction. Rule 2A deals with disobedience. Rule 3 requires notice. Rule 4 allows the order to be discharged, varied or set aside.

## Meaning and Definition
A temporary injunction is an order that holds the position until a set time or further order. It does not decide who is right.

A perpetual injunction is the final order granted by the decree. It decides the dispute on the merits and binds the defendant for good. The word "perpetual" refers to the permanence of the decree, not to a different kind of act.

The question this topic answers. At what stage of a suit can each kind of injunction be granted, on what basis, and what is its effect?

Why the law has two kinds. A trial takes time. If nothing could be done until the decree, the property in dispute might be sold, a wall might be built, or a secret might be leaked. The temporary injunction keeps things as they are until the court can decide. The perpetual injunction then gives the final answer.

## Key Points to Remember
- Temporary: any stage, until a time or further order, Order XXXIX. Perpetual: decree on the merits only. Three tests for a temporary injunction: prima facie case, balance of convenience, irreparable injury. A temporary order does not decide the suit. Section 38(3): four cases of invasion of property rights for a perpetual injunction. A bare suit for a perpetual injunction needs possession and no real cloud on title. Disobedience: Order XXXIX, Rule 2A for a temporary injunction; Order XXI, Rule 32 for a decree.
