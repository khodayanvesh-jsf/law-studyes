# 2. Recovering possession of property
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/recovery-possession-property/

## In One Minute
If someone throws you out of your land, the law gives you two doors. One asks you to prove that you own it (Section 5). The other asks only whether you were in possession and were removed without due process of law (Section 6). Sections 7 and 8 do the same work for goods. No one may force a person out of his property by self-help, not even the true owner. A person in settled possession can be removed only through a court. In exams, the six-month possessory suit under Section 6 is asked most often. In real life, it protects tenants, shopkeepers and farmers from being put on the street overnight.

## Legal Provision
- Section 5. Recovery of specific immovable property. A person entitled to the possession of specific immovable property may recover it in the manner provided by the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (5 of 1908).
- Section 6. Suit by person dispossessed of immovable property. (1) If any person is dispossessed without his consent of immovable property otherwise than in due course of law, he or any person through whom he has been in possession or any person claiming through him may, by suit, recover possession thereof, notwithstanding any other title that may be set up in such suit. (2) No suit under this section shall be brought— (a) after the expiry of six months from the date of dispossession; or (b) against the Government. (3) No appeal shall lie from any order or decree passed in any suit instituted under this section, nor shall any review of any such order or decree be allowed. (4) Nothing in this section shall bar any person from suing to establish his title to such property and to recover possession thereof.
- (The words "through whom he has been in possession or any person" were inserted in sub-section (1) by Act 18 of 2018, with effect from 1 October 2018.)
- Section 7. Recovery of specific movable property. A person entitled to the possession of specific movable property may recover it in the manner provided by the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (5 of 1908). Explanation 1. —A trustee may sue under this section for the possession of movable property to the beneficial interest in which the person for whom he is trustee is entitled. Explanation 2. —A special or temporary right to the present possession of movable property is sufficient to support a suit under this section.
- Section 8. Liability of person in possession, not as owner, to deliver to persons entitled to immediate possession. Any person having the possession or control of a particular article of movable property, of which he is not the owner, may be compelled specifically to deliver it to the person entitled to its immediate possession, in any of the following cases:— (a) when the thing claimed is held by the defendant as the agent or trustee of the plaintiff; (b) when compensation in money would not afford the plaintiff adequate relief for the loss of the thing claimed; (c) when it would be extremely difficult to ascertain the actual damage caused by its loss; (d) when the possession of the thing claimed has been wrongfully transferred from the plaintiff. Explanation. —Unless and until the contrary is proved, the court shall, in respect of any article of movable property claimed under clause (b) or clause (c) of this section, presume— (a) that compensation in money would not afford the plaintiff adequate relief for the loss of the thing claimed, or, as the case may be; (b) that it would be extremely difficult to ascertain the actual damage caused by its loss.
- Related provisions (Limitation Act, 1963, Schedule)
- Article 64. For possession of immovable property based on previous possession and not on title, when the plaintiff while in possession of the property has been dispossessed. – Twelve years. – The date of dispossession.
- Article 65. For possession of immovable property or any interest therein based on title. – Twelve years. – When the possession of the defendant becomes adverse to the plaintiff.
- In simple words
- Section 5: a person entitled to possess particular land or a building may sue for it in the ordinary way under the Code of Civil Procedure. Section 6: a person put out of land or a building without his consent and without due process of law may sue to get it back. The defendant cannot defend by saying he owns it. The suit must be filed within six months and cannot be against the Government. There is no appeal or review. Anyone may still file a separate suit on title. Section 7: the same as Section 5, for particular goods. A trustee may sue, and a special or temporary right to present possession is enough. Section 8: a person who holds a particular article but does not own it can be made to hand over that very article, in four cases. In two of them the court presumes that money is not enough. Articles 64 and 65: an ordinary suit on earlier possession, or on title, may be filed within 12 years.

## Meaning and Definition
Possession here means actual control of the property. Settled possession means possession that has continued for some time with the owner's knowledge. The law protects it even against the owner. In Krishna Ram Mahale (1989) the Supreme Court held that such a person "cannot be dispossessed by the owner of the property except by recourse to law", even if he has no right to remain.

The question this part of the Act answers. Who is entitled to possession, and how may he get it back?

The answer for land is a choice between two suits. A title suit under Section 5 proves ownership and decides it for good. A possessory suit under Section 6 is quick, but it proves only earlier possession and unlawful removal. It does not decide who owns the land.

Why the law has this rule. If every owner could retake property by force whenever he believed he was right, disputes would end in violence. The Privy Council's words, approved by the Supreme Court, are: "In India persons are not permitted to take forcible possession; they must obtain such possession as they are entitled to through a Court."

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 5 is a title suit under the Code of Civil Procedure (12 years, Article 65). Section 6 is a quick suit on possession. Section 6: six months, not against the Government, no appeal or review, title no defence. Since 2018, a person through whom the dispossessed person was in possession can also sue. Section 6(4) always saves a separate suit on title. After six months, a suit on earlier possession lies within 12 years (Article 64), but the defendant may plead title. Settled possession is protected even against the true owner ( Lallu Yeshwant Singh ; Krishna Ram Mahale ). Section 7: recovery of movables. A trustee may sue, and a special or temporary right to possession is enough. Section 8: four cases where a non-owner must deliver the article, with a presumption for the plaintiff in clauses (b) and (c).
