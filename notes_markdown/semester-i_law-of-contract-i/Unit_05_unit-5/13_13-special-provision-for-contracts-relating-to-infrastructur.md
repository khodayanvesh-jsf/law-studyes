# 13. Special provision for contracts relating to infrastructure projects
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/infrastructure-projects-special-provisions/

## In One Minute
A single injunction can stall a road, a power plant or a metro line for years, over a private dispute between a contractor and a public body. The Specific Relief (Amendment) Act, 2018 (Act 18 of 2018), in force from 1 October 2018, built a small code to prevent this. It bars injunctions that would delay an infrastructure project, creates Special Courts, and sets a time limit for suits. The code is in Sections 20A, 20B, 20C and 41(ha), with a Schedule that lists the projects covered. In exams, you must state each section, name the five categories in the Schedule and explain why the bar is limited to injunctions.

## Legal Provision
- Section 20A. Special provisions for contract relating to infrastructure project. (1) No injunction shall be granted by a court in a suit under this Act involving a contract relating to an infrastructure project specified in the Schedule, where granting injunction would cause impediment or delay in the progress or completion of such infrastructure project. Explanation. —For the purposes of this section, section 20B and clause (ha) of section 41, the expression “infrastructure project” means the category of projects and infrastructure Sub-Sectors specified in the Schedule. (2) The Central Government may, depending upon the requirement for development of infrastructure projects, and if it considers necessary or expedient to do so, by notification in the Official Gazette, amend the Schedule relating to any Category of projects or Infrastructure Sub-Sectors. (3) Every notification issued under this Act by the Central Government shall be laid, as soon as may be after it is issued, before each House of Parliament, while it is in session, for a total period of thirty days which may be comprised in one session or in two or more successive sessions, and if, before the expiry of the session immediately following the session or the successive sessions aforesaid, both Houses agree in making any modification in the notification or both Houses agree that the notification should not be made, the notification shall thereafter have effect only in such modified form or be of no effect, as the case may be; so, however, that any such modification or annulment shall be without prejudice to the validity of anything previously done under that notification.
- Section 20B. Special Courts. The State Government, in consultation with the Chief Justice of the High Court, shall designate, by notification published in the Official Gazette, one or more Civil Courts as Special Courts, within the local limits of the area to exercise jurisdiction and to try a suit under this Act in respect of contracts relating to infrastructure projects.
- Section 20C. Expeditious disposal of suits. Notwithstanding anything contained in the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (5 of 1908), a suit filed under the provisions of this Act shall be disposed of by the court within a period of twelve months from the date of service of summons to the defendant: Provided that the said period may be extended for a further period not exceeding six months in aggregate after recording reasons in writing for such extension by the court.
- Section 41. Injunction when refused. An injunction cannot be granted— . . . (ha) if it would impede or delay the progress or completion of any infrastructure project or interfere with the continued provision of relevant facility related thereto or services being the subject matter of such project.

- In simple words
- Section 20A(1): no court shall grant an injunction in a suit under the Act involving a contract relating to an infrastructure project specified in the Schedule, where the injunction would cause impediment (obstacle) or delay in the progress or completion of the project. Explanation to Section 20A: for Sections 20A, 20B and 41(ha), "infrastructure project" means the category of projects and infrastructure sub-sectors specified in the Schedule. Section 20A(2): the Central Government may amend the Schedule by notification (official announcement) in the Official Gazette, depending on the needs of infrastructure development, if it considers it necessary or expedient (useful and suitable). Section 20A(3): every such notification is laid before each House of Parliament for a total of 30 days. If both Houses agree to modify it, it has effect only as modified. If both Houses agree it should not be made, it has no effect. Anything already done under it stays valid. Section 20B: the State Government, in consultation with the Chief Justice of the High Court, designates by notification one or more Civil Courts as Special Courts, within local limits, to try suits under the Act about contracts relating to infrastructure projects. Section 20C: notwithstanding the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, a suit filed under the Act shall be disposed of within 12 months from the date of service of summons on the defendant. The court may extend this by not more than six months in aggregate, after recording reasons in writing. The section speaks of "a suit filed under the provisions of this Act", so it is not limited to infrastructure suits. Section 41(ha): an injunction cannot be granted if it would impede or delay the progress or completion of any infrastructure project, or interfere with the continued provision of the related facility or services that are the subject matter of the project. The Schedule: five categories of projects (Transport; Energy; Water and Sanitation; Communication; Social and Commercial Infrastructure), each with listed sub-sectors.

## Meaning and Definition
An infrastructure project, for this purpose, is a project listed in the Schedule. The Act does not use the ordinary meaning of the word. If a project is not in the Schedule, Sections 20A, 20B and 41(ha) do not apply to it.

The question these sections answer. When a dispute arises under a contract for a public project, can a court freeze the project by injunction while the dispute is decided?

Why the law has this rule. The public needs the road, the power line or the water plant. The parties' quarrel is usually about money. The 2018 scheme lets the money dispute go on, by suit, damages or arbitration, while the project keeps moving.

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 20A: no injunction that would impede or delay a Schedule infrastructure project. Schedule: five categories. The Central Government may amend it; Parliament supervises. Section 41(ha): the same bar in the list of refusals, plus protection of the facility or services. Section 20B: Special Courts. Section 20C: 12 months from service of summons, extendable by up to six months in aggregate. Only injunctions are barred. Damages, arbitration and other claims remain.
