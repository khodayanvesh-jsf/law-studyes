# 9. Mistake
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/mistake/

## In One Minute
A mistake is a wrong belief about a fact or about the law, held when the agreement is made. The Act does not define it; it states its effect : Kind Effect Section Both parties mistaken about a fact essential to the agreement Agreement void 20 Wrong opinion about value Not a mistake of fact; no relief 20, Explanation Mistake about Indian law Contract not voidable 21 Mistake about foreign law Treated as a mistake of fact 21 One party mistaken about a fact Contract not voidable merely for that 22 So only one kind spoils the agreement completely: a shared mistake about an essential fact . A mistake by one party alone gives relief only where the other side caused it (fraud or misrepresentation ), or where a person signed a document radically different from what he thought ( non est factum ). In exams, the three sections and their illustrations are the core. In real life the rule tells you that you carry the risk of your own error.

## Legal Provision
- Section 20. Agreement void where both parties are under mistake as to matter of fact. Where both the parties to an agreement are under a mistake as to a matter of fact essential to the agreement, the agreement is void. Explanation. —An erroneous opinion as to the value of the thing which forms the subject-matter of the agreement, is not to be deemed a mistake as to a matter of fact. Illustrations. (a) A agrees to sell to B a specific cargo of goods supposed to be on its way from England to Bombay. It turns out that, before the day of the bargain, the ship conveying the cargo had been cast away and the goods lost. Neither party was aware of the facts. The agreement is void. (b) A agrees to buy from B a certain horse. It turns out that the horse was dead at the time of the bargain, though neither party was aware of the fact. The agreement is void. (c) A, being entitled to an estate for the life of B, agrees to sell it to C. B was dead at the time of the agreement, but both parties were ignorant of the fact. The agreement is void.
- Section 21. Effect of mistakes as to law. A contract is not voidable because it was caused by a mistake as to any law in force in India; but a mistake as to a law not in force in India has the same effect as a mistake of fact. Illustration. A and B make a contract grounded on the erroneous belief that a particular debt is barred by the Indian Law of Limitation: the contract is not voidable.
- Section 22. Contract caused by mistake of one party as to matter of fact. A contract is not voidable merely because it was caused by one of the parties to it being under a mistake as to a matter of fact.
- Section 14 (relevant part). Consent is said to be free when it is not caused by— … (5) mistake, subject to the provisions of sections 20, 21 and 22.
- In simple words
- Section 20: if both sides are wrong about a basic fact that the deal rests on, there is no deal at all. Being wrong about the price or worth of the thing does not count. Section 21: you cannot escape a contract by saying you did not know Indian law. Foreign law is treated as a fact. Section 22: if only you were wrong, that alone does not let you escape the contract.

## Meaning and Definition
Mistake means an erroneous belief, held at the time of the agreement , about a fact or about the law. The effect depends on three questions:

Whose mistake? Both parties, or only one? About what? A fact, Indian law, or foreign law? How important? Is the fact essential to the agreement, or only about value?

Section 20: three conditions.

Condition What it means 1. Both parties are mistaken A shared (mutual or common) mistake 2. About a matter of fact Not law, not opinion about value 3. The fact is essential to the agreement It goes to the root: existence of the thing, its identity, or a basic quality the deal rests on

Why the law treats mistakes differently. A shared mistake on an essential fact means there was nothing real to agree about; the agreement fails. But where only one side is wrong, the law protects the other side, who did nothing wrong and relied on the deal. Every person must look after his own interests.

Mistake and consent. Where the parties do not agree on the same thing in the same sense (Section 13), there is no consent at all. Raffles v. Wichelhaus (1864) is the standard example: two ships named Peerless .

## Key Points to Remember
- A mistake of both parties on an essential fact makes the agreement void (Section 20). A wrong opinion about value is not a mistake of fact. A mistake of one party does not make the contract voidable merely for that reason (Section 22). A mistake of Indian law has no effect. A mistake of foreign law is a mistake of fact (Section 21). A mistake caused by the other side's fraud or misrepresentation gives relief under Section 19. A signature on a radically different document may be disowned by non est factum, if the signer was not careless. Money paid under a void agreement or by mistake must be returned (Sections 65, 72).
