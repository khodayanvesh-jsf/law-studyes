# 13. Uncertain agreements
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/uncertain-agreements/

## In One Minute
An uncertain agreement is one whose meaning is not certain and cannot be made certain. Section 29 makes such an agreement void . A court cannot enforce a promise when it cannot tell what was promised. The problem is vagueness, not illegality. Nothing wrong is being done; the words simply do not say enough. The key phrase is "capable of being made certain" . If the agreement itself, the nature of the trade, or an agreed method settles the meaning, the agreement stands. In exams this is a short, predictable question built on the Act's six Illustrations. In real life it decides whether a bargain with a missing price, an unclear quantity or two possible figures can be enforced at all.

## Legal Provision
- Section 29. Agreements void for uncertainty. Agreements, the meaning of which is not certain, or capable of being made certain, are void. Illustrations (a) A agrees to sell to B "a hundred tons of oil". There is nothing whatever to show what kind of oil was intended. The agreement is void for uncertainty. (b) A agrees to sell to B one hundred tons of oil of a specified description, known as an article of commerce. There is no uncertainty here to make the agreement void. (c) A, who is a dealer in cocoanut-oil only, agrees to sell to B "one hundred tons of oil". The nature of A's trade affords an indication of the meaning of the words, and A has entered into a contract for the sale of one hundred tons of cocoanut-oil. (d) A agrees to sell to B "all the grain in my granary at Ramnagar". There is no uncertainty here to make the agreement void. (e) A agrees to sell B "one thousand maunds of rice at a price to be fixed by C". As the price is capable of being made certain, there is no uncertainty here to make the agreement void. (f) A agrees to sell to B "my white horse for rupees five hundred or rupees one thousand". There is nothing to show which of the two prices was to be given. The agreement is void.
- Sale of Goods Act, 1930, Section 9. Ascertainment of price. (1) The price in a contract of sale may be fixed by the contract or may be left to be fixed in manner thereby agreed or may be determined by the course of dealing between the parties. (2) Where the price is not determined in accordance with the foregoing provisions, the buyer shall pay the seller a reasonable price. What is a reasonable price is a question of fact dependent on the circumstances of each particular case.
- Sale of Goods Act, 1930, Section 10. Agreement to sell at valuation. (1) Where there is an agreement to sell goods on the terms that the price is to be fixed by the valuation of a third party and such third party cannot or does not make such valuation, the agreement is thereby avoided: Provided that, if the goods or any part thereof have been delivered to, and appropriated by, the buyer, he shall pay a reasonable price therefor. (2) Where such third party is prevented from making the valuation by the fault of the seller or buyer, the party not in fault may maintain a suit for damages against the party in fault.
- In simple words
- If the meaning of an agreement is not certain, and nothing can make it certain, the agreement is void. If the meaning can be made certain (by a trade description, the seller's trade, a clear identification, or an agreed method), the agreement is valid. In a sale of goods , a price left open is not fatal: it may be fixed by an agreed method or by the course of dealing, or else a reasonable price is paid.

## Meaning and Definition
The Act does not define "uncertain". Section 29 simply says that an agreement whose meaning is "not certain, or capable of being made certain" is void.

An agreement is uncertain when its terms are too vague to give a clear meaning. Section 29 covers two kinds of vagueness alike:

Kind What is wrong Illustration A missing or unclear term A term (kind of goods, price, quantity) is not stated and cannot be found (a) "a hundred tons of oil" Two readings with no way to choose The agreement gives alternatives and nothing shows which applies (f) "rupees five hundred or rupees one thousand"

The rule rests on a simple point: a court can enforce only what it can identify. The Latin maxim id certum est quod certum reddi potest ("that is certain which can be made certain") states the other side of the rule. A term need not be stated in exact figures, so long as there is a way to fix it.

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 29: an agreement whose meaning is not certain, or capable of being made certain, is void. The agreement stands if a trade description, the seller's trade, a clear identification or an agreed method makes the meaning certain. Illustration (f): two prices with no way to choose make the agreement void. In a sale of goods, a price left open is fixed by the agreed method or course of dealing, or a reasonable price is paid (SoGA Section 9). If a named valuer does not value, the agreement is avoided, but goods taken must be paid for at a reasonable price (SoGA Section 10). Uncertainty is about the words of the agreement. Mistake is about the parties' beliefs. Uncertain agreements are void, not illegal.
