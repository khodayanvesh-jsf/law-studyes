# 3. Contracts with insane persons and persons disqualified by law
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/unsound-mind-disqualified-persons/

## In One Minute
Section 11 says that, besides being a major, a person must be of sound mind and not disqualified by law to be competent to contract. Insane persons. The Act does not use the word "insane". It uses "sound mind" and "unsound mind". Section 12 gives a working test , not a medical label: at the time of making the contract, can the person understand it and form a rational judgment about its effect on his interests? A person who is usually of unsound mind may contract in a lucid interval . A person who is usually of sound mind may not contract while he is delirious from fever or so drunk that he cannot understand. An agreement made while of unsound mind is void . Suppliers of necessaries are paid from his property (Section 68). Persons disqualified by law. Some adults of sound mind are still limited by a particular law: an alien enemy , a foreign State, ruler or ambassador (cannot be sued without the Central Government's consent), an insolvent (cannot deal with property that has vested in the receiver ), and others. Each limit comes from its own law and has its own extent. Exams ask for the Section 12 test, the two rules on intervals, the effect of unsoundness and the list of disqualified persons. In real life these rules decide whether a buyer can enforce a deal with a very ill seller, or whether a shop can sue an embassy.

## Legal Provision
- Section 11. Who are competent to contract. Every person is competent to contract who is of the age of majority according to the law to which he is subject, and who is of sound mind, and is not disqualified from contracting by any law to which he is subject.
- Section 12. What is a sound mind for the purposes of contracting. A person is said to be of sound mind for the purpose of making a contract, if, at the time when he makes it, he is capable of understanding it and of forming a rational judgment as to its effect upon his interests. A person who is usually of unsound mind, but occasionally of sound mind, may make a contract when he is of sound mind. A person who is usually of sound mind, but occasionally of unsound mind, may not make a contract when he is of unsound mind. Illustrations (a) A patient in a lunatic asylum, who is at intervals of sound mind, may contract during those intervals. (b) A sane man, who is delirious from fever or who is so drunk that he cannot understand the terms of a contract, or form a rational judgment as to its effect on his interests, cannot contract whilst such delirium or drunkenness lasts.
- Section 68. Claim for necessaries supplied to person incapable of contracting, or on his account. If a person, incapable of entering into a contract, or any one whom he is legally bound to support, is supplied by another person with necessaries suited to his condition in life, the person who has furnished such supplies is entitled to be reimbursed from the property of such incapable person. Illustrations (a) A supplies B, a lunatic, with necessaries suitable to his condition in life. A is entitled to be reimbursed from B's property. (b) A supplies the wife and children of B, a lunatic, with necessaries suitable to their condition in life. A is entitled to be reimbursed from B's property.
- Section 183. Who may employ agent. Any person who is of the age of majority according to the law to which he is subject, and who is of sound mind, may employ an agent.
- Section 184. Who may be an agent. As between the principal and third persons, any person may become an agent, but no person who is not of the age of majority and of sound mind can become an agent, so as to be responsible to his principal according to the provisions in that behalf herein contained.
- Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, Section 83. When aliens may sue. Alien enemies residing in India with the permission of the Central Government, and alien friends, may sue in any Court otherwise competent to try the suit, as if they were citizens of India, but alien enemies residing in India without such permission, or residing in a foreign country, shall not sue in any such Court. Explanation. Every person residing in a foreign country, the Government of which is at war with India and carrying on business in that country without a licence in that behalf granted by the Central Government, shall, for the purpose of this section, be deemed to be an alien enemy residing in a foreign country.
- Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, Section 86(1). No foreign State may be sued in any Court otherwise competent to try the suit except with the consent of the Central Government certified in writing by a Secretary to that Government: Provided that a person may, as a tenant of immovable property, sue without such consent as aforesaid a foreign State from whom he holds or claims to hold the property.
- In simple words
- Section 11: sound mind and freedom from legal disqualification are two of the three tests of capacity. Section 12: the test is understanding plus rational judgment, at the time of contracting . Paragraphs 2 and 3 apply it to people whose state of mind changes. Section 68: a person who supplies necessaries to a person of unsound mind, or to his wife and children, is paid from his property . Sections 183 and 184: a person of unsound mind cannot employ an agent, and cannot be an agent who is answerable to his principal. CPC Section 83: an alien enemy living abroad, or living in India without the Central Government's permission, cannot sue in Indian courts. CPC Section 86: a foreign State (and, under Section 86(4), a foreign ruler, ambassador, envoy, High Commissioner and notified staff) cannot be sued without the Central Government's written consent, except by its tenant.

## Meaning and Definition
Person of unsound mind. A person who, at the time of making a contract , is not capable of understanding it or not capable of forming a rational judgment about its effect on his interests (Section 12). The Act's older words "lunatic" and "lunatic asylum" appear only in the illustrations.

The test has two limbs. Both must be present:

Understanding: can he grasp what the contract is, what he gives and what he gets? Rational judgment: can he weigh whether it is good or bad for him?

A person who can read the words but cannot judge whether selling his house for a third of its value harms him fails the second limb.

Three kinds of unsoundness under Section 12.

Kind Example Rule Usually unsound, at times sound A patient with clear periods between episodes May contract during a clear (lucid) interval (paragraph 2; Illustration (a)) Usually sound, at times unsound A healthy man in high fever, or dead drunk May not contract while that state lasts (paragraph 3; Illustration (b)) Unsound all the time A person who never developed the ability to understand (the older word is "idiot") Can never pass the Section 12 test

Person disqualified by law. An adult of sound mind whom a particular law prevents from contracting, or limits in contracting, suing or being sued. The words "by any law to which he is subject" in Section 11 point to that other law. The Contract Act itself lists no disqualified persons.

Why the law does this.

Unsound mind: consent means a real choice. A person who cannot understand or judge cannot really agree. The law protects him from being exploited. Disqualification: some limits protect the State (alien enemies in war), respect other countries (foreign States and envoys), or protect creditors (insolvents).

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 12 tests understanding and rational judgment at the time of contracting . A usually unsound person may contract in a lucid interval; a usually sound person may not while delirious or very drunk. An adult is presumed to be of sound mind; the person alleging unsoundness must prove it. An agreement made while of unsound mind is void; the court may order restoration of a benefit still held (Specific Relief Act Section 33(2)(b)). Necessaries are paid from the incapable person's property (Section 68), not by him personally. A person of unsound mind cannot employ an agent and is not responsible as an agent (Sections 183, 184). Disqualified persons are adults of sound mind limited by a particular law: alien enemy (CPC Section 83), foreign State and envoys (CPC Section 86), insolvent.
