# 7. Misrepresentation
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/misrepresentation/

## In One Minute
Misrepresentation is a false statement, or a similar wrong, that misleads the other party without any intent to deceive (Section 18). The person who makes it may honestly believe it. The other party relies on it and enters into the contract. Section 18 has three kinds: a positive assertion of something untrue, made without proper grounds, though the maker believes it (Section 18(1)); a breach of duty that, without intent to deceive, gains an advantage by misleading the other (Section 18(2)); causing, however innocently , a party to make a mistake about the substance of the thing (Section 18(3)). Effect. The contract is voidable at the misled party's option. He may instead insist on performance and be put in the position he would have been in if the statement had been true (Section 19). He has no right to avoid it if he could have found the truth "with ordinary diligence " (Exception), or if the statement did not cause his consent (Explanation). In exams, the line between misrepresentation and fraud earns the marks. In real life this covers the honest but careless seller, broker or agent.

## Legal Provision
- Section 18. "Misrepresentation" defined. "Misrepresentation" means and includes— (1) the positive assertion, in a manner not warranted by the information of the person making it, of that which is not true, though he believes it to be true; (2) any breach of duty which, without an intent to deceive, gains an advantage to the person committing it, or any one claiming under him; by misleading another to his prejudice, or to the prejudice of any one claiming under him; (3) causing, however innocently, a party to an agreement, to make a mistake as to the substance of the thing which is the subject of the agreement.
- Section 19 (relevant parts). When consent to an agreement is caused by coercion, fraud or misrepresentation, the agreement is a contract voidable at the option of the party whose consent was so caused. A party to a contract whose consent was caused by fraud or misrepresentation, may, if he thinks fit, insist that the contract shall be performed, and that he shall be put in the position in which he would have been if the representations made had been true. Exception. —If such consent was caused by misrepresentation or by silence, fraudulent within the meaning of section 17, the contract, nevertheless, is not voidable, if the party whose consent was so caused had the means of discovering the truth with ordinary diligence. Explanation. —A fraud or misrepresentation which did not cause the consent to a contract of the party on whom such fraud was practised, or to whom such misrepresentation was made, does not render a contract voidable. Illustration (b). A, by a misrepresentation, leads B erroneously to believe that, five hundred maunds of indigo are made annually at A's factory. B examines the accounts of the factory, which show that only four hundred maunds of indigo have been made. After this B buys the factory. The contract is not voidable on account of A's misrepresentation.
- In simple words
- Section 18(1): saying something untrue as a fact, when your information does not justify saying it, even though you believe it. Section 18(2): failing in a duty you owe, without meaning to deceive, in a way that misleads the other to his loss and brings you (or someone claiming under you) a gain. Section 18(3): innocently making the other party mistaken about what the thing really is. Section 19: the misled party may avoid the contract , or keep it and insist on being put where he would be if the statement were true. He loses the right to avoid if he could easily have found the truth, or if the statement did not make him agree.

## Meaning and Definition
Misrepresentation is an untrue statement of fact (or a breach of duty, or an innocent cause of mistake) that induces the other party to contract, made without intent to deceive .

Why the law allows avoidance. The misled party's consent was not free (Section 14). The law does not treat the maker as a cheat; it simply lets the misled party undo a bargain that rested on a false basis.

Misrepresentation and fraud . Both are false statements; the difference is the mind of the maker. Fraud (Section 17) needs an intent to deceive (or to induce the contract by deceit ). In Derry v. Peek (1889), the House of Lords held that "fraud is proved when it is shewn that a false representation has been made (1) knowingly, or (2) without belief in its truth, or (3) recklessly, careless whether it be true or false". A statement made in an honest belief is not fraud, even if there were no reasonable grounds for it. Under the Indian Act, such an honest but unwarranted statement falls within Section 18(1).

## Key Points to Remember
- Misrepresentation is an untrue statement, breach of duty or innocent cause of mistake, made without intent to deceive (Section 18). It must be a statement of fact, and it must have caused the consent. It makes the contract voidable at the misled party's option; he may instead insist on performance (Section 19). No avoidance if the truth could be discovered with ordinary diligence (Exception; Illustration (b)). On rescission: restore benefits (Section 64), compensation for damage (Section 75). Honest belief, even careless, is not fraud ( Derry v. Peek ). Right to rescind may be lost by affirmation, delay, third-party rights or impossibility of restitution.
