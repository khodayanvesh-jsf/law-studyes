# 4. Concepts of Free Consent
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/concepts-of-free-consent/

## In One Minute
Consent means two or more persons "agree upon the same thing in the same sense" (Section 13). This is the meeting of minds, or consensus ad idem . Free consent means that this consent was not caused by any of five factors (Section 14): Coercion (Section 15) Undue influence (Section 16) Fraud (Section 17) Misrepresentation (Section 18) Mistake (Sections 20, 21 and 22) Section 10 makes free consent an essential of every contract. Consent is "caused" by a factor if it "would not have been given but for" that factor. Effect. Coercion, undue influence, fraud and misrepresentation make the contract voidable at the option of the party whose consent was so caused (Sections 19, 19A). A mistake of fact by both parties makes the agreement void (Section 20). A mistake of Indian law, or a mistake of fact by one party only, does not make the contract voidable (Sections 21, 22). In exams, this is the base of every answer on Sections 13 to 22. In real life it decides whether a signature obtained by a threat, a trick or a false statement can be undone.

## Legal Provision
- Section 13. "Consent" defined. Two or more persons are said to consent when they agree upon the same thing in the same sense.
- Section 14. "Free consent" defined. Consent is said to be free when it is not caused by— (1) coercion, as defined in section 15, or (2) undue influence, as defined in section 16, or (3) fraud, as defined in section 17, or (4) misrepresentation, as defined in section 18, or (5) mistake, subject to the provisions of sections 20, 21 and 22. Consent is said to be so caused when it would not have been given but for the existence of such coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation or mistake.
- Section 19 (first and second paragraphs, Exception and Explanation). When consent to an agreement is caused by coercion, fraud or misrepresentation, the agreement is a contract voidable at the option of the party whose consent was so caused. A party to a contract whose consent was caused by fraud or misrepresentation, may, if he thinks fit, insist that the contract shall be performed, and that he shall be put in the position in which he would have been if the representations made had been true. Exception. —If such consent was caused by misrepresentation or by silence, fraudulent within the meaning of section 17, the contract, nevertheless, is not voidable, if the party whose consent was so caused had the means of discovering the truth with ordinary diligence. Explanation. —A fraud or misrepresentation which did not cause the consent to a contract of the party on whom such fraud was practised, or to whom such misrepresentation was made, does not render a contract voidable.
- Section 19A. Power to set aside contract induced by undue influence. When consent to an agreement is caused by undue influence, the agreement is a contract voidable at the option of the party whose consent was so caused. Any such contract may be set aside either absolutely or, if the party who was entitled to avoid it has received any benefit thereunder, upon such terms and conditions as to the Court may seem just.
- Section 20. Where both the parties to an agreement are under a mistake as to a matter of fact essential to the agreement, the agreement is void.
- Section 21. A contract is not voidable because it was caused by a mistake as to any law in force in India; but a mistake as to a law not in force in India has the same effect as a mistake of fact.
- Section 22. A contract is not voidable merely because it was caused by one of the parties to it being under a mistake as to a matter of fact.
- Section 2(i). An agreement which is enforceable by law at the option of one or more of the parties thereto, but not at the option of the other or others, is a voidable contract;
- In simple words
- Section 13: both sides must mean the same thing, in the same way. Section 14: that agreement must not have been produced by any of the five factors. The test is "but for": would he have agreed without it? Section 19: coercion , fraud or misrepresentation make the contract voidable at the victim's choice. After fraud or misrepresentation he may instead insist on performance as if the statement were true. He loses this right if he could have found the truth with ordinary care (Exception), or if the statement did not cause his consent (Explanation). Section 19A: undue influence also makes the contract voidable, and the court may set it aside on just terms if the victim got a benefit. Sections 20 to 22: a shared mistake of an essential fact makes the agreement void; a mistake of Indian law, or a one-sided mistake of fact, does not.

## Meaning and Definition
Consent (Section 13). Two or more persons "agree upon the same thing in the same sense". It has two parts:

the same thing (the same subject matter), and in the same sense (the same meaning of the terms).

If either is missing, there is no consent and no agreement at all . This is called absence of consent .

Free consent (Section 14). Consent that was not caused by coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation or mistake. Here the parties did agree on the same thing, but the way the agreement came about is defective. This is called consent that is not free .

Causation: the "but for" test. Section 14, last paragraph: consent is caused by a factor "when it would not have been given but for the existence of" that factor. So the question is not only "was there a threat or a lie?" but " did it make him agree? "

Why the law requires free consent.

A contract binds because both parties chose it. A choice forced by a threat, or based on a lie, is not a real choice. But the law also values certainty. So, in most cases, it lets the victim decide whether to keep the contract or set it aside, instead of wiping it out.

Where free consent sits among the essentials. Section 10: "All agreements are contracts if they are made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for a lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and are not hereby expressly declared to be void." Free consent is one of these essentials; the others are covered in their own topics.

## Key Points to Remember
- Consent is agreeing on the same thing in the same sense (Section 13). Consent is free if not caused by coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation or mistake (Section 14). "Caused" means it would not have been given "but for" the factor. Coercion, undue influence, fraud and misrepresentation make a contract voidable; a mutual mistake of essential fact makes it void. A mistake of Indian law, or a one-sided mistake of fact, does not make a contract voidable. No avoidance for misrepresentation or fraudulent silence if the truth could be discovered with ordinary diligence. A party who rescinds must restore benefits (Section 64) and may claim compensation (Section 75). Suit to rescind: three years from knowledge of the facts (Article 59).
