# 5. Coercion
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/coercion/

## In One Minute
Coercion is forcing a person to agree by: committing, or threatening to commit, an act forbidden by the Indian Penal Code (now read with the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023), or unlawfully detaining, or threatening to detain, any property , "to the prejudice of any person whatever", with the intention of causing any person to enter into an agreement (Section 15). Three things make Section 15 wide: the threat alone is enough; the act need not be carried out; the coercer may be anyone , even a stranger to the contract; the person harmed may be anyone , not only the person who signs. Effect. A contract caused by coercion is voidable at the option of the party coerced (Section 19). Money paid or goods delivered under coercion must be repaid or returned (Section 72). Coercion is a regular short-answer and problem question. In real life it covers threats of violence, and a creditor or pawnbroker who keeps someone's goods until they sign something new.

## Legal Provision
- Section 15. "Coercion" defined. "Coercion" is the committing, or threatening to commit, any act forbidden by the Indian Penal Code (45 of 1860) or the unlawful detaining, or threatening to detain, any property, to the prejudice of any person whatever, with the intention of causing any person to enter into an agreement. Explanation. —It is immaterial whether the Indian Penal Code (45 of 1860) is or is not in force in the place where the coercion is employed. Illustration A, on board an English ship on the high seas, causes B to enter into an agreement by an act amounting to criminal intimidation under the Indian Penal Code (45 of 1860). A afterwards sues B for breach of contract at Calcutta. A has employed coercion, although his act is not an offence by the law of England, and although section 506 of the Indian Penal Code (45 of 1860) was not in force at the time when or place where the act was done.
- Section 19 (first paragraph). When consent to an agreement is caused by coercion, fraud or misrepresentation, the agreement is a contract voidable at the option of the party whose consent was so caused.
- Section 72. A person to whom money has been paid, or anything delivered, by mistake or under coercion, must repay or return it. Illustration (b). A railway company refuses to deliver up certain goods to the consignee, except upon the payment of an illegal charge for carriage. The consignee pays the sum charged in order to obtain the goods. He is entitled to recover so much of the charge as was illegally excessive.
- Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, Section 358(1). The Indian Penal Code (45 of 1860) is hereby repealed.
- General Clauses Act, 1897, Section 8(1). Where this Act, or any Central Act or Regulation made after the commencement of this Act, repeals and re-enacts, with or without modification, any provision of a former enactment, then references in any other enactment or in any instrument to the provision so repealed shall, unless a different intention appears, be construed as references to the provision so re-enacted.
- In simple words
- Section 15: coercion = a crime (done or threatened) or unlawful holding of property (done or threatened) + aimed at making someone agree. Anyone may use it, and anyone may be harmed by it. Explanation: the place does not matter. The act is judged by the Indian criminal code even if that code does not apply where it was done. Section 19: the coerced party may choose to avoid the contract . Section 72: money or goods given under coercion must be returned. BNS Section 358 and General Clauses Act Section 8(1): the Indian Penal Code has been repealed and re-enacted as the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023. The words "Indian Penal Code" in Section 15 are still in the Act, but by Section 8(1) they are read as references to the matching provisions of the Sanhita, unless a different intention appears.

## Meaning and Definition
Coercion is pressure that the criminal law or the law of property forbids, used to make a person agree.

The elements of Section 15.

Element What it means 1. Committing or threatening to commit an act forbidden by the Indian Penal Code (now the BNS) Hurt, wrongful confinement, criminal intimidation, mischief to property, and so on. A threat is enough or unlawfully detaining, or threatening to detain, any property Keeping someone's goods with no legal right to keep them 2. To the prejudice of any person whatever Someone must be harmed or threatened with harm. It may be the person who signs, a relative, a stranger, or even the coercer himself ( Chikkam Ammiraju ) 3. With the intention of causing any person to enter into an agreement The act must be aimed at getting an agreement. A threat made for some other reason is not coercion 4. Causation (Section 14) Consent must have been caused by it: it "would not have been given but for" the coercion

Why the law treats it this way. Consent obtained through fear is not a real choice. But the coerced party may still want the bargain, so the law lets him choose (voidable), instead of making the contract void.

Coercion and duress. English law uses the word duress . Duress in its classic form is narrower: it is actual or threatened violence or unlawful constraint to the person, usually by a party to the contract or someone acting for him. Section 15 is wider: it covers threats to property, threats by strangers and threats to anyone.

## Key Points to Remember
- Coercion is committing or threatening an act forbidden by the IPC (now read with the BNS), or unlawfully detaining or threatening to detain property, to make any person agree. The coercer may be a stranger; the prejudice may be to any person. The place where it is used does not matter (Explanation). The contract is voidable at the coerced party's option (Section 19). Money paid or goods delivered under coercion must be returned (Section 72). Chikkam Ammiraju : threat of suicide is coercion (majority). Undue influence needs a dominating relationship; coercion does not.
