# 11. Immoral agreements and various heads of public policy
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/immoral-agreements-and-various-heads-of-public-policy/

## In One Minute
An agreement is void if the Court regards its object or consideration as immoral or opposed to public policy (Section 23). The Act does not list the heads of public policy. Courts apply a settled set of heads worked out in decided cases, and use the doctrine cautiously . Immoral in Section 23 has a narrow meaning. The Supreme Court has said that judicial decisions confined it to sexual immorality ( Gherulal Parakh ). It does not need a crime: Illustration (k) is void "though the letting may not be punishable". The Act also declares three kinds of agreement void by their own sections: restraint of marriage of any person other than a minor (Section 26); restraint of trade , with one exception for sale of goodwill (Section 27); restraint of legal proceedings , with exceptions for arbitration and bank guarantees (Section 28). In exams, restraint of trade is the favourite problem. In real life it decides whether a non-compete clause in your job letter can be enforced.

## Legal Provision
- Section 23 (relevant part). The consideration or object of an agreement is lawful, unless— … the Court regards it as immoral, or opposed to public policy. In each of these cases, the consideration or object of an agreement is said to be unlawful. Every agreement of which the object or consideration is unlawful is void. Illustrations. (f) A promises to obtain for B an employment in the public service and B promises to pay 1,000 rupees to A. The agreement is void, as the consideration for it is unlawful. (h) A promises B to drop a prosecution which he has instituted against B for robbery, and B promises to restore the value of the things taken. The agreement is void, as its object is unlawful. (j) A, who is B's mukhtar, promises to exercise his influence, as such, with B in favour of C, and C promises to pay 1,000 rupees to A. The agreement is void, because it is immoral. (k) A agrees to let her daughter to hire to B for concubinage. The agreement is void, because it is immoral, though the letting may not be punishable under the Indian Penal Code.
- Section 26. Agreement in restraint of marriage, void. Every agreement in restraint of the marriage of any person, other than a minor, is void.
- Section 27. Agreement in restraint of trade, void. Every agreement by which any one is restrained from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business of any kind, is to that extent void. Exception 1. One who sells the good-will of a business may agree with the buyer to refrain from carrying on a similar business, within specified local limits, so long as the buyer, or any person deriving title to the good-will from him, carries on a like business therein, provided that such limits appear to the Court reasonable, regard being had to the nature of the business.
- Section 28. Agreements in restraint of legal proceedings, void. Every agreement,— (a) by which any party thereto is restricted absolutely from enforcing his rights under or in respect of any contract, by the usual legal proceedings in the ordinary tribunals, or which limits the time within which he may thus enforce his rights; or (b) which extinguishes the rights of any party thereto, or discharges any party thereto, from any liability, under or in respect of any contract on the expiry of a specified period so as to restrict any party from enforcing his rights, is void to the extent. Exception 1. A contract by which two or more persons agree that any dispute which may arise between them in respect of any subject or class of subjects shall be referred to arbitration, and that only the amount awarded in such arbitration shall be recoverable in respect of the dispute so referred. Exception 2. Any contract in writing, by which two or more persons agree to refer to arbitration any question between them which has already arisen, or any provision of any law in force for the time being as to references to arbitration. Exception 3. A contract in writing by which any bank or financial institution stipulate a term in a guarantee … for extinguishment of the rights or discharge of any party thereto from any liability … on the expiry of a specified period which is not less than one year from the date of occurring or non-occurring of a specified event.
- Indian Partnership Act, 1932. Each of these provisions applies " notwithstanding anything contained in section 27 of the Indian Contract Act":
- Section 11(2): partners may agree that a partner "shall not carry on any business other than that of the firm while he is a partner". Section 36(2): an outgoing partner may agree not to carry on a similar business within a specified period or local limits, valid if the restrictions are reasonable. Section 54: partners may, on or in anticipation of dissolution, agree not to carry on a similar business within a specified period or local limits, valid "if the restrictions imposed are reasonable". Section 55(3): a partner may, on sale of the firm's goodwill , agree with the buyer not to carry on a similar business within a specified period or local limits, valid if the restrictions are reasonable.
- In simple words
- Section 23: if the Court finds the purpose or the price of a deal immoral, or harmful to the public, the deal is void. Section 26: you cannot validly bind an adult not to marry. Section 27: you cannot validly bind a person not to carry on his work or business, even partly, except when he sells his business's goodwill (and in a few partnership cases). Section 28: you cannot validly shut someone out of the courts or shorten the time the law gives him to sue; but you can agree to go to arbitration.

## Meaning and Definition
Immoral agreement . An agreement whose object or consideration the Court regards as immoral. In Gherulal Parakh v. Mahadeodas Maiya (1959), the Supreme Court said "immoral" is "a very comprehensive word", but Section 23 shows "the legislative intention to give it a restricted meaning". Decided cases and text-book writers "confined it, with every justification, only to sexual immorality", and the words "the court regards it as immoral" show it "should be confined to the principles recognized and settled by Courts".

Public policy . The general good of the community, as the courts protect it. In Gherulal Parakh the Supreme Court summarised the doctrine:

it is "an illusive concept", described as an "unruly horse"; the primary duty of a court is "to enforce a promise which the parties have made and to uphold the sanctity of contracts"; the doctrine "is only a branch of common law" and "is governed by precedents; the principles have been crystallized under different heads"; it "should only be invoked in clear and incontestable cases of harm to the public"; "though the heads are not closed … it is advisable in the interest of stability of society not to make any attempt to discover new heads in these days".

Immoral, opposed to public policy, forbidden by law. All three make an agreement void under Section 23. They differ in how the Court identifies them.

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 23 voids agreements that the Court regards as immoral or opposed to public policy. "Immoral" is confined to settled heads (sexual immorality); no crime is needed (Illustration (k)). Courts apply public policy to settled heads and only in clear cases of harm to the public. Section 26: restraint of marriage of an adult is void. Section 27: even a partial restraint of trade is void, unless within the goodwill exception or a Partnership Act exception; no reasonableness test. Covenants operating during employment are generally valid; post-employment restraints are void. Section 28: an absolute bar on suing, or a shorter time to sue, is void; arbitration and bank guarantees of at least one year are saved.
