# 7. Quantum merit
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4

## In One Minute
Quantum meruit is Latin for "as much as he has earned". The syllabus calls it "quantum merit". It is a claim for a reasonable price for work done or goods supplied, when no contract price binds the parties. The contract may be void, the price may never have been fixed, or the other side may have stopped the work. The claim is for the value of what was given. It is not a claim for profit, and it is not a claim for damages. In exams, the grounds, the limit (no claim where a valid contract fixes the price) and Alopi Parshad earn the marks. In real life, it pays freelancers, builders and professionals whose paperwork failed.

## Legal Provision
- The Act has no section called " quantum meruit ". The claim rests on Sections 65 and 70 and on the law of implied contract .
- Section 65. Obligation of person who has received advantage under void agreement, or contract that becomes void. When an agreement is discovered to be void, or when a contract becomes void, any person who has received any advantage under such agreement or contract is bound to restore it, or to make compensation for it to the person from whom he received it. Illustrations (b) A contracts with B to deliver to him 250 maunds of rice before the first of May. A delivers 130 maunds only before that day, and none after. B retains the 130 maunds after the first of May. He is bound to pay A for them. (c) A, a singer, contracts with B, the manager of a theatre, to sing at his theatre for two nights in every week during the next two months, and B engages to pay her a hundred rupees for each night's performance. On the sixth night, A wilfully absents herself from the theatre, and B, in consequence, rescinds the contract. B must pay A for the five nights on which she had sung. (d) A contracts to sing for B at a concert for 1,000 rupees, which are paid in advance. A is too ill to sing. A is not bound to make compensation to B for the loss of the profits which B would have made if A had been able to sing, but must refund to B the 1,000 rupees paid in advance.
- Section 70. Obligation of person enjoying benefit of non-gratuitous act. Where a person lawfully does anything for another person, or delivers anything to him, not intending to do so gratuitously, and such other person enjoys the benefit thereof, the latter is bound to make compensation to the former in respect of, or to restore, the thing so done or delivered.
- Section 73 (third paragraph). Compensation for failure to discharge obligation resembling those created by contract. —When an obligation resembling those created by contract has been incurred and has not been discharged, any person injured by the failure to discharge it is entitled to receive the same compensation from the party in default, as if such person had contracted to discharge it and had broken his contract.
- In simple words
- Section 65: when an agreement turns out to be void , or a contract becomes void, a person who has received any benefit under it must give it back, or pay for it. Illustration (b): B keeps 130 of the 250 maunds of rice, so he must pay for them. A maund is an old Indian unit of weight. Section 70: a person who lawfully does something for another, not meaning it as a gift, is paid compensation if the other enjoys the benefit. Section 73, third paragraph: when an obligation resembling a contract is not discharged, the injured person gets the same compensation as if there had been a contract and it had been broken.

## Meaning and Definition
The Latin phrase quantum meruit means "as much as he has earned". The syllabus spells it "quantum merit"; it means the same claim.

The Supreme Court's words. In Alopi Parshad and Sons Ltd. v. Union of India (1960) the Court said: "Compensation quantum meruit is awarded for work done or services rendered, when the price thereof is not fixed by a contract". It added that "Quantum meruit is but reasonable compensation awarded on implication of a contract to remunerate".

Why the law has this rule. The reason is unjust enrichment . If a person has taken the benefit of another's work or goods, he should pay a fair price for it. The law fixes that price by the value of the work, not by any promise.

Where it fits. It is one of the claims based on quasi-contract (Sections 65 and 70), and it also serves as a remedy when a contract is broken.

## Key Points to Remember
- Quantum meruit means "as much as he has earned": a reasonable price for work done or goods supplied. It is available where a contract is void, no price was fixed, the other party stopped the work, or part performance was accepted. It is not available where a valid contract fixes the price. It measures the value of the benefit, not profit and not damages. A party who abandons an entire contract halfway cannot claim for the part done. The claimant cannot have both quantum meruit and damages for the same work.
