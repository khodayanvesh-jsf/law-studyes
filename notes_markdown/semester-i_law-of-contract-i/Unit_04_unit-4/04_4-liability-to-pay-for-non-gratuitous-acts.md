# 4. Liability to pay for non-gratuitous acts
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/gratuitous-non-gratuitous-acts/

## In One Minute
A person does work for you, or hands you goods, expecting to be paid. There is no valid contract . You accept the work or goods and use them. Section 70 says you must pay for them, or give them back. Three conditions must be met: a lawful act, no intention to give it free, and a benefit that you enjoyed. The person asked to pay must have been free to refuse. In exams, the three conditions and State of West Bengal v. B.K. Mondal & Sons earn the marks. In real life, it protects contractors and suppliers whose contract fails for a technical reason.

## Legal Provision
- Section 70. Obligation of person enjoying benefit of non-gratuitous act. Where a person lawfully does anything for another person, or delivers anything to him, not intending to do so gratuitously, and such other person enjoys the benefit thereof, the latter is bound to make compensation to the former in respect of, or to restore, the thing so done or delivered. Illustrations. (a) A, a tradesman, leaves goods at B's house by mistake. B treats the goods as his own. He is bound to pay A for them. (b) A saves B's property from fire. A is not entitled to compensation from B, if the circumstances show that he intended to act gratuitously.
- Related provisions
- Constitution of India, Article 299(1). All contracts made in the exercise of the executive power of the Union or of a State shall be expressed to be made by the President, or by the Governor of the State, as the case may be, and all such contracts and all assurances of property made in the exercise of that power shall be executed on behalf of the President or the Governor by such persons and in such manner as he may direct or authorise.
- Government of India Act, 1935, Section 175(3) (as summarised in the headnote to B.K. Mondal ): all contracts made in the exercise of the executive authority of a province shall be expressed to be made by the Governor of the province and shall be executed on behalf of the Governor by such persons and in such manner as he may direct or authorise.
- In simple words
- Where a person lawfully does something for another, or delivers something to him, not meaning it to be free of charge, and the other receives and uses the benefit, the other must pay compensation for it or give it back. Illustration (a): goods left at the wrong house by mistake, and used as one's own, must be paid for. Illustration (b): a person who saves another's property from fire, meaning it as a favour, cannot claim payment. Contracts of the Government must be made in the form Article 299(1) lays down (earlier, Section 175(3) of the 1935 Act).

## Meaning and Definition
Section 70 is the widest of the five quasi-contractual claims. It covers any lawful act or delivery that was not meant as a gift .

Gratuitous and non-gratuitous. A "gratuitous" act is one done free of charge, as a favour or gift. A "non-gratuitous" act is one done in the expectation of payment.

Why the law has this rule. The reason is unjust enrichment . The person who accepted the benefit should not keep it for nothing. In B.K. Mondal the Supreme Court said that Section 70 "deals with cases where there is no valid contract". The claim is for compensation or restoration. It is not for damages for breach of contract .

If no, there is no claim. Question 2: did he act without intending it as a gift? If it was meant as a gift, there is no claim (Illustration (b)). Question 3: could the other person have refused or returned the thing, and did he instead accept and enjoy the benefit? If he had no choice or did not accept, there is no claim. Question 4: is there a valid contract that fixes the payment for the same work? If yes, the claim lies under the contract, not Section 70. If no, all tests are met: the other person must pay compensation for the thing done or delivered, or restore it.

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 70 has three conditions: a lawful act, no intention to give it free, and a benefit enjoyed. "Lawfully" is an essential part of the section; the act must not be fraudulent, dishonest or gratuitous. The receiver must be free to refuse or return, and must voluntarily accept. The claim is for compensation or restoration, not damages for breach. It applies where there is no valid contract, including a Government contract made without the prescribed form. A request is not an element of Section 70. It does not apply where a valid contract fixes the payment. A minor is outside Section 70 because Section 68 provides for his case.
