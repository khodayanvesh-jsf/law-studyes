## In One Minute
Damages are money paid to a party who has suffered by a breach of contract. The aim is to put him, as far as money can, where he would have been if the contract had been performed. Damages compensate . They do not punish . Section 73 sets the limits. The loss must arise naturally from the breach, or be one the parties knew was likely when they made the contract. Remote and indirect loss is not paid. The kinds of damages are general (ordinary), special, nominal, exemplary, liquidated and unliquidated . Damages for mental distress are not given in an ordinary commercial contract. In exams, the kinds of damages with an example each, the rule in Hadley v. Baxendale , the market-price measure and the Act's illustrations earn the marks. In real life, they decide how much a wronged party can actually recover.

## Legal Provision
- Section 73. When a contract has been broken, the party who suffers by such breach is entitled to receive, from the party who has broken the contract, compensation for any loss or damage caused to him thereby, which naturally arose in the usual course of things from such breach, or which the parties knew, when they made the contract, to be likely to result from the breach of it. Such compensation is not to be given for any remote and indirect loss or damage sustained by reason of the breach. Compensation for failure to discharge obligation resembling those created by contract. When an obligation resembling those created by contract has been incurred and has not been discharged, any person injured by the failure to discharge it is entitled to receive the same compensation from the party in default, as if such person had contracted to discharge it and had broken his contract. Explanation. In estimating the loss or damage arising from a breach of contract, the means which existed of remedying the inconvenience caused by the non-performance of the contract must be taken into account.
- Section 74 (first part). When a contract has been broken, if a sum is named in the contract as the amount to be paid in case of such breach, or if the contract contains any other stipulation by way of penalty, the party complaining of the breach is entitled, whether or not actual damage or loss is proved to have been caused thereby, to receive from the party who has broken the contract reasonable compensation not exceeding the amount so named or, as the case may be, the penalty stipulated for.
- In simple words
- Section 73, first paragraph: pay for loss that arises naturally from the breach (first limb), and for loss both parties knew was likely when they contracted (second limb). Section 73, second paragraph: do not pay for remote and indirect loss. Section 73, third paragraph: the same rule applies to quasi-contractual obligations. Explanation: count what the injured party could have done to reduce the loss. Section 74: if the contract names a sum for breach, the court gives reasonable compensation up to that sum.

## Meaning and Definition
Damages are the compensation, in money, that the law awards to a person for loss caused to him by a breach of contract. The Act uses the word "compensation". "Damages" is the word used in courts and exam questions.

The aim is compensation, not punishment. The injured party should be no worse off and no better off than if the contract had been performed. That is why:

he gets his actual, proved loss, not a reward; he cannot claim remote loss; he cannot claim loss he could have avoided (mitigation); a penalty clause is cut down to reasonable compensation (Section 74).

## Key Points to Remember
- Damages compensate for loss caused by the breach. They do not punish. Section 73 pays for natural loss and for loss both parties knew was likely, but not for remote and indirect loss. The kinds are general, special, nominal, exemplary, liquidated and unliquidated . Special damages need knowledge of the special circumstances when the contract was made . Nominal damages are given when breach is proved but no loss. Exemplary damages are generally not given in contract. No damages for mental agony in an ordinary commercial contract. Hadley v. Baxendale (1854) is the source of the two limbs of Section 73. For goods, the measure is the market price against the contract price at the date of breach .
