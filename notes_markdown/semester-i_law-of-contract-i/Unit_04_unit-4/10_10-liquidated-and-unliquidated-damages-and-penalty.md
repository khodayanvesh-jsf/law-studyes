# 10. liquidated and unliquidated damages and penalty
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/liquidated-and-unliquidated-damages-and-penalty/

## In One Minute
Sometimes a contract fixes in advance what a party must pay if he breaks it. That is a liquidated sum, or, if it is meant to frighten, a penalty . Sometimes the contract says nothing, and the court works out the loss after the breach. Those are unliquidated damages, under Section 73. Section 74 governs every sum named for breach. The injured party gets reasonable compensation, never more than the sum named . Indian law does not split the named sum into a valid "liquidated damages" clause and a void "penalty" clause. Both are treated alike ( Fateh Chand , 1963). Loss is still essential, and where loss can be proved it must be proved ( Kailash Nath , 2015). In exams, Section 74, Fateh Chand , Kailash Nath , the upper limit and proof of loss earn the marks. In real life, the rule decides how much a seller can keep of an advance, or how much a buyer can deduct for late delivery.

## Legal Provision
- Section 74. When a contract has been broken, if a sum is named in the contract as the amount to be paid in case of such breach, or if the contract contains any other stipulation by way of penalty, the party complaining of the breach is entitled, whether or not actual damage or loss is proved to have been caused thereby, to receive from the party who has broken the contract reasonable compensation not exceeding the amount so named or, as the case may be, the penalty stipulated for. Explanation. A stipulation for increased interest from the date of default may be a stipulation by way of penalty. Exception. When any person enters into any bail-bond, recognizance or other instrument of the same nature, or, under the provisions of any law, or under the orders of the Central Government or of any State Government, gives any bond for the performance of any public duty or act in which the public are interested, he shall be liable, upon breach of the condition of any such instrument, to pay the whole sum mentioned therein. Explanation. A person who enters into a contract with Government does not necessarily thereby undertake any public duty, or promise to do an act in which the public are interested.
- Section 73 (summary). Compensation is given for loss that naturally arose in the usual course of things from the breach, or that the parties knew, when they made the contract, to be likely to result from it. It is not given for remote and indirect loss.
- In simple words
- Section 74: if the contract names a sum for breach, or contains any other penalty (such as forfeiting money already paid), the court gives reasonable compensation , and the sum named is the ceiling . Explanation: a jump in interest on default may be a penalty. Exception: bail-bonds, recognizances and bonds for a public duty are payable in full . A contract with the Government is not, by itself, a public duty. Section 73: where nothing is named, the court assesses the loss itself.

## Meaning and Definition
Liquidated damages are a sum the contract fixes in advance as payable on breach, as a genuine pre-estimate of the likely loss. Unliquidated damages are not fixed in advance. The court assesses them after the breach, under Section 73 . A penalty is a sum fixed mainly to frighten a party into performing ( in terrorem ). It is out of proportion to any likely loss. Earnest money is a deposit paid when the contract is made, as a sign that the payer means to perform. It usually goes towards the price.

The English background. Under English common law, a genuine pre-estimate was enforced in full as liquidated damages , and a penalty was not enforced at all. In Fateh Chand v. Balkishan Dass (1963), the Supreme Court said Section 74 is "an attempt to eliminate the somewhat elaborate refinements" of that English distinction. India has one rule for both : reasonable compensation not exceeding the sum named.

Why the law has this rule. Damages compensate and do not punish. A party should not get a windfall just because the contract named a large figure. But a fair pre-estimate saves the trouble of proving a loss that is hard to measure.

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 74 applies to a named sum and to any penalty , including forfeiture. The court awards reasonable compensation . The sum named is the upper limit . India does not distinguish liquidated damages from a penalty ( Fateh Chand ). Loss is a sine qua non. Where loss can be proved, it must be proved ( Kailash Nath ). Proof is excused where loss is hard to prove and the sum is a genuine pre-estimate ( ONGC v. Saw Pipes ). Forfeiture of reasonable earnest money: two lines of authority. Increased interest on default may be a penalty. An acceleration clause for a real debt is not. Bail-bonds and public duty bonds are payable in full . Unliquidated damages are assessed by the court under Section 73 .
