# 5. Rights of finder of lost goods
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester I) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/finder-of-goods/

## In One Minute
You find a wallet on the road and take it home. You do not become its owner. The law treats you as a bailee , a person who looks after another's goods. You must take care of the goods and return them to the owner. In return, you have a right to keep the goods until your expenses are paid. You may sue for a reward only if the owner offered one. In some cases you may sell the goods. In exams, the duties, Section 168, Section 169 and the loose-or-attached rule earn the marks. In real life, it settles who may keep a lost phone, a bracelet or a ring.

## Legal Provision
- Section 71. Responsibility of finder of goods. A person who finds goods belonging to another, and takes them into his custody, is subject to the same responsibility as a bailee.
- Section 168. Right of finder of goods, may sue for specific reward offered. The finder of goods has no right to sue the owner for compensation for trouble and expense voluntarily incurred by him to preserve the goods and to find out the owner; but he may retain the goods against the owner until he receives such compensation; and, where the owner has offered a specific reward for the return of goods lost, the finder may sue for such reward, and may retain the goods until he receives it.
- Section 169. When finder of thing commonly on sale may sell it. When a thing which is commonly the subject of sale is lost, if the owner cannot with reasonable diligence be found, or if he refuses, upon demand, to pay the lawful charges of the finder, the finder may sell it— (1) when the thing is in danger of perishing or of losing the greater part of its value, or, (2) when the lawful charges of the finder, in respect of the thing found, amount to two-thirds of its value.
- Related provisions (the bailee 's duties that Section 71 applies)
- Section 151. Care to be taken by bailee. In all cases of bailment the bailee is bound to take as much care of the goods bailed to him as a man of ordinary prudence would, under similar circumstances, take of his own goods of the same bulk, quality and value as the goods bailed.
- Section 152. Bailee when not liable for loss, etc., of thing bailed. The bailee, in the absence of any special contract, is not responsible for the loss, destruction or deterioration of the thing bailed, if he has taken the amount of care of it described in section 151.
- Section 154 (first sentence). If the bailee makes any use of the goods bailed which is not according to the conditions of the bailment, he is liable to make compensation to the bailor for any damage arising to the goods from or during such use of them.
- Section 160. It is the duty of the bailee to return, or deliver according to the bailor's directions, the goods bailed, without demand, as soon as the time for which they were bailed has expired, or the purpose for which they were bailed has been accomplished.
- Section 161. If, by the default of the bailee, the goods are not returned, delivered or tendered at the proper time, he is responsible to the bailor for any loss, destruction or deterioration of the goods from that time.
- Section 163. In the absence of any contract to the contrary, the bailee is bound to deliver to the bailor, or according to his directions, any increase or profit which may have accrued from the goods bailed. Illustration. A leaves a cow in the custody of B to be taken care of. The cow has a calf. B is bound to deliver the calf as well as the cow to A.
- Section 25(2) and Illustration (c). An agreement made without consideration is void, unless ... (2) it is a promise to compensate, wholly or in part, a person who has already voluntarily done something for the promisor ... (c) A finds B's purse and gives it to him. B promises to give A Rs. 50. This is a contract.
- In simple words
- Section 71: a person who finds another's goods and takes them into his keeping has the same responsibility as a bailee. Section 168: the finder cannot sue the owner for the trouble and expense he voluntarily took to preserve the goods and find the owner. He may keep the goods until he is paid. If the owner offered a specific reward, the finder may sue for it, and keep the goods until it is paid. Section 169: the finder may sell a thing commonly sold only if (a) the owner cannot be found with reasonable effort, or refuses to pay the finder's lawful charges, and (b) the thing may perish or lose most of its value, or the charges reach two-thirds of its value. Bailee's sections: ordinary care (Sections 151, 152), no unauthorised use (Section 154), return in time (Sections 160, 161), hand over any increase (Section 163). Section 25(2): a later promise by the owner to pay the finder is a valid contract even without fresh consideration .

## Meaning and Definition
A finder of goods is a person who finds goods belonging to another and takes them into his custody. Section 71 does not make him a bailee by contract. There is no contract between him and the owner. The Act treats him as a bailee so that the full set of bailee's duties applies to him.

Why the law has this rule. The reason is fairness to both sides. The owner has lost his goods through no wish of his own, and he should get them back unharmed. The finder is a stranger who took on a burden, so the law gives him rights to protect him.

Where it fits. This is one of the five quasi-contractual situations in Chapter V of the Act, "Of Certain Relations Resembling Those Created by Contract".

## Key Points to Remember
- A finder who takes goods into custody has the responsibility of a bailee (Section 71). He must take ordinary care, not use the goods, and return them with any increase. He cannot sue for his trouble and expense, but may keep the goods until he is paid (Section 168). He may sue for a reward only if a specific reward was offered. He may sell only a thing commonly sold, and only in the cases of Section 169. His title is good against everyone but the true owner ( Armory v. Delamirie ). Loose goods in a public place go to the finder; goods attached to land go to the landowner. A promise of payment made after the goods are returned is enforceable (Section 25(2)).
