# 12. Liability without fault
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/liability-without-fault/

## In One Minute
Most tort liability depends on fault. In some cases the law makes a person pay although he took every care and meant no harm. This is liability without fault, or no-fault liability. It rests on risk. A person who creates a danger and gains from it should bear the loss when the danger hurts others. The main forms are strict liability, absolute liability, statutory no-fault liability and vicarious liability.

## Legal Provision
- Section 164, Motor Vehicles Act, 1988: the owner of the motor vehicle or the insurer must pay Rs 5 lakh on death and Rs 2.5 lakh on grievous hurt . The claimant need not prove any wrongful act, neglect or default. Sections 140 to 144, the older no-fault provisions, were omitted from 1 April 2022. Employees' Compensation Act, 1923: an employer must pay compensation for injury caused by an accident arising out of and in the course of employment, whether or not he was at fault (section 3(1)). The employee gets fixed amounts by formula (section 4). The employer is not liable if the injury disables the employee for three days or less (section 3(1), first proviso). Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991, section 3: the owner must pay relief for death, injury or damage to property from an accident while handling a hazardous substance. The injured person, other than a workman , need not plead or prove any wrongful act, neglect or default (section 3(2)). Accident and hazardous substance (section 2): an accident is a fortuitous , sudden or unintended occurrence while handling a hazardous substance. It does not include an accident by reason only of war or radio-activity. Handling includes manufacture, storage, transport and use. Insurance and fund (sections 4 and 7A): the owner must take out insurance before handling the substance (section 4). The Environmental Relief Fund pays relief under awards of the Collector. It is built from an extra payment with the premium , penalties and interest. Claim procedure (sections 5 to 8): the Collector verifies the accident and invites claims. A claim is made to the Collector within five years of the accident (section 6(3)). The Collector holds a summary inquiry and tries to decide within three months (section 7(7)). The relief is in addition to other compensation, which is reduced by the relief paid (section 8). National Green Tribunal Act, 2010, section 17: for death, injury or damage to property or the environment from an accident or the adverse impact of an activity under an Act in Schedule I, the person responsible must pay relief. The Tribunal fixes it under the heads in Schedule II. A workman is excluded (section 17(1)). No-fault and apportionment: in case of an accident the Tribunal applies the principle of no fault (section 17(3)). If the harm is the combined effect of several activities, it may apportion liability equitably (section 17(2)). Sections 14, 15 and 20: the Tribunal hears civil cases raising a substantial question relating to environment, within six months of the cause of action (section 14). It may order relief, compensation and restitution of property and the environment, within five years, in addition to relief under the 1991 Act (section 15). It applies sustainable development, the precautionary principle and the polluter pays principle (section 20). Consumer Protection Act, 2019, sections 82 to 87 (product liability): a complainant may bring a product liability action against a product manufacturer, product service provider or product seller for harm caused by a defective product. Under section 84(2), a manufacturer is liable even if he proves he was not negligent or fraudulent in making the express warranty .

## Meaning and Definition
Liability without fault means that the defendant is liable for harm although he was not negligent and did not intend it. The claimant proves the harm and its link to the defendant's activity, and does not need to prove fault.

It is an exception to the fault theory. The law allows the exception only where the activity is dangerous or the law has a policy reason to protect victims.

## Key Points to Remember
- Liability without fault means liability without proof of negligence or intention. Reasons: risk, profit, difficulty of proving fault, prevention. Strict liability (Rylands v. Fletcher) has defences. Absolute liability (M.C. Mehta) has none. Section 164 of the Motor Vehicles Act: Rs 5 lakh on death and Rs 2.5 lakh on grievous hurt, no proof of fault. Sections 140 to 144 of the Motor Vehicles Act were omitted from 1 April 2022. Vicarious liability is also liability without the employer's fault. Rylands v. Fletcher needs a non-natural use of land and an escape. The House of Lords affirmed it in 1868. M.C. Mehta: compensation matches the size and capacity of the enterprise. Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action held that law binding and applied the polluter pays principle.
