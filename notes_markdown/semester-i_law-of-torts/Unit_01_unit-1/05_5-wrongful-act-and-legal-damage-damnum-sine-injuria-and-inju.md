# 5. Wrongful Act and Legal Damage - Damnum Sine Injuria and Injuria Sine Damno
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/wrongful-act-and-legal-damage/

## In One Minute
A tort needs a wrongful act and legal damage. Legal damage means the violation of a legal right. Plain loss is not enough, and a violated right can be enough even when no loss follows. Two Latin maxims mark the line. Damnum sine injuria is loss without a violated right, and there is no claim. Injuria sine damno is a violated right without loss, and the claim succeeds. In exams, expect these maxims, the cases, and a question on damages and remoteness.

## Legal Provision
- No Indian statute states the rule. The two maxims and the rules of damages come from decided cases. Section 2(m), Limitation Act, 1963: a tort is a civil wrong not exclusively a breach of contract or trust. A wrong needs a violated right to be a civil wrong. Articles 21, 32 and 226, Constitution of India: money compensation for violation of fundamental rights is a public law remedy. It sits beside the tort claim for damages.

## Meaning and Definition
A wrongful act is conduct that breaks a duty the law imposes. It may be an act, an omission or words, and it may be intended, careless or without fault where the law makes liability strict.

Damage in everyday speech is any loss. Legal damage is the violation of a legal right. The two do not always go together.

Damnum sine injuria : damage without legal injury. A real loss occurs, but no legal right is violated. No action lies. Injuria sine damno : legal injury without damage. A legal right is violated but no loss follows. The action lies.

Damages (with an s) are the money the court awards when the claim succeeds. Damage and damages are different words with different meanings.

## Key Points to Remember
- Legal damage means violation of a legal right. Loss alone is not enough. Damnum sine injuria: loss without legal injury, not actionable. Injuria sine damno: legal injury without loss, actionable per se. A lawful act is not made unlawful by a bad motive. Kinds of damages: nominal, contemptuous, ordinary (general and special), aggravated, exemplary. Damages aim at restitutio in integrum, are awarded once and for all, and are reduced by failure to mitigate. Remoteness: reasonable foresight of the kind of damage (The Wagon Mound No. 1). Thin skull rule applies.
