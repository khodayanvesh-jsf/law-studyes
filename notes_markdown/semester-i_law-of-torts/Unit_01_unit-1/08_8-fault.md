# 8. Fault
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/fault/

## In One Minute
Fault means blameworthy conduct. In tort, a person is at fault when he acts with intention, with recklessness , or without the care a reasonable person would take. The general rule is that a defendant is liable only if he is at fault. There are exceptions, where the law makes a person liable although he took every care. In exams, expect the fault theory, its contrast with no-fault liability, and how a plaintiff proves fault.

## Legal Provision
- No statute defines fault in tort. The idea comes from decided cases and writers. Section 114, Indian Evidence Act, 1872 (now section 119, Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023): the court may presume facts that are likely, given the common course of natural events, human conduct and business. The word is "may", so the court is not bound to presume. This supports the use of res ipsa loquitur. Statutes with no fault: section 164, Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 and the Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991 make some liability independent of fault. Under the 1991 Act the claimant need not plead or prove a wrongful act, neglect or default (section 3(2)). Section 164, Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, in detail: Sub-section (1) makes the owner of the motor vehicle or the authorised insurer liable to pay Rs 5 lakh for death and Rs 2.5 lakh for grievous hurt caused by an accident arising out of the use of a motor vehicle. Sub-section (2) says the claimant need not plead or establish that the death or hurt was due to any wrongful act, neglect or default of the owner, of the vehicle or of any other person. Sub-section (3) reduces the amount by compensation already paid under any other law. Sub-section (4) says ex gratia payments are not deducted. The older Chapter X, headed "Liability without fault in certain cases", included section 140 (compensation on the principle of no fault). The Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act, 2019 omitted the whole Chapter with effect from 1 April 2022.

## Meaning and Definition
Fault is conduct for which the law blames a person. It has three forms.

Intention. The person aimed at the result, or knew it was almost certain to follow. Recklessness. The person knew of the risk and went ahead without caring whether harm followed. Negligence. The person did not take the care that a reasonable person would take in the same situation.

The fault theory says that liability in tort should depend on fault. The opposite idea is liability without fault, which makes a person pay although he was careful.

## Key Points to Remember
- Fault is blameworthy conduct: intention, recklessness or negligence. The general rule: liability follows fault. The plaintiff must prove fault. Res ipsa loquitur lets the court infer it. Contributory negligence reduces damages. Exceptions: strict, absolute, vicarious and statutory liability. No-fault liability rests on risk, not blame. Foresight judged by the reasonable person is the test of carelessness (Wagon Mound No. 1). Intention needs no spite (Wilkinson v. Downton). A lawful act is not made wrongful by bad motive (Bradford v. Pickles). Care is no defence to strict liability: a hazardous activity makes the enterprise liable irrespective of negligence (Shail Kumari).
