# 14. Parties to proceedings
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/parties-to-proceedings/

## In One Minute
Parties to proceedings are the claimant who sues and the defendant who is sued. Civil courts try all civil suits unless a law bars them. This note covers the parties that the sources deal with. The Union and the States sue and are sued by their own names under Article 300. A suit against the Government needs two months' notice under section 80 of the Code of Civil Procedure. A minor sues through a next friend. A firm is liable for the wrongful acts of its partners under the Partnership Act.

## Legal Provision
- Section 9, Code of Civil Procedure, 1908: the courts have jurisdiction to try all suits of a civil nature, except suits whose cognizance is expressly or impliedly barred. Article 300, Constitution of India: the Government of India may sue or be sued by the name of the Union of India. The Government of a State may sue or be sued by the name of the State. This is in the like cases as the Dominion of India and the Provinces or Indian States could have sued or been sued before the Constitution, subject to any law made by Parliament or the State Legislature. Section 25, Indian Partnership Act, 1932: every partner is liable, jointly with all the other partners and also severally , for all acts of the firm done while he is a partner. Section 26, Indian Partnership Act: where a partner causes loss or injury to a third party by a wrongful act or omission in the ordinary course of the firm's business or with his partners' authority, the firm is liable to the same extent as the partner. Section 69(2), Indian Partnership Act: no suit to enforce a right arising from a contract can be filed by or for a firm against a third party unless the firm is registered and the persons suing are shown in the Register of Firms as partners. Order XXXII, Code of Civil Procedure: suits by or against minors and persons of unsound mind. A minor sues by a next friend, and a minor defendant has a guardian for the suit. Section 79, Code of Civil Procedure: in a suit by or against the Central Government the party is named "the Union of India". For a State Government it is "the State". Section 80, Code of Civil Procedure: no suit can be filed against the Government, or a public officer for an official act, until two months after written notice. Section 80(2) allows a suit for urgent or immediate relief with the leave of the Court. Sections 83, 84 and 86, Code of Civil Procedure: aliens suing, foreign States suing, and suits against foreign States, Rulers, Ambassadors and Envoys. A foreign State can be sued only with the consent of the Central Government, certified in writing by a Secretary. Section 9, Companies Act, 2013: a registered company is a body corporate that can sue and be sued by its name. Orders XXIX and XXX, Code of Civil Procedure: suits by or against corporations, and by or against firms in the firm's name.

## Meaning and Definition
The parties to a tort proceeding are the plaintiff (the person claiming) and the defendant (the person against whom the claim is made). Capacity is the legal ability to sue or to be sued.

The question has two sides. Who can bring a claim for a tort ? Who can be made to answer it? The answers differ for minors, persons of unsound mind, companies, the State, firms and foreign States.

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 9 CPC: civil courts try all civil suits unless barred. In Mohori Bibee the infant sued through his mother as next friend. The Union and States sue and are sued by their names (Article 300). A suit against the Government needs two months' notice (section 80 CPC). A minor sues by a next friend and a minor defendant has a guardian for the suit (Order XXXII). A foreign State can be sued only with the Central Government's consent (section 86 CPC). State liable for non-sovereign acts (Vidyawati). Kasturi Lal's sovereign-act defence was later limited (Chandrima Das). A foreign national can claim compensation from the Union as a public-law remedy (Chandrima Das). A firm is liable for a partner's wrongful act in the ordinary course of business (section 26 Partnership Act).
