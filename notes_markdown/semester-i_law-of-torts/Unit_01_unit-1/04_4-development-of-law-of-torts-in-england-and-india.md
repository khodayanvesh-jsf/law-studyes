# 4. Development of Law of Torts in England and India
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/development-of-law-of-torts-in-england-and-india/

## In One Minute
English tort law began with royal writs , which were fixed forms of complaint. Judges stretched them to cover new harms, and the Judicature Acts of 1873 to 1875 removed the writ system. Negligence then grew into a general principle. India received English common law through colonial courts. It uses the English rules only as far as they suit Indian conditions, and has added its own rules by statute and by Supreme Court decisions. This topic is asked as a long answer, usually with a timeline of cases.

## Legal Provision
- Judicature Acts, 1873 to 1875 (England): merged the common law and equity courts into one Supreme Court and ended the forms of action. Charter of 1726: set up Mayor's Courts in Bombay, Calcutta and Madras, which applied English law. "Justice, equity and good conscience": the standard that Indian courts used to decide cases with no statute or custom. It lets them take English rules only where suitable. Article 372, Constitution of India: continues the law in force before 26 January 1950 until it is changed. Article 300, Constitution of India: the Union and States may sue and be sued. It carries forward the pre-1950 position on suits against the Government. Section 9, Code of Civil Procedure, 1908: civil courts may try all suits of a civil nature, which lets them hear claims in tort.

## Meaning and Definition
The development of the law of torts is the story of how a list of narrow remedies became a body of general principles. In England the change came through judges working within the writ system and then outside it. In India the law arrived from England and was then reshaped by courts and Parliament.

A writ was a royal order that began a civil action, and each kind of wrong needed its own writ. A plaintiff who chose the wrong writ lost, even if the wrong was real.

## Key Points to Remember
- England: writ of trespass (direct harm) and trespass on the case (indirect harm). Judicature Acts, 1873 to 1875, ended the forms of action and merged law and equity courts. Donoghue v. Stevenson (1932) is the foundation of the modern law of negligence. India: Charter of 1726 and the standard of "justice, equity and good conscience". English rules apply in India only where they suit Indian conditions. India's own additions: absolute liability (M.C. Mehta), public law compensation, special statutes. Pollock's Civil Wrongs Bill of 1886 was never enacted. Before 1932 a maker owed no duty to a stranger to the contract (Winterbottom v. Wright); Donoghue v. Stevenson (3 to 2) removed that barrier. State liability in India runs from section 65 of the 1858 Act, through the 1915 and 1935 Acts, to Article 300(1). M.C. Mehta: "We no longer need the crutches of a foreign legal order."
