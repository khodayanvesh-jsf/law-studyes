# 7. General Principles of Liability in Torts
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/general-principles-of-liability-in-torts/

## In One Minute
A defendant is liable in tort when the main conditions come together. The core four are these. He did a wrongful act. It caused legal damage. The law attaches a mental element, or none, to that kind of wrong. No defence excuses him. The mental element is the part that varies. Some torts need intention, many need only negligence, and some impose liability without fault. Examiners ask for these conditions as a long answer, so learn the list and the three bases of liability.

## Legal Provision
- No single statute lists the conditions. They come from decided cases and from the statutes on particular torts. Section 2(m), Limitation Act, 1963: a tort is a civil wrong not exclusively a breach of contract or trust. Statutes that fix liability: the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 (section 164), the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 and the Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991 each impose liability on set facts.

## Meaning and Definition
The general principles of liability are the common conditions that decide whether a defendant must pay for a tort . They apply across torts, whatever the particular tort is.

A principle is "general" when it applies to most torts and not to one only. The specific rules of assault, nuisance or defamation then add their own special requirements.

## Key Points to Remember
- Conditions: wrongful act, legal damage, causation, fault where required, capacity, no defence. Fault means intention or negligence. It is the general basis of liability. No-fault liability applies to hazardous activities and under some statutes. Causation has a factual and a legal side. Motive is the reason for an act. Intention is its aim. Motive is generally irrelevant. Capacity and defences can end a claim. M.C. Mehta: absolute liability for hazardous industry, even if all care was taken, and without the Rylands exceptions.
