# 11. Negligence
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/negligence-as-a-basis-of-liability/

## In One Minute
Negligence is carelessness that the law blames. A person is negligent when he fails to take the care a reasonable person would take, and harm follows. It is the most common basis of liability in tort. The test is objective . The court does not ask what the defendant felt, but whether his conduct fell below the standard of a reasonable person. In exams, this appears as "Is negligence a state of mind or conduct?" and as a short definition.

## Legal Provision
- No Indian statute defines civil negligence in general. The meaning comes from case law, following English decisions. Section 106, Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023: causing death by a rash or negligent act, not amounting to culpable homicide, is punishable with up to five years and fine. If a registered medical practitioner does the act while performing a medical procedure, the term is up to two years. Section 125, Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023: doing any act so rashly or negligently as to endanger human life or the personal safety of others is an offence. The punishment rises if hurt or grievous hurt is caused. The civil and criminal standards differ. Statutes that make negligence actionable: the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 (compensation claims) and the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. Section 2(11) of that Act says deficiency in service includes any act of negligence or omission or commission that causes loss or injury to the consumer.

## Meaning and Definition
In Blyth v. Birmingham Waterworks Co. (1856), Alderson B described negligence as the omission to do something which a reasonable man, guided by the considerations that ordinarily regulate human affairs, would do, or doing something which a prudent and reasonable man would not do.

Writers use the word in two ways.

As a mode of committing a tort. Carelessness is the mental element or conduct standard in many torts, such as trespass committed by careless driving. As a separate tort. Negligence is a tort of its own: breach of a legal duty to take care, which causes damage to the claimant . This is the view of Winfield.

Negligence is also a standard of conduct. It describes how the defendant acted, not what he intended.

## Key Points to Remember
- Negligence is failure to take the care a reasonable person would take. The test is objective, not the defendant's personal view. Factors: likelihood and seriousness of harm, cost of precautions, social value of the activity. A professional is judged by the standard of his profession. Carelessness must cause the damage (Barnett v. Chelsea & Kensington Hospital). Negligence differs from recklessness and intention. A small risk may be ignored only for a valid reason. If the precaution is cheap and easy, it must be taken (The Wagon Mound No. 2). The damage must be of a kind that was foreseeable. The exact manner need not be (Hughes v. Lord Advocate). The claimant must prove negligence. Res ipsa loquitur applies only where the defendant had exclusive control and the event does not happen without negligence.
