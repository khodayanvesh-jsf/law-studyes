# 3. Elements of Tort
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/elements-of-tort/

## In One Minute
A tort has three elements. There must be a wrongful act or omission by the defendant . The act must cause legal damage, which means the violation of a legal right. The law must give a remedy for it. All three must be present for a claim to succeed. Exam questions ask for the elements and for the remedies, both judicial (court orders) and extra-judicial (lawful self-help). Real life tests the same points when a neighbour's act harms you.

## Legal Provision
- Section 2(m), Limitation Act, 1963: a tort is a civil wrong not exclusively a breach of contract or trust. It does not list the elements, which come from decided cases. Specific Relief Act, 1963: sections 5, 7 and 8 allow recovery of specific immovable and movable property. Sections 36 to 42 deal with injunctions , which are the commonest non-money remedy in tort. Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, sections 34 to 44: the right of private defence of body and property, which is the nearest statutory source of the self-help remedies. Cattle Trespass Act, 1871: the Indian statute that regulates the seizure of trespassing cattle.

## Meaning and Definition
The elements of a tort are the facts the plaintiff must prove to win. They are three:

a wrongful act or omission by the defendant; legal damage to the plaintiff; and a legal remedy for the wrong.

The third element rests on the maxim ubi jus ibi remedium : where there is a right, there is a remedy. The first two rest on the idea that a lawful act, or a loss without a violated right, gives no claim.

Is there a tort? The three questions How to read this: start at the top. Each question must be answered "yes" to move down. Red text shows where the claim fails. 1. Did the defendant do a wrongful act, or omit a legal duty? No: no tort. A lawful act is not a tort, even if it causes loss. 2. Did the act violate a legal right of the plaintiff (legal damage)? No: damnum sine injuria (loss without legal injury). No claim. 3. Does the law give a remedy for that wrong? No: a moral wrong only. No claim. 4. Result A tort is made out. The plaintiff may claim damages, an injunction or other relief.

## Key Points to Remember
- Three elements: wrongful act or omission, legal damage, legal remedy. An omission is wrongful only when the law imposes a duty to act. Legal damage means violation of a legal right. Loss alone is not enough. Ubi jus ibi remedium: where there is a right, there is a remedy. Judicial remedies: damages, injunction, specific restitution of property. Extra-judicial remedies: self-defence, expulsion of trespasser, re-entry, recaption, distress damage feasant, abatement of nuisance. Self-help must be prompt, reasonable and peaceful. Intention is not a general element: Wilkinson v Downton (intention), Donoghue v Stevenson (duty of care), Rylands v Fletcher (no personal fault needed). A bad motive does not make a lawful act a tort: Bradford Corporation v Pickles.
