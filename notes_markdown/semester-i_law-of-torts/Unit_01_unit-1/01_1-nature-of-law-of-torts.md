## In One Minute
The law of torts decides when one person must pay another for a civil wrong. The duty is fixed by law, not by a promise. The usual remedy is money whose amount the court decides. Most of this law is judge-made. It grows as new harms appear, from a leaking gas plant to an online insult. In exams, expect questions on its features, its objects and the debate on "Law of Tort" versus "Law of Torts".

## Legal Provision
- No single Tort Act. India has no code of torts. The subject rests mainly on English common law as adapted by Indian courts, with statutes added on particular points. Section 2(m), Limitation Act, 1963: "tort" is a civil wrong that is not exclusively a breach of contract or a breach of trust. This is the only general statutory description. Statutes on special areas: the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 and the Information Technology Act, 2000 each add rules to the common law. The Constitution: Articles 21, 32 and 226 let the Supreme Court and High Courts award compensation for violation of fundamental rights. This is a public law remedy and is separate from a tort claim.

## Meaning and Definition
A tort is a civil wrong. The word comes from the Latin tortum , meaning twisted or crooked. In law it means conduct that wrongs another person and for which the law gives a private claim for compensation.

The person who commits the wrong is the tortfeasor . The person wronged sues in a civil court. The state does not prosecute , and no punishment follows. The aim is to repair the harm.

The law of torts is the body of rules that says which wrongs carry this liability and how much the wrongdoer must pay. Definitions by Salmond, Winfield and Fraser are explained in the note on the definition of tort.

## Key Points to Remember
- A tort is a civil wrong that is not only a breach of contract or trust (section 2(m), Limitation Act, 1963). The duty is fixed by law and owed to people in general, so the right is a right in rem. The usual remedy is unliquidated damages. Objects: compensation, deterrence, protection of rights, fair division of loss. Salmond: fixed list of torts. Winfield: general principle. Courts lean to the wider view. Compensation under Articles 32 and 226 is a public law remedy, not a tort award. Section 36 and section 38, Specific Relief Act, 1963 give injunctions (temporary or perpetual) as a tort remedy. Article 372(1), subject to the rest of the Constitution, continues the law in force before 1950 until a competent authority changes it.
