# 15. Procedure for filing a consumer dispute
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/procedure-for-filing-a-consumer-dispute/

## In One Minute
A consumer files a written complaint, with the prescribed fee, before the right Commission within two years. The Commission decides admission within 21 days. The opposite party gets 30 days to reply. The Commission hears the case on affidavits and documents and tries to decide within three months, or five months if goods must be tested. In exams, this topic is asked as "describe the procedure for filing and disposal of a consumer complaint". The usual traps are the deemed admission after 21 days, the time for the reply and the time for the laboratory report.

## Legal Provision
- Section 2(6): defines "complaint": an allegation in writing, made by a complainant for obtaining relief under the Act. Section 35: manner in which a complaint is made, and who may make it. Section 34(2): the place where the complaint is filed. Section 36: proceedings before the District Commission, including admission. Section 37: reference to mediation . Section 38: procedure on admission of a complaint. Section 39: findings and reliefs. Section 69: limitation period of two years. Section 65: service of notices.

## Meaning and Definition
A consumer dispute is a dispute where the person against whom a complaint is made denies or disputes the allegations (section 2(8)). The procedure is the series of steps from the filing of the complaint to the order. It is meant to be quick, simple and cheap, and it is not bound by the technical rules of a civil suit.

## Key Points to Remember
- Written complaint with the prescribed fee. E-filing allowed. Admission ordinarily within 21 days. Deemed admitted if not decided. Reply in 30 days, extendable by up to 15 days. Laboratory report in 45 days. Decision in three or five months. Limitation: two years from the cause of action. Late complaint: sufficient cause and recorded reasons (section 69(2)). A continuing wrong gives a fresh period (Limitation Act, section 22).
