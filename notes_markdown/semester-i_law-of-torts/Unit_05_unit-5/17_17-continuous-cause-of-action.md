# 17. Continuous cause of action
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/continuous-cause-of-action/

## In One Minute
A consumer complaint must be filed within two years from the date on which the cause of action arose. If the wrong goes on, as when a builder keeps failing to hand over a flat, the cause of action continues, and a fresh period of limitation begins every day that the wrong continues. In exams, this topic is asked as a short note on continuous cause of action and limitation, and as a problem on a long delay in a housing or insurance claim. The usual trap is to count two years from the first default alone.

## Legal Provision
- Consumer Protection Act, 2019, section 69(1): a Commission shall not admit a complaint unless it is filed within two years from the date on which the cause of action has arisen. Section 69(2): a complaint may be entertained after that period if the complainant shows sufficient cause, and the Commission records its reasons for condoning the delay. Consumer Protection Act, 1986, section 24A: the same two-year rule in the earlier Act. Limitation Act, 1963, section 22: in the case of a continuing breach of contract or a continuing tort, a fresh period of limitation begins to run at every moment of the time during which the breach or the tort continues. Section 34(2)(c): a complaint may be filed in the District Commission within whose limits the cause of action arises wholly or in part. Section 47(4) uses the same test for the State Commission. Section 58 has no place-of-filing test for the National Commission. Section 107 (repeal and savings): anything done under the 1986 Act is deemed done under the corresponding provision of the 2019 Act, so far as it is not inconsistent.

## Meaning and Definition
A cause of action is the set of facts that gives a person the right to sue. A continuous cause of action arises when the wrong is not complete in one act but goes on, so that the right to sue renews as long as the wrong continues.

## Key Points to Remember
- Limitation is two years from the cause of action (section 69). Late complaints can be admitted with reasons recorded. A continuing wrong gives a fresh period every day. Non-delivery of possession and non-refund are common continuing wrongs. File promptly even when the wrong continues. Section 5 of the Limitation Act covers appeals and applications. Section 69(2) has its own sufficient-cause clause for complaints. For civil suits on torts, libel, slander, false imprisonment and malicious prosecution have one year (Schedule, Articles 73 to 76).
