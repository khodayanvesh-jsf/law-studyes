# 14. Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions: Jurisdiction and Powers
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/consumer-disputes-redressal-commissions/

## In One Minute
Each Commission has original jurisdiction over complaints of a certain value, appellate jurisdiction over the tier below, and a revisional power to correct jurisdictional errors. Territorial jurisdiction decides in which place the complaint is filed. The Commissions have the powers of a civil court for certain purposes. In exams, this topic is asked as "discuss the jurisdiction and powers of the Consumer Commissions", and as a problem on where a complaint must be filed. The usual traps are the pecuniary limits and the consideration-paid test.

## Legal Provision
- Section 34: jurisdiction of the District Commission, pecuniary and territorial. Section 47: jurisdiction of the State Commission, including appeals and revision. Section 58: jurisdiction of the National Commission, including appeals and revision. Section 36 and 38: proceedings, admission, procedure and powers. Sections 40, 50 and 60: review. Sections 48 and 62: transfer of cases. Section 61: power of the National Commission to set aside ex parte orders. Section 65: service of notice by registered post, speed post, courier or electronic means. Section 66: experts to assist the State or National Commission. Section 69: limitation of two years.

## Meaning and Definition
Jurisdiction is the authority of a Commission to hear and decide a matter. It has several parts. Pecuniary jurisdiction depends on the value of the consideration paid. Territorial jurisdiction depends on the place. Appellate jurisdiction is the power to hear appeals. Under revisional jurisdiction, a Commission can call for the record and correct a jurisdictional error.

## Key Points to Remember
- Pecuniary jurisdiction is by consideration paid. Territorial jurisdiction includes the complainant's residence or place of work. State and National Commissions also have appellate and revisional jurisdiction. Commissions have civil court powers and can pass interim orders. Limitation is two years. District and State Commission President and members are appointed by the State Government on a Selection Committee's recommendation, serve four years or to age 65, and can be reappointed once. State Commission President: a High Court Judge, serving or former. District Commission President: a District Judge, serving, former or qualified.
