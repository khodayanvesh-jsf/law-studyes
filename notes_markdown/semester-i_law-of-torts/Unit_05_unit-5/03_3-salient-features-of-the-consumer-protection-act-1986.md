# 3. Salient features of the Consumer Protection Act, 1986
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/salient-features-of-the-consumer-protection-act-1986/

## In One Minute
The Consumer Protection Act, 1986 gave Indian consumers a cheap and quick remedy outside the civil courts. Its main features are the Consumer Protection Councils, a three-tier forum of District Forum, State Commission and National Commission, a list of reliefs, and a limitation period of two years. In exams, this topic is asked as "state the salient features of the Consumer Protection Act, 1986". The trap is to mix its features with those of the 2019 Act that replaced it.

## Legal Provision
- Section 1: the Act extends to the whole of India except Jammu and Kashmir. It applies to all goods and services unless the Central Government provides otherwise. Section 2: definitions, including consumer, complainant, defect, deficiency, unfair trade practice and restrictive trade practice. Section 3: the Act is in addition to, and not in derogation of, any other law. Sections 4 to 8B: Central, State and District Consumer Protection Councils. Section 9: establishment of the District Forum, the State Commission and the National Commission. Sections 11, 17 and 21: pecuniary jurisdiction of each agency. Section 14: findings and reliefs of the District Forum. Sections 15, 19 and 23: appeals. Sections 24A, 25, 26 and 27: limitation, enforcement of orders, frivolous complaints and penalties.

## Meaning and Definition
The Act was passed in 1986 to provide for better protection of the interests of consumers and for the settling of consumer disputes. It was amended several times, notably in 1991, 1993 and 2002. The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 replaced it, and the 2019 Act came into force in July 2020.

## Key Points to Remember
- Three tiers: District Forum (up to Rs 20 lakh), State Commission (up to Rs 1 crore), National Commission (above Rs 1 crore). Remedy is additional to other laws (section 3). Limitation is two years (section 24A). Appeal period is 30 days at each level, with a pre-deposit. Failure to obey an order: imprisonment of one month to three years, or a fine, or both.
