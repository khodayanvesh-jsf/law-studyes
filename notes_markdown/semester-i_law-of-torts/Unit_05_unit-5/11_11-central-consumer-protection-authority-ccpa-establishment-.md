# 11. Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA): establishment, composition and powers
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/central-consumer-protection-authority/

## In One Minute
The Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) is a regulator created by section 10 of the 2019 Act. It protects, promotes and enforces consumer rights as a class. It can investigate, recall goods, order refunds, impose penalties on false advertisements, and search and seize. In exams, this topic is asked as "explain the establishment, composition and powers of the CCPA". The usual trap is to confuse it with the Commissions, which decide individual complaints.

## Legal Provision
- Section 10: establishment, objects and composition. Section 11: rules on qualifications and service conditions of the Chief Commissioner and Commissioners. Sections 12 to 14: vacancies, officers and procedure. Section 15: the Investigation Wing. Section 16: power of the District Collector. Section 17: complaints to the authorities. Section 18: powers and functions. Section 19: preliminary inquiry and investigation. Section 20: power to recall goods. Section 21: directions and penalties for false or misleading advertisements. Section 22: search and seizure. Section 23: designation of another body to function as the Authority. Section 24: appeal to the National Commission. Sections 88 and 89: penalties for non-compliance and for false advertisements. Section 2(4): the Central Authority means the Central Consumer Protection Authority established under section 10. Section 92: a court takes cognizance of an offence under section 88 or 89 only on a complaint by the Central Authority or an officer it authorises. Section 93: an officer who searches or seizes under section 22 knowing there are no reasonable grounds is punishable with imprisonment up to one year, or fine up to Rs 10,000, or both.

## Meaning and Definition
The Central Authority is defined in section 2(4) as the Central Consumer Protection Authority established under section 10. Its purpose is to regulate matters relating to violation of rights of consumers, unfair trade practices and false or misleading advertisements which are prejudicial to the interests of the public and consumers. It also promotes, protects and enforces the rights of consumers as a class.

## Key Points to Remember
- Established under section 10; a Chief Commissioner and other Commissioners. Acts for consumers as a class. Powers: inquire, file complaints, intervene, recall goods, search and seize. Penalty on a false advertisement: up to Rs 10 lakh, and up to Rs 50 lakh for each subsequent contravention. Appeal to the National Commission within 30 days.
