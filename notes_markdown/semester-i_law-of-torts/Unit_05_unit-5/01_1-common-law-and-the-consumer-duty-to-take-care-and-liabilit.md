# 1. Common Law and the Consumer-Duty to take care and liability for negligence
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/common-law-and-the-consumer/

## In One Minute
Before the Consumer Protection Acts, a buyer had to rely on the contract and on the tort of negligence. The old rule was "let the buyer beware". Privity of contract also meant that only the buyer, and only against the seller, could sue. Donoghue v. Stevenson broke the privity barrier by giving a duty of care to the ultimate consumer. In exams, this topic is asked as "explain the common law protection of consumers and the duty of care", and as a problem on a defective product or a careless hospital. The usual trap is to forget that the Consumer Protection Act supplies the forum, and the common law supplies the idea of carelessness.

## Legal Provision
- Sale of Goods Act, 1930, section 16: the buyer must beware, subject to the implied conditions as to quality or fitness set out in the section. Indian Contract Act, 1872, section 2(d): consideration is what the promisee "or any other person" does at the promisor's desire. Section 73: a party who suffers by a breach of contract may receive compensation for loss that arose naturally from the breach, or that the parties knew was likely. Remote and indirect loss is not compensated. The rule that only a party to a contract can sue on it comes from the common law cases discussed below. It is not stated in the text of the Contract Act. Consumer Protection Act, 2019, section 2(11): "deficiency" includes any act of negligence or omission or commission by the service provider that causes loss or injury to the consumer. Consumer Protection Act, 2019, sections 82 to 87: product liability, which has a statutory basis of its own. Sale of Goods Act, 1930, sections 14, 15, 17 and 62: section 14 implies a condition that the seller has a right to sell, and warranties of quiet possession and freedom from encumbrances . Section 15 implies correspondence with description. Section 17 covers sale by sample. Section 62 lets such implied terms be removed or changed by express agreement, course of dealing or binding usage. Sale of Goods Act, 1930, sections 12 and 13: a condition is essential to the main purpose of the contract. A warranty is collateral to it. Limitation Act, 1963, Articles 55 and 113, and Consumer Protection Act, 2019, section 69: time limits for the suit and for the complaint. See below. Consumer Protection Act, 2019, section 2(34) to (37): definitions of product liability, product liability action, product manufacturer and product seller.

## Meaning and Definition
The common law is the law made by judges. For a consumer, it means two things. The contract gives rights against the seller. The tort of negligence gives rights against anyone who owes a duty of care and fails to take reasonable care.

## Key Points to Remember
- Old rule: caveat emptor and privity of contract. Donoghue v. Stevenson: duty of care to the ultimate consumer without a contract. The consumer must still prove negligence. The standard is of the trade or profession. The Consumer Protection Act gives the same right a quick and cheap forum. Sale of Goods Act: implied condition of right to sell and implied warranties (section 14); fitness and merchantable quality (section 16); condition and warranty (sections 12, 13). Time limits: two years for the consumer complaint (section 69); three years for a contract suit (Article 55).
