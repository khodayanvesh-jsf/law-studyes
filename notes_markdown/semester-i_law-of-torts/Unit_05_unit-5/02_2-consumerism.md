## In One Minute
Consumerism is the organised movement to protect and strengthen the position of buyers against sellers. It started from the idea that consumers, as a class, are weaker than manufacturers and traders. It moved the law from "let the buyer beware" to a statute of consumer rights. In exams, this topic is asked as a short note on consumerism, its growth and its link with the Consumer Protection Act. The usual trap is to treat consumerism as only a matter of individual shopping habits.

## Legal Provision
- Consumer Protection Act, 1986: the first law in India with consumer rights and a three-tier forum. It extended to the whole of India except Jammu and Kashmir, and applied to all goods and services. Consumer Protection Act, 2019: replaced the 1986 Act. Section 2(9) defines consumer rights. Articles 38, 39 and 47, Constitution of India: Directive Principles on social justice, the equitable distribution of resources and public health. The Supreme Court has used them in consumer cases. United Nations Guidelines for Consumer Protection: adopted in 1985 and widened later. They are not binding, but they shaped national laws.

## Meaning and Definition
Consumerism is the organised effort of consumers, consumer groups and governments to improve the rights and power of buyers in relation to sellers. It is more than personal caution while shopping. It works through consumer associations, the media and legislation.

## Key Points to Remember
- Consumerism is an organised movement for consumer rights. It arose from unequal information and bargaining power. Caveat emptor gave way to caveat venditor. 15 March 1962 (four rights) and 1985 (UN Guidelines) are world milestones. India: Acts of 1986 and 2019; National Consumer Day is 24 December.
