# 4. Consumer Protection Act,2019
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/consumer-protection-act-2019/

## In One Minute
The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 replaced the 1986 Act. It adds a regulator (the Central Consumer Protection Authority), a chapter on product liability, mediation, e-commerce rules, e-filing, and heavier penalties for false advertisements. In exams, this topic is asked as "explain the scheme and new features of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019". The usual trap is to forget the new features and to mix up the appeal periods and the penalties.

## Legal Provision
- Section 1: the Act extends to the whole of India. A notification of 30 October 2019 made it applicable to the Union territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh. It applies to all goods and services unless the Central Government provides otherwise. Section 2: definitions, including consumer (2(7)), consumer rights (2(9)), defect (2(10)), deficiency (2(11)), e-commerce (2(16)), product liability (2(34)), restrictive trade practice (2(41)), unfair contract (2(46)) and unfair trade practice (2(47)). Chapter II (sections 3 to 9): Consumer Protection Councils. Chapter III (sections 10 to 27): Central Consumer Protection Authority. Chapter IV (sections 28 to 73): Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions. Chapter V (sections 74 to 81): mediation. Chapter VI (sections 82 to 87): product liability. Chapter VII (sections 88 to 93): offences and penalties. Chapter VIII (sections 94 to 107): miscellaneous, including section 94 on e-commerce and direct selling, section 100 (the Act is in addition to other laws) and section 107 (repeal of the 1986 Act).

## Meaning and Definition
The 2019 Act is the present law that protects consumers in India. It does more than settle disputes. It also prevents unfair practices through a regulator, and it covers modern markets such as e-commerce and direct selling.

## Key Points to Remember
- 2019 Act replaced the 1986 Act (section 107). New: Central Authority, product liability, mediation, e-commerce and e-filing. Jurisdiction by consideration paid: Rs 50 lakh, Rs 2 crore under the 2021 Rules. Appeals: 45 days to the State Commission, then 30 days each, with a 50 per cent deposit. Limitation: two years. False advertisement: up to two years and Rs 10 lakh.
