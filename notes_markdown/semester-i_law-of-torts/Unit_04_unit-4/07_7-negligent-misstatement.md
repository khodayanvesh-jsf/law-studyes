# 7. Negligent Misstatement
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/negligent-misstatement/

## In One Minute
Negligent misstatement is careless advice or information that causes pure economic loss to a person who relied on it. The law allows the claim only where there is a special relationship of reliance, in which the speaker has assumed responsibility. In exams, this topic is asked as "explain liability for negligent misstatement with Hedley Byrne", and as a problem on a bank reference or a professional report. The usual trap is to forget that a clear disclaimer can remove the duty.

## Legal Provision
- No Indian Act defines this tort. It comes from English common law and is applied in India as justice, equity and good conscience. Indian Contract Act, 1872, sections 17 and 18: fraud and misrepresentation in contracts. These give contract remedies and differ from the tort. Consumer Protection Act, 2019: deficiency in service covers careless advice by professionals and banks supplied for a fee. Chartered Accountants Act, 1949 and Advocates Act, 1961: professional misconduct rules for auditors and lawyers.

## Meaning and Definition
Negligent misstatement is the careless giving of false or misleading information or advice, in a relationship of proximity, to a person who reasonably relies on it and suffers pure economic loss. Pure economic loss means a money loss with no physical injury to a person or property. The common law once refused such claims, as the loss seemed too remote and open-ended. Hedley Byrne made an exception.

## Key Points to Remember
- Negligent misstatement: careless advice causing pure economic loss. Needs a special relationship and assumed responsibility. Hedley Byrne: duty in principle, but the disclaimer defeated it. Caparo: the duty is limited to the known purpose and persons. Only carelessness is needed, not fraud.
