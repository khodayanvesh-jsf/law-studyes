# 3. Defamation
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/defamation/

## In One Minute
Defamation is the publication of a false statement about a person that lowers him in the estimation of right-thinking people. It protects reputation, not hurt feelings. The claimant proves three things: a defamatory statement, reference to him, and publication to a third person. In exams, this topic is asked as "define defamation and explain its defences", and as a problem on a newspaper report, a rumour or a privileged communication. The usual traps are saying that good faith is a defence, and mixing insult with defamation.

## Legal Provision
- Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, section 356: criminal defamation. It replaced sections 499 and 500 of the Indian Penal Code. The section lists ten exceptions: truth for the public good, good-faith opinion on a public servant, good-faith opinion on a public question, a true report of Court proceedings, opinion on a decided case, opinion on a public performance, censure by a person with lawful authority, a good-faith accusation to a person with lawful authority, a good-faith imputation to protect interests or for the public good, and a good-faith caution. The punishment is simple imprisonment up to two years, or a fine, or both, or community service (section 356(2)). Article 19(1)(a) and 19(2), Constitution of India: free speech is limited by reasonable restrictions in respect of defamation. Article 21: the right to reputation is part of the right to life. Information Technology Act, 2000: section 79 gives intermediaries conditional protection for third-party content, which matters for online defamation. No Indian Act defines civil defamation. The civil law comes from English common law and is applied in India as justice, equity and good conscience.

## Meaning and Definition
Defamation is the publication of a false statement about a person that has one of these effects: it lowers him in the estimation of right-thinking members of society generally. It makes them shun or avoid him. It exposes him to hatred, contempt or ridicule. It injures him in his office, trade or profession. It protects the opinion that others hold of him, and not his own opinion of himself.

## Key Points to Remember
- Three essentials: defamatory statement, reference to the claimant, publication. Test: lowers him in the estimation of right-thinking members of society (Sim v. Stretch). Good faith is no defence. Truth is. Malice defeats fair comment and qualified privilege, not absolute privilege. Civil and criminal defamation (section 356 BNS) can both be brought.
