## Meaning and Definition
Explanation. An injunction is a judicial order — prohibitory (restraining an act) or mandatory (compelling an act) — aimed at preventing or remedying an ongoing or threatened wrong, rather than merely compensating past loss.

What is the distinction between a 'prohibitory' and a 'mandatory' injunction?

Explanation. A prohibitory injunction restrains the defendant from doing (or continuing to do) something; a mandatory injunction compels the defendant to take positive action, such as removing an obstruction.

A builder keeps dumping debris on Nirmala's plot. After a full trial, the court orders him to stop for good. This is:

Explanation. A prohibitory injunction forbids a continuing or repeated wrong. A perpetual injunction is granted at the end of the trial. The court may also award damages.

In American Cyanamid Co v Ethicon Ltd (1975), what was the first stage of Lord Diplock's test for granting an interim injunction?

Explanation. The first stage of the American Cyanamid test asks only whether there is a serious question to be tried — the court does not, at this stage, assess the relative strength of the parties' cases in detail.
