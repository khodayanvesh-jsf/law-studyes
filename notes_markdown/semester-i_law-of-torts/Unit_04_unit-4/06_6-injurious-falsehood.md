# 6. Injurious falsehood
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/injurious-falsehood/

## In One Minute
Injurious falsehood is a false statement, made with malice, that causes special damage to the claimant's trade, goods or property interests. It protects business interests, not reputation as such. In exams, this topic is asked as "what is injurious falsehood and how does it differ from defamation", and as a problem on a rival's false claim about goods. The usual trap is forgetting that malice and special damage are both required.

## Legal Provision
- No Indian Act defines injurious falsehood. It comes from English common law and is applied in India as justice, equity and good conscience. Trade Marks Act, 1999, section 29(8): a registered trade mark is infringed by advertising of that mark if the advertising takes unfair advantage of it and is contrary to honest practices in industrial or commercial matters. It is also infringed if the advertising is detrimental to the mark's distinctive character or is against its reputation. This is a statutory remedy for related harm. Consumer Protection Act, 2019, section 2(47)(j): giving false or misleading facts that disparage the goods, services or trade of another person is an unfair trade practice. Consumer Protection Act, 2019, sections 2(28), 21 and 89: a misleading advertisement is defined. The Central Authority can order it withdrawn and impose a penalty up to Rs 10 lakh (Rs 50 lakh for a repeat). Section 89 punishes the manufacturer or service provider with up to two years in prison and a fine up to Rs 10 lakh. Specific Relief Act, 1963, sections 36 to 42: injunctions to stop repetition. Section 37 covers temporary and perpetual injunctions, and section 40 allows damages in addition to an injunction.

## Meaning and Definition
Injurious falsehood is also called trade libel, malicious falsehood or slander of goods or title. It is the making of a false statement about the claimant's business, goods or property, maliciously, which causes him actual money loss. The statement need not harm his personal reputation. It can even praise him, as in a false announcement that his shop has closed.

## Key Points to Remember
- Injurious falsehood: false statement, malice, special damage. It protects trade, goods and property, not reputation as such. General loss of trade is proof of special damage (Ratcliffe v. Evans). Puffing is not actionable. Remedies: damages, injunction.
