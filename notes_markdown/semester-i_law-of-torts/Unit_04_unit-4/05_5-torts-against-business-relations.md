# 5. Torts against Business Relations
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/torts-against-business-relations/

## In One Minute
A business can be hurt without any physical damage. A rival can spread a lie, copy its name, or give careless advice that makes it lose money. Several people can also combine to drive it out. Four torts deal with these wrongs: injurious falsehood, negligent misstatement, passing off and conspiracy. In exams, this topic is asked as "name the torts against business relations and distinguish them", and as a problem on a rival's conduct. The trap is to mix up the core of each tort.

## Legal Provision
- No Indian Act defines these four torts. They come from English common law and are applied in India as justice, equity and good conscience. Trade Marks Act, 1999, section 27(2): the Act does not affect the right of action for passing off goods or services as those of another. Trade Marks Act, 1999, section 29(1): a registered mark is infringed by use of an identical or deceptively similar mark in the course of trade on the registered goods or services. Passing off is the remedy for unregistered goodwill. Competition Act, 2002, section 3: an agreement that causes or is likely to cause an appreciable adverse effect on competition in India is void. Price fixing, supply limits, market sharing and bid rigging by competitors are presumed to have such an effect (section 3(3)). Section 4: no enterprise or group shall abuse its dominant position. Abuse includes unfair or discriminatory conditions or prices (including predatory price), and limiting production or market access. Section 27 lets the Commission order the conduct to stop and impose a penalty. This is a separate statutory regime, not a tort.

## Meaning and Definition
Torts against business relations protect trade, goodwill and economic interests. The loss is usually pure economic loss, which means a money loss that is not caused by physical injury to a person or property. Each tort has its own core: a false statement, a careless statement, a deceptive imitation, or a combination of persons.

## Key Points to Remember
- Injurious falsehood: false statement, malice, special damage. Negligent misstatement: special relationship, duty, careless advice. Passing off: goodwill, misrepresentation, damage. Conspiracy: two or more persons, purpose to injure or unlawful means, actual damage. Lawful competition is not a tort.
