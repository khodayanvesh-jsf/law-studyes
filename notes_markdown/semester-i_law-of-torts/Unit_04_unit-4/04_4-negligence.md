# 4. Negligence
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/negligence/

## In One Minute
Negligence is the breach of a legal duty to take care that causes damage to the claimant. He must prove three things: a duty of care, a breach of it, and damage caused by the breach that is not too remote. In exams, this topic is asked as "define negligence and state its essentials", and as a problem on a careless act, a doctor or a road accident. The usual traps are forgetting causation, and applying the wrong standard to professionals.

## Legal Provision
- No Indian Act defines civil negligence. The tort comes from English common law and is applied in India as justice, equity and good conscience. Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023: these negligence offences also exist. Section 106(1): causing death by a rash or negligent act is punishable with up to five years and fine. A registered medical practitioner performing a medical procedure faces up to two years and fine. Section 106(2): rash and negligent driving that causes death, followed by escape without reporting, is punishable with up to ten years and fine. Section 125: a rash or negligent act that endangers life or personal safety is punishable with up to three months or a fine of up to Rs 2,500. The punishment is higher where hurt or grievous hurt is caused. Section 281: rash or negligent driving on a public way is punishable with up to six months or a fine of up to Rs 1,000. Consumer Protection Act, 2019: deficiency in service, including medical service, is remedied before Consumer Commissions. Motor Vehicles Act, 1988: compensation for road accidents. Article 21, Constitution of India: compensation for the State's careless acts that violate the right to life.

## Meaning and Definition
In Blyth v. Birmingham Waterworks Co. (1856), Alderson B described negligence as the omission to do something that a reasonable man would do, or doing something that a prudent and reasonable man would not do. As a tort, it means a breach of a legal duty to take care, which results in damage to the claimant that the defendant did not desire.

## Key Points to Remember
- Negligence has three essentials: duty, breach, damage caused and not too remote. The standard is that of the reasonable man. Professionals are judged by their own field. But-for test for causation (Barnett). Foreseeability for remoteness (Wagon Mound). Res ipsa loquitur allows an inference of negligence. Defences: contributory negligence, volenti non fit injuria.
