# 2. Liability arising out of accidents -Relevant provisions of the Motor Vehicles Act
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/liability-arising-out-of-accidents/

## In One Minute
A road accident victim can claim in two ways. The ordinary route is a suit in negligence, where fault must be proved. The statutory route is the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, which gives fixed no-fault compensation under section 164 and full just compensation before a Claims Tribunal under section 166. In exams, this topic is asked as "explain liability for motor accidents under the Motor Vehicles Act", and as a problem on a death or injury with compensation to be worked out. The usual trap is to mix the fixed no-fault sum with the full just compensation.

## Legal Provision
- Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, section 164: no-fault liability. The owner or the insurer pays Rs 5 lakh for death and Rs 2.5 lakh for grievous hurt, without proof of fault. Section 161: compensation for hit and run accidents, paid under a scheme of the Central Government. Rs 2 lakh for death and Rs 50,000 for grievous hurt, or higher if prescribed. Section 165: the State Government may constitute Motor Accident Claims Tribunals. Section 166: application for compensation by the injured person, the owner of damaged property or the legal representatives . Section 168: the Tribunal makes an award of just compensation and states who must pay. Sections 146 and 147: compulsory insurance against third-party risks. Sections 140 to 144 (omitted from 1 April 2022): the earlier no-fault scheme with smaller fixed sums. The Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act, 2019 replaced it with section 164. Section 164A: the Central Government may make schemes for interim relief to claimants. The scheme must also provide for recovering the money from the vehicle owner, where the claim arises out of use of that vehicle, or from other prescribed sources.

## Meaning and Definition
Accident liability means the duty of the driver, the owner and the insurer to pay for death, injury and property damage caused by a motor vehicle. Its root is the tort of negligence. The Motor Vehicles Act has added a faster statutory scheme, special tribunals and compulsory insurance, so that a victim does not have to depend on the driver's ability to pay.

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 164: Rs 5 lakh for death and Rs 2.5 lakh for grievous hurt, no fault proof. Claims Tribunals under sections 165 and 166 bar civil courts. Pranay Sethi: 50, 30, 15 per cent for permanent jobs; 40, 25, 10 per cent for the self-employed. Fixed heads: Rs 15,000, Rs 40,000, Rs 15,000, raised 10 per cent every three years. Third-party insurance is compulsory.
