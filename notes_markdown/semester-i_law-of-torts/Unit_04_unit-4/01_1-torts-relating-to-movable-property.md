# 1. Torts relating to movable property
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/torts-relating-to-movable-property/

## In One Minute
Three torts protect goods. Trespass to goods is any direct interference with goods in another's possession. Conversion is a serious dealing with goods that denies the owner's rights. Detinue is the wrongful refusal to return goods on demand. Possession is enough to sue. In exams, this topic is asked as "distinguish trespass to goods, conversion and detinue", and as a problem on a finder, a buyer from a thief or a refusal to return goods.

## Legal Provision
- Specific Relief Act, 1963, section 7: a person entitled to the possession of specific movable property may recover it in the manner provided by the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908. Explanation 1: a trustee may sue under section 7 for movable property in which the beneficiary is entitled. Explanation 2: a special or temporary right to present possession is enough to sue. Section 8: a person in possession or control of a specific article, who is not its owner, can be made to deliver it to the person entitled to its immediate possession. This applies when the article is held as agent or trustee, when compensation in money would not be adequate, when it is difficult to find the actual damage, or when possession has been wrongfully transferred from the plaintiff. In the cases of inadequate compensation and difficult assessment, the court presumes these facts unless the contrary is proved. Indian Contract Act, 1872, Chapter IX: sections 148 to 171 deal with bailment and sections 172 to 179 with pledge. Sections 180 and 181 deal with suits by bailees or bailors against wrong-doers. Section 71 makes a finder of goods answerable like a bailee. Section 180 lets a bailee, or the bailor, sue a stranger who wrongfully deprives the bailee of the goods or injures them. Limitation Act, 1963, Schedule: the period is three years for each suit relating to movable property. Articles 68, 69, 70 and 91 fix when the period starts. See Limitation below. Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023: theft (section 303), dishonest misappropriation of property (section 314) and mischief (section 324) are also offences. The civil claim is separate.

## Meaning and Definition
Movable property means goods and chattels, which are things that can be moved. Torts relating to it protect two interests: possession of the goods and the owner's right to them. The law gives three remedies for three degrees of interference.

## Key Points to Remember
- Trespass to goods is direct interference. Conversion is a serious denial of title. Detinue is refusal to return on demand. Possession is enough to sue. Innocence is no defence to conversion. Detinue allows an order for return of the goods themselves. Specific Relief Act sections 7 and 8 give recovery of movable property. Bailee: care of an ordinarily prudent man (sections 151, 152), return without demand (section 160). A bailee can sue a stranger (section 180). Finder: bailee's responsibility (section 71); no suit for expenses but may keep the goods (section 168). Limitation: three years (Articles 68, 69, 70, 91).
