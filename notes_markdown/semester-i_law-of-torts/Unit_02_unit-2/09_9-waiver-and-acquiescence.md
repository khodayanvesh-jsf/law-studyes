# 9. Waiver and Acquiescence
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/waiver-and-acquiescence/

## In One Minute
Waiver is the knowing and voluntary giving up of a right. Acquiescence is losing a right by standing by, with full knowledge, while a wrong is done and not objecting. Both end the right to sue, in whole or in part. Acquiescence matters most in continuing wrongs such as nuisance, where it may bar an injunction or even create a right by long use. Exams ask for the two meanings and an example.

## Legal Provision
- Section 121, Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023: estoppel . A person who by his declaration, act or omission intentionally caused or permitted another to believe a thing true and to act on it cannot later deny it. His representative is bound too. The Adhiniyam repealed the Indian Evidence Act, 1872 (section 170). Illustration: A intentionally and falsely leads B to believe that land belongs to A, and B buys it. A later becomes the owner. He cannot set the sale aside by proving that he had no title when he sold. Section 25, Limitation Act, 1963: access and use of light or air to a building, a way, a watercourse , use of water or any other easement becomes absolute and indefeasible after twenty years. The enjoyment must be peaceable and as of right, without interruption. For a way, water or any other easement it must also be open. Each twenty-year period must end within two years before the suit in which the claim is contested (sub-section (2)). The period is thirty years if the property belongs to the Government (sub-section (3)). Nothing is an interruption unless there is an actual stoppage by an obstruction from someone other than the claimant. Even then, the obstruction counts only if it is submitted to or acquiesced in for one year after the claimant has notice of it and of the person responsible. Section 15, Indian Easements Act, 1882: the same twenty-year rule (thirty years for Government property) applies in the territories to which that Act extends. It also covers support that land under artificial pressure, or things fixed on it, received from another person's land for twenty years. Section 25 of the Limitation Act does not apply there (section 29(4) of the Limitation Act). Section 15, Explanations I to IV: enjoyment under an agreement that shows no easement was granted does not count (I). An interruption needs an actual stoppage by another person's obstruction, accepted for one year after notice (II). Suspension of enjoyment under a contract between the two owners is not an interruption (III). For an easement to pollute water, the twenty years begin when the pollution first perceptibly prejudices the servient heritage (IV). Section 15, illustration (b): if the claimant held the land as a lessee for one year of the twenty, the right was not enjoyed "as an easement" for twenty years. The suit fails. Section 15, illustration (c): if the claimant once admitted that his use was not of right and asked leave, the use was not "as of right". The suit fails. Section 16, Indian Easements Act: if the servient land was held for a life interest or for a term of more than three years, that time is left out of the twenty years. This applies when the owner resists the claim within three years after the interest ends. Section 63, Indian Contract Act, 1872: a promisee may remit performance. Specific Relief Act, 1963, section 41: an injunction cannot be granted to prevent a continuing breach in which the plaintiff has acquiesced (clause (g)). Nor can it be granted when the plaintiff's conduct disentitles him to the court's help (clause (i)). Under section 40(1), the court may award damages in addition to, or in place of, an injunction.

## Meaning and Definition
Waiver is the voluntary and intentional giving up of a known right. The person must know the right exists and mean to give it up.

Acquiescence is silent consent shown by conduct. A person who knows of the wrong, and knows his rights, stands by while the wrong continues and does not object.

S.K. Das J. gave a working meaning of waiver in Basheshar Nath v. Commissioner of Income-Tax (1958), speaking for himself. He said that waiver needs an "intentional relinquishment of a known right". He added that waiver is an agreement to release or not to assert a right, while estoppel is a rule of evidence.

## Key Points to Remember
- Waiver: knowing, voluntary giving up of a right. Acquiescence: standing by with knowledge, without objection. Section 41(g) bars an injunction against a continuing breach the plaintiff acquiesced in. Damages may be awarded (section 40(1)). Twenty years' open use as of right can create an easement (section 25, Limitation Act, or section 15, Easements Act). Acquiescence needs knowledge and a power to stop the act ( Sturges v. Bridgman ). Estoppel (section 121, Sakshya Adhiniyam): no denying what you made another believe and act on. A concession in court creates no estoppel against a fundamental right ( Olga Tellis ). Article 14 cannot be waived ( Basheshar Nath ).
