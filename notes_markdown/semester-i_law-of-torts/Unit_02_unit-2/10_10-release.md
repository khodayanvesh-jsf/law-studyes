# 10. Release
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/release/

## In One Minute
A release is the giving up of a claim by the person who holds it. A valid release ends the claim. It is voidable, and can be set aside, if consent was caused by coercion, undue influence, fraud or misrepresentation. A minor's claim cannot be compromised without the court's leave (Order XXXII, rule 7). Exams ask for the meaning, the conditions of validity, the rule for minors, and how a release is set aside.

## Legal Provision
- Section 11, Indian Contract Act, 1872: a person is competent to contract if he has reached the age of majority, is of sound mind and is not disqualified by law. Sections 14 to 19A, Indian Contract Act: consent is free only if it is not caused by coercion , undue influence , fraud, misrepresentation or mistake (mistake subject to sections 20, 21 and 22). Under section 19, consent caused by coercion, fraud or misrepresentation makes the agreement voidable at the option of that party. Section 19A says the same for undue influence and lets the court set the contract aside. Sections 20 to 22, Contract Act: a mistake of fact shared by both parties, about something essential, makes the agreement void. A mistake by one party alone does not make a contract voidable (section 22). Section 25, Contract Act: an agreement without consideration is void, with three exceptions. Section 44, Contract Act: the release of one joint promisor does not discharge the others. Sections 62 to 64, Contract Act: the parties may rescind or alter a contract, a promisee may remit performance or accept any satisfaction he thinks fit, and a person who rescinds a voidable contract must restore any benefit received. Order XXIII, rules 1, 3, 3A and 3B, Code of Civil Procedure, 1908: abandoning a suit, compromise of a suit, bar to a suit against a compromise decree, and leave for compromise in a representative suit. Order XXXII, rules 6, 7 and 15, CPC: money received for a minor, compromise by a next friend or guardian , and persons of unsound mind. Sections 31 and 33, Specific Relief Act, 1963: suit to have a written instrument cancelled, and the power to require the benefit to be restored. Section 17(1) and Article 59, Limitation Act, 1963: time to sue to cancel an instrument, and the effect of fraud or mistake on the start of time.

## Meaning and Definition
A release is the giving up of a claim by the person who holds it. A valid release ends the claim that was released.

The old report of Ashby v. White (1703) states the point with a bond: "He who loses or quits his Remedy, loses his Right." The report adds that a man who holds a bond for a thousand pounds and "releases all Actions" loses his right to the money, because he has given away the means to recover it.

Release is a mode of extinction of liability, a topic covered in the Extinction of liability note. This note deals with what makes a release valid and what can undo it.

## Key Points to Remember
- A release is the giving up of a claim, and a valid release ends the claim. It needs capacity, free consent and consideration (sections 11, 14, 25). Consent caused by coercion, fraud or misrepresentation makes it voidable (section 19); undue influence, section 19A. A minor's compromise needs the court's leave (Order XXXII, rule 7). Section 44 says release of one joint promisor does not discharge the others. A suit to cancel an instrument has a three-year limit from knowledge (Article 59).
