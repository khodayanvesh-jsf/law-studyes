# 1. Specific Torts
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/specific-torts/

## In One Minute
The general principles say when any tort exists. Specific torts are the named wrongs, each with its own essentials. They are grouped by the interest they protect: the person, property, reputation, business and family. A claimant must fit his facts into a named tort, or ask the court to extend the law. In exams, this topic is asked as a list with a one-line meaning for each tort, and as the question "Is the law of torts a closed list?"

## Legal Provision
- No Act lists the specific torts. They come from case law, and some are touched by statute. Information Technology Act, 2000: deals with wrongs online, such as hacking and publishing obscene material, and gives compensation under section 43. A body corporate that is negligent in protecting sensitive personal data must pay compensation under section 43A. An Adjudicating Officer decides claims up to five crore rupees under section 46(1A). Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023: many specific torts are also crimes, such as assault, wrongful restraint, defamation and criminal trespass. Constitution of India, Articles 21, 32 and 226: violations of life and liberty by the State give public law compensation as well.

## Meaning and Definition
A specific tort is a wrong with a name, a settled definition and its own elements, such as battery or nuisance. It differs from the general principles of liability, which apply to all torts. The note on the nature of the law of torts explains the pigeon-hole theory, which says that only named torts are actionable, and the wider theory, which allows new torts.

## Key Points to Remember
- Specific torts are named wrongs with their own essentials. Groups: person, property, reputation, business, family. Many are also crimes. The list is not closed. Courts add new torts. Nuisance and trespass differ: indirect and direct interference. Choose a tort by interest harmed, manner of harm, state of mind and need to prove loss. Public law compensation against the State (Rudul Sah, Nilabati Behera) is in addition to a tort suit.
