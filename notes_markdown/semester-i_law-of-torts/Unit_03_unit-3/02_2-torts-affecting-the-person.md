# 2. Torts affecting the person
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/torts-affecting-the-person/

## In One Minute
A person can be harmed without any damage to property. The law protects four interests: the body, personal liberty, the mind and the good name. Each has its own tort. In Stephens v. Myers an assault earned damages although the blow never landed. Malicious prosecution and nervous shock turn on proof of harm. In exams, this topic is asked as a comparison of these torts and as a fact problem where one incident supports several claims.

## Legal Provision
- Article 21, Constitution of India: no person is deprived of life or personal liberty except by procedure established by law. It supports compensation for unlawful detention. Article 22(1) and (2): an arrested person must be told the grounds as soon as may be, may consult a lawyer of choice, and must be produced before the nearest Magistrate within 24 hours, excluding journey time. Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (arrest provisions): says when a police officer or a private person may arrest. An arrest outside these limits can be false imprisonment. Section 35 lists when police may arrest without a warrant. Section 40 lets a private person arrest only a person who commits a non-bailable and cognizable offence in his presence, or a proclaimed offender , and hand him over within six hours. Section 47 requires the grounds of arrest to be told, and section 58 bars detention beyond twenty-four hours. Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023: assault, wrongful restraint, wrongful confinement and defamation are also offences. Compensation for the same act is a separate matter, as Rudul Sah and Bhim Singh show. Information Technology Act, 2000, section 43: gives compensation for unauthorised access to a computer and damage to data.

## Meaning and Definition
Torts affecting the person are civil wrongs that interfere with a human being, not with property. The interests protected are bodily integrity, freedom of movement, mental wellbeing and reputation. Each wrong has a separate name and separate essentials, so a student must first identify what was interfered with.

The group in this syllabus covers assault, battery, false imprisonment, malicious prosecution, nervous shock and digital torts. Each has its own note in this unit. This note shows how the group fits together.

## Key Points to Remember
- Person torts protect body, liberty, mind and name. Assault needs no contact (Stephens v. Myers). False imprisonment needs total restraint (Bird v. Jones). Malicious prosecution: no reasonable and probable cause, malice and a favourable end. Nervous shock: harm must be shown. False imprisonment needs total restraint. Contact is not needed. One incident can support several claims. The same act may also be an offence under the Sanhita. The State can be made to pay compensation for unlawful detention under Article 21.
