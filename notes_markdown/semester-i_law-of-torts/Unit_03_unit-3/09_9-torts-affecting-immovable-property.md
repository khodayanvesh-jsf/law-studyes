# 9. Torts affecting Immovable Property
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/torts-affecting-immovable-property/

## In One Minute
Land can be harmed in three main ways. A person can enter it without right (trespass). A person can interfere with its use and enjoyment from outside (nuisance). A dangerous thing can escape from a neighbour's land (the rule in Rylands v. Fletcher). Trespass is actionable without proof of damage. Nuisance needs substantial interference. In exams, this topic is asked as a comparison of trespass and nuisance and as a problem on a neighbour dispute.

## Legal Provision
- Specific Relief Act, 1963, section 5: a person entitled to the possession of specific immovable property may recover it by a suit. Section 6: a person dispossessed without his consent and not by due process of law can recover possession by a suit filed within six months, without proving title. Sections 36 to 42: temporary and perpetual injunctions. Section 37 says a temporary injunction lasts until a stated time or further order and is regulated by the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908. A perpetual injunction is granted only by a decree at the hearing, on the merits. Section 38: a perpetual injunction may be granted to prevent breach of an obligation in favour of the claimant. When the defendant invades or threatens to invade the claimant's right to, or enjoyment of, property, it may be granted in four cases. They are: the defendant is trustee for the claimant; there is no standard to measure the damage; money would not be adequate relief; and the injunction prevents many separate suits. Section 39: a mandatory injunction can also compel acts the court is able to enforce. Section 40: the claimant can claim damages in addition to, or in place of, the injunction. The claim must be in the plaint, but the court may allow an amendment. Section 41: an injunction cannot be granted, among other cases, to prevent on the ground of nuisance an act which is not reasonably clear to be a nuisance (clause f). It also cannot be granted where equally efficacious relief is certainly available by another usual mode (clause h), or where the plaintiff has acquiesced in a continuing breach (clause g). Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, section 2(12): mesne profits are the profits the wrongful possessor actually received or might with ordinary diligence have received, with interest. Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, section 91: a suit for a declaration and injunction against a public nuisance may be filed by the Advocate-General, or by two or more persons with leave of the Court, even without special damage to them. Limitation Act, 1963: Article 87 (compensation for trespass upon immovable property), Article 85 (obstructing a way or water-course) and Article 86 (diverting a water-course) each allow three years from the date of the act. Indian Easements Act, 1882, section 4: easements , such as a right of way or light. The land that enjoys the right is the dominant heritage. The land that carries the burden is the servient heritage. Sections 15 and 17: a right of way or other easement is acquired by twenty years of peaceable, open enjoyment as of right (thirty years against the Government). A right to free passage of light or air to an open space cannot be acquired this way. Sections 33, 35 and 36: the owner or occupier of the dominant land may sue for compensation if the disturbance caused substantial damage. An injunction may be granted against actual or threatened disturbance. The dominant owner cannot himself abate a wrongful obstruction of an easement. Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, section 329: criminal trespass and house-trespass. Punishment for criminal trespass is up to three months or fine up to five thousand rupees, or both. Section 270: a public nuisance is an act or illegal omission causing common injury, danger or annoyance to the public or to people living or occupying property nearby. It is not excused because it causes some convenience or advantage. Section 292: public nuisance not otherwise punishable carries a fine up to one thousand rupees. Section 293: continuing it after an injunction by a public servant carries up to six months, or fine up to five thousand rupees, or both.

## Meaning and Definition
Immovable property includes land, buildings and things attached to the earth. Torts affecting it protect two interests: possession of the land and the use and enjoyment of it. The maxim cujus est solum ejus est usque ad coelum et ad inferos says that the owner of land owns up to the sky and down to the centre of the earth. Courts now apply it with limits.

## Key Points to Remember
- Trespass is direct entry. Nuisance is indirect interference. Trespass is actionable per se. Nuisance usually needs damage. Possession gives the right to sue for trespass. Section 6, Specific Relief Act: recovery of possession within six months, no title needed. Remedies: damages, injunction, recovery of possession, mesne profits.
