# 10. Hazardous Wastes (Management, Handling and Transboundary) Regulations etc
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester 1) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/hazardous-wastes-regulations/

## In One Minute
India controls hazardous waste by rules, not by a separate Act. The Hazardous and Other Wastes (Management and Transboundary Movement) Rules, 2016 were made under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986. Anyone who handles such waste needs authorisation from the State Pollution Control Board. Every movement within India needs a manifest. Import for disposal is not allowed.

## Legal Provision
- The rules below are read from the Gazette notification G.S.R. 395(E) of 4 April 2016.
- Making of the Rules: they were made under sections 6, 8 and 25 of the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, in supersession of the Hazardous Wastes (Management, Handling and Transboundary Movement) Rules, 2008. They came into force on the date of publication. Rule 3: definitions, including "hazardous waste", "other wastes" and "manifest". Rule 4: responsibilities of the occupier. Rule 6: authorisation. Rule 7: suspension or cancellation. Rules 11 to 15: import and export, and illegal traffic. Rule 16: treatment, storage and disposal facilities. Rule 19: manifest. Rule 22: accident reporting. Rule 23: liability. Rule 24: appeal.

## Meaning and Definition
Rule 3(1)(17) defines hazardous waste as waste that is physical, chemical, biological, reactive, toxic, flammable, explosive or corrosive in a way that causes or is likely to cause danger to health or environment. It includes waste listed in Schedule I and waste above the limits in Schedule II.

"Other wastes" are the wastes in Parts B and D of Schedule III for import or export, and they include such waste generated in India. A manifest is the transporting document prepared and signed by the sender.

Life of hazardous waste under the Rules How to read this: follow the boxes from top to bottom. Each step must be completed before the next. 1. The occupier obtains authorisation from the State Board (rule 6). 2. The waste is packed and labelled, and moved by an authorised transporter (rules 17 and 18). 3. The sender prepares a manifest for the movement (rule 19). 4. Result The waste reaches a treatment, storage and disposal facility or an authorised recycler (rule 16).

## Key Points to Remember
- Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules, 2016: G.S.R. 395(E), 4 April 2016, under sections 6, 8 and 25 of the 1986 Act. Authorisation from the State Board: rule 6, Form 1. Manifest: seven copies in Form 10 (rule 19). No import for disposal; import for recycling needs permission (rule 12). Liability for damage: rule 23. Appeal in thirty days: rule 24.
