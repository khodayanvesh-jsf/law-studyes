# 6. Air Pollution
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester 1) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/air-pollution/

## In One Minute
The Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981 controls smoke, gas, dust and noise in the air. It mostly uses the Pollution Control Boards of the Water Act, and gives them extra work. A State can declare an air pollution control area. Industrial plants there need the Board's consent and must stay within emission standards. The Board can also give written directions, including closure. Its preamble says it carries out the decisions of the Stockholm Conference. Article 253 is the constitutional power for such laws.

## Legal Provision
- The main provisions, read from the Act as stored on this site, are these.
- Preamble: the Act carries out decisions of the Stockholm Conference of June 1972 on preserving air quality. Article 253: Parliament may make any law for the whole or any part of India to implement a treaty, agreement or decision of an international conference. Section 2(a) and (b): an "air pollutant" is any solid, liquid or gaseous substance, including noise, present in the atmosphere in a concentration that is or may be injurious to living creatures, plants, property or the environment. "Air pollution" is the presence of any air pollutant. Sections 3 and 4: the Central Board and State Boards formed under the Water Act also act as the Boards under this Act. Section 19: the State Government may declare air pollution control areas. Section 21: previous consent of the State Board to establish or operate an industrial plant in a control area. Section 22: no emission above the standards laid down by the State Board. Section 31A: written directions by a Board. Section 37: penalty for breach of section 22 or of a section 31A direction.

## Meaning and Definition
Air pollution is the presence in the atmosphere of any substance, including noise, in a quantity that harms living things, property or the environment. The Act aims at prevention, control and abatement. Abatement existing pollution is as important as stopping new pollution.

A new idea in this Act is the air pollution control area . It is an area that the State Government declares, after consulting the State Board. Special rules on fuels, appliances and plants apply there.

How the Act controls an industrial plant How to read this: follow the boxes from top to bottom. Each step leads to the next. The last box is the Board's strongest tool. 1. The State Government declares a control area (section 19). 2. A plant there needs the State Board's consent (section 21). 3. The plant must stay within the emission standards (section 22). 4. The Board may ask a court to restrain a breach (section 22A). 5. Result The Board may also direct closure or regulation, and a penalty follows (sections 31A and 37).

## Key Points to Remember
- Air Act, 1981 rests on Article 253 and the Stockholm decisions. "Air pollutant" includes noise. The Water Act Boards also act as Boards under the Air Act. Section 19: control areas. Section 21: consent. Section 22: emission standards. Section 22A: court application. Section 31A: Board's own directions. Section 37: penalty of Rs 10,000 to Rs 15 lakh, plus Rs 10,000 a day.
