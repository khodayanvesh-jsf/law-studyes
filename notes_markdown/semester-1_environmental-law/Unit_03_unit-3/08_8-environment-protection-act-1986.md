# 8. Environment (Protection) Act, 1986
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester 1) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/environment-protection-act-1986/

## In One Minute
The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 is the umbrella Act. Section 3 gives the Central Government a wide power to take all measures needed to protect and improve the environment. Section 5 lets it give written directions, including closure. Many rules and notifications flow from it, such as rules on hazardous waste. It acts on any pollutant, not on water or air alone. It was made to carry out the Stockholm decisions of 1972.

## Legal Provision
- The main sections, read from the Act as stored on this site, are these.
- Preamble: the Act implements decisions of the Stockholm Conference of June 1972 on protecting and improving the human environment. Section 2(a): "environment" includes water, air and land, and the interrelationship between them and human beings, other living creatures, plants, micro-organism and property. Section 2(b) and (c): an " environmental pollutant " is a substance in a concentration that is or tends to be injurious to the environment. "Environmental pollution" is its presence in the environment. Section 2(d): "handling" means manufacture, processing, treatment, package, storage, transportation, use, collection, destruction, conversion, offering for sale, transfer or the like of a substance. Section 2(f): an "occupier" is the person who has control over the affairs of the factory or premises. For a substance, it includes the person in possession of it. Section 2(e): a "hazardous substance" is a substance or preparation that can cause harm to human beings, other creatures, plants, property or the environment, by its properties or its handling. Section 3: power of the Central Government to take all measures it considers necessary or expedient. Section 5: power to give written directions, including closure, prohibition or regulation of an industry and stopping of electricity, water or other service. Section 6: power to make rules on the matters in section 3. Section 7: no discharge or emission above the prescribed standards. Section 8: hazardous substances may be handled only by the prescribed procedure and safeguards. Sections 14A, 14B and 15: penalties.

## Meaning and Definition
The Act is called an umbrella Act because it gives the Central Government one general power that covers all types of pollution and all steps to prevent it. The Water Act and the Air Act deal with one medium each. This Act reaches soil, hazardous substances, accidents and anything else that harms the environment.

The definition of "environment" joins water, air and land with people and other living things. A harm to any link in that chain is a harm to the environment.

Powers that flow from the Act How to read this: start at the top box and follow the lines down. Each box is a power or duty in the Act. Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 Section 3: measures All steps the Central Government thinks necessary, including standards and restrictions on areas. Section 5: directions Written orders, including closure and stopping electricity or water. Section 6: rules Standards, limits, safeguards for hazardous substances. Sections 7 and 8: duties Stay within standards. Handle hazardous substances safely. Sections 14A, 14B, 15: penalties Money penalties, with a daily penalty for continuing breach.

## Key Points to Remember
- Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 carries out the Stockholm decisions. Section 3: all measures. Section 5: directions. Section 6: rules. Section 7: standards. Section 8: hazardous substances. Section 14A: Rs 1 lakh to Rs 15 lakh for breach of section 7 or 8. Section 15: Rs 10,000 to Rs 15 lakh for other breaches. Section 3(3) allows the Central Government to constitute authorities.
