# 12. National Environmental Tribunal and National Environmental Appellate Authority
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester 1) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/national-environmental-tribunal-and-national-environmental-appellate-authority/

## In One Minute
Before the National Green Tribunal, two Acts set up specialised bodies. The National Environment Tribunal Act, 1995 gave strict, no-fault liability for accidents with hazardous substances. The National Environment Appellate Authority Act, 1997 created an Authority to hear appeals on environmental clearance. The National Green Tribunal Act, 2010 repealed both Acts and took over their work.

## Legal Provision
- National Environment Tribunal Act, 1995 (Act 27 of 1995): strict liability for damage from any accident while handling a hazardous substance, and a Tribunal for quick disposal of such cases. National Environment Appellate Authority Act, 1997: an Authority to hear appeals on environmental clearance. Section 38, National Green Tribunal Act, 2010: repeal and savings. It repeals both Acts, dissolves the Authority when the Tribunal is established and moves its pending cases to the Tribunal. Section 15, National Green Tribunal Act, 2010: relief and compensation, including for accidents with hazardous substances, in addition to relief under the Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991.

## Meaning and Definition
A tribunal is a body outside the ordinary courts that decides disputes in a special field. An appellate authority hears appeals against decisions of other bodies. The two Acts set up one of each, for environmental matters .

## Key Points to Remember
- National Environment Tribunal Act, 1995: no-fault liability for hazardous substance accidents. National Environment Appellate Authority Act, 1997: clearance appeals. Nayudu: technical questions referred to the Authority. Both Acts repealed by section 38 of the NGT Act, 2010. 1995 Act: five-year limit to claim (section 4); appeal to the Supreme Court in 90 days (section 24); penalty up to 3 years or Rs 10 lakh (section 25). 1997 Act: appeal in 30 days (condonable up to 90) against clearance; disposal in 90 days (section 11); penalty up to 7 years or Rs 1 lakh (section 19).
