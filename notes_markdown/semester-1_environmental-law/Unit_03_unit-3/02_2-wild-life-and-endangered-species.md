## In One Minute
The Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972 protects wild animals and plants. It does this through schedules that rank species by their level of protection, and through protected areas such as national parks and sanctuaries. The 2022 Amendment cut the number of schedules and added rules for trade in species listed under CITES. The Supreme Court supervises forest and wild life protection through continuing orders.

## Legal Provision
- The Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972 (Act 53 of 1972): the central Act on wild animals, birds and plants and their habitats. Entry 17B of the Concurrent List: protection of wild animals and birds, inserted by the Constitution (Forty-second Amendment) Act, 1976. Both Parliament and the States can make laws on it. Article 48A and Article 51A(g): the State must safeguard wild life, and every citizen must have compassion for living creatures. Sections 18 and 35 of the 1972 Act: the sections under which a sanctuary and a national park are notified, as the Godavarman order records. The Wild Life (Protection) Amendment Act, 2022: restructured the schedules and added trade rules for species under CITES.

## Meaning and Definition
An endangered species is one at serious risk of extinction in the near future. The law protects such species in two ways. It lists them in schedules with strong penalties, and it protects the places where they live.

Killing or trapping wild animals for profit is called poaching . The Act aims to stop it, and it also regulates the trade in wildlife and its parts.

## Key Points to Remember
- Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972 (Act 53 of 1972); Entry 17B of the Concurrent List. 2022 Amendment: six schedules reduced to four; vermin schedule removed; CITES schedule added. Vermin is declared under section 62. Protected areas: national park (35), sanctuary (18 and later), conservation reserve (36A), community reserve (36C), tiger reserve (38V). CITES: Management Authority and Scientific Authority. Hunting of Schedule I or II animals is banned (section 9); exceptions are sections 11 and 12 and self-defence. Penalty: up to 3 years or fine up to Rs 1 lakh (section 51(1)); aggravated offences 3 to 7 years and fine of at least Rs 25,000. Cognizance only on a complaint of listed officers or after 60 days' notice (section 55); compounding up to Rs 5 lakh (section 54).
