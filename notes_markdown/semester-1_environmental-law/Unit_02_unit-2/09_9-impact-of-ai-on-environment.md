# 9. Impact of AI on Environment
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester 1) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/impact-of-ai-on-environment/

## In One Minute
Artificial intelligence (AI) seems clean but depends on electricity, water, metals and land. It also helps forecast floods, track forest loss and save energy. India has no AI-specific environmental law. The general environmental laws and principles apply to the physical side of AI, such as data centres and electronic waste.

## Legal Provision
- No statute deals with AI and the environment. These existing sources apply.
- Section 3(1), Environment (Protection) Act, 1986: the Central Government may take all measures it considers necessary. The aim is to protect and improve the environment and to prevent, control and abate pollution. Section 25, Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974: no one may set up an industry likely to discharge sewage or trade effluent without the State Board's previous consent. Section 21, Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981: no industrial plant may operate in an air pollution control area without the State Board's consent. E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022: notified on 2 November 2022 and in force from 1 April 2023. They set up extended producer responsibility, under which producers must meet recycling targets for electronic waste. Principles: the precautionary principle, the polluter pays principle and sustainable development, which Indian courts treat as part of environmental law.

## Meaning and Definition
The impact of AI on the environment has two sides. The first is the footprint of building and running AI systems. The second is the use of AI to protect the environment. The law deals with the footprint through ordinary rules on energy, water, waste and land.

Harm Electricity use, cooling water, electronic waste, mining for minerals, land and heat. Existing law EP Act, 1986, Water and Air Act consents, E-Waste Rules, 2022 and court-made principles. Benefit Weather and flood forecasting, forest and pollution monitoring, precision farming and efficient power grids. The law reaches the harm through the physical facilities. The benefits do not remove the need to measure and cut the footprint. There is no separate AI statute for the environment in India.

## Key Points to Remember
- AI has a physical footprint: electricity, water, e-waste, mining, land. AI also helps: forecasting, monitoring, precision farming, grid efficiency. No AI-specific environmental statute exists in India. E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 apply extended producer responsibility. Precaution, polluter pays and sustainable development guide the courts.
