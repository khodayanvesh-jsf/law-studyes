# 8. Noise Pollution
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester 1) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/noise-pollution/

## In One Minute
Noise is unwanted or excessive sound. Indian law treats it as a pollutant. The Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000 fix limits by area, protect silence zones and restrict loudspeakers. Courts read Article 21 to include freedom from noise. Exams ask the limits, the rules and the leading cases.

## Legal Provision
- Environment (Protection) Act, 1986: the parent Act. The Noise Rules are made under it. Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000: ambient noise standards by area, silence zones and loudspeaker restrictions. Section 2(a), Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981: "air pollutant" includes noise. The words "including noise" were added by amendment. Section 270, BNS and section 152, BNSS: public nuisance and the Magistrate's order to remove it. Article 21, Constitution of India: the right to life, read to include a life free from noise pollution.

## Meaning and Definition
Noise pollution is unwanted or excessive sound that disturbs people or harms health. Courts ask whether the listener wants the sound. A melody that a person chooses to hear is not a pollutant. The same sound forced on others can be.

## Key Points to Remember
- Noise is a pollutant under the Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000. Limits (day/night, dB(A)): industrial 75/70, commercial 65/55, residential 55/45, silence zone 50/40. Day is 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. A silence zone is at least 100 metres around hospitals, educational institutions and courts. Loudspeakers need written permission and are barred at night, with a limited festival relaxation. Article 21 includes freedom from noise. Articles 19(1)(a) and 25 do not give a right to make noise. Noise Rules, 2000: complaint to the authority when noise exceeds the standard by 10 dB(A) or more (rule 7); prohibitory order (rule 8); horns, crackers and construction equipment banned at night in residential areas and silence zones (rule 5A). Loudspeaker boundary limit: 10 dB(A) above the area standard or 75 dB(A), whichever is lower; private systems 5 dB(A) above the standard (rule 5(4) and (5)).
