# 7. Reliefs against smoke and noise
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester 1) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/reliefs-against-smoke-and-noise/

## In One Minute
Smoke and noise are classic continuing nuisances. A person can sue for an injunction and damages, ask a Magistrate to act, or complain to the State Pollution Control Board. Smoke that damages property can be stopped even in an industrial area. The Air Act, 1981 treats noise as an air pollutant.

## Legal Provision
- Sections 38 and 40, Specific Relief Act, 1963: perpetual injunction, and damages with or instead of it. Section 2(a), Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981: "air pollutant" includes any solid, liquid or gaseous substance, including noise, in such concentration as may be or tend to be injurious to people, other living creatures, plants, property or environment. Section 19, Air Act: the State Government may declare air pollution control areas. It may prohibit the use of fuel other than an approved fuel there. Section 21, Air Act: no industrial plant may operate in such an area without the previous consent of the State Board. Section 22, Air Act: no emission above the standards laid down by the State Board. Section 22A, Air Act: where excess emission is apprehended , the Board may apply to a court to restrain it. The court must be at least that of a Metropolitan Magistrate or a Judicial Magistrate of the first class. Section 31A, Air Act: the Board may issue written directions, including closure of an industry or stoppage of electricity and water. Section 270, BNS and section 152, BNSS: public nuisance and the Magistrate's order to remove it.

## Meaning and Definition
Reliefs against smoke and noise are the remedies for a nuisance that is continuing. The harm travels through the air, so it is indirect . It is nuisance and not trespass.

## Key Points to Remember
- Smoke and noise are indirect, so the tort is nuisance. Injunction is the main remedy. Damages come with it or instead. St Helen's: locality is no defence to material injury to property. Air Act: noise is included in "air pollutant". The Board uses sections 19, 21, 22, 22A and 31A. Public nuisance proceedings under section 270 BNS and section 152 BNSS also apply.
