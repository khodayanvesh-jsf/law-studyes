# 5. Relevant provisions of BNS and BNSS, for the abatement of public nuisance in pollution cases
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester 1) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/provisions-of-bns-and-bnss-for-abatement-of-public-nuisance/

## In One Minute
Pollution that harms the public is a public nuisance. Three general-law routes deal with it. The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) punishes it. The Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) gives a quick Magistrate's order. Section 91 of the Code of Civil Procedure allows a civil suit. BNS and BNSS replaced the Indian Penal Code and the Code of Criminal Procedure on 1 July 2024. Exams ask this as a long answer, so know both the new and old numbers.

## Legal Provision
- Section 270, BNS (earlier IPC section 268): defines public nuisance. Section 279, BNS (earlier IPC section 277): fouling the water of a public spring or reservoir. Section 280, BNS (earlier IPC section 278): making the atmosphere noxious to health. Section 292, BNS (earlier IPC section 290): general punishment for public nuisance where no other provision applies. Section 293, BNS (earlier IPC section 291): continuing a nuisance after a public servant has ordered it stopped. Section 152, BNSS (earlier CrPC section 133): conditional order of a Magistrate for removal of nuisance. Section 91, Code of Civil Procedure, 1908: a civil suit on a public nuisance. The Code was not replaced.

## Meaning and Definition
Public nuisance under section 270 BNS is an act or illegal omission that causes common injury, danger or annoyance to the public or to people who live nearby. It also covers acts that must cause injury, obstruction, danger or annoyance to people using a public right. A common nuisance is not excused on the ground that it causes some convenience or advantage.

Smoke, foul discharge, noise and dust that trouble a whole locality fit this definition. The remedy is not left to one victim. The State, a Magistrate or a group of citizens can act.

How a Magistrate acts against a nuisance under section 152 BNSS How to read this: follow the boxes from top to bottom. Each step leads to the next. The last box is the result. 1. Information A police report or other information reaches the Magistrate. 2. Conditional order The Magistrate orders the person to remove or stop the nuisance within a fixed time. 3. Choice The person either obeys, or appears and shows cause why the order should not be made absolute. 4. Result The Magistrate makes the order absolute, or drops it.

## Key Points to Remember
- BNS and BNSS replaced the IPC and CrPC from 1 July 2024. The CPC was not replaced. BNS section 270 defines public nuisance. Sections 279, 280, 292, 293 punish it. BNSS section 152 (earlier CrPC 133): conditional order of a Magistrate. CPC section 91: Advocate-General, or two or more persons with leave, can sue without special damage. Ratlam (1980): lack of funds is no defence to a public body's duty.
