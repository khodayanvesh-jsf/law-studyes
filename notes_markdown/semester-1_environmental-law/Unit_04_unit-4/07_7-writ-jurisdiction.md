# 7. Writ Jurisdiction
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester 1) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/writ-jurisdiction/

## In One Minute
A writ is a formal court order. Article 32 lets a person go straight to the Supreme Court to enforce a Fundamental Right. Article 226 lets a High Court issue writs for a Fundamental Right and also "for any other purpose". Environmental cases mostly arrive through public interest litigation. A public-spirited person or group can sue for people who cannot go to court. The Court can also keep a case open for years and supervise the government, which is called continuing mandamus.

## Legal Provision
- Article 32(1) and (2): the right to move the Supreme Court to enforce the rights in Part III is guaranteed. The Court may issue directions, orders or writs, including habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, quo warranto and certiorari. Article 32(4): this right shall not be suspended except as the Constitution provides. Article 226(1): every High Court may issue directions, orders or writs throughout its territories "for the enforcement of any of the rights conferred by Part III and for any other purpose". Article 226(2): a High Court may also act where the cause of action arises wholly or in part within its territories, even if the authority sits elsewhere. Article 21: the usual Fundamental Right pleaded in environmental petitions.

## Meaning and Definition
Writ jurisdiction is a court's power to issue writs. The five writs are habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, quo warranto and certiorari. In environmental cases the most useful is mandamus, a command to a public authority to do its duty.

Habeas corpus : produce a detained person before the court. Mandamus : perform a public duty. Prohibition : stop a lower court or tribunal from acting beyond its power. Quo warranto : show by what authority a public office is held. Certiorari : quash an order of a lower authority.

## Key Points to Remember
- Article 32: Supreme Court, Part III rights only, itself in Part III. Article 226: High Courts, Part III rights and "any other purpose". PIL relaxes locus standi; a petition must still be in genuine public interest. Rural Litigation Kendra (1985): a letter treated as an Article 32 petition.
