# 10. Nairobi Declaration, 1982
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester 1) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/nairobi-declaration-1982/

## In One Minute
There is no binding "Nairobi Convention, 1982". What happened was a special session of the UNEP Governing Council, held in Nairobi from 10 to 18 May 1982. It marked ten years since Stockholm. The session adopted the Nairobi Declaration, a soft-law statement. Its importance is historical. It belongs to the chain that led to the Brundtland Commission and the idea of sustainable development.

## Legal Provision
- Special session of the UNEP Governing Council, Nairobi, 10 to 18 May 1982: a session of special character called by the General Assembly. It marked the tenth anniversary of the Stockholm conference. Nairobi Declaration: adopted on 18 May 1982. It expressed satisfaction at progress since 1972 and anxiety about the future. General Assembly resolution 38/161 (19 December 1983): set up the special commission later called the World Commission on Environment and Development.

## Meaning and Definition
The Nairobi Declaration is a political statement of governments, not a treaty. Like the Stockholm Declaration, it is soft law . It creates no duty that a court or tribunal can enforce .

The syllabus title sometimes appears as "Nairobi Convention". That name is wrong for this topic. The correct instrument is the Nairobi Declaration of 1982.

## Key Points to Remember
- Nairobi session: UNEP Governing Council, 10 to 18 May 1982; Declaration adopted 18 May. It is a declaration, not a convention. Resolution 38/161 (19 December 1983) led to the Brundtland Commission. Our Common Future (1987) gave the main definition of sustainable development.
