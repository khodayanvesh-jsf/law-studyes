# 1. International Environmental Regime
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester 1) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5

## In One Minute
Pollution, climate change and loss of species do not stop at a border. No single country can solve them. The international environmental regime is the set of principles, treaties and bodies that States have built to deal with them together. It has four layers: customary rules, non-binding declarations, binding treaties and institutions. Exam answers score by keeping these layers apart and placing each instrument in the right one.

## Legal Provision
- Article 51(c) of the Constitution of India: the State shall endeavour to foster respect for international law and treaty obligations. Article 253: Parliament may make any law for India or any part of it to implement a treaty, agreement or convention. The same Article covers any decision of an international conference or body. Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 and Air Act, 1981: their preambles refer to the Stockholm conference of June 1972, which shows how an international event led to Indian statutes. Biological Diversity Act, 2002: its preamble refers to the Convention on Biological Diversity signed at Rio de Janeiro on 5 June 1992.

## Meaning and Definition
A regime is a set of principles, rules and decision-making bodies that States accept for one subject. The international environmental regime covers the rules on pollution crossing borders, protection of species and habitats, the ozone layer, the climate and sustainable development.

## Key Points to Remember
- Four layers: customary rules, soft law, treaties, institutions. Stockholm, Nairobi and Johannesburg are declarations; Ramsar, Bonn, the Climate Convention, Kyoto and Paris are treaties. UNEP was created by General Assembly resolution of 15 December 1972. Article 253 lets Parliament legislate to implement a treaty. Vellore: sustainable development is part of customary international law.
