# 13. Johannesburg Declaration 2002
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester 1) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/johannesburg-declaration-2002/

## In One Minute
There is no binding "Johannesburg Convention". The event was the World Summit on Sustainable Development, held in Johannesburg from 26 August to 4 September 2002. It reviewed ten years of progress since the Rio Earth Summit, so it is called Rio+10. It produced two non-binding documents, the Johannesburg Declaration and a Plan of Implementation. It is best remembered for the three pillars of sustainable development and for voluntary partnerships.

## Legal Provision
- World Summit on Sustainable Development, Johannesburg, 26 August to 4 September 2002: held under General Assembly resolution 55/199 as a ten-year review of the Rio conference. Johannesburg Declaration on Sustainable Development, paragraph 5: the participants assume a collective responsibility to advance and strengthen the interdependent and mutually reinforcing pillars of sustainable development. The text names those pillars: economic development, social development and environmental protection.

## Meaning and Definition
The Johannesburg Declaration is a political statement of the governments at the Summit. It is soft law . The Plan of Implementation is a long action plan that goes with it. Neither is a treaty and neither can be enforced in court.

The syllabus sometimes writes "Johannesburg Convention". That is wrong. The correct name is the Johannesburg Declaration, 2002.

## Key Points to Remember
- World Summit on Sustainable Development, 26 August to 4 September 2002; Rio+10. Outputs: Johannesburg Declaration and Plan of Implementation, both non-binding. Three pillars: economic development, social development, environmental protection. Type II outcomes: voluntary partnerships.
