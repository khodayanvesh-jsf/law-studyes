# 8. Ramsar Convention 1971
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester 1) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/ramsar-convention-1971/

## In One Minute
The Ramsar Convention is the treaty on wetlands. It was signed on 2 February 1971 at Ramsar, Iran. It asks each member country to list wetlands of international importance and to use all its wetlands wisely. It predates the Stockholm conference of 1972. It binds only the States that join, and the Convention entered into force for India on 1 February 1982.

## Legal Provision
- Convention on Wetlands of International Importance especially as Waterfowl Habitat: signed at Ramsar, Iran, on 2 February 1971 and in force from 21 December 1975. Core duties of each Party: to designate at least one wetland for the List of Wetlands of International Importance. The second duty is to promote the wise use of all wetlands in its territory. Constitution of India, Article 253: Parliament may legislate to implement a treaty, so the Convention enters Indian law through Indian statutes and rules.

## Meaning and Definition
A wetland is an area of marsh, fen, peatland or water, whether natural or artificial, permanent or temporary, fresh, brackish or salt. Article 1 also includes marine water not deeper than six metres at low tide. The Convention protects these areas because they control floods, refill groundwater, clean water and give homes to waterbirds that migrate across countries.

The treaty is an early one among the modern global environmental treaties. Its concern is one type of ecosystem.

## Key Points to Remember
- Signed 2 February 1971 at Ramsar, Iran; in force 21 December 1975. Duties: designate at least one wetland and promote wise use of all. Montreux Record: listed sites whose ecological character is changing. In force for India from 1 February 1982. Art 2(3): listing keeps sovereignty. Art 2(5): deletion only in urgent national interest. Art 4(2): compensate by new reserves. Art 3(2): report changes in ecological character. Art 11: denunciation after five years.
