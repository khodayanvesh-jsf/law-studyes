# 5. Liability of Multinational corporations/Companies
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester 1) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/liability-of-multinational-corporations-companies/

## In One Minute
International law was built for States, not companies. A victim of a multinational company's pollution cannot sue it before an international tribunal in the way one State can arbitrate against another. The company's own structure adds a second barrier. A parent and its local subsidiary are separate legal persons. The Bhopal gas leak showed this problem.

## Legal Provision
- Bhopal Gas Leak Disaster (Processing of Claims) Act, 1985: authorised the Government of India to act as the exclusive representative of all victims, and so to consolidate their claims. United Nations Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights (2011): 31 principles on the State's duty to protect, the company's responsibility to respect and access to remedy. They are soft law.

## Meaning and Definition
A multinational company is a company group that operates in several countries, usually through locally incorporated subsidiaries. The home State is where the parent sits. The host State is where the subsidiary runs its plant.

The question here is when the parent, or the group, answers in law for environmental harm done by the subsidiary in the host State.

## Key Points to Remember
- International law binds States, not companies. Separate corporate personality shields a parent. Bhopal: Government of India as exclusive representative of victims under the 1985 Act. Guiding Principles, 2011: soft law with 31 principles. Home-court claims against a parent turn on a duty of care shown on the facts.
