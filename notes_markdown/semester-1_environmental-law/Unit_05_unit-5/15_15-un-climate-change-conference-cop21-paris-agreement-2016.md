# 15. UN Climate Change Conference (COP21) & Paris Agreement 2016
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester 1) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/cop21-and-paris-agreement-2016/

## In One Minute
The Paris Agreement was adopted at the 21st Conference of the Parties (COP21) on 12 December 2015. It came into force on 4 November 2016. Its goal is to hold warming well below 2 degrees Celsius and to pursue 1.5 degrees. Unlike Kyoto, every country makes its own plan, called a Nationally Determined Contribution. India ratified the Agreement on 2 October 2016.

## Legal Provision
- Adoption: at COP21, Paris, on 12 December 2015, under the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change. Entry into force: 4 November 2016, once at least 55 countries representing at least 55 per cent of emissions had ratified. Article 2: hold the rise in global average temperature well below 2 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels, and pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5 degrees. Article 4: each Party prepares, communicates and updates its Nationally Determined Contribution, and each new one should show progression. Article 14: a global stocktake assesses collective progress. The first concluded at COP28 in Dubai in 2023.

## Meaning and Definition
The Paris Agreement is a treaty adopted under the Climate Change Convention. A Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC) is a Party's own plan to cut or limit its greenhouse gas emissions. COP21 is the name of the conference, the 21st Conference of the Parties, where the Agreement was adopted.

## Key Points to Remember
- Adopted at COP21 on 12 December 2015; in force 4 November 2016. Goal: well below 2 degrees, pursue 1.5 degrees. Every Party files its own NDC; each new one should show progression. Global stocktake under Article 14; first concluded at COP28, Dubai, 2023. India signed 22 April 2016 and ratified 2 October 2016. Art 2(1)(a)-(c): temperature, adaptation, finance. Art 4(9): NDC every five years. Art 4(4): developed countries lead with absolute targets. Art 6: voluntary cooperation. Art 8: loss and damage. Art 9: finance. Art 15: non-punitive compliance. Art 21: 55 Parties and 55 per cent of emissions.
