# 9. Climate Justice
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester 1) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/climate-justice/

## In One Minute
Climate change does not hurt everyone equally. Poor communities and small island States suffered least from causing it, but they often suffer most from its floods, droughts and heat. Climate justice is the idea that law and policy must correct this imbalance. Exams ask for its meaning, its dimensions and its basis in treaties and Indian law.

## Legal Provision
- UN Framework Convention on Climate Change, 1992: parties should protect the climate system on the basis of equity . They act in line with their common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities. COP27 and COP28 decisions: at COP27 in Sharm el-Sheikh (Decision 2/CP.27 and 2/CMA.4) governments decided to set up new funding arrangements and a dedicated fund to help developing countries respond to loss and damage. A transitional committee was to recommend how to operationalise them at COP28. At COP28 in Dubai, the first decision adopted (Decision 1/CP.28) approved the Fund's governing instrument and invited the World Bank to host it on an interim basis (UNFCCC). Paris Agreement: it carries forward that principle, and recognises loss and damage associated with the adverse effects of climate change. Article 21, Constitution of India: no person shall be deprived of life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law. The Supreme Court reads this to include a healthy environment. Article 14, Constitution of India: the State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws.

## Meaning and Definition
Climate justice is the principle that the burdens and benefits of climate change and of climate action must be shared fairly. It treats climate change as a question of rights and equity, not only as a scientific problem. It asks three things: who caused the problem, who is hurt by it, and who should bear the cost of fixing it.

Four dimensions of climate justice How to read this: start at the top box and follow the lines down to the four dimensions. Each box says what fairness means in that dimension. Climate justice Distributive Costs shared fairly; those who did least carry least. Procedural Affected people can take part, get information and go to court. Intergenerational Present generations hold the planet in trust for future ones. Corrective Those who caused the harm help repair it; victims are supported.

## Key Points to Remember
- Climate justice: fairness in who causes, suffers and pays. Dimensions: distributive, procedural, intergenerational, corrective. Treaty anchor: CBDR (UNFCCC 1992, Paris Agreement), finance and technology, loss and damage. Loss and damage fund: agreed at COP27, Sharm el-Sheikh, in November 2022; funding arrangements and the Fund operationalised at COP28 in 2023. India: Article 21, Vellore Citizens principles. UNFCCC Art 3(1) equity and Art 4(3)-(5), 4(7) support; Kyoto Art 12(8) adaptation share; Paris Art 2(2), 4(4), 8 (loss and damage), 9 (finance).
