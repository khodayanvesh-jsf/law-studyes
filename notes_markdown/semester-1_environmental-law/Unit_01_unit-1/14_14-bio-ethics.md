# 14. Bio ethics
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester 1) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/bio-ethics/

## In One Minute
Science lets people change crops, patent genes, clone animals and test medicines on living creatures. Bio ethics asks where the limits should be. In environmental law it guides how biodiversity, genes and animals may be used. Exams ask for its principles, its three viewpoints and the laws that apply it.

## Legal Provision
- Section 3(1), Biological Diversity Act, 2002: certain persons (foreign nationals, non-resident Indians and certain bodies) need the previous approval of the National Biodiversity Authority. Without it, they cannot obtain a biological resource occurring in India, or knowledge associated with it, for research, commercial use or bio-survey. Section 2(c), Biological Diversity Act, 2002: biological resources include plants, animals, micro-organisms or parts of their genetic material and derivatives (naturally occurring biochemical compounds, but not value added products), and not human genetic material. Section 21, Biological Diversity Act, 2002: the Authority must ensure that its terms of approval secure fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from the use of the resource. Convention on Biological Diversity, 1992, Nagoya Protocol, 2010 and Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety, 2000: the treaties on conservation, sharing of benefits and safe handling of living modified organisms. Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960 and Article 51A(g), Constitution of India: the law on animal suffering and the duty of compassion for living creatures.

## Meaning and Definition
Bio ethics is the study of the moral questions that arise when living organisms are used in medicine, biotechnology, agriculture and research. It began in medicine and now covers the environment: how people may use biodiversity, genes, animals and ecosystems.

Core principles of bio ethics How to read this: start at the top box and follow the lines down to the five principles. Each box says what the principle asks. Bio ethics Autonomy and consent People and communities must be told and be free to agree or refuse. Beneficence Aim to do good for people and nature. Non-maleficence Avoid harm that can be avoided. Justice Share benefits fairly; do not load risks on the poor. Respect for life Living beings, not only humans, have value.

## Key Points to Remember
- Bio ethics: ethical limits on using living organisms and biological resources. Principles: consent, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice, respect for life. Viewpoints: anthropocentric, biocentric, ecocentric. Biological Diversity Act, 2002: approval (section 3) and benefit sharing (section 21). CBD 1992, Nagoya 2010 (access and benefit sharing), Cartagena 2000 (biosafety).
