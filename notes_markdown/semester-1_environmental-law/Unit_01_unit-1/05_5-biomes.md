## In One Minute
A biome is a large region of the Earth with a distinct climate and the plants and animals suited to it. Examples are a desert, a tropical rainforest and a tundra. Biomes are either on land or in water. Exams ask for the kinds of biome, one example of each, and the threats they face.

## Legal Provision
- No Indian Act defines a biome. The concept matters because many protections are designed around regions of distinct life.
- Section 2(b), Biological Diversity Act, 2002: biological diversity includes diversity "of eco-systems", which covers the variety found across biomes. Conservation tools: sanctuaries, national parks and biosphere reserves are used to protect samples of different biomes.

## Meaning and Definition
A biome is a large geographical region marked by a distinct climate and by the plant and animal communities adapted to it. An ecosystem can be as small as a pond. A biome spreads across a continent.

## Key Points to Remember
- Biome: large region defined by climate and its adapted life. Terrestrial: rainforest, savanna, desert, grassland, temperate forest, taiga, tundra. Aquatic: freshwater, marine, estuary. Threats: deforestation, climate change, pollution, overuse, habitat loss. Biomes are larger than ecosystems and smaller than the biosphere.
