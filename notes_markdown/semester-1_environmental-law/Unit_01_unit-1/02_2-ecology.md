# 2. Ecology
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester 1) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1

## In One Minute
Ecology is the scientific study of how living things relate to each other and to their surroundings. It is a science, not a statute. Environmental law uses ecological facts to decide whether an activity will cause harm. Exams ask for the meaning of ecology, its parts and how it differs from environment.

## Legal Provision
- No Indian statute defines ecology. The idea enters the law in two ways.
- Section 2(a), Environment (Protection) Act, 1986: environment includes the "inter-relationship" among water, air, land and living beings. This is the relationship ecology studies. Environmental impact assessment: a study of how a project will affect its surroundings, which relies on ecological data.

## Meaning and Definition
Ecology is the scientific study of the relationships between living organisms and their physical surroundings. It looks at how organisms depend on each other and how energy and materials move among them.

The German biologist Ernst Haeckel coined the term in 1866. It joins two Greek words: oikos , meaning house or habitat , and logos , meaning study. The first part of the word shows the idea: living things share one house.

Levels at which ecology studies life How to read this: read from the smallest level to the largest, left to right on a wide screen or top to bottom on a phone. Each level contains the one before it. Level 1 Organism: one living being. Level 2 Population: organisms of one species in an area. Level 3 Community: all populations of different species in an area. Level 4 Ecosystem: a community together with its non-living surroundings. Level 5 Biosphere: all ecosystems of the Earth.

## Key Points to Remember
- Ecology: scientific study of organisms and their surroundings. Coined by Ernst Haeckel in 1866 from oikos and logos . Biotic factors are living; abiotic factors are non-living. Levels: organism, population, community, ecosystem, biosphere. No Indian Act defines ecology; section 2(a) of the 1986 Act covers the inter-relationship.
