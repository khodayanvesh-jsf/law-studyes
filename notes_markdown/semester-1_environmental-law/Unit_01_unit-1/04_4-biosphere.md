## In One Minute
The biosphere is the whole zone of the Earth where life exists. It is the sum of all ecosystems, found where land, water and air meet. Pollution in one place moves through it and harms places far away. That is why many environmental treaties talk about the biosphere. Exams ask for its meaning and how it differs from an ecosystem and a biome.

## Legal Provision
- No Indian Act defines the biosphere. The idea appears in policy and in international law.
- Section 2(a), Environment (Protection) Act, 1986: environment covers water, air and land and the inter-relationship among them and with human beings, other living creatures, plants, micro-organisms and property. This is related to the biosphere idea. Biosphere reserves: India uses biosphere reserves as a conservation tool to protect whole-ecosystem function. They are a policy designation, not a statutory category.

## Meaning and Definition
The biosphere is the global sum of all ecosystems, the thin zone of the planet in which life exists. It forms where the lithosphere (land), the hydrosphere (water) and the atmosphere (air) overlap .

## Key Points to Remember
- Biosphere: the global sum of all ecosystems. It is where lithosphere, hydrosphere and atmosphere overlap. Pollution moves through it, so harm crosses borders. A biosphere reserve protects whole-ecosystem function. Biosphere is one; biomes are its large regions.
