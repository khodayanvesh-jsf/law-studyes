# 6. Relations of principal and agent with third parties
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/relations-of-principal-and-agent-with-third-parties/

## In One Minute
Contracts made through an agent have the same legal consequences as if the principal had made them himself (section 226). Where an agent does more than he is authorized to do, the principal is bound only by what is within the authority if it can be separated (section 227), and not at all if it cannot (section 228). Notice given to the agent in the course of the business is notice to the principal (section 229). The Act also deals with a contract made with an agent who is supposed to be the principal (sections 231 and 232), with inducing a belief that only one of them will be held liable (section 234), with a principal who induces belief in the agent's authority (section 237), and with an agent's misrepresentation or fraud (section 238). Key points for the answer: each section with the Act's illustration.

## Meaning and Definition
Enforcement and consequences of the agent's contracts (section 226)

Rule. Contracts entered into through an agent, and obligations from acts done by him, are enforced and have the same consequences as if the principal had done them.

Example. Ravi's agent Sita, with authority to receive money, receives Rs 10,000 due to Ravi from Chandu. Chandu is discharged of his obligation to pay it to Ravi (illustration (b)).

Agent exceeds authority (sections 227 and 228)

Rule. If the part within authority can be separated, the principal is bound by that part only (section 227). If it cannot be separated, the principal is not bound to recognize the transaction (section 228).

Example. Anil authorizes Babu to buy 500 sheep. Babu buys 500 sheep and 200 lambs for one sum. The excess cannot be separated. Anil may repudiate the whole transaction (the Act's illustration to section 228).

Notice to the agent (section 229)

Rule. Notice given to, or information obtained by, the agent in the course of the business he transacts for the principal has the same consequences as if given to or obtained by the principal.

Contract with an agent supposed to be the principal (sections 231, 232 and 234)

Rule. Where a person contracts with an agent without knowing or having reason to suspect that he is an agent, the principal may require performance (section 231). The other party has against the principal the rights he would have had against the agent. If the principal discloses himself before the contract is completed, the other party may refuse to fulfil it if he can show he would not have entered into it had he known who the principal was or that the agent was not a principal. The principal obtains performance only subject to the rights and obligations between the agent and the other party (section 232). A person who induces the agent to believe that the principal only will be held liable, or the principal to believe that the agent only will be held liable, cannot afterwards hold the other liable (section 234).

Example. A, who owes B 500 rupees, sells B rice worth 1,000 rupees as agent for C, and B does not know this. C cannot compel B to take the rice without allowing B to set off A's debt (the Act's illustration to section 232).

Principal who induces belief in authority (section 237)

Rule. The principal is bound by the agent's unauthorized acts if by his words or conduct he induced third persons to believe they were within the agent's authority.

Misrepresentation or fraud by the agent (section 238)

Rule. Misrepresentation or fraud by agents acting in the course of their business has the same effect on agreements as if by the principals. In matters that do not fall within the agents' authority, it does not affect the principals. Illustration (a): A, B's agent for sale, induces C to buy by a misrepresentation he was not authorized to make. The contract is voidable, as between B and C, at the option of C.

Is the principal bound? How to read this: start at the top. 1. Was the contract entered into through an agent? Yes: same consequences as if the principal made it (section 226). 2. Did the agent exceed his authority? Can the excess be separated? Yes: the principal is bound only by the part within authority (section 227). No: he is not bound to recognize the transaction (section 228). 3. Did the principal by words or conduct induce the third person to believe the act was authorized? Yes: he is bound (section 237).

## Key Points to Remember
- An agent's contract has the same consequences as the principal's own (section 226). Excess of authority: separable, the principal is bound by the part within authority (section 227). Not separable, he is not bound (section 228). Notice to the agent in the course of the business is notice to the principal (section 229). Undisclosed principal: sections 231 and 232, with set-off. Inducing belief of exclusive liability bars a later claim against the other (section 234). The principal is bound where his words or conduct induced belief in the agent's authority (section 237). An agent's misrepresentation in the course of business binds the principal as his own (section 238).
