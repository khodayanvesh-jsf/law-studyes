# 7. Termination of Agency
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/termination-of-agency/

## In One Minute
An agency is terminated by the principal revoking his authority, by the agent renouncing the business, by completion of the business, by the death or unsoundness of mind of either, or by the principal being adjudicated an insolvent (section 201). Where the agent has an interest in the subject-matter, the agency cannot be terminated to the prejudice of that interest without an express contract (section 202). Reasonable notice is needed (section 206), and the termination takes effect only when it becomes known (section 208). Key points for the answer: the ways of termination, the limits on revocation, and the effect of notice.

## Meaning and Definition
Ways an agency ends (section 201)

Rule. An agency is terminated in these ways.

The principal revokes the authority. The agent renounces the business of the agency. The business of the agency is completed. Either the principal or the agent dies or becomes of unsound mind. The principal is adjudicated an insolvent under any Act for the relief of insolvent debtors.

Example. Ravi appoints Sita as his agent to sell his scooter. Sita sells it and hands over the price. The business of the agency is completed, and the agency ends.

Agent with an interest in the subject-matter (section 202)

Rule. Where the agent has himself an interest in the property which is the subject-matter of the agency, then, without an express contract, the agency cannot be terminated to the prejudice of that interest.

Example. Anil authorizes Babu to sell Anil's land and to pay himself out of the proceeds the debts Anil owes him. Anil cannot revoke the authority, and Anil's death or insanity does not end it (the Act's illustration (a)).

Revocation (sections 203 and 204)

Rule. Subject to section 202, the principal may revoke at any time before the authority has been exercised so as to bind him (section 203). He cannot revoke after it has been partly exercised, as regards acts and obligations arising from acts already done (section 204).

Example. Chandu authorizes Dev to buy 1,000 bales of cotton for him, to be paid for from Chandu's money in Dev's hands. Dev buys in his own name, making himself personally liable. Chandu cannot revoke Dev's authority as regards payment for that cotton (section 204, illustration (a)).

Compensation and notice (sections 205 and 206)

Rule. Where there is an express or implied contract that the agency will continue for a period, the party who revokes or renounces earlier without sufficient cause must compensate the other (section 205). Reasonable notice must be given of revocation or renunciation. Otherwise the damage must be made good by the one to the other (section 206).

Express or implied revocation (section 207)

Rule. Revocation and renunciation may be express or implied in conduct. If A empowers B to let A's house and then lets it himself, this implies revocation of B's authority.

When termination takes effect (section 208)

Rule. Termination does not take effect as regards the agent before it becomes known to him, or as regards third persons before it becomes known to them.

Example. A directs B to sell goods at five per cent commission and later revokes by letter. B sells for 100 rupees after the letter is sent but before he receives it. The sale binds A, and B is entitled to 5 rupees commission (the Act's illustration (a)).

Duty on the principal's death or insanity, and sub-agents (sections 209 and 210)

Rule. When the agency ends by the principal's death or unsoundness of mind, the agent must take all reasonable steps, on behalf of the principal's representatives, to protect and preserve the interests entrusted to him (section 209). Termination of the agent's authority terminates the authority of his sub-agents, subject to the rules in the Chapter (section 210).

Can the principal revoke? How to read this: start at the top. 1. Does the agent have an interest in the subject-matter, and is there no express contract to the contrary? Then the agency cannot be terminated to the prejudice of that interest (section 202). 2. Has the authority been partly exercised? The principal cannot revoke as regards acts and obligations arising from acts already done (section 204). 3. Otherwise he may revoke before the authority has been exercised so as to bind him (section 203), giving reasonable notice (section 206). The termination takes effect when it becomes known to the agent and to third persons (section 208).

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 201: revocation, renunciation, completion, death or unsound mind of either, insolvency of the principal. An agent's interest in the subject-matter limits termination (section 202). No revocation after partial exercise, as regards acts already done (section 204). Reasonable notice (section 206). Compensation for earlier termination without sufficient cause (section 205). Termination takes effect when it becomes known (section 208). Termination ends sub-agents' authority (section 210).
