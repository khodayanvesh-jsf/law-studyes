# 2. Creation of Agency
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/creation-of-agency/

## In One Minute
An agent's authority may be express or implied (sections 186 and 187). No consideration is necessary (section 185). An agent has authority in an emergency to protect his principal from loss (section 189). A person may also ratify acts done on his behalf without his authority (sections 196 to 200). A principal who leads third persons to believe that an agent's acts were authorized is bound by them (section 237). Key points for the answer: express and implied authority, emergency, ratification with its limits, and a principal who induces belief in authority.

## Meaning and Definition
Express and implied authority (sections 186 and 187)

Rule. An agent's authority is express when given by words spoken or written, and implied when inferred from the circumstances. The ordinary course of dealing may be a circumstance. No consideration is necessary (section 185).

Example. Ravi tells Sita in writing to buy stock for his shop. That is express authority. Sita has long ordered goods in Ravi's name for the shop, and Ravi, with knowledge, has paid for them from his own funds. Sita has implied authority to order further goods for the shop. This follows the illustration to section 187.

Extent of authority (section 188)

Rule. An agent authorized to do an act may do every lawful thing necessary to do it. An agent authorized to carry on a business may do every lawful thing necessary, or usually done in conducting that business.

Authority in an emergency (section 189)

Rule. In an emergency the agent may do all such acts to protect the principal from loss as a person of ordinary prudence would do in his own case.

Example. Kiran, agent of Lata, receives fruit to forward to another town. The fruit will spoil on the journey. Kiran may sell it where he is, as in the Act's illustration (b).

Ratification (sections 196 to 199)

Rule. Where acts are done on behalf of a person without his knowledge or authority, he may ratify or disown them. If he ratifies them, the effects are the same as if they had been done with his authority (section 196). Ratification may be express or implied in his conduct (section 197). It is not valid if his knowledge of the facts is materially defective (section 198). Ratifying one act ratifies the whole transaction of which it was a part (section 199).

Example. Dev buys goods in Esha's name without authority. Esha later sells those goods to Farid on her own account. Her conduct implies ratification of the purchase. This follows illustration (a) to section 197.

Limits on ratification (section 200)

Rule. An unauthorized act that would subject a third person to damages, or end a right or interest of a third person, cannot be given that effect by ratification.

Example. Gopi holds a lease from Hema, ending on three months' notice. Ibrahim, without Hema's authority, gives Gopi notice to end the lease. Hema cannot ratify the notice so as to bind Gopi. This is illustration (b) to section 200.

A principal who induces belief in authority (section 237)

Rule. A principal is bound by his agent's unauthorized acts if by his words or conduct he induced third persons to believe they were within the agent's authority.

Example. A consigns goods to B for sale with a private instruction not to sell below a fixed price. C, knowing nothing of the instruction, buys below that price. A is bound by the contract (illustration (a) to section 237).

Ratification: can the principal ratify? How to read this: start at the top. 1. An act was done on his behalf without his knowledge or authority. He may ratify or disown it (section 196). 2. Is his knowledge of the facts materially defective? If yes, no valid ratification (section 198). 3. Would the act subject a third person to damages or end his right or interest? If yes, ratification cannot give it that effect (section 200). 4. Otherwise he ratifies the whole transaction (section 199), with the same effect as if authorized.

## Key Points to Remember
- No consideration is necessary to create an agency (section 185). Authority may be express or implied (sections 186 and 187). In an emergency the agent may act as a person of ordinary prudence would to protect the principal (section 189). Ratification: same effect as authority (section 196). Not valid on materially defective knowledge (section 198). The whole transaction (section 199). Cannot hurt a third person (section 200). Holding out binds the principal (section 237).
