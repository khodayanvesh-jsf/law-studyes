# 4. Delegation of authority
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/delegation-of-authority/

## In One Minute
An agent cannot lawfully employ another to perform acts he has undertaken to perform personally, unless trade custom allows a sub-agent or the nature of the agency requires one (section 190). A sub-agent is a person employed by, and acting under the control of, the original agent in the business of the agency (section 191). Where an agent holds authority to name another person to act for the principal, the person named is not a sub-agent but an agent of the principal (section 194). The rules on the principal's and agent's responsibility are in sections 192, 193 and 195. Key points for the answer: the definition, the three situations, and who is responsible to whom.

## Meaning and Definition
When an agent cannot delegate (section 190)

Rule. An agent cannot lawfully employ another to perform acts he has expressly or impliedly undertaken to perform personally. The exceptions are: the ordinary custom of trade allows a sub-agent, or the nature of the agency requires one.

Example. Ravi appoints Sita to negotiate a sale herself. Sita hands the negotiation to her cousin. She undertook to perform the acts personally, and neither custom of trade nor the nature of the agency is stated. She could not lawfully do so (section 190).

A properly appointed sub-agent (sections 191 and 192)

Rule. A sub-agent is a person employed by, and acting under the control of, the original agent in the business of the agency. Where he is properly appointed, three things follow.

The principal is represented by the sub-agent as regards third persons, and is bound by and responsible for his acts as if he were an agent originally appointed by the principal. The agent is responsible to the principal for the acts of the sub-agent. The sub-agent is responsible to the agent, but not to the principal, except in cases of fraud or wilful wrong.

Example. Anil, an agent for sale, properly appoints Babu as his sub-agent to sell goods in another town. Babu's sale binds the principal as to the buyer. Anil answers to the principal for Babu's acts. Babu answers to Anil.

A sub-agent appointed without authority (section 193)

Rule. Where an agent, without authority, appoints a person as sub-agent, the agent stands towards that person as principal to agent. He is responsible for that person's acts to the principal and to third persons. The principal is not represented by, or responsible for the acts of, that person. That person is not responsible to the principal.

Example. Chandu is an agent with no authority to delegate. He appoints Dev to act. Chandu is Dev's principal and is responsible for Dev's acts to the real principal and to third persons. The real principal is not bound by Dev's acts.

A person named under authority to name (sections 194 and 195)

Rule. Where an agent has an express or implied authority to name another person to act for the principal, the person named is not a sub-agent. He is an agent of the principal for the part of the business entrusted to him (section 194). In selecting him the agent must exercise the discretion of a man of ordinary prudence in his own case. If he does, he is not responsible to the principal for the acts or negligence of the agent so selected (section 195).

Example. Esha authorizes her solicitor Farid to sell her estate by auction and to employ an auctioneer. Farid names Gopi. Gopi is not a sub-agent but Esha's agent for the sale (illustration (a) to section 194).

Termination (section 210)

Rule. The termination of an agent's authority causes the termination, subject to the rules on termination, of the authority of all sub-agents appointed by him.

Who is the other person? How to read this: start at the top. 1. Did the agent have authority to name another person to act for the principal? Yes: the person named is the principal's agent for that part (section 194), and the agent must exercise ordinary prudence in choosing (section 195). 2. Otherwise, was a sub-agent properly appointed (custom of trade, nature of the agency, or authority)? Yes: the principal is bound by his acts. The agent answers to the principal for him (section 192). 3. Was a sub-agent appointed without authority? The agent is principal to him and responsible to the principal and third persons. The principal is not bound (section 193).

## Key Points to Remember
- No delegation of acts undertaken personally, unless trade custom allows or the nature of the agency requires (section 190). Sub-agent: employed by and under the control of the agent (section 191). Properly appointed: principal bound. Agent responsible to principal. Sub-agent responsible to agent, to principal only for fraud or wilful wrong (section 192). Without authority: agent is principal to the sub-agent. The principal is not bound (section 193). Person named under authority to name: the principal's agent (section 194). Ordinary prudence in choosing (section 195). Termination of the agent's authority ends sub-agents' authority (section 210).
