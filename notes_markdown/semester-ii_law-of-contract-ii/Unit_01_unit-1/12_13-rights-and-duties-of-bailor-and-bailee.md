# 13. Rights and duties of bailor and bailee
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/rights-and-duties-of-bailor-and-bailee/

## In One Minute
The Act gives the bailor and the bailee duties and rights. The bailor must disclose known faults (section 150), repay necessary expenses where the bailee is unpaid (section 158) and answer for his title (section 164). The bailee must take proper care (section 151), use the goods only as the bailment allows (sections 153 and 154), bear the consequences of mixing goods (sections 155 to 157), and return them with any increase (sections 160, 161 and 163). The bailee has a lien for his service (section 170). Bankers, factors and certain others have a general lien (section 171). Key points for the answer: the duties of each party, the liens and the Act's illustrations.

## Meaning and Definition
Duties of the bailor

Rule. The bailor has these duties under the Act.

Disclose faults (section 150). He must disclose faults he is aware of that materially interfere with the use of the goods or expose the bailee to extraordinary risks. If goods are bailed for hire, he is responsible for damage from faults whether aware or not. Illustration (a): A lends B a horse he knows to be vicious without saying so. The horse runs away and B is injured. A is responsible. Illustration (b): A hires a carriage from B. It is unsafe, though B is not aware, and A is injured. B is responsible. Repay necessary expenses (section 158) where the bailee receives no remuneration. Indemnify for early return of a gratuitous loan (section 159) , to the extent the borrower's loss exceeds the benefit he derived. Answer for his title (section 164). He is responsible to the bailee for loss the bailee sustains because the bailor was not entitled to make the bailment, to receive back the goods or to give directions.

Example. Ravi lends his scooter to Sita knowing that its brakes are faulty and does not tell her. The brakes fail and Sita is hurt. Ravi is responsible for the damage arising directly from the fault (section 150).

Duties of the bailee: care, and no unauthorised use

Rule. The bailee must take the care of section 151. If he has taken that care, and there is no special contract, he is not responsible for loss, destruction or deterioration (section 152).

If the bailee does an act inconsistent with the conditions, the bailor may avoid the bailment (section 153). If he uses the goods otherwise than as the bailment allows, he must compensate the bailor for damage arising from or during that use (section 154).

Section 153, illustration: A lets a horse to B for hire for his own riding. B drives it in his carriage. At A's option, this terminates the bailment. Section 154, illustration (a): A lends a horse to B for his own riding only. B allows C, a member of his family, to ride it. C rides with care, but the horse accidentally falls and is injured. B must compensate A. Section 154, illustration (b): A hires a horse in Calcutta from B expressly to march to Benares. A marches to Cuttack instead. The horse accidentally falls and is injured. A must compensate B.

Example. Kiran borrows Lata's sewing machine for his own use at home. He lets his neighbour use it, and it is damaged during that use. Kiran must compensate Lata for the damage arising during the use (section 154).

Mixing the goods: consequences for the bailee (sections 155 to 157)

Rule. The effect of mixing depends on consent and on whether the goods can be separated.

With the bailor's consent: both have an interest in the mixture in proportion to their shares (section 155). Without consent, goods can be separated: the property remains with the parties. The bailee bears the expense of separation and any damage from the mixture (section 156). Illustration: A bails 100 marked bales of cotton to B, who mixes them with his own differently marked bales. A may have his 100 bales back and B bears all the expense of separation. Without consent, goods cannot be separated: the bailee must compensate the bailor for the loss of the goods (section 157). Illustration: A bails a barrel of Cape flour worth Rs 45 to B, who mixes it with country flour worth Rs 25 a barrel. B must compensate A.

Duties of the bailee: return, and increase or profit

Rule. The bailee must return the goods, or deliver them as the bailor directs, without demand, as soon as the time has expired or the purpose is accomplished (section 160). If by his default they are not returned at the proper time, he is responsible for loss, destruction or deterioration from that time (section 161). In the absence of a contract to the contrary, he must deliver any increase or profit from the goods (section 163). Illustration: A leaves a cow with B to be taken care of. The cow has a calf. B must deliver the calf as well as the cow.

Example. Dev leaves his goat with Esha for a month. The goat has a kid. At the end of the month Esha must return the goat and the kid (section 163).

Joint owners, a bailor without title, and rival claimants (sections 165 to 167)

Rule. If joint owners bail goods, the bailee may deliver them back to one of them without the consent of all, unless they agreed otherwise (section 165). If the bailor has no title and the bailee in good faith delivers them back to the bailor, the bailee is not responsible to the owner (section 166). A person other than the bailor who claims the goods may apply to the Court to stop delivery to the bailor and to decide the title (section 167).

Rights of the bailee: liens (sections 170 and 171)

Rule. A bailee who has rendered service involving labour or skill on the goods, in accordance with the purpose of the bailment, may retain them until he receives due remuneration, unless the contract says otherwise (section 170). This is the particular lien.

Illustration (a): A delivers a rough diamond to B, a jeweller, to be cut and polished. B may retain the stone until he is paid. Illustration (b): A gives cloth to B, a tailor, to make a coat. B promises to deliver the coat as soon as it is finished and to give three months' credit for the price. B is not entitled to retain the coat until he is paid.

Bankers, factors, wharfingers, attorneys of a High Court and policy-brokers may retain goods bailed to them as security for a general balance of account, in the absence of a contract to the contrary. No other persons may do so unless there is an express contract (section 171). This is the general lien.

Rights against third persons (sections 180 and 181)

Rule. If a third person wrongfully deprives the bailee of the use or possession of the goods, or injures them, the bailee may use the remedies the owner could have used. Either the bailor or the bailee may sue the third person (section 180). What is obtained is dealt with between them according to their respective interests (section 181).

Rights of the bailor

Rule. The bailor may avoid the bailment where the bailee does an act inconsistent with its conditions (section 153). He is entitled to compensation for damage from unauthorised use (section 154), for goods lost by mixing (section 157), and for loss after default in returning (section 161). He may require return of a gratuitous loan at any time (section 159). He is entitled to any increase or profit (section 163).

Can the bailee keep the goods until he is paid? How to read this: start at the top. 1. Did the bailee render a service involving labour or skill on the goods, in accordance with the purpose of the bailment? Yes: particular lien (section 170), unless the contract says otherwise. 2. Is he a banker, factor, wharfinger, attorney of a High Court or policy-broker? Yes: general lien for a general balance of account (section 171), unless the contract says otherwise. 3. Neither? No right to retain for a general balance, unless there is an express contract (section 171).

## Key Points to Remember
- Bailor: disclose known faults (section 150), and for hire he is responsible whether aware or not. Repay necessary expenses where the bailee is unpaid (section 158). Bailee: ordinary prudence (section 151). Not responsible if he took that care and there is no special contract (section 152). Unauthorised use: bailor may avoid (section 153). Bailee compensates for damage from or during such use (section 154). Mixing: with consent, shared in proportion (section 155). Without consent, separable: bailee bears the cost (section 156). Not separable: compensation (section 157). Return without demand (section 160). Increase or profit goes to the bailor (section 163). Particular lien: service involving labour or skill (section 170). General lien: bankers, factors, wharfingers, attorneys of a High Court, policy-brokers (section 171).
