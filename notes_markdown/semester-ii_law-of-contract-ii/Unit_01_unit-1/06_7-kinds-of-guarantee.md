## In One Minute
The Indian Contract Act, 1872 names one kind of guarantee: the continuing guarantee. A guarantee which extends to a series of transactions is a continuing guarantee (section 129). The surety may revoke it by notice to the creditor, as to future transactions (section 130). The death of the surety also revokes it, as to future transactions, unless the contract says otherwise (section 131). Key points for the answer: the definition, the Act's illustrations, and the two ways of revocation with their effect.

## Meaning and Definition
What is a continuing guarantee? (section 129)

Rule. A guarantee which extends to a series of transactions is a continuing guarantee. The test is whether the guarantee extends to a series of transactions.

The Act's illustrations show the test at work.

In illustration (a), the guarantee covers the collection and payment of rents. This is a continuing guarantee. In illustration (b), the guarantee covers any tea supplied from time to time. It is a continuing guarantee, so A is liable up to £100 for the later supply of £200. In illustration (c), the guarantee covers the price of five sacks of flour to be delivered by B to C. It is not a continuing guarantee, so A is not liable for the later four sacks.

Example. Anil guarantees payment to Babu, a rice dealer in Guntur, of up to Rs 50,000 for any rice Babu supplies to Chandu from time to time. The guarantee covers a series of supplies. It is a continuing guarantee.

The other side: a guarantee that is not continuing

Rule. A guarantee which does not extend to a series of transactions is not a continuing guarantee. Illustration (c) to section 129 shows one. The 2017 paper calls such a guarantee a "simple guarantee". The Act does not use that label.

Example. Dev guarantees payment of the price of one consignment of cement that Esha will deliver to Farid. This covers one transaction. It is not a continuing guarantee.

Revocation by notice (section 130)

Rule. A continuing guarantee may at any time be revoked by the surety, as to future transactions, by notice to the creditor. The effect is limited to future transactions. Past transactions remain covered.

The Act's illustrations show this.

In illustration (a), A revokes after B has discounted bills for 2,000 rupees. A is discharged from liability for any later discount. A is still liable for the 2,000 rupees if C defaults. In illustration (b), B has already drawn on C and C has accepted the bill before A gives notice of revocation. C dishonours the bill at maturity. A is liable.

Example. In the rice example, Anil gives notice to Babu after Babu has supplied rice worth Rs 20,000. Rice supplied after the notice is no longer covered. Anil remains liable for the Rs 20,000 if Chandu defaults on it.

Revocation by the surety's death (section 131)

Rule. The death of the surety operates as a revocation of a continuing guarantee, as regards future transactions. This is so in the absence of any contract to the contrary. The guarantee may say otherwise.

Example. Anil dies. Babu then supplies rice to Chandu. The guarantee is revoked as to that future supply, unless the guarantee contract says otherwise.

Revoking a continuing guarantee How to read this: start at the top. Check whether the guarantee is continuing. Then check the way of revocation and its effect. 1. Does the guarantee extend to a series of transactions (section 129)? Yes: it is a continuing guarantee. No: it is not. 2. The surety gives notice to the creditor (section 130), or the surety dies (section 131). 3. Effect: the guarantee is revoked as to future transactions. Transactions already made stay covered (section 130, illustrations).

## Key Points to Remember
- A continuing guarantee extends to a series of transactions (section 129). The surety may revoke it by notice to the creditor, as to future transactions (section 130). The surety's death revokes it as to future transactions, unless the contract says otherwise (section 131). Revocation does not affect transactions already made (illustrations to section 130). The Act names only the continuing guarantee. It does not use the label "simple guarantee".
