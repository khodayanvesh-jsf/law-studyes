## Meaning and Definition
Under section 129 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, a guarantee which extends to a series of transactions is called a:

Explanation. Section 129 calls a guarantee which extends to a series of transactions a continuing guarantee.

A guarantees B, a tea-dealer, payment to the amount of £100 for any tea B may from time to time supply to C. This guarantee is:

Explanation. Illustration (b) to section 129 says this is a continuing guarantee, because it covers tea supplied from time to time.

A guarantees payment of the price of five sacks of flour to be delivered by B to C. B later delivers four more sacks which C does not pay for. A is:

Explanation. Illustration (c) to section 129 says the guarantee was not a continuing guarantee, so A is not liable for the price of the four sacks.
