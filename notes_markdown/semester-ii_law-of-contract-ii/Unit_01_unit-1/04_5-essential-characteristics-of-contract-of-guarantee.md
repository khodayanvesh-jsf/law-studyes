# 5. Essential characteristics of Contract of Guarantee

## In One Minute
The Indian Contract Act, 1872 does not list the essentials of a guarantee in one place. They come from several sections. A guarantee is a contract to answer for a third person's default (section 126). It needs consideration (section 127). It is invalid if the creditor obtained it by misrepresentation or by keeping silent about material circumstances (sections 142 and 143). Key points for the answer: the third person's default, the three persons, consideration under section 127 with its illustrations, and the invalidity rules in sections 142 to 144.

## Meaning and Definition
Characteristic 1: a contract about a third person's default (section 126)

Rule. A guarantee is a contract to perform the promise, or discharge the liability, of a third person in case of his default. The third person is the principal debtor.

Example. Ravi borrows Rs 1 lakh from a bank. His sister Sita promises the bank to repay if Ravi does not. This is a contract about a third person's default.

Characteristic 2: three persons and any form (section 126)

Rule. A guarantee has a surety, a principal debtor and a creditor. It may be oral or written.

Example. In the example above, Sita is the surety, Ravi the principal debtor and the bank the creditor. Sita may give the guarantee in a signed letter or by word of mouth.

Characteristic 3: consideration (section 127)

Rule. Anything done, or any promise made, for the benefit of the principal debtor, may be a sufficient consideration to the surety for giving the guarantee. The section speaks of a benefit to the principal debtor.

The Act gives three illustrations.

Illustration (a): B asks A to sell and deliver goods to him on credit. A agrees, provided C will guarantee payment of the price. C promises in consideration of A's promise to deliver the goods. This is sufficient consideration for C's promise. Illustration (b): A sells and delivers goods to B. C afterwards asks A to forbear to sue B for the debt for a year, and promises to pay if B does not. A agrees to forbear. This is sufficient consideration for C's promise. Illustration (c): A sells and delivers goods to B. C afterwards, without consideration, agrees to pay for them if B defaults. The agreement is void.

Example. Hema delivers goods to Gopi on credit because Ibrahim promises to pay if Gopi does not. Hema's delivery is for the benefit of Gopi, the principal debtor. It is sufficient consideration for Ibrahim's guarantee.

Characteristic 4: no misrepresentation by the creditor (section 142)

Rule. A guarantee is invalid if the creditor obtained it by misrepresentation concerning a material part of the transaction. The misrepresentation must have been made by the creditor, or with his knowledge and assent.

Example. A creditor tells Chandu that the principal debtor's shop is free of debt, knowing it is not. Chandu gives a guarantee on that statement. The guarantee is invalid under section 142.

Characteristic 5: no concealment by the creditor (section 143)

Rule. A guarantee is invalid if the creditor obtained it by keeping silence as to material circumstances.

The Act gives two illustrations.

Illustration (a): A engages B as clerk to collect money. B fails to account for some of his receipts, so A asks him to furnish security. C guarantees B's duly accounting. A does not tell C about B's previous conduct. B makes default. The guarantee is invalid. Illustration (b): A guarantees to C payment for iron to be supplied to B, to the amount of 2,000 tons. B and C have privately agreed that B should pay five rupees per ton beyond the market price, the excess to pay off an old debt. This agreement is concealed from A. A is not liable as a surety.

Characteristic 6: a guarantee on a condition about a co-surety (section 144)

Rule. Where a person gives a guarantee on a contract that the creditor shall not act on it until another person has joined as co-surety, the guarantee is not valid if that other person does not join.

Example. Kiran agrees to guarantee a loan to Lata, on the condition that the bank does not act on it until Mohan also joins as co-surety. Mohan never joins. Kiran's guarantee is not valid.

Checking a guarantee How to read this: start at the top and go down. A "no" at any box may make the guarantee invalid or void. 1. Is it a contract to answer for a third person's default (section 126)? 2. Is there consideration for the benefit of the principal debtor (section 127)? Where goods are already delivered and the promise has no consideration, the agreement is void (illustration (c)). 3. Did the creditor obtain it without misrepresentation (section 142) and without concealment (section 143)? Otherwise it is invalid. 4. If it was given on a co-surety condition, did the co-surety join (section 144)? Otherwise it is not valid.

## Key Points to Remember
- A guarantee is a contract about a third person's default (section 126). Three persons: surety, principal debtor, creditor. It may be oral or written. Consideration: anything done or promised for the principal debtor's benefit is enough (section 127). A guarantee after delivery, with no consideration, is void (illustration (c) to section 127). Misrepresentation by the creditor makes it invalid (section 142). So does keeping silence as to material circumstances (section 143). A co-surety condition that is not met makes it not valid (section 144).
