# 17. Pledge by non-owner
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/pledge-by-non-owner/

## In One Minute
The Act makes a pledge by a person who is not the owner valid in three cases. A mercantile agent in possession of the goods with the owner's consent may pledge them in the ordinary course of business (section 178). A person who got the goods under a voidable contract that has not been rescinded may give a good title to a pawnee (section 178A). A person with only a limited interest in the goods may pledge them to the extent of that interest (section 179). Key points for the answer: the three sections, with the conditions of each, especially good faith and no notice of the defect.

## Meaning and Definition
Case 1: pledge by a mercantile agent (section 178)

Rule. A pledge made by a mercantile agent is as valid as if the owner had expressly authorised it, if these conditions are met.

The mercantile agent is in possession of the goods or the document of title to them, with the owner's consent. He makes the pledge when acting in the ordinary course of business of a mercantile agent. The pawnee acts in good faith. The pawnee has no notice at the time of the pledge that the pawnor has no authority to pledge.

Example. Ravi gives his cloth stock to a mercantile agent, Sita, to sell on his behalf. Sita, acting in the ordinary course of her business, pledges the cloth to a lender, Babu, who acts in good faith and has no notice that Sita lacks authority to pledge. The pledge is as valid as if Ravi had expressly authorised it. If Babu had known Sita had no authority to pledge, the pledge would not be protected.

Case 2: pledge by a person in possession under a voidable contract (section 178A)

Rule. The pawnee acquires a good title if all of these are met.

The pawnor obtained possession of the goods under a contract voidable under section 19 or section 19A. The contract has not been rescinded at the time of the pledge. The pawnee acts in good faith. The pawnee has no notice of the pawnor's defect of title.

Example. Chandu obtains goods from Dev on a contract that Dev may cancel under section 19. Dev has not cancelled it. Chandu pledges the goods to Esha, who acts in good faith and has no notice of any defect in Chandu's title. Esha acquires a good title to the goods as pawnee.

Case 3: pledge by a person with a limited interest (section 179)

Rule. Where a person pledges goods in which he has only a limited interest, the pledge is valid to the extent of that interest.

Example. Farid holds a limited interest in certain goods. He pledges the goods to Gopi. The pledge is valid only to the extent of Farid's limited interest.

When is a pledge by a non-owner valid? How to read this: find the pawnor's position and follow it to the section. 1. The pawnor is a mercantile agent in possession with the owner's consent, acting in the ordinary course of business. Valid as if authorised, if the pawnee acts in good faith without notice (section 178). 2. The pawnor got the goods under a contract voidable under section 19 or 19A that has not been rescinded. The pawnee gets a good title if he acts in good faith without notice of the defect (section 178A). 3. The pawnor has only a limited interest. Valid to the extent of that interest (section 179).

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 178: pledge by a mercantile agent in possession with the owner's consent, in the ordinary course of business, valid if the pawnee acts in good faith without notice. Section 178A: pledge by a person holding under a voidable contract not rescinded, valid if the pawnee acts in good faith without notice of the defect. Section 179: pledge of a limited interest is valid to the extent of that interest. Good faith and no notice are central to sections 178 and 178A.
