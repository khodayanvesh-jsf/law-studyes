# 16. Rights and duties of Pawnor and Pawnee
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/rights-and-duties-of-pawnor-and-pawnee/

## In One Minute
The pawnee may retain the pledged goods for the debt, the interest and his necessary expenses (section 173). He may not retain them for any other debt unless there is a contract to that effect (section 174). If the pawnor defaults, the pawnee may sue on the debt and keep the goods as collateral security , or sell the goods after giving reasonable notice (section 176). The defaulting pawnor may redeem the goods before they are actually sold (section 177). Key points for the answer: the pawnee's right of retainer, his right on default, the duty to give notice and pay over any surplus, and the pawnor's right to redeem.

## Meaning and Definition
Rights of the pawnee

Rule. The pawnee has these rights.

Retainer (section 173). He may retain the goods for payment of the debt or performance of the promise, for the interest of the debt, and for all necessary expenses incurred in respect of the possession or preservation of the goods. Extraordinary expenses (section 175). He is entitled to receive from the pawnor extraordinary expenses incurred for the preservation of the goods. Remedies on default (section 176). He may sue on the debt and retain the goods as collateral security. Or he may sell the goods after reasonable notice.

Example. Anil pledges his gold chain to Babu for a loan of Rs 50,000. Babu may keep the chain until Anil pays the Rs 50,000, the interest, and the necessary expenses Babu incurred in keeping it safe (section 173).

Limits and conditions on the pawnee

Rule. The Act sets these limits.

He may not retain the goods for any debt or promise other than that for which they are pledged, in the absence of a contract to that effect (section 174). Such a contract is presumed for subsequent advances he makes, in the absence of anything to the contrary. He must give the pawnor reasonable notice of a sale (section 176). He must pay over to the pawnor any surplus if the sale proceeds exceed the amount due (section 176).

Example. Babu holds Anil's chain for the Rs 50,000 loan. Anil also owes Babu Rs 10,000 on a separate old debt. Without a contract to that effect, Babu may not retain the chain for the old debt (section 174). If Babu lends Anil a further Rs 20,000 later, a contract to retain the chain for that advance is presumed, unless there is anything to the contrary.

The pawnee's remedies on default (section 176)

Rule. If the pawnor makes default in payment, or in performance of the promise at the stipulated time, the pawnee has two remedies.

He may sue the pawnor on the debt or promise, and retain the goods as collateral security. He may sell the thing pledged, on giving the pawnor reasonable notice of the sale.

After a sale, the accounts work as follows.

Proceeds less than the amount due: the pawnor is still liable to pay the balance. Proceeds more than the amount due: the pawnee must pay the surplus to the pawnor.

Example. Anil defaults on the Rs 50,000 loan. Babu gives Anil reasonable notice and sells the chain for Rs 45,000. The amount due is Rs 50,000. Anil still owes Babu the balance of Rs 5,000. Had the chain fetched Rs 55,000, Babu would have to pay Anil the surplus of Rs 5,000.

Rights and duties of the pawnor

Rule. The pawnor has the right to redeem the goods after default, at any subsequent time before the actual sale, if a time was stipulated for payment (section 177). He must then pay, in addition, any expenses that arose from his default. He must pay extraordinary expenses incurred by the pawnee for the preservation of the goods (section 175). He is liable for any balance after a sale (section 176), and entitled to any surplus.

Example. Anil defaults on the due date. Before Babu sells the chain, Anil pays the Rs 50,000 with the expenses that arose from his default. Anil may redeem the chain (section 177).

## Key Points to Remember
- Retainer: for the debt or promise, the interest and necessary expenses (section 173). No retainer for another debt without a contract. Presumed for subsequent advances (section 174). Extraordinary expenses for preservation: from the pawnor (section 175). On default: sue and retain as collateral security, or sell on reasonable notice. Shortfall owed. Surplus paid over (section 176). The pawnor may redeem before the actual sale, paying the expenses of his default (section 177).
