# 14. Termination of bailment
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/termination-of-bailment/

## In One Minute
The Act has no single section on how a bailment ends. Sections 153, 159, 160, 161 and 162 deal with these events. The bailor may avoid the contract when the bailee acts inconsistently with its conditions (section 153). A gratuitous lender may require the goods back at any time (section 159). A gratuitous bailment ends on the death of the bailor or of the bailee (section 162). On expiry of time, or accomplishment of the purpose, the bailee must return the goods (sections 160 and 161). Key points for the answer: each event with its section and the Act's illustration.

## Meaning and Definition
Event 1: the bailee's act inconsistent with the conditions (section 153)

Rule. The bailment is avoidable at the bailor's option if the bailee does any act with regard to the goods that is inconsistent with the conditions of the bailment. The bailment is not ended automatically. It ends if the bailor chooses.

Example. Ravi hires out a horse to Sita for her own riding. Sita yokes it to her cart. This is inconsistent with the condition. At Ravi's option, the bailment is terminated. This follows the Act's illustration.

Event 2: return of a gratuitous loan on demand (section 159)

Rule. The lender of a thing for use may at any time require its return if the loan was gratuitous, even though he lent it for a specified time or purpose. If early return causes the borrower loss exceeding the benefit he derived, the lender must indemnify him for the excess.

Example. Dev lends his bicycle free of charge to Esha for a month. After a week he asks for it back. He may require its return (section 159).

Event 3: death in a gratuitous bailment (section 162)

Rule. A gratuitous bailment is terminated by the death either of the bailor or of the bailee. Section 162 speaks only of a gratuitous bailment.

Example. Farid lends his cart to Gopi free of charge for a fortnight. Farid dies in the first week. The bailment is terminated (section 162).

Event 4: expiry of time or accomplishment of purpose (sections 160 and 161)

Rule. When the time for which the goods were bailed has expired, or the purpose has been accomplished, the bailee must return them, or deliver them as the bailor directs, without demand (section 160). If, by his default, he does not return, deliver or tender them at the proper time, he is responsible for any loss, destruction or deterioration from that time (section 161).

Example. Hema gives her scooter to a garage for repair. When the repair is done, the garage must return it without waiting for Hema to ask. If the garage fails to return it at the proper time by its own default, it is responsible for any loss or damage to the scooter from that time.

How a bailment ends How to read this: find the event and follow it to the section. 1. The bailee does an act inconsistent with the conditions. Bailor may avoid the contract (section 153). 2. The loan is gratuitous and the lender asks for the thing back. He may require return at any time (section 159). 3. The bailor or the bailee of a gratuitous bailment dies. Bailment is terminated (section 162). 4. The time expires, or the purpose is accomplished. Bailee returns the goods without demand (section 160). Default makes him responsible (section 161).

## Key Points to Remember
- Bailee's inconsistent act: bailment avoidable at the bailor's option (section 153). Gratuitous loan: the lender may require return at any time (section 159). Gratuitous bailment: terminated by the death of the bailor or the bailee (section 162). Expiry or accomplishment: return without demand (section 160). Default: responsible for loss from that time (section 161).
