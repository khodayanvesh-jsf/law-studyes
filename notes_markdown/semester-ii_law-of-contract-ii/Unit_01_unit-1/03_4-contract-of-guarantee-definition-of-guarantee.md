# 4. Contract of Guarantee-Definition of Guarantee
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/contract-of-guarantee/

## In One Minute
A contract of guarantee is a contract to perform the promise, or discharge the liability, of a third person if he defaults. There are three persons: the surety , the principal debtor and the creditor . Section 126 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 defines all four terms. It also says a guarantee may be oral or written. Key points for the answer: the definition word for word, the three persons, and the oral or written rule.

## Meaning and Definition
The meaning of the definition (section 126)

Rule. A contract of guarantee is a contract to perform the promise, or discharge the liability, of a third person in case of his default. Take the definition in parts.

It is a contract. The contract is to perform the promise, or discharge the liability, of a third person. This is to happen in case of that third person's default.

Example. Ravi borrows Rs 1 lakh from a bank in Guntur. His sister Sita tells the bank: "If Ravi does not repay, I will repay." Sita's contract with the bank is a contract to discharge the liability of a third person, Ravi, in case of his default. It is a contract of guarantee.

The three persons: surety, principal debtor and creditor

Rule. Section 126 names the three persons by their roles.

The surety is the person who gives the guarantee. The principal debtor is the person in respect of whose default the guarantee is given. The creditor is the person to whom the guarantee is given.

Example. In the example above, Sita is the surety. Ravi is the principal debtor. The bank is the creditor.

Form: oral or written

Rule. Section 126 says a guarantee may be either oral or written. The Act does not require writing.

Example. Sita could give the bank a signed letter of guarantee. She could also give the guarantee by word of mouth. Section 126 allows both.

The three persons in a guarantee How to read this: start at the top. The principal debtor owes the creditor. The surety promises the creditor to answer for the principal debtor's default. 1. The principal debtor owes a promise or liability to the creditor. 2. The surety gives the creditor a guarantee. The guarantee is to perform the promise, or discharge the liability, of the principal debtor in case of his default. 3. The principal debtor defaults. The surety's guarantee comes into play (see "Rights and liabilities of Surety").

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 126 defines a contract of guarantee. It is a contract to perform the promise, or discharge the liability, of a third person in case of his default. The surety gives the guarantee. The principal debtor is the person whose default is guaranteed. The creditor is the person to whom the guarantee is given. A guarantee may be oral or written.
