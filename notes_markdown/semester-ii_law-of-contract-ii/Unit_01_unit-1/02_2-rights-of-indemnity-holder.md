# 2. Rights of Indemnity holder
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/rights-of-indemnity-holder/

## In One Minute
In a contract of indemnity, the person who is protected is the promisee. He is also called the indemnity-holder. Section 125 gives him three rights against the promisor when he is sued. He can recover the damages he is compelled to pay, the costs he is compelled to pay, and the sums he paid under a compromise. The first right has no extra condition. The second and third rights depend on conditions. Key points for the answer: the opening words "acting within the scope of his authority", the three clauses, and the conditions on costs and compromise.

## Meaning and Definition
The opening words: "acting within the scope of his authority"

Rule. Section 125 gives the three rights to a promisee "acting within the scope of his authority". The rights are therefore for a promisee who acts within his authority.

The section does not define "scope of his authority" in other words.

Right 1: damages he is compelled to pay (clause 1)

Rule. The promisee can recover all damages which he may be compelled to pay in any suit. The damages must be in respect of a matter to which the promise to indemnify applies. Clause (1) has no condition about the promisor's orders or about prudence.

Example. Ravi promises Meena: "If anyone sues you over the stock you bought through my broker, I will indemnify you." A supplier sues Meena over that stock. The court orders Meena to pay Rs 50,000 as damages. The suit is about the matter the promise covers. Meena can recover the Rs 50,000 from Ravi under clause (1).

Right 2: costs he is compelled to pay (clause 2)

Rule. The promisee can recover all costs which he may be compelled to pay in the suit. Clause (2) lets him recover costs in two cases.

He did not contravene the orders of the promisor in bringing or defending the suit, and he acted as it would have been prudent for him to act in the absence of any contract of indemnity. The promisor authorized him to bring or defend the suit.

Example. The supplier sues Meena. Ravi had authorized her to defend the suit. She defends it, loses, and is ordered to pay Rs 5,000 as costs. She can recover the Rs 5,000 because the promisor authorized her to defend. Suppose instead that Ravi had told her not to defend, and she defended anyway. She would have contravened his orders, so the first case in clause (2) would not help her.

Right 3: sums paid under a compromise (clause 3)

Rule. The promisee can recover all sums which he may have paid under the terms of any compromise of the suit. Clause (3) lets him recover in two cases.

The compromise was not contrary to the orders of the promisor, and it was one which it would have been prudent for the promisee to make in the absence of any contract of indemnity. The promisor authorized him to compromise the suit.

Example. The supplier's claim against Meena is Rs 50,000. Ravi has not given any orders. Meena settles for Rs 30,000, a figure a careful person would have accepted even without an indemnity. The compromise was not contrary to Ravi's orders. She can recover the Rs 30,000 under clause (3).

What can the promisee recover under section 125? How to read this: start at the top. The promisee has been sued on a matter the promise covers. Then check each kind of sum. 1. The promisee, acting within the scope of his authority, is sued on a matter the promise to indemnify applies to. 2. Damages he is compelled to pay (clause 1). Recoverable. No extra condition in the clause. 3. Costs he is compelled to pay (clause 2). Recoverable if he did not contravene the promisor's orders and acted prudently, or if the promisor authorized the suit. 4. Sums paid under a compromise (clause 3). Recoverable if the compromise was not contrary to the promisor's orders and was prudent, or if the promisor authorized it.

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 125 gives rights to the promisee "acting within the scope of his authority". Clause (1): all damages he is compelled to pay in the suit, on a matter the promise covers. Clause (2): costs, if he did not contravene the promisor's orders and acted prudently, or if the promisor authorized the suit. Clause (3): compromise sums, if the compromise was not contrary to the promisor's orders and was prudent, or if the promisor authorized it. The test of prudence is what he would do "in the absence of any contract of indemnity". The definition of a contract of indemnity is in section 124.
