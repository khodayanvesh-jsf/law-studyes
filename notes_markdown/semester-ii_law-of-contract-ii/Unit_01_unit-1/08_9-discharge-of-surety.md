# 9. Discharge of surety
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/discharge-of-surety/

## In One Minute
A surety is discharged when the Act says so. The Act gives several ways. A continuing guarantee may be revoked (sections 130 and 131). A change in the terms of the contract between the creditor and the principal debtor, made without the surety's consent, discharges him as to later transactions (section 133). The surety is also discharged in these cases. The principal debtor is released (section 134). The creditor makes a composition with the debtor, gives him time or promises not to sue (section 135). The creditor's act or omission impairs the surety's remedy (section 139). A security is lost (section 141). Key points for the answer: each section, its words and the Act's illustration.

## Meaning and Definition
Discharge by revocation of a continuing guarantee (sections 130 and 131)

Rule. The surety may revoke a continuing guarantee by notice to the creditor (section 130). His death also revokes it, unless the contract says otherwise (section 131). Both operate as to future transactions only. The full treatment is in the topic "Kinds of Guarantee".

Example. Anil guarantees all rice supplied to Chandu by Babu for a year. After four months Anil gives notice to Babu. Supplies after the notice are no longer covered. Supplies before the notice remain covered.

Discharge by variance in the terms of the contract (section 133)

Rule. Any variance, made without the surety's consent, in the terms of the contract between the principal debtor and the creditor, discharges the surety as to transactions subsequent to the variance.

The Act gives five illustrations. Three of them show the point clearly.

(a) A is surety for B's conduct as a manager in C's bank. B and C agree, without A's consent, to raise B's salary and to make him liable for one-fourth of overdraft losses. B allows an overdraft and the bank loses money. A is discharged and is not liable. (c) A is surety for B, a clerk paid a fixed salary. C and B agree, without A's knowledge or consent, to pay B a commission instead. A is not liable for B's later misconduct. (e) C contracts to lend B 5,000 rupees on 1 March and A guarantees repayment. C pays the money on 1 January. A is discharged, because C might sue B for the money before 1 March.

Illustration (b) covers a later Act that materially alters the office. Illustration (d) covers a continuing guarantee of 3,000 rupees for oil supplied on credit, which the debtor and creditor change, without A's knowledge, into supply for ready money. A is not liable for goods supplied after the new arrangement.

Example. Hema sells goods to Gopi on three months' credit, with Ibrahim as surety. Without telling Ibrahim, Hema and Gopi change the credit period to one month. Ibrahim is discharged as to later transactions under section 133.

Discharge by release of the principal debtor (section 134)

Rule. The surety is discharged by any contract between the creditor and the principal debtor by which the principal debtor is released. He is also discharged by any act or omission of the creditor, the legal consequence of which is the discharge of the principal debtor.

(a) B becomes embarrassed and contracts with his creditors, including C, to assign them his property in return for releasing him from their demands. B is released from his debt to C, and A, his surety, is discharged. (b) A contracts with B to grow indigo and C guarantees A's performance. B diverts the water that A needs and prevents the crop. C is no longer liable. (c) A contracts to build a house for B, who is to supply the timber. C guarantees A's performance. B omits to supply the timber. C is discharged.

Example. A co-operative society lends Rs 1 lakh to Ravi on Sita's guarantee. The society and Ravi agree that the society releases Ravi from the loan. Sita is discharged under section 134.

Discharge by composition, time or promise not to sue (section 135)

Rule. A contract between the creditor and the principal debtor, by which the creditor makes a composition with, or promises to give time to, or not to sue, the principal debtor, discharges the surety, unless the surety assents to such contract.

Example. The bank agrees with Ravi, without asking Sita, to give him one more year to repay. Sita is discharged unless she assents to the agreement.

Not discharged: time given to the debtor by a contract with a third person (section 136)

Rule. Where a contract to give time to the principal debtor is made by the creditor with a third person, and not with the principal debtor, the surety is not discharged.

The Act's illustration: C, the holder of an overdue bill of exchange drawn by A as surety for B and accepted by B, contracts with M to give time to B. A is not discharged.

Example. The bank promises Ravi's neighbour Kiran, not Ravi, that it will give Ravi time to repay. The promise is made with a third person, not with the principal debtor. Sita is not discharged.

Not discharged: forbearance and release of a co-surety (sections 137 and 138)

Rule. Mere forbearance by the creditor to sue the principal debtor, or to enforce any other remedy against him, does not discharge the surety, in the absence of any provision in the guarantee to the contrary (section 137). The Act's illustration: B owes C a debt guaranteed by A. The debt becomes payable. C does not sue B for a year. A is not discharged.

Where there are co-sureties, a release by the creditor of one does not discharge the others. It also does not free the released surety from his responsibility to the other sureties (section 138).

Discharge by the creditor's act or omission impairing the surety's eventual remedy (section 139)

Rule. If the creditor does any act which is inconsistent with the rights of the surety, or omits to do any act which his duty to the surety requires him to do, and the eventual remedy of the surety against the principal debtor is thereby impaired, the surety is discharged.

(a) B contracts to build a ship for C, payable by instalments as the work reaches certain stages. A is surety. C, without A's knowledge, prepays the last two instalments to B. A is discharged by the prepayment. (b) C lends money to B on a promissory note made by B and by A as surety, with a bill of sale of B's furniture. C sells the furniture, but through his misconduct and wilful negligence only a small price is realized. A is discharged from liability on the note. (c) A puts M as apprentice to B and guarantees M's fidelity. B promises to see M make up the cash at least once a month. B omits to do so and M embezzles. A is not liable to B on his guarantee.

Example. A builder is to be paid by instalments as work proceeds. The owner, without telling the builder's surety, pays the last two instalments in advance. The surety's remedy against the builder is impaired. The surety is discharged, as in illustration (a) to section 139.

Discharge by loss of securities (section 141)

Rule. The surety is entitled to the benefit of every security the creditor holds against the principal debtor when the guarantee is given. If the creditor loses it or parts with it without the surety's consent, the surety is discharged to the extent of its value.

(a) C lends B 2,000 rupees on A's guarantee and also holds a mortgage of B's furniture. C cancels the mortgage. A is discharged to the value of the furniture. (b) C holds a decree against B and a guarantee from A. C takes B's goods in execution and then, without A's knowledge, withdraws the execution. A is discharged. (c) A is surety for B, and C afterwards obtains from B a further security and later gives it up. A is not discharged.

Is the surety discharged? How to read this: find what happened and follow the line to the section and the result. 1. The surety revokes a continuing guarantee by notice, or the surety dies. Discharged as to future transactions (sections 130, 131). 2. The creditor and the principal debtor vary their contract without the surety's consent. Discharged as to later transactions (section 133). 3. The creditor releases the principal debtor by contract, or an act or omission of the creditor discharges him. Discharged (section 134). 4. The creditor compounds with, gives time to, or agrees not to sue the principal debtor, and the surety does not assent. Discharged (section 135). 5. The creditor's act or omission impairs the surety's eventual remedy. Discharged (section 139). 6. The creditor loses or parts with a security held when the guarantee was given. Discharged to the extent of its value (section 141).

## Key Points to Remember
- Revocation of a continuing guarantee, by notice or by the surety's death, operates as to future transactions (sections 130, 131). A variance without the surety's consent discharges him as to later transactions (section 133). Release of the principal debtor discharges the surety (section 134). Composition, time or promise not to sue the principal debtor discharges the surety, unless he assents (section 135). Time given by a contract with a third person does not discharge the surety (section 136). Mere forbearance does not (section 137). Release of one co-surety does not discharge the others (section 138). Impairing the surety's eventual remedy discharges him (section 139). So does loss of a security, to the extent of its value (section 141).
