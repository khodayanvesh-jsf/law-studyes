# 6. Distinction between Indemnity and Guarantee
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/distinction-between-indemnity-and-guarantee/

## In One Minute
In a contract of indemnity, one party promises to save the other from loss (section 124). In a contract of guarantee, a surety promises the creditor to answer for the principal debtor's default (section 126). The two are different contracts with different rules. The differences that follow from the Act are in the persons, in what is promised, in what sets the promise working, and in the rights after payment. Key points for the answer: a table of differences based on sections 124 and 126, with the sections on rights (125, 140 and 145).

## Meaning and Definition
Difference 1: the definitions (sections 124 and 126)

Rule. Section 124 speaks of a promise to save the other party from loss. Section 126 speaks of a contract to perform the promise, or discharge the liability, of a third person in case of his default.

Example. Dev promises Esha: "If Farid takes proceedings against you over Rs 200, I will save you from the loss." That promise is a promise to save from loss. Now Sita promises a bank: "If my brother Ravi does not repay his loan, I will." That promise is to discharge the liability of a third person in case of his default.

Difference 2: the persons

Rule. Section 124 speaks of "one party" promising "the other". These are the promisor and the promisee. Section 126 names three persons: the surety, the principal debtor and the creditor.

Example. In Dev's promise to Esha there are two parties, Dev and Esha. In Sita's promise to the bank there are three persons: Sita (surety), Ravi (principal debtor) and the bank (creditor).

Difference 3: what sets the promise working

Rule. In indemnity the loss is caused by the conduct of the promisor himself or of any other person (section 124). In guarantee the promise operates "in case of his default", meaning the default of the third person (section 126).

Difference 4: rights after payment (sections 125, 140 and 145)

Rule. The indemnity-holder's rights against the promisor are in section 125: damages, costs and compromise sums, on the conditions stated. The surety's rights are in sections 140 and 145. Under section 140 he takes the creditor's rights against the principal debtor once he has paid all he is liable for. Under section 145 the principal debtor impliedly promises to indemnify him.

Example. Esha is sued by Farid and is ordered to pay Rs 200 as damages. She recovers it from Dev under section 125, if the conditions of that section are met. If Sita pays the bank Ravi's loan, she takes the bank's rights against Ravi (section 140) and can recover what she rightfully paid from Ravi (section 145).

Difference 5: form

Rule. Section 126 says a guarantee may be oral or written. Section 124 does not say anything about the form of a contract of indemnity.

Which contract is it? How to read this: start at the top and follow the answer for the promise you are testing. 1. Does one party promise to save the other from loss caused by the promisor's or any other person's conduct? Yes: contract of indemnity (section 124). 2. Does a person promise a creditor to perform the promise, or discharge the liability, of a third person if that third person defaults? Yes: contract of guarantee (section 126).

## Key Points to Remember
- Indemnity: section 124. Guarantee: section 126. Indemnity has a promisor and a promisee. Guarantee has a surety, a principal debtor and a creditor. Indemnity: a promise to save from loss. Guarantee: a contract to discharge a third person's liability in case of his default. The indemnity-holder's rights are in section 125. The surety's rights are in sections 140 and 145. A guarantee may be oral or written. Section 124 is silent on form.
