## Meaning and Definition
Under section 127 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, anything done or any promise made for the benefit of whom may be sufficient consideration to the surety?

Explanation. Section 127 says anything done, or any promise made, for the benefit of the principal debtor may be a sufficient consideration to the surety for giving the guarantee.

A sells and delivers goods to B. C afterwards, without consideration, agrees to pay for them if B defaults. Under illustration (c) to section 127, the agreement is:

Explanation. Illustration (c) to section 127 says the agreement is void because C gave no consideration.

A sells and delivers goods to B. C afterwards asks A to forbear to sue B for a year and promises to pay if B does not. A agrees. This is:

Explanation. Illustration (b) to section 127 says A's agreement to forbear is sufficient consideration for C's promise.
