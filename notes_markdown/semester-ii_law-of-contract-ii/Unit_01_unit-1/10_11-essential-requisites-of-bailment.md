# 11. Essential requisites of bailment
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/essential-requisites-of-bailment/

## In One Minute
The Act does not list the essentials of bailment in a separate section. They come from the words of section 148 and section 149. Every bailment needs a delivery of goods, between two persons, for some purpose, upon a contract, with a duty to return the goods or to dispose of them as the person delivering directs. Key points for the answer: the elements in the order of the definition, with section 149 on delivery and the explanation to section 148 for goods already in the bailee's possession.

## Meaning and Definition
Requisite 1: delivery of goods

Rule. Bailment is the "delivery of goods". Section 149 says delivery may be made by doing anything which puts the goods in the possession of the intended bailee, or of any person authorized to hold them on his behalf. The explanation to section 148 adds the case where the bailee is already in possession of the goods.

Example. Anil's goods are placed in the possession of a clerk whom Babu has authorized to hold goods for him. Delivery to Babu is made. In a separate case, Chandu only promises Dev that he will look after Dev's goods one day, but the goods never reach Chandu or anyone authorized for him. There is no delivery.

Requisite 2: one person delivers to another

Rule. The goods are delivered "by one person to another". The deliverer is the bailor. The receiver is the bailee.

Example. Esha leaves her bicycle with Farid. Esha is the bailor. Farid is the bailee.

Requisite 3: for some purpose

Rule. The goods are delivered "for some purpose". Section 148 says "some purpose" and lists none.

Example. Gopi gives cloth to a tailor to be made into a coat. The purpose is stitching. Hema leaves her car with a friend while she travels. The purpose is safe keeping.

Requisite 4: upon a contract

Rule. The delivery is "upon a contract". Section 148 itself speaks of a contract that the goods will be returned or otherwise disposed of.

Example. Ibrahim delivers goods to Kiran on the agreed terms that Kiran will return them after a month. The delivery is upon a contract.

Requisite 5: return or disposal as directed

Rule. The contract must provide that the goods shall, when the purpose is accomplished, be returned or otherwise disposed of according to the directions of the person delivering them.

Example. Lata gives wheat to a mill to be ground. The mill must return the flour, or deliver it to a person Lata names. This meets the requisite of return or disposal as directed.

The requisites How to read this: all of them must be present for a bailment under section 148. 1. Delivery of goods (section 149). 2. By one person to another. 3. For some purpose. 4. Upon a contract. 5. That the goods will be returned or disposed of as the person delivering directs, when the purpose is accomplished. All present: bailment.

## Key Points to Remember
- Elements in section 148: delivery of goods, by one person to another, for some purpose, upon a contract, with return or disposal as the person delivering directs. Delivery: anything putting the goods in the possession of the bailee or of a person authorized for him (section 149). A person already holding another's goods may become a bailee by contract (explanation to section 148). If one requisite is missing, the transaction does not fit the definition.
