# 12. Kinds of bailment
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/kinds-of-bailment/

## In One Minute
The Act has no section that lists the kinds of bailment. Its words refer to a bailment for hire (section 150) and a gratuitous bailment (sections 158, 159 and 162). A bailment of goods as security for a debt or a promise is a pledge (section 172). Key points for the answer: the two kinds, the rules the Act gives for each, and the link to pledge.

## Meaning and Definition
Kind 1: bailment for hire

Rule. Section 150 speaks of goods "bailed for hire". There the bailor is responsible for damage arising directly from faults in the goods whether he was or was not aware of them.

Example. Anil hires a carriage from Babu. The carriage is unsafe, though Babu is not aware of it, and Anil is injured. Babu is responsible to Anil for the injury. This is the Act's own illustration (b) to section 150.

Kind 2: gratuitous bailment

Rule. The Act uses the word "gratuitous" in sections 159 and 162. Section 158 speaks of a bailee who is to receive no remuneration. The Act gives these rules.

The bailor must repay the bailee the necessary expenses incurred for the purpose of the bailment, where the goods are to be kept, carried or have work done on them and the bailee gets no remuneration (section 158). The lender may at any time require the return of the thing, even though he lent it for a specified time or purpose. If the borrower acted on the faith of the loan so that early return would cause him loss exceeding the benefit he derived, the lender who compels return must indemnify him for the excess (section 159). The bailment is terminated by the death of the bailor or of the bailee (section 162). The first part of section 150, the bailor's duty to disclose faults of which he is aware, applies to every bailment. The second part adds the rule for goods bailed for hire.

Example. Chandu lends his bicycle to Dev for a month without charge. After a week Chandu asks for it back. Under section 159 he may require its return at any time. If Dev had, on the faith of the month-long loan, made arrangements so that early return would cost him more than the benefit he got, Chandu would have to indemnify him for the excess. If Chandu dies during the month, the gratuitous bailment ends (section 162).

Pledge: bailment as security (section 172)

Rule. A bailment of goods as security for payment of a debt or performance of a promise is called a pledge. The Act gives pledge its own sections (172 to 179).

Example. Esha borrows Rs 50,000 and hands her gold chain to the lender as security until she repays. This is a bailment of goods as security for payment of a debt. It is a pledge.

Which kind of bailment? How to read this: start at the top and follow the answer. 1. Are the goods bailed as security for a debt or a promise? Yes: pledge (section 172). 2. If not, are the goods bailed for hire? Yes: bailment for hire (section 150). 3. Otherwise, where the Act's words speak of a gratuitous loan or a bailee who is to receive no remuneration: Gratuitous bailment (sections 158, 159, 162).

## Key Points to Remember
- The Act does not list kinds. Its words show bailment for hire and gratuitous bailment, and pledge is bailment as security. For hire: the bailor is responsible for damage from faults whether aware or not (section 150). Gratuitous: the bailor repays necessary expenses (section 158). The lender may require return at any time (section 159). Death of either party ends it (section 162). Pledge: bailment as security for a debt or promise (section 172).
