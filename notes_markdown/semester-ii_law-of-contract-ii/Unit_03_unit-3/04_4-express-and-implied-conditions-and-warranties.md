# 4. Express and implied conditions and warranties
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/express-and-implied-conditions-and-warranties/

## In One Minute
Some terms of a contract of sale are written by the parties (express). Others are implied by the Sale of Goods Act unless the contract shows a different intention. The Act implies a condition that the seller has a right to sell, and warranties of quiet possession and freedom from encumbrance (section 14). It also implies a condition that goods sold by description correspond with it (section 15). It implies conditions of fitness and merchantable quality in the cases set out in section 16, and conditions in a sale by sample (section 17). Section 62 lets the parties negative or vary implied terms by express agreement, course of dealing or usage. Key points for the answer: each section and whether it implies a condition or a warranty.

## Meaning and Definition
Express and implied terms

Rule. An express term is one the parties themselves state in the contract. An implied term is one the law reads in. Section 62 speaks of a right, duty or liability arising "by implication of law". Section 16(4) says an express warranty or condition does not negative an implied one unless it is inconsistent with it.

Example. A contract says "warranty: six months service". That is an express term. The law adds an implied condition that the seller has a right to sell (section 14(a)) even though the contract does not mention it.

Implied undertaking as to title (section 14)

Rule. Unless a different intention appears, three terms are implied.

Condition: in a sale, the seller has a right to sell the goods. In an agreement to sell, he will have a right to sell when the property is to pass (section 14(a)). Warranty: the buyer shall have and enjoy quiet possession of the goods (section 14(b)). Warranty: the goods shall be free from any charge or encumbrance in favour of a third party not declared or known to the buyer before or at the time of the contract (section 14(c)).

Example. Dev buys a motorcycle from Eshan. Later a bank proves that it holds an undisclosed charge on the motorcycle, which Dev did not know about. The implied warranty in section 14(c) is broken.

Sale by description (section 15)

Rule. In a sale of goods by description there is an implied condition that the goods shall correspond with the description. In a sale by sample as well as by description, it is not enough that the bulk corresponds with the sample. The goods must also correspond with the description.

Example. Farid orders "Grade A basmati rice" by description. The goods delivered are another grade. The implied condition in section 15 is broken.

Quality or fitness (section 16)

Rule. There is no implied warranty or condition as to quality or fitness for any particular purpose, except as follows.

Fitness for purpose (section 16(1)). The buyer makes known the particular purpose, expressly or by implication, so as to show he relies on the seller's skill or judgment, and the goods are of a description the seller supplies in the course of his business. There is then an implied condition that the goods are reasonably fit for that purpose. Exception: where a specified article is sold under its patent or other trade name, there is no implied condition as to fitness. Merchantable quality (section 16(2)). Goods are bought by description from a seller who deals in goods of that description. There is an implied condition that they are of merchantable quality. Exception: if the buyer has examined the goods, there is no implied condition as regards defects which the examination ought to have revealed. Usage of trade (section 16(3)). An implied warranty or condition as to quality or fitness for a particular purpose may be annexed by the usage of trade. Express terms (section 16(4)). An express warranty or condition does not negative a warranty or condition implied by the Act unless inconsistent with it.

Example. Geeta tells a chemist she needs a cream for an infant's skin rash and relies on the chemist's advice. The chemist supplies a cream of a kind he sells in his business. There is an implied condition that it is reasonably fit for that purpose (section 16(1)).

Sale by sample (section 17)

Rule. A contract is by sample where it has a term, express or implied, to that effect. There is then an implied condition (a) that the bulk corresponds with the sample in quality, (b) that the buyer has a reasonable opportunity of comparing the bulk with the sample, and (c) that the goods are free from any defect, rendering them unmerchantable, which would not be apparent on reasonable examination of the sample.

Excluding implied terms (section 62)

Rule. A right, duty or liability arising by implication of law may be negatived or varied by express agreement, by the course of dealing between the parties, or by usage if the usage binds both parties.

Which implied term applies? How to read this: each step names the section and whether it creates a condition or a warranty. 1. Title and possession (section 14) Condition: right to sell. Warranties: quiet possession, freedom from encumbrance. 2. Description (section 15) Condition: goods correspond with the description. 3. Quality or fitness (section 16) Conditions in sub-sections (1) and (2), and by trade usage in (3). Otherwise none. 4. Sample (section 17) Three conditions: bulk like the sample, chance to compare, no hidden defect. 5. Can the parties change these (section 62)? Yes, by express agreement, course of dealing or usage binding both.

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 14: condition of right to sell. Warranties of quiet possession and freedom from encumbrance. Section 15: sale by description. Goods must correspond with the description. Section 16(1): fitness for a known purpose when the buyer relies on the seller's skill. Not for patent or trade name articles. Section 16(2): merchantable quality in a purchase by description from a dealer. Not for defects the buyer's examination ought to have revealed. Section 16(3): trade usage may annex terms. Section 16(4): express terms do not displace implied ones unless inconsistent. Section 17: sale by sample has three conditions. Section 62: implied terms may be negatived or varied.
