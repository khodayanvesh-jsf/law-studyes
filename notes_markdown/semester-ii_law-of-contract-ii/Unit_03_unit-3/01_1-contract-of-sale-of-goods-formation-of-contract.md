# 1. Contract of Sale of Goods - Formation of contract
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/contract-of-sale-of-goods/

## In One Minute
A contract of sale of goods is a contract in which the seller transfers or agrees to transfer the property in goods to the buyer for a price (section 4(1)). It is made by an offer to buy or sell goods for a price and its acceptance (section 5(1)). If the property passes at once, the contract is a sale. If it passes at a future time or after a condition is met, it is an agreement to sell (section 4(3)). Key points for the answer: the definition, the two kinds, how the contract is made, and the rules of an auction sale (section 64).

## Meaning and Definition
Contract of sale: the definition (section 4(1))

Rule. A contract of sale of goods is a contract whereby the seller transfers or agrees to transfer the property in goods to the buyer for a price.

Four things follow from the words of the section.

There are two parties, a seller and a buyer. The subject-matter is goods. Goods are moveable property other than actionable claims and money (section 2(7)). There is a transfer, or an agreement to transfer, of the property in the goods. "Property" means the general property in goods and not merely a special property (section 2(11)). The consideration is a price. "Price" means the money consideration for a sale of goods (section 2(10)).

There may be a contract of sale between one part-owner and another (section 4(1)). A contract of sale may be absolute or conditional (section 4(2)).

Example. Ravi agrees to sell his scooter to Sita for Rs 40,000. Ravi is the seller, Sita is the buyer, the scooter is the goods, and Rs 40,000 is the price. This is a contract of sale.

Sale and agreement to sell (section 4(3) and (4))

Rule. If the property in the goods is transferred from the seller to the buyer under the contract, the contract is called a sale. If the transfer is to take place at a future time, or subject to some condition to be fulfilled later, it is called an agreement to sell. An agreement to sell becomes a sale when the time elapses or the conditions are fulfilled.

Example. Anil sells a specific table to Babu and the property in it passes to Babu now. This is a sale. If Anil instead agrees that the property will pass to Babu on 1 January, this is an agreement to sell. On 1 January it becomes a sale.

How a contract of sale is made (section 5)

Rule. A contract of sale is made by an offer to buy or sell goods for a price and the acceptance of the offer.

The contract may provide for immediate delivery, immediate payment, or both. It may provide for delivery or payment by instalments. It may provide that delivery or payment, or both, are postponed. Subject to any law in force, it may be in writing, by word of mouth, partly in writing and partly by word of mouth, or implied from the conduct of the parties (section 5(2)).

Example. A customer picks up a packet of rice from a shop shelf and pays at the counter. The contract is implied from the conduct of the parties. No writing is needed unless some other law requires it.

Application of the Indian Contract Act (section 3)

Rule. The unrepealed provisions of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 continue to apply to contracts for the sale of goods, except where they are inconsistent with the express provisions of the Sale of Goods Act. Section 2(15) also gives expressions defined in the Contract Act but not here the meaning assigned in the Contract Act.

Auction sale (section 64)

Rule. Section 64 sets six rules for a sale by auction.

Goods put up in lots: each lot is prima facie a separate contract of sale. The sale is complete when the auctioneer announces it by the fall of the hammer or in another customary manner. Until then any bidder may retract his bid. The seller may reserve a right to bid, expressly. Only where it is so reserved may the seller, or one person on his behalf, bid. If the sale is not notified to be subject to a right to bid on behalf of the seller, the seller may not bid or employ anyone to bid, and the auctioneer may not knowingly take such a bid. The buyer may treat a sale in breach of this rule as fraudulent. The sale may be notified to be subject to a reserved or upset price. If the seller uses pretended bidding to raise the price, the sale is voidable at the option of the buyer.

Example. At an auction, a bidder says Rs 5,000 for a lamp. Before the auctioneer brings down the hammer, the bidder changes his mind. He may retract his bid, because the sale is not yet complete (section 64(2)).

Sale or agreement to sell? How to read this: start at the top. The property means the general property in the goods. 1. Is there a contract by which the seller transfers or agrees to transfer the property in goods to the buyer for a price? If yes, it is a contract of sale (section 4(1)). If not, it is not a contract of sale under this Act. 2. Is the property transferred from the seller to the buyer under the contract now? If yes, it is a sale (section 4(3)). 3. Is the transfer to take place at a future time, or after a condition is fulfilled? Then it is an agreement to sell. It becomes a sale when the time elapses or the condition is fulfilled (section 4(3) and (4)).

## Key Points to Remember
- A contract of sale: seller transfers or agrees to transfer the property in goods to the buyer for a price (section 4(1)). Sale: property transferred now. Agreement to sell: property to pass later or on a condition (section 4(3)). An agreement to sell becomes a sale when the time elapses or the conditions are fulfilled (section 4(4)). Made by offer and acceptance. It may be in writing, by words, or implied from conduct (section 5). The Contract Act applies unless inconsistent (section 3). Auction: the sale is complete at the fall of the hammer, and a bidder may retract before it (section 64(2)). Seller bidding without a reserved right: the buyer may treat the sale as fraudulent (section 64(4)).
