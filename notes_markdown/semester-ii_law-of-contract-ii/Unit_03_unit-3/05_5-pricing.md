# 5. Pricing
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/pricing/

## In One Minute
The price in a contract of sale may be fixed by the contract, left to be fixed in the manner agreed, or determined by the course of dealing between the parties (section 9(1)). If it is not determined in any of these ways, the buyer must pay a reasonable price , which is a question of fact (section 9(2)). If the price is to be fixed by a third party's valuation and he does not make it, the agreement is avoided, except that the buyer who has received and appropriated goods pays a reasonable price (section 10). A change in customs, excise or sales tax after the contract can change the price, unless the contract shows a different intention (section 64A). Key points for the answer: the three ways in section 9(1), the reasonable-price fall-back in section 9(2), and the effect of failure of valuation.

## Meaning and Definition
How the price is ascertained (section 9)

Rule. The price may be (1) fixed by the contract, (2) left to be fixed in the manner agreed, or (3) determined by the course of dealing between the parties. If none of these applies, the buyer pays a reasonable price. What is reasonable is a question of fact in each case.

Example. Rita regularly buys milk from a dairy, and the dairy has always charged the same rate. They agree to continue. The price is determined by the course of dealing (section 9(1)). If Rita and the dairy had agreed nothing about price at all, Rita would pay a reasonable price (section 9(2)).

Agreement to sell at valuation (section 10)

Rule. If the agreement is to sell at a price fixed by a third party's valuation and the third party cannot or does not make the valuation, the agreement is avoided. But if the goods or any part have been delivered to and appropriated by the buyer, he must pay a reasonable price for them.

If the third party is prevented from making the valuation by the fault of the seller or the buyer, the party not at fault may sue the party at fault for damages (section 10(2)).

Example. Sunil agrees to sell his painting to Tara at the price a named valuer will fix. The valuer dies before valuing it. The agreement is avoided (section 10(1)). If Sunil had already delivered the painting and Tara had taken it as her own, she would have to pay a reasonable price (proviso).

Reasonable time (section 63)

Rule. Wherever the Act refers to a reasonable time, what is a reasonable time is a question of fact.

Changes in taxes after the contract (section 64A)

Rule. Unless a different intention appears from the terms of the contract, the seller and the buyer share the effect of changes in customs duty, excise duty, or a tax on the sale or purchase of goods after the contract is made.

If a tax or an increase takes effect, the seller may add to the price the amount paid or payable, and may sue to recover it (section 64A(1)(a)). If a decrease or remission takes effect, the buyer may deduct the amount of the decrease or the remitted tax from the price (section 64A(1)(b)). The taxes covered are duties of customs or excise on goods and taxes on the sale or purchase of goods (section 64A(2)).

Example. Umesh agrees to sell goods at Rs 1,000 with no mention of tax, when no tax was chargeable. A sales tax of Rs 50 is imposed afterwards and Umesh has to pay it. Unless the contract shows a different intention, he may add Rs 50 to the price and sue for it (section 64A(1)(a)).

What price must the buyer pay? How to read this: start at the top. 1. Is the price fixed by the contract, left to be fixed in the agreed manner, or determined by course of dealing (section 9(1))? That price is payable. 2. Is the price to be fixed by a third party's valuation (section 10)? If he does not make the valuation, the agreement is avoided, but a buyer who has received and appropriated goods pays a reasonable price. 3. Is the price not determined in any of these ways? The buyer pays a reasonable price, a question of fact (section 9(2)). 4. Did a customs, excise or sales tax change after the contract (section 64A)? Unless a different intention appears, the seller adds an increase and the buyer deducts a decrease.

## Key Points to Remember
- Price: the money consideration (section 2(10)). Fixed by the contract, left to be fixed as agreed, or determined by course of dealing (section 9(1)). Otherwise a reasonable price, a question of fact (section 9(2)). Valuation by a third party fails: agreement avoided, but a buyer who has received and appropriated goods pays a reasonable price (section 10(1)). Party at fault for preventing valuation: suit for damages (section 10(2)). Reasonable time is a question of fact (section 63). Tax changes after the contract: seller may add, buyer may deduct (section 64A).
