# 3. Conditions and Warranties
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/conditions-and-warranties/

## In One Minute
A stipulation in a contract of sale may be a condition or a warranty (section 12(1)). A condition is essential to the main purpose of the contract, and its breach gives a right to treat the contract as repudiated (section 12(2)). A warranty is collateral to the main purpose, and its breach gives a claim for damages only (section 12(3)). Whether a stipulation is a condition or a warranty depends on the construction of the contract, not on the name used (section 12(4)). A buyer may waive a condition or treat its breach as a breach of warranty, and after accepting the goods in a contract that is not severable he can generally only claim for breach of warranty (section 13). Time of payment is not of the essence unless a different intention appears (section 11).

## Meaning and Definition
Condition and warranty (section 12)

Rule. A stipulation about the goods may be a condition or a warranty.

A condition is essential to the main purpose of the contract. Its breach gives a right to treat the contract as repudiated. A warranty is collateral to the main purpose. Its breach gives a claim for damages, but no right to reject the goods and treat the contract as repudiated.

Example. Asha orders a "new" refrigerator. The refrigerator delivered is old. Being new is essential to what she bought, so this is a breach of a condition, and she may treat the contract as repudiated (section 12(2)). If instead the seller had promised a free carry-bag with it and did not give it, that stipulation is collateral to the main purpose and the claim is for damages (section 12(3)).

Name does not decide (section 12(4))

Rule. Whether a stipulation is a condition or a warranty depends in each case on the construction of the contract. A stipulation may be a condition though called a warranty in the contract.

Stipulations as to time (section 11)

Rule. Unless a different intention appears from the terms of the contract, stipulations as to time of payment are not of the essence of a contract of sale. For any other stipulation as to time, whether it is of the essence depends on the terms of the contract.

Example. A buyer agrees to pay on the 1st of the month and pays on the 5th. Unless the contract shows a different intention, the seller cannot treat the contract as repudiated only for the late payment (section 11).

When a condition is treated as a warranty (section 13)

Rule. Section 13 gives three rules.

Where the contract is subject to a condition to be fulfilled by the seller, the buyer may waive it, or elect to treat its breach as a breach of warranty (section 13(1)). Where the contract is not severable and the buyer has accepted the goods or part of them, the breach of any condition to be fulfilled by the seller can only be treated as a breach of warranty. He cannot reject the goods and treat the contract as repudiated, unless there is a term of the contract, express or implied, to that effect (section 13(2)). The section does not affect a condition or warranty whose fulfilment is excused by law by reason of impossibility or otherwise (section 13(3)).

Example. Bala buys a whole lot of 100 chairs under one contract that is not severable. He accepts the lot. Later he finds that the chairs do not match the stipulated condition. Unless the contract says otherwise, he can only treat it as a breach of warranty and claim damages. He cannot reject the chairs (section 13(2)).

Breach by the seller: reject the goods or claim damages? How to read this: start at the top. 1. Is the broken stipulation essential to the main purpose of the contract (section 12(2))? If yes, it is a condition. Go to step 2. If it is only collateral, it is a warranty: the buyer claims damages and cannot reject the goods (section 12(3)). 2. Has the buyer waived the condition or chosen to treat the breach as a breach of warranty (section 13(1))? If yes, he claims damages only. 3. Is the contract non-severable and has the buyer accepted the goods or part of them (section 13(2))? If yes, and the contract has no term to the contrary, he can treat the breach only as a breach of warranty. Otherwise he may treat the contract as repudiated.

## Key Points to Remember
- Condition: essential to the main purpose. Breach gives a right to repudiate (section 12(2)). Warranty: collateral to the main purpose. Breach gives damages only (section 12(3)). The construction of the contract decides, not the name (section 12(4)). Time of payment is not of the essence unless a different intention appears (section 11). The buyer may waive a condition or treat its breach as a breach of warranty (section 13(1)). After acceptance in a non-severable contract, breach of condition is treated as breach of warranty, unless the contract says otherwise (section 13(2)).
