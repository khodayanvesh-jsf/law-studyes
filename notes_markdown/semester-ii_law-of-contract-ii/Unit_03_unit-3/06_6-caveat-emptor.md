# 6. Caveat Emptor
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/caveat-emptor/

## In One Minute
Caveat emptor is a Latin phrase meaning "let the buyer beware ". The Sale of Goods Act, 1930 does not use the phrase. Its rule is in the opening words of section 16: there is no implied warranty or condition as to the quality or fitness for any particular purpose of goods supplied under a contract of sale, except as the Act provides. The exceptions are the implied terms of the Act: the seller's right to sell and the other terms in section 14, correspondence with description (section 15), fitness for a known purpose and merchantable quality (section 16(1) and (2)), terms annexed by trade usage (section 16(3)), and the terms of a sale by sample (section 17). Key points for the answer: the rule, then each exception with its section.

## Meaning and Definition
The rule (opening words of section 16)

Rule. The Act implies no warranty or condition as to quality or fitness for any particular purpose of goods supplied under a contract of sale, except as the Act provides. The buyer must therefore satisfy himself about quality and fitness, or get an express term.

Example. Rohit buys a second-hand pump from a neighbour, who is not in the business of selling pumps. Rohit did not state any particular purpose. If the pump is weak, section 16 gives no implied condition of fitness or quality, and none of the exceptions below applies on these facts.

Exceptions: terms the Act implies

Rule. The Act gives these exceptions.

Title (section 14). Implied condition of right to sell. Implied warranties of quiet possession and freedom from undisclosed encumbrance. Description (section 15). Implied condition that goods correspond with the description. Fitness for a known purpose (section 16(1)). Where the buyer makes known the purpose, relies on the seller's skill or judgment, and the goods are of a description the seller supplies in his business. Not for a specified article sold under its patent or other trade name. Merchantable quality (section 16(2)). Where goods are bought by description from a seller who deals in such goods. Not for defects which the buyer's examination ought to have revealed. Usage of trade (section 16(3)). A term as to quality or fitness annexed by trade usage. Sample (section 17). Three implied conditions in a sale by sample.

Example. Sheela tells a dealer in bicycles that she needs a bicycle for daily use on a rough village road, relying on the dealer's advice. He supplies one that breaks on the first day. The exception in section 16(1) applies: there is an implied condition of reasonable fitness for that purpose.

Express terms and exclusion (sections 16(4) and 62)

Rule. An express warranty or condition does not negative an implied one unless inconsistent with it (section 16(4)). Implied terms may also be negatived or varied by express agreement, course of dealing or usage binding both parties (section 62).

Is the buyer protected, or must he beware? How to read this: start at the top. Stop at the first step that fits. 1. Is it a question of the seller's title, possession or an undisclosed charge (section 14)? The Act implies terms. The buyer is protected. 2. Was the sale by description (section 15), or by sample (section 17)? The Act implies conditions. The buyer is protected. 3. Did the buyer make known the purpose and rely on the seller's skill, or buy by description from a dealer (section 16(1), (2)), or does trade usage supply a term (section 16(3))? The Act implies conditions, subject to the provisos. The buyer is protected. 4. None of the above? No implied warranty or condition of quality or fitness (section 16). The buyer must beware.

## Key Points to Remember
- Caveat emptor: "let the buyer beware". The rule is in the opening words of section 16. No implied warranty or condition as to quality or fitness, except as the Act provides. Exceptions: section 14 (title), 15 (description), 16(1) (fitness), 16(2) (merchantable quality), 16(3) (trade usage), 17 (sample). Fitness needs the purpose made known and reliance on the seller's skill or judgment. No fitness condition for patent or trade name articles. No quality condition for defects the buyer's examination ought to have revealed. Implied terms can be negatived or varied (section 62).
