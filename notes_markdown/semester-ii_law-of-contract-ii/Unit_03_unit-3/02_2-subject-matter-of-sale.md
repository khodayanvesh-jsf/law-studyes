# 2. Subject matter of sale
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/subject-matter-of-sale/

## In One Minute
The subject-matter of a contract of sale is goods: every kind of moveable property other than actionable claims and money (section 2(7)). Goods may be existing goods, owned or possessed by the seller, or future goods (section 6(1)). A contract for specific goods is void if, without the seller's knowledge, the goods had already perished when the contract was made (section 7). An agreement to sell specific goods is avoided if they perish later, without fault of either party, before the risk passes to the buyer (section 8). Key points for the answer: the definition of goods, the kinds of goods, and sections 7 and 8.

## Meaning and Definition
What counts as goods (section 2(7))

Rule. Goods are every kind of moveable property other than actionable claims and money.

Included: stock and shares, growing crops, grass. Included: things attached to or forming part of the land which are agreed to be severed before sale or under the contract of sale. Excluded: actionable claims and money.

Example. A farmer sells the standing sugarcane in his field to a mill. The sugarcane is a growing crop and so falls within the definition of goods.

Existing goods and future goods (sections 2(6) and 6(1))

Rule. Goods that are the subject of a contract of sale may be existing goods, owned or possessed by the seller, or future goods. Future goods are goods to be manufactured or produced or acquired by the seller after the contract is made.

Example. A furniture maker agrees on 1 March to sell a cupboard to Rani. He will build it in April. The cupboard is future goods on 1 March.

Goods depending on a contingency (section 6(2))

Rule. There may be a contract for the sale of goods the acquisition of which by the seller depends upon a contingency which may or may not happen.

Example. A trader agrees to sell a consignment he hopes to buy at an auction next week. His acquiring it depends on whether he wins the bid. The contract is allowed by section 6(2).

Present sale of future goods (section 6(3))

Rule. Where the seller purports to effect a present sale of future goods, the contract operates as an agreement to sell the goods.

This links to the topic on sale and agreement to sell. A sale needs the property in the goods to pass under the contract (section 4(3)). Goods the seller does not yet have cannot be transferred now, so the contract is an agreement to sell.

Specific goods (section 2(14))

Rule. Specific goods are goods identified and agreed upon at the time a contract of sale is made.

Example. Karan agrees to buy "that black horse in the stable", which both have seen. The horse is identified and agreed upon when the contract is made. It is specific goods.

Goods perishing before the contract (section 7)

Rule. If a contract is for specific goods, and the goods, without the seller's knowledge, had perished or become so damaged as no longer to answer to their description in the contract when the contract was made, the contract is void.

Example. Lata agrees on Monday to sell a specific car to Mohan. Unknown to Lata, the car was burnt in a fire on Sunday night. The contract is void under section 7.

Goods perishing after the agreement, before the risk passes (section 8)

Rule. If there is an agreement to sell specific goods, and afterwards the goods perish or become so damaged as no longer to answer to their description in the agreement, without fault of the seller or the buyer, before the risk passes to the buyer, the agreement is avoided.

Example. Nisha agrees to sell a specific tractor to Om, delivery next month. Before the risk passes to Om, lightning destroys the tractor. Neither party is at fault. The agreement is avoided under section 8.

Did the goods perish? Which section applies? How to read this: start at the top. Both sections speak only of specific goods. 1. Are the goods specific, that is, identified and agreed upon when the contract was made (section 2(14))? If not, sections 7 and 8 do not apply on their words. 2. Had they perished, without the seller's knowledge, when the contract was made? The contract is void (section 7). 3. Did they perish later, without fault of seller or buyer, before the risk passed to the buyer, under an agreement to sell? The agreement is avoided (section 8).

## Key Points to Remember
- Goods: all moveable property except actionable claims and money (section 2(7)). Includes stock and shares, growing crops, grass, and things agreed to be severed. Existing goods or future goods (sections 2(6), 6(1)). Contingent goods are allowed (section 6(2)). A present sale of future goods is an agreement to sell (section 6(3)). Specific goods: identified and agreed upon at the time of the contract (section 2(14)). Section 7: specific goods already perished at the contract, without the seller's knowledge, so the contract is void. Section 8: specific goods perish later, without fault, before the risk passes, so the agreement is avoided.
