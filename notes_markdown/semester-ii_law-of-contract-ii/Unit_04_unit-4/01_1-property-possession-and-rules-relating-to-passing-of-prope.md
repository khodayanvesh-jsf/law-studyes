# 1. Property - Possession and Rules relating to passing of property
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/passing-of-property/

## In One Minute
"Property" in goods means the general property in them, not merely a special property (section 2(11)). "Delivery" means voluntary transfer of possession (section 2(2)). A seller who gives possession does not necessarily pass the property, and the Act has separate rules for when the property passes to the buyer. No property passes in unascertained goods until they are ascertained (section 18). Otherwise the property passes when the parties intend it to pass, judged from the contract, conduct and circumstances (section 19). Sections 20 to 24 give the rules for specific goods, unascertained goods and goods on approval. Section 25 allows the seller to reserve the right of disposal. Risk prima facie passes with the property (section 26).

## Meaning and Definition
Property and possession (sections 2(2) and 2(11))

Rule. Property is the general property in the goods. Delivery is the voluntary transfer of possession. The Act treats them as different things: section 25(1) says that even after delivery to a buyer or carrier, the property does not pass until conditions imposed by the seller are fulfilled. Section 26 says the goods are at the buyer's risk once the property passes, whether delivery has been made or not.

Example. Kavya hands a laptop to Lalit to try for a week. Lalit has possession. Whether the property has passed depends on the rules in section 24 and on the intention of the parties.

Unascertained goods (sections 18 and 23)

Rule. No property passes in unascertained goods unless and until they are ascertained (section 18). Under section 23(1), in a contract for unascertained or future goods by description, the property passes when goods of that description and in a deliverable state are unconditionally appropriated to the contract, by the seller with the buyer's assent or by the buyer with the seller's assent. Under section 23(2), delivery to the buyer or to a carrier or other bailee for transmission, without reserving the right of disposal, is deemed unconditional appropriation.

Example. Manoj agrees to sell Nita 100 kg of rice out of a godown full of rice. Until the 100 kg is set apart for Nita with her assent, no property passes (section 18). When Manoj puts 100 kg in sacks marked for Nita, and she agrees, the property passes (section 23(1)).

Intention of the parties (section 19)

Rule. For specific or ascertained goods, the property passes when the parties intend. Regard is had to the contract's terms, the parties' conduct and the circumstances. Unless a different intention appears, sections 20 to 24 are the rules for finding that intention.

Specific goods (sections 20, 21 and 22)

Rule. Three rules apply to specific goods.

Unconditional contract, goods in a deliverable state: the property passes when the contract is made, even if payment or delivery is postponed (section 20). Seller bound to put the goods in a deliverable state: the property does not pass until he does so and the buyer has notice (section 21). Goods in a deliverable state, but the seller must weigh, measure, test or do some other act to ascertain the price: the property does not pass until it is done and the buyer has notice (section 22).

Example. Om buys a particular secondhand bike, in running order, under an unconditional contract. He will pay and collect it next week. The property passes to Om when the contract is made (section 20).

Goods on approval or on sale or return (section 24)

Rule. The property passes (a) when the buyer signifies approval or acceptance or does any other act adopting the transaction, or (b) if he does not, but keeps the goods without giving notice of rejection, when the fixed time for return expires, or, if no time was fixed, when a reasonable time expires.

Example. A jeweller sends a necklace to Pooja on approval with no time fixed. Pooja keeps it, gives no notice of rejection, and a reasonable time passes. The property passes to her (section 24(b)).

Reservation of the right of disposal (section 25)

Rule. The seller may reserve the right of disposal until certain conditions are fulfilled. The property then does not pass until they are fulfilled, even if the goods are delivered to the buyer or a carrier. Where goods are shipped or delivered to a railway for carriage and the bill of lading or railway receipt makes them deliverable to the order of the seller or his agent, the seller is prima facie deemed to reserve the right (section 25(2)). If the seller sends the bill of exchange with the bill of lading or railway receipt and the buyer does not honour the bill, he must return the document, and if he wrongfully retains it the property does not pass to him (section 25(3)).

Risk (section 26)

Rule. Unless otherwise agreed, the goods are at the seller's risk until the property passes, and at the buyer's risk afterwards, whether or not delivery has been made. If delivery is delayed by the fault of either party, that party bears any loss which would not have occurred but for the fault. The section does not affect the duties of either as a bailee of the other's goods.

When does the property pass? How to read this: start at the top. 1. Are the goods unascertained? No property passes until they are ascertained (section 18). Then it passes on unconditional appropriation with assent (section 23). 2. Are they specific or ascertained goods? The property passes when the parties intend (section 19). The rules in sections 20 to 22 help find that intention. 3. Sent on approval or sale or return? The property passes under section 24. 4. Has the seller reserved the right of disposal? The property does not pass until the conditions are fulfilled (section 25). 5. Who bears the risk? The owner of the property, unless otherwise agreed (section 26).

## Key Points to Remember
- Unascertained goods: no property until ascertained (section 18). Passes on unconditional appropriation with assent (section 23). Specific goods: property passes when the parties intend (section 19). Section 20: unconditional contract, deliverable state, property passes when the contract is made. Sections 21 and 22: property passes after the seller's act and notice to the buyer. Section 24: approval or sale or return, property passes on adoption or on expiry of time. Section 25: the seller may reserve the right of disposal. Section 26: risk prima facie passes with the property.
