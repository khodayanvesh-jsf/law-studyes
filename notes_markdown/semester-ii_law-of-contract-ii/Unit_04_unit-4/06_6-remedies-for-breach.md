# 6. Remedies for breach
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/remedies-for-breach-of-contract-of-sale/

## In One Minute
The seller may sue the buyer for the price where the property has passed and the buyer wrongfully refuses to pay, or where the price is payable on a day certain irrespective of delivery (section 55). Where the buyer wrongfully neglects or refuses to accept and pay for the goods, the seller may sue for damages for non-acceptance (section 56). Where the seller wrongfully neglects or refuses to deliver, the buyer may sue for damages for non-delivery (section 57). The Court may order specific performance in a suit for breach of a contract to deliver specific or ascertained goods (section 58). For a breach of warranty the buyer may set it up against the price or sue for damages (section 59). Section 60 deals with repudiation before the due date, and section 61 with interest and special damages.

## Meaning and Definition
Seller's suit for the price (section 55)

Rule. The seller may sue for the price in two cases. First, the property has passed and the buyer wrongfully neglects or refuses to pay according to the contract. Second, the price is payable on a day certain irrespective of delivery and the buyer wrongfully neglects or refuses to pay; here the seller may sue although the property has not passed and the goods have not been appropriated to the contract.

Example. Anwar sells a tractor to Balaji, and the property passes to Balaji. Balaji wrongfully refuses to pay. Anwar may sue for the price (section 55(1)).

Damages for non-acceptance and non-delivery (sections 56 and 57)

Rule. If the buyer wrongfully neglects or refuses to accept and pay, the seller may sue for damages for non-acceptance (section 56). If the seller wrongfully neglects or refuses to deliver, the buyer may sue for damages for non-delivery (section 57).

Specific performance (section 58)

Rule. In a suit for breach of a contract to deliver specific or ascertained goods, the Court may, if it thinks fit, on the plaintiff's application, direct by its decree that the contract be performed specifically, without giving the defendant the option of retaining the goods on payment of damages. The section is subject to Chapter II of the Specific Relief Act, 1877. The decree may be unconditional or on terms about damages, payment of the price or otherwise. The application may be made at any time before the decree.

Breach of warranty (section 59)

Rule. The buyer cannot reject the goods only because of a breach of warranty, including where he elects or is compelled to treat a breach of condition as a breach of warranty. He may set up the breach in diminution or extinction of the price, or sue for damages. Setting it up against the price does not stop him suing for the same breach if he has suffered further damage.

Example. Chitra buys a machine with a warranty of one year's free servicing. The seller refuses to service it. Chitra may deduct a part of the price on that account or sue for damages, but she cannot reject the machine for that breach alone (section 59(1)).

Repudiation before the due date (section 60)

Rule. If either party repudiates the contract before the date of delivery, the other may treat the contract as subsisting and wait till the date of delivery, or treat it as rescinded and sue for damages for the breach.

Interest and special damages (section 61)

Rule. The Act does not affect the right to recover interest or special damages where the law allows them, or to recover money paid where the consideration has failed (section 61(1)). Absent a contrary contract, the Court may award interest on the price at a rate it thinks fit: to the seller from the date of tender of the goods or the date the price was payable, and to the buyer, in a suit for refund of the price for the seller's breach, from the date of payment (section 61(2)).

Which remedy? How to read this: start at the top. 1. Buyer will not pay. Has the property passed, or is the price payable on a day certain? Suit for the price (section 55). Otherwise damages for non-acceptance (section 56). 2. Seller will not deliver? Damages for non-delivery (section 57). For specific or ascertained goods, specific performance may be sought (section 58). 3. Breach of warranty by the seller? The buyer cannot reject for it alone. He may reduce the price or sue for damages (section 59). 4. Repudiation before the delivery date? Wait for the date, or treat the contract as rescinded and sue for damages (section 60).

## Key Points to Remember
- Seller's suit for the price: property passed and wrongful refusal to pay, or price payable on a day certain (section 55). Damages for non-acceptance (section 56). Damages for non-delivery (section 57). Specific performance may be ordered for specific or ascertained goods (section 58). Breach of warranty: no right to reject for it alone. Set it against the price or sue for damages (section 59). Repudiation before the due date: wait, or rescind and sue (section 60). Interest and special damages are not affected, and money paid on failed consideration can be recovered (section 61).
