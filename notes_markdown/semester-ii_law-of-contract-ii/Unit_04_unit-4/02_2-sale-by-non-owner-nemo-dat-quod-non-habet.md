# 2. Sale by non-owner – Nemo dat quod non habet
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/sale-by-non-owner/

## In One Minute
Nemo dat quod non habet is a Latin maxim meaning "no one gives what he does not have". Under section 27 of the Sale of Goods Act, 1930, where goods are sold by a person who is not the owner and who does not sell with the owner's authority or consent, the buyer acquires no better title than the seller had. The Act itself lists exceptions where a non-owner can pass a good title: the owner is precluded by his conduct, or a mercantile agent sells in the ordinary course (section 27); a joint owner in sole possession sells (section 28); a seller who obtained possession under a voidable contract, not yet rescinded, sells (section 29); a seller or buyer in possession after sale disposes of the goods (section 30); and an unpaid seller resells (section 54(3)).

## Meaning and Definition
The rule (section 27)

Rule. If a person who is not the owner, and who does not sell under the owner's authority or with his consent, sells goods, the buyer acquires no better title than the seller had. The exception in the same section is where the owner is by his conduct precluded from denying the seller's authority to sell.

Example. Tarun finds a camera lying in a park and sells it to Usha, who knows nothing of this. Tarun has no title, and the owner did not authorise the sale. Usha gets no better title than Tarun had, and the owner can claim the camera.

Exceptions in the Act

Rule. A buyer gets a good title in these cases.

Owner precluded by conduct (section 27). The owner by his conduct cannot deny the seller's authority to sell. Mercantile agent (proviso to section 27). A mercantile agent in possession of the goods or a document of title with the owner's consent sells in the ordinary course of business of a mercantile agent. The buyer acts in good faith and has no notice that the agent lacks authority. Joint owner (section 28). One of several joint owners has sole possession by permission of the co-owners, and sells to a buyer in good faith without notice. Voidable contract (section 29). The seller obtained possession under a contract voidable under section 19 or 19A of the Contract Act, which had not been rescinded when he sold. The buyer buys in good faith and without notice of the defect of title. Seller in possession after sale (section 30(1)). Having sold, he continues in possession of the goods or documents of title, and delivers or transfers them under a sale, pledge or other disposition to a person in good faith without notice of the previous sale. Buyer in possession after sale (section 30(2)). Having bought or agreed to buy, he obtains possession with the seller's consent and transfers under a sale, pledge or other disposition to a person in good faith without notice of any lien or other right of the original seller. Resale by an unpaid seller (section 54(3)). An unpaid seller who has exercised lien or stoppage in transit re-sells. The buyer gets a good title against the original buyer.

Example. Vimal gives his goods to a commission agent, who customarily sells goods for others, to sell. The agent sells in the ordinary course of business to Wasim, who acts in good faith and does not know of any limit on the agent's authority. Wasim gets a good title (proviso to section 27).

Does the buyer get a good title? How to read this: start at the top. Good faith and lack of notice are stated in sections 27 (proviso), 28, 29 and 30. Section 54(3) and the owner's-conduct exception do not state them. 1. Did the seller sell with the owner's authority or consent? If yes, the title passes in the ordinary way. 2. Is the owner precluded by his conduct from denying the seller's authority (section 27)? If yes, the buyer gets a good title. 3. Is the seller a mercantile agent in possession with the owner's consent (section 27 proviso), a joint owner in sole possession (section 28), a person with a voidable title not yet rescinded (section 29), or a seller or buyer in possession after sale (section 30)? If yes, and the buyer acted in good faith and without notice, he gets a good title. 4. Otherwise The buyer acquires no better title than the seller had (section 27).

## Key Points to Remember
- Nemo dat: a buyer from a non-owner gets no better title than the seller had (section 27). Exceptions: owner's conduct, mercantile agent (section 27), joint owner (section 28), voidable title (section 29), seller or buyer in possession (section 30), unpaid seller's resale (section 54(3)). Good faith and no notice are stated for sections 27 (proviso), 28, 29 and 30. Section 54(3) does not state them. Mercantile agent: section 2(9). Section 27 is subject to the Act and to any other law.
