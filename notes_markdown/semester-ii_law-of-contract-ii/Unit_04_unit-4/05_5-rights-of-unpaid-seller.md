# 5. Rights of unpaid seller
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/rights-of-unpaid-seller/

## In One Minute
A seller is an "unpaid seller" when the whole of the price has not been paid or tendered, or when a bill of exchange or other negotiable instrument received as conditional payment has been dishonoured or its condition otherwise not fulfilled (section 45). Even if the property has passed to the buyer, the unpaid seller has by implication of law a lien on the goods while in possession, a right of stopping them in transit if the buyer becomes insolvent, and a right of re-sale as limited by the Act (section 46(1)). If the property has not passed, he has a right of withholding delivery similar to and co-extensive with lien and stoppage in transit (section 46(2)). Sections 47 to 49 deal with lien, sections 50 to 52 with stoppage in transit , and sections 53 and 54 with sub-sales and re-sale.

## Meaning and Definition
Who is an unpaid seller (section 45)

Rule. A seller is an unpaid seller if the whole of the price has not been paid or tendered, or if he received a negotiable instrument as conditional payment and the condition failed by dishonour or otherwise. Within the Chapter, "seller" includes an agent of the seller to whom the bill of lading has been endorsed, or a consignor or agent who has himself paid, or is directly responsible for, the price.

Example. Rajan sells goods to Salim for Rs 20,000. Salim gives a cheque as payment on the condition that it is honoured. The cheque is dishonoured. Rajan is an unpaid seller (section 45(1)(b)).

The unpaid seller's three rights (section 46)

Rule. Even if the property has passed, the unpaid seller has by implication of law: (a) a lien while in possession; (b) a right of stopping the goods in transit if the buyer becomes insolvent; (c) a right of re-sale as limited by the Act. If the property has not passed, he has a right of withholding delivery similar to and co-extensive with his rights of lien and stoppage in transit.

Right of lien (sections 47 to 49)

Rule. The unpaid seller in possession may retain the goods until payment or tender of the price in three cases: sold without stipulation as to credit, sold on credit but the credit term has expired, or the buyer becomes insolvent. He may exercise the lien even if he holds the goods as agent or bailee for the buyer. After part delivery he may exercise it on the remainder unless the part delivery shows an agreement to waive it (section 48). He loses it when he delivers to a carrier or other bailee for transmission without reserving the right of disposal, when the buyer or his agent lawfully obtains possession, or by waiver. Obtaining a decree for the price does not by itself end it (section 49).

Example. Tara sells a machine to Umesh with no credit term and keeps it in her godown. Umesh has not paid. Tara may retain the machine until she is paid or tendered the price (section 47(1)(a)).

Stoppage in transit (sections 50 to 52)

Rule. When the buyer becomes insolvent, the unpaid seller who has parted with possession may resume possession of the goods while they are in the course of transit, and retain them until payment or tender of the price (section 50). Goods are in transit from delivery to the carrier until the buyer or his agent takes delivery. Transit ends if the buyer obtains delivery before arrival, or if the carrier acknowledges that he holds the goods for the buyer after arrival, or if the carrier wrongfully refuses to deliver to the buyer. Rejection by the buyer does not end transit if the carrier still holds the goods (section 51). The seller exercises the right by taking actual possession or by notice to the carrier or bailee. The carrier then re-delivers to the seller or as he directs, at the seller's expense (section 52).

Example. Vimla sends goods to Waqar by rail. Before they arrive, she learns Waqar is insolvent. She gives notice to the railway. Because the goods are in transit, she may stop them and retain them until she is paid (sections 50 and 52).

Effect of a sub-sale or pledge by the buyer (section 53)

Rule. The seller's right of lien or stoppage is not affected by the buyer's sale or other disposition unless the seller assented to it. But if a document of title has been issued or lawfully transferred to a person as buyer or owner, and that person transfers it to someone who takes in good faith and for consideration, then a transfer by sale defeats the seller's right, and a transfer by pledge or other disposition for value allows it to be exercised only subject to the transferee's rights. Where the transfer is a pledge, the seller may require the pledgee to satisfy the debt first, as far as possible, out of the buyer's other goods or securities in his hands (section 53(2)).

Re-sale (section 54)

Rule. Lien or stoppage does not by itself rescind the contract of sale (section 54(1)). The unpaid seller may re-sell in two cases, and the effect differs.

Perishable goods, or notice to the buyer of intention to re-sell followed by the buyer's failure to pay or tender within a reasonable time: the seller may re-sell within a reasonable time and recover damages for loss. The buyer is not entitled to any profit. Without that notice, the seller cannot recover damages and the buyer is entitled to any profit (section 54(2)). On re-sale after exercising lien or stoppage, the new buyer gets a good title against the original buyer, even without notice to the original buyer (section 54(3)). Where the seller expressly reserved a right of re-sale on default and re-sells on default, the original contract is rescinded without prejudice to a claim for damages (section 54(4)).

Which right does the unpaid seller use? How to read this: start at the top. 1. Is he an unpaid seller (section 45)? If not, none of these rights arises. 2. Is he still in possession of the goods? He has a lien (sections 46(1)(a), 47) if the goods were sold without credit stipulation, the credit has expired, or the buyer is insolvent. 3. Has he parted with possession, and is the buyer insolvent, with the goods in transit? He may stop them in transit (sections 50 to 52). 4. Has the buyer failed to pay after notice of re-sale, or are the goods perishable? He may re-sell and claim damages (section 54).

## Key Points to Remember
- Unpaid seller: price not paid or tendered, or conditional payment instrument dishonoured (section 45). Rights even after the property passes: lien, stoppage in transit, re-sale (section 46(1)). If the property has not passed: right of withholding delivery (section 46(2)). Lien: seller in possession, no credit stipulated, credit expired, or buyer insolvent (section 47). Lien lost: delivery to a carrier without reserving disposal, buyer lawfully takes possession, waiver (section 49). Stoppage: buyer insolvent, seller out of possession, goods in transit (section 50). Good-faith transferee of a document of title for consideration defeats the seller's right on a sale (section 53 proviso). Re-sale: notice and reasonable time (section 54(2)). New buyer gets a good title (section 54(3)).
