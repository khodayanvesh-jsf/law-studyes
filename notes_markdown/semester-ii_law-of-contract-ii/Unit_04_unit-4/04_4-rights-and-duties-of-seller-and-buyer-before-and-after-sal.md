# 4. Rights and duties of seller and buyer -Before and after sale
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/rights-and-duties-of-seller-and-buyer/

## In One Minute
It is the duty of the seller to deliver the goods and of the buyer to accept and pay for them, in accordance with the terms of the contract of sale (section 31). Unless otherwise agreed, delivery and payment are concurrent conditions (section 32). The buyer has a right to a reasonable opportunity of examining the goods (section 41). He is deemed to have accepted them in the cases listed in section 42, and is not bound to return rejected goods (section 43). If he neglects or refuses to take delivery, he is liable for loss and the cost of custody (section 44). Key points for the answer: the duties in sections 31 and 32, then each right with its section.

## Meaning and Definition
Duties of seller and buyer (section 31)

Rule. The seller must deliver the goods and the buyer must accept and pay for them, in accordance with the terms of the contract.

Payment and delivery together (section 32)

Rule. Unless otherwise agreed, delivery and payment are concurrent conditions. The seller must be ready and willing to give possession in exchange for the price. The buyer must be ready and willing to pay the price in exchange for possession.

Example. Naveen agrees to sell a cycle to Omkar for Rs 5,000. Naveen need not hand over the cycle unless Omkar is ready and willing to pay. Omkar need not pay unless Naveen is ready and willing to give possession.

Examining the goods (section 41)

Rule. A buyer who has not previously examined the goods is not deemed to have accepted them until he has had a reasonable opportunity of examining them to see whether they conform to the contract. Unless otherwise agreed, the seller who tenders delivery must, on request, give that opportunity.

Acceptance (section 42)

Rule. The buyer is deemed to have accepted the goods in any of three cases.

He intimates to the seller that he has accepted them. The goods have been delivered to him and he does an act in relation to them that is inconsistent with the seller's ownership. After a reasonable time he retains the goods without intimating to the seller that he has rejected them.

Rejected goods (section 43)

Rule. Unless otherwise agreed, a buyer who has the right to refuse the goods and does so is not bound to return them. It is enough that he intimates to the seller that he refuses to accept them.

Buyer's neglect or refusal to take delivery (section 44)

Rule. If the seller is ready and willing to deliver and asks the buyer to take delivery, and the buyer does not do so within a reasonable time, he is liable for any loss caused by his neglect or refusal and for a reasonable charge for the care and custody of the goods. The seller's rights are not affected where the neglect or refusal amounts to repudiation of the contract.

Example. Pooja has bought 50 chairs. The seller is ready to deliver and asks her to take them. She does not collect them for two months. She is liable for the seller's loss and for a reasonable charge for keeping the chairs (section 44).

Duties, examination, acceptance How to read this: start at the top. 1. Duties (section 31) and concurrent conditions (section 32) Seller delivers, buyer accepts and pays, each ready and willing in exchange for the other, unless otherwise agreed. 2. Goods delivered but not yet examined (section 41) No acceptance until a reasonable opportunity to examine. 3. Acceptance (section 42) By intimation, by an act inconsistent with the seller's ownership, or by retaining beyond a reasonable time without intimating rejection. 4. Rejection (section 43) and neglect to take delivery (section 44) A buyer who rightly rejects need not return the goods; a buyer who neglects delivery pays for loss and custody.

## Key Points to Remember
- Seller delivers, buyer accepts and pays, as the contract says (section 31). Delivery and payment are concurrent unless otherwise agreed (section 32). No acceptance without a reasonable opportunity to examine (section 41). Acceptance: intimation, an act inconsistent with the seller's ownership, or retention beyond a reasonable time (section 42). A buyer who rightly rejects need not return the goods (section 43). Neglect to take delivery: liable for loss and custody charges (section 44).
