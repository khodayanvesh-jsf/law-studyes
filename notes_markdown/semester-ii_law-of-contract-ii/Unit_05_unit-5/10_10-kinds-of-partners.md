# 10. Kinds of partners
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/kinds-of-partners/

## In One Minute
The Indian Partnership Act, 1932 does not divide partners into labelled classes such as active, sleeping or nominal partners. Those words do not appear in the Act. The Act does give separate rules for persons in different positions in a firm: a partner (section 4), a person who holds himself out as a partner (section 28), a minor admitted to the benefits of partnership (section 30), an incoming partner (section 31), a retiring partner (section 32), an expelled partner (section 33), an insolvent partner (section 34), and the estate of a deceased partner (section 35). This note sets out each position from the Act. A textbook classification by activity in the business is not in the Act and is not given here.

## Meaning and Definition
Partner (section 4)

Rule. A person who has entered into partnership with another is a partner, and the partners together are the firm.

Person held out as a partner (section 28)

Rule. A person who represents himself, or knowingly permits himself to be represented, as a partner is liable as a partner to anyone who gave credit to the firm on the faith of the representation, whether or not he knows the representation reached that person.

Example. Pranav, who is not a partner, allows a firm's letterhead to list him as a partner. A supplier gives credit to the firm because of that. Pranav is liable as a partner to the supplier (section 28(1)).

Minor admitted to the benefits (section 30)

Rule. A minor is not a partner. He shares property and profits as agreed, and only his share is liable for the firm's acts. The topic on minor as partner covers this in full.

Incoming partner (section 31)

Rule. No person may be introduced as a partner without the consent of all the existing partners, subject to contract and section 30. He is not liable for acts of the firm done before he became a partner.

Retiring, expelled, insolvent and deceased partners (sections 32 to 35)

Rule. A partner retires with the consent of all, under an express agreement, or by written notice in a partnership at will. He and the partners remain liable to third parties until public notice is given, but a retired partner is not liable to a third party who did not know he was a partner. An expelled partner is treated like a retired partner for these purposes. An insolvent partner ceases to be a partner on the date of the adjudication order. Where the contract keeps the firm going after a partner's death, his estate is not liable for the firm's later acts.

Which position is the person in? How to read this: start at the top. 1. Has he entered into partnership by agreement (section 4)? He is a partner. 2. Only represented or allowed to be represented as one (section 28)? Liable as a partner to those who gave credit on the faith of it. 3. A minor admitted to the benefits (section 30)? Not a partner. His share, not he, is liable. 4. Joining, retiring, expelled, insolvent or dead (sections 31 to 35)? The rules of those sections apply.

## Key Points to Remember
- The words active, sleeping and nominal partner are not in the Act. Partner (section 4). Held out as a partner (section 28). Minor admitted to benefits (section 30). Incoming partner needs everyone's consent and is not liable for earlier acts (section 31). Retired partner is liable until public notice (section 32). Expelled is treated like retired (section 33). Insolvent partner ceases to be a partner on the order (section 34). Deceased partner's estate not liable for later acts (section 35).
