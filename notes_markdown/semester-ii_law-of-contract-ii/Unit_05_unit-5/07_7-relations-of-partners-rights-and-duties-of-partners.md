# 7. Relations of partners - Rights and duties of partners
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/relations-and-duties-of-partners/

## In One Minute
The partners' mutual rights and duties may be fixed by their contract (section 11). Unless the contract says otherwise, the Act supplies these rules: every partner has a right to take part in the conduct of the business and a duty to attend to it diligently, differences on ordinary matters are decided by a majority, and no change in the nature of the business may be made without the consent of all (section 12). Unless the contract says otherwise, no partner is entitled to remuneration , the partners share profits equally and bear losses equally, and interest on a partner's advance beyond his capital is six per cent per annum (section 13). A partner must account to the firm for personal profits from the firm's transactions, property or business connection (section 16). Key points for the answer: the duties in sections 9 and 10, then sections 12, 13 and 16.

## Meaning and Definition
General duties (section 9)

Rule. Partners must carry on the business of the firm to the greatest common advantage, be just and faithful to each other, and render true accounts and full information of all things affecting the firm to any partner or his legal representative.

Indemnity for fraud (section 10)

Rule. A partner must indemnify the firm for any loss caused to it by his fraud in the conduct of the business.

Rights and duties by contract (section 11)

Rule. The partners' mutual rights and duties are fixed by their contract, express or implied by a course of dealing, and varied by all partners' consent. The contract may bar a partner from other business while he is a partner (section 11(2)).

Conduct of the business (section 12)

Rule. Subject to contract, each partner has a right to take part in the conduct of the business and must attend diligently to his duties. Ordinary differences may be decided by a majority, after every partner has expressed his opinion. A change in the nature of the business needs the consent of all. Each partner may inspect and copy the firm's books.

Example. Of three partners in a shop, two want to stock a new brand of soap and one objects. This is an ordinary matter, so the two-partner majority may decide, after hearing the third (section 12(c)). If they wanted to change the shop into a restaurant, that is a change in the nature of the business and needs the consent of all.

Mutual rights and liabilities (section 13)

Rule. Subject to contract: no remuneration for taking part in the business; equal share in profits and equal contribution to losses; interest on capital only out of profits; six per cent per annum on payments or advances beyond agreed capital; the firm indemnifies a partner for payments and liabilities in the ordinary and proper conduct of the business and in an emergency act of ordinary prudence; a partner indemnifies the firm for loss caused by his wilful neglect.

Example. Three partners made a profit of Rs 90,000 and their contract says nothing about shares. Each is entitled to Rs 30,000 (section 13(b)). One partner lent the firm Rs 1,00,000 beyond his capital. He is entitled to interest on it at six per cent per annum (section 13(d)).

Personal profits (section 16)

Rule. Subject to contract, a partner who derives profits for himself from a transaction of the firm, or from the use of the firm's property, business connection or name, must account for and pay that profit to the firm. A partner who carries on a competing business of the same nature must account for and pay to the firm all profits made in that business.

Rights and duties after a change (section 17)

Rule. Subject to contract: after a change in the constitution of the firm, the partners' mutual rights and duties remain the same as before as far as may be; after a fixed-term firm continues past its term, they remain the same so far as consistent with a partnership at will; and for other adventures or undertakings carried out by a firm formed for particular ones, they are the same as for the original ones.

Which rule governs the partners' rights? How to read this: start at the top. 1. Is there a contract between the partners, express or implied by a course of dealing, on the point (section 11)? The contract governs. 2. If not, the Act's rules apply: conduct of business (section 12), mutual rights and liabilities (section 13), personal profits (section 16), changes (section 17). These apply "subject to contract between the partners". 3. Duties of good faith and indemnity for fraud (sections 9 and 10). Stated in the Act. Section 9 is not qualified by "subject to contract".

## Key Points to Remember
- Rights and duties may be fixed by contract (section 11). Sections 12, 13, 16, 17 apply subject to it. Duties: greatest common advantage, just and faithful, true accounts (section 9). Indemnify the firm for fraud (section 10). Every partner may take part in the business. Ordinary matters by majority. Change in nature by all (section 12). No remuneration. Equal sharing of profits and losses. Interest on capital only out of profits. Six per cent on extra advances (section 13). Personal profits and competing business: account to the firm (section 16).
