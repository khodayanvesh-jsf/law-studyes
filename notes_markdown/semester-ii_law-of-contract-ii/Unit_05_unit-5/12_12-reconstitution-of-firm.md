# 12. Reconstitution of firm
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/reconstitution-of-firm/

## In One Minute
Yes, a firm can be reconstituted. The Act deals with changes in the constitution of a firm: a person may be introduced as a partner with the consent of all the existing partners (section 31), a partner may retire (section 32), a partner may be expelled only in good faith under a power in the partners' contract (section 33), and a partner ceases to be a partner on being adjudicated an insolvent (section 34). Where the contract keeps the firm going after a partner's death, the estate is not liable for later acts (section 35). After such a change, the mutual rights and duties of the partners in the reconstituted firm remain the same as before, as far as may be (section 17(a)). Sections 36 and 37 give the rights of an outgoing partner. Section 38 says a continuing guarantee given to a firm is revoked as to future transactions by a change in the firm, unless otherwise agreed.

## Meaning and Definition
Can a firm be reconstituted?

Rule. Yes. A firm is reconstituted when a person is introduced as a partner (section 31), a partner retires (section 32), is expelled (section 33), is adjudicated an insolvent (section 34), or dies where the contract keeps the firm going (section 35). Subject to contract, the partners' mutual rights and duties in the reconstituted firm stay the same as far as may be (section 17(a)).

Example. A, B and C run a firm. C retires with the consent of A and B. The firm continues as a firm of A and B, and the mutual rights and duties of A and B remain as before so far as may be (section 17(a)).

Introducing a partner (section 31)

Rule. No person may be introduced without the consent of all the existing partners, subject to contract and section 30. He is not liable for acts of the firm done before he became a partner.

Retirement (section 32)

Rule. A partner may retire with everyone's consent, under an express agreement, or by written notice in a partnership at will. He may be discharged by agreement with the third party and the partners of the reconstituted firm, and that agreement may be implied from a course of dealing. Until public notice is given, he and the partners remain liable to third parties as partners. He is not liable to a third party who dealt with the firm without knowing he was a partner.

Expulsion (section 33)

Rule. No majority may expel a partner, except in the good-faith exercise of powers given by the partners' contract. Section 32(2), (3) and (4) apply to him as if he had retired.

Insolvency and death (sections 34 and 35)

Rule. A partner adjudicated an insolvent ceases to be a partner on the date of the order, whether or not the firm is dissolved. If the contract keeps the firm going, the insolvent's estate is not liable for the firm's acts after that date, and the firm is not liable for the insolvent's acts. Where the contract keeps the firm going after a death, the deceased's estate is not liable for acts after his death.

Rights of an outgoing partner (sections 36 and 37)

Rule. An outgoing partner may carry on and advertise a competing business, but, subject to contrary contract, may not use the firm name, represent himself as carrying on the firm's business, or solicit the firm's former customers. A reasonable agreement restraining him within a specified period or place is valid despite section 27 of the Contract Act (section 36). If the continuing partners carry on the business with the firm's property without a final settlement, the outgoing partner or his estate may choose a share of profits attributable to the use of his share of the property, or interest at six per cent per annum on his share (section 37).

Guarantees (section 38)

Rule. A continuing guarantee given to a firm, or to a third party about the firm's transactions, is revoked as to future transactions from the date of a change in the firm's constitution, unless agreed otherwise.

Change in the firm: what follows? How to read this: start at the top. 1. A partner joins (section 31), retires (section 32), is expelled (section 33), is adjudicated insolvent (section 34), or dies (section 35)? The firm is reconstituted. Mutual rights and duties continue as far as may be (section 17(a)). 2. Third parties dealing with the firm. The outgoing partner stays liable until public notice (section 32(3)), unless the third party did not know he was a partner. 3. The outgoing partner's claims. Share of profits or six per cent interest if the business continues with the firm's property without settlement (section 37). 4. A continuing guarantee. Revoked as to future transactions, unless agreed otherwise (section 38).

## Key Points to Remember
- A firm can be reconstituted. Rights and duties continue as far as may be (section 17(a)). New partner: consent of all existing partners (section 31). Retirement: consent of all, agreement, or written notice if at will. Liability continues until public notice (section 32). Expulsion only in good faith under a contractual power (section 33). Outgoing partner: competing business allowed, with limits (section 36). Profits or six per cent interest (section 37). Continuing guarantee revoked as to future transactions (section 38).
