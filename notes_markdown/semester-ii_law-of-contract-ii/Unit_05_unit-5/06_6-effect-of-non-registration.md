# 6. Effect of non- registration
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/effect-of-non-registration/

## In One Minute
Section 69 of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932 bars two kinds of suit unless the firm is registered. First, a suit to enforce a right arising from a contract or conferred by the Act, by or on behalf of a person suing as a partner against the firm or any person alleged to be or to have been a partner, needs the firm to be registered and the person suing to be shown in the Register as a partner (section 69(1)). Second, a suit to enforce a right arising from a contract by or on behalf of a firm against a third party needs the firm to be registered and the persons suing to be shown in the Register as partners (section 69(2)). Set-off claims are treated the same way (section 69(3)). Some matters are not affected, and the section has two exclusions (section 69(3) and (4)).

## Meaning and Definition
Suit by a partner against the firm or another partner (section 69(1))

Rule. A person suing as a partner to enforce a right arising from a contract or conferred by the Act against the firm or against a person alleged to be or to have been a partner cannot sue unless two conditions are met. The firm must be registered, and the person suing must be or have been shown in the Register as a partner.

Example. Jai is a partner in an unregistered firm. He wants to sue his partner Karan to enforce a right under their partnership contract. Section 69(1) bars the suit unless the firm is registered and Jai is shown in the Register as a partner.

Suit by the firm against a third party (section 69(2))

Rule. A suit by or on behalf of a firm against any third party to enforce a right arising from a contract cannot be instituted unless the firm is registered and the persons suing are or have been shown in the Register as partners.

Example. An unregistered firm supplies goods to a customer who does not pay. The firm cannot sue the customer on the contract unless the firm is registered and the suing partners are shown in the Register (section 69(2)).

Set-off and other proceedings; what is not affected (section 69(3))

Rule. The bars also apply to a claim of set-off or other proceeding to enforce a right arising from a contract. They do not affect the right to sue for dissolution of a firm or for accounts of a dissolved firm, any right or power to realise the property of a dissolved firm, or the powers under the insolvency Acts to realise an insolvent partner's property.

When section 69 does not apply (section 69(4))

Rule. The section does not apply to firms with no place of business in the territories to which the Act extends, or whose places of business there are in areas exempted under section 56. It does not apply to small suits or set-off claims of the kind and value stated in section 69(4)(b), nor to proceedings in execution or incidental to them.

Can the suit be instituted? How to read this: start at the top. 1. Is it a suit to enforce a right arising from a contract (or a right conferred by the Act, in a partner's suit)? If not, section 69 does not bar it. A suit for dissolution or for accounts of a dissolved firm is also not affected (section 69(3)(a)). 2. Is the firm registered, and are the persons suing shown in the Register as partners? If yes, the suit may be instituted. 3. If not, does section 69(4) exclude the case (no place of business in the territories, an area exempted under section 56, or a small suit)? If yes, the bar does not apply. Otherwise the suit cannot be instituted.

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 69(1): a partner cannot sue the firm or another partner to enforce a contract right or a right under the Act unless the firm is registered and he is shown in the Register. Section 69(2): the firm cannot sue a third party on a contract unless registered and the suing partners are shown in the Register. Set-off and other proceedings are covered (section 69(3)). Not affected: suit for dissolution or accounts of a dissolved firm, realisation of a dissolved firm's property, insolvency powers. Excluded: no place of business in the territories, exempted areas, small suits up to one hundred rupees (section 69(4)).
