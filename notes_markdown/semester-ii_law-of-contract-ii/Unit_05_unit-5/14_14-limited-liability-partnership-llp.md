# 14. Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/limited-liability-partnership/

## In One Minute
A limited liability partnership (LLP) is a partnership formed and registered under the Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008 (section 2(1)(n)). It is a body corporate and a legal entity separate from its partners, with perpetual succession (section 3). The Indian Partnership Act, 1932 does not apply to it, save as otherwise provided (section 4). It needs at least two partners (section 6) and at least two designated partners who are individuals, at least one resident in India (section 7). It is incorporated by filing an incorporation document with the Registrar (sections 11 and 12). The obligations of the LLP are its own, and a partner is not personally liable for them solely by being a partner (sections 27 and 28), except in fraud (section 30).

## Meaning and Definition
Nature of an LLP (sections 2, 3, 4 and 14)

Rule. An LLP is a body corporate and a legal entity separate from its partners, with perpetual succession. On registration it can sue and be sued, own property, have a common seal, and do what bodies corporate may do. The Partnership Act does not apply to it, save as otherwise provided.

Example. Three friends register an LLP to run a design studio. The studio, and not the friends personally, owns its computers and signs its contracts. If one friend leaves, the LLP continues (section 3(3)).

Partners and designated partners (sections 5 to 8)

Rule. Any individual or body corporate may be a partner, except an individual of unsound mind found by a Court, an undischarged insolvent, or one with a pending application to be adjudicated insolvent. At least two partners are required. At least two designated partners who are individuals are required, one resident in India. A designated partner is responsible for the LLP's compliance and liable to the penalties imposed on the LLP for contraventions.

Incorporation (sections 11 and 12)

Rule. Two or more persons associated for a lawful business with a view to profit subscribe their names to an incorporation document, filed with the Registrar of the State of the registered office, with a statement by an advocate, Company Secretary, Chartered Accountant or Cost Accountant, and a subscriber, that the requirements are complied with. The Registrar registers within fourteen days and gives a certificate which is conclusive evidence of incorporation.

Mutual rights and exit (sections 23 and 24)

Rule. The LLP agreement governs mutual rights and duties, and is filed with the Registrar. Without agreement on a matter, the First Schedule applies. A partner may leave by agreement or on thirty days' written notice, and a former partner is still treated as a partner towards a person who has no notice, unless notice was delivered to the Registrar.

Liability (sections 26 to 30)

Rule. A partner is the agent of the LLP and not of other partners. The LLP is bound by acts of a partner except where he has no authority and the other person knows it. The LLP's obligations are its own, met out of its property. A partner is not personally liable for them solely by being a partner, nor for another partner's wrongful act, but is liable for his own. Holding out as a partner creates liability. In fraud, the liability of the LLP and of the partners who acted is unlimited.

Example. A partner of an LLP signs a contract with a supplier without any authority, and the supplier knows he has none. The LLP is not bound (section 27(1)).

Contributions and transfer (sections 32, 33 and 42)

Rule. A contribution may be money, property, benefits or services. The obligation to contribute follows the LLP agreement, and a creditor who relied on it, without notice of a compromise between partners, may enforce the original obligation. A partner's share of profits and losses is transferable, but the transfer does not by itself end his partnership or the LLP and gives the transferee no right in management or information.

Who is liable for an LLP's debt? How to read this: start at the top. 1. Is it an obligation of the LLP, in contract or otherwise (section 27(3))? It is solely the LLP's obligation, met out of its property (section 27(4)). 2. Is a partner being sued only because he is a partner (section 28(1))? He is not personally liable. 3. Did the partner commit a wrongful act or omission himself (section 28(2))? He is liable for his own act, not for another partner's. 4. Was there intent to defraud creditors or a fraudulent purpose (section 30)? Liability of the LLP and the partners who acted is unlimited.

## Key Points to Remember
- An LLP is a body corporate and a separate legal entity with perpetual succession (section 3). The Partnership Act does not apply, save as otherwise provided (section 4). At least two partners (section 6); at least two designated partners who are individuals, one resident in India (section 7). Incorporated by registration with the Registrar; the certificate is conclusive evidence (section 12). Obligations are the LLP's own; partners are not personally liable solely as partners (sections 27 and 28); unlimited liability in fraud (section 30). The LLP agreement governs, with the First Schedule as default (section 23).
