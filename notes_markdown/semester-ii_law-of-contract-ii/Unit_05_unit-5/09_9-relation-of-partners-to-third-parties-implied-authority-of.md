# 9. Relation of partners to third parties-Implied authority of partners
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/partners-and-third-parties/

## In One Minute
A partner is the agent of the firm for the purposes of the business of the firm (section 18). The act of a partner done to carry on, in the usual way, business of the kind carried on by the firm binds the firm. This authority is called his implied authority (section 19(1)). Section 19(2) lists acts that implied authority does not cover, unless a usage or custom of trade is to the contrary. The partners may extend or restrict implied authority by contract, but a restriction does not bind a third person who does not know of it or does not know or believe the partner to be a partner (section 20). Every partner is liable, jointly with all the others and also severally, for all acts of the firm done while he is a partner (section 25). Key points for the answer: sections 18 and 19, then 20 to 22 and 25.

## Meaning and Definition
Partner as agent and implied authority (sections 18 and 19)

Rule. A partner is the agent of the firm for the purposes of its business. An act of a partner done to carry on, in the usual way, business of the kind carried on by the firm binds the firm. This is his implied authority. Unless a usage or custom of trade is to the contrary, implied authority does not cover eight acts: submitting a dispute to arbitration; opening a bank account in his own name for the firm; compromising or relinquishing a claim; withdrawing a suit; admitting liability in a suit against the firm; acquiring immovable property for the firm; transferring the firm's immovable property; and entering into partnership on behalf of the firm.

Example. Rakesh is a partner in a firm that sells groceries. He buys rice from a wholesaler for the shop. This is an act done to carry on the firm's business in the usual way, so it binds the firm (section 19(1)). If he sold the firm's shop building, that is a transfer of immovable property, which implied authority does not cover (section 19(2)).

Extension and restriction (section 20)

Rule. The partners may by contract extend or restrict a partner's implied authority. But an act within his implied authority still binds the firm unless the person dealing with him knows of the restriction or does not know or believe him to be a partner.

Emergency (section 21)

Rule. A partner may do, in an emergency, all such acts to protect the firm from loss as a person of ordinary prudence would do in his own case in similar circumstances, and such acts bind the firm.

Mode of binding the firm (section 22)

Rule. To bind a firm, an act or instrument must be done or executed in the firm name, or in another manner expressing or implying an intention to bind the firm.

Admissions and notice (sections 23 and 24)

Rule. A partner's admission or representation about the affairs of the firm, made in the ordinary course of business, is evidence against the firm (section 23). Notice to a partner who habitually acts in the business operates as notice to the firm, except in a fraud on the firm committed by or with the consent of that partner (section 24).

Liability for acts of the firm (sections 25 to 27)

Rule. Every partner is liable jointly with all the others and also severally for all acts of the firm done while he is a partner (section 25). The firm is liable to the same extent as the partner for a third party's loss or injury, or a penalty, caused by a partner's wrongful act or omission in the ordinary course of business or with his partners' authority (section 26). The firm must make good money or property misapplied by a partner acting within apparent authority, or misapplied by any partner while in the firm's custody (section 27).

Example. A firm owes a supplier Rs 1,00,000 for goods bought while Sana and Tariq were partners. The supplier may claim the whole amount from Sana alone, from Tariq alone, or from both together (section 25).

Holding out (section 28)

Rule. A person who by words or conduct represents himself, or knowingly permits himself to be represented, as a partner in a firm is liable as a partner to anyone who has on the faith of the representation given credit to the firm. It does not matter whether he knows that the representation has reached the person giving credit. Continued use of the old firm name after a partner's death does not itself make his legal representative or estate liable for later acts (section 28(2)).

Rights of a transferee of a partner's interest (section 29)

Rule. A transferee of a partner's interest, absolute or by mortgage or charge, cannot interfere in the business, require accounts or inspect the books while the firm continues. He is entitled only to the transferring partner's share of profits. On dissolution, or when the transferring partner ceases to be a partner, he is entitled against the remaining partners to the share of the assets to which the transferring partner is entitled, and to an account from the date of the dissolution.

Does the partner's act bind the firm? How to read this: start at the top. 1. Was the act done to carry on, in the usual way, business of the kind the firm carries on (section 19(1))? If yes, it binds the firm, if done in the firm name or in a manner implying an intention to bind it (section 22). 2. Is it one of the eight acts in section 19(2), with no usage or custom of trade to the contrary? Then implied authority does not cover it. 3. Did the partners restrict his authority (section 20)? The act still binds the firm unless the third person knows of the restriction or does not know or believe him to be a partner. 4. Was it an emergency act to protect the firm from loss (section 21)? Such acts bind the firm.

## Key Points to Remember
- A partner is the agent of the firm (section 18). His acts in the usual course of business bind the firm: implied authority (section 19(1)). Eight acts are outside implied authority unless usage or custom says otherwise (section 19(2)). A restriction by the partners does not bind a third person who does not know of it or does not know or believe him to be a partner (section 20). Emergency acts bind the firm (section 21). Act must be in the firm name or imply an intention to bind (section 22). Partners are liable jointly and severally (section 25). The firm is liable for a partner's wrongful acts in the ordinary course (section 26). Holding out makes a person liable as a partner (section 28).
