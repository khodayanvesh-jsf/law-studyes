# 2. Formation of partnership
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5

## In One Minute
A partnership is formed by contract, not by status (section 5). The contract may fix the mutual rights and duties of the partners. It may be expressed, or implied by a course of dealing, and it may be varied by the consent of all the partners (section 11(1)). Where the partners make no provision for the duration or determination of the partnership, it is a partnership at will (section 7). A person may become a partner with another in particular adventures or undertakings (section 8). Key points for the answer: contract, express or implied, partnership at will and particular partnership.

## Meaning and Definition
Formation by contract (sections 3, 5 and 11)

Rule. A partnership is formed by contract and not by status (section 5). The general law of contract continues to apply unless the Partnership Act is inconsistent (section 3). The mutual rights and duties of the partners may be fixed by contract, which may be expressed or implied by a course of dealing, and may be varied by the consent of all the partners (section 11(1)).

Example. Girish and Hema start a tailoring business and agree orally to share the profits equally. For years Hema takes a fixed monthly sum and Girish agrees every time. The way they have dealt may imply a contract on their rights (section 11(1)). The Act does not require the agreement to be in writing.

Restraint on other business (section 11(2))

Rule. Despite section 27 of the Contract Act, the partners' contract may provide that a partner shall not carry on any business other than that of the firm while he is a partner.

Partnership at will (section 7)

Rule. Where the contract makes no provision for the duration or for the determination of the partnership, it is a partnership at will.

Example. Imran and Jaya agree to run a shop together and share profits. They say nothing about how long it will last or how it will end. It is a partnership at will.

Particular partnership (section 8)

Rule. A person may become a partner with another person in particular adventures or undertakings.

Example. Kamal and Lata agree to partner only to build one apartment block and share its profits. This is a partnership in a particular undertaking (section 8).

How is the partnership formed and what kind is it? How to read this: start at the top. 1. Is there a contract between the persons (section 5)? Without a contract there is no partnership. It may be expressed or implied from a course of dealing as to the partners' rights (section 11). 2. Does the contract provide for the duration or determination of the partnership? If not, it is a partnership at will (section 7). 3. Is it for particular adventures or undertakings? A person may become a partner in such adventures or undertakings (section 8).

## Key Points to Remember
- Partnership arises from contract, not status (section 5). The partners' mutual rights and duties may be fixed by contract, express or implied by a course of dealing, and varied by all (section 11(1)). Contract may bar a partner from other business (section 11(2)). No provision on duration or determination: partnership at will (section 7). A person may become a partner in particular adventures or undertakings (section 8).
