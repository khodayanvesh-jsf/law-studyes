# 4. Partnership and other associations
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5

## In One Minute
A partnership arises from contract and is the relation between persons who share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all (sections 4 and 5 of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932). It is different from a Hindu undivided family carrying on a family business, whose members are not partners in it (section 5), and from persons who merely share the income of property they hold jointly (Explanation 1 to section 6). A limited liability partnership (LLP) is a different thing. It is a body corporate and a legal entity separate from its partners (section 3 of the Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008). A company is also a body corporate, with perpetual succession, formed by subscribing names to a memorandum and registering under the Companies Act, 2013 (sections 3 and 9 of that Act).

## Meaning and Definition
Partnership and a Hindu undivided family (section 5)

Rule. Members of a Hindu undivided family carrying on a family business as such are not partners in it. Partnership arises from contract and not from status.

Partnership and co-ownership (Explanation 1 to section 6)

Rule. Persons who hold a joint or common interest in property and share its profits or gross returns are not, for that reason alone, partners.

Example. Three sisters inherit a shop building and share its rent. They are co-owners. Sharing the rent does not of itself make them partners. If they also agree to carry on a business together and share its profits, section 4 may apply.

Partnership and a company

Rule. A company is a body corporate from incorporation, with perpetual succession, and can contract, hold property, and sue and be sued by its own name (Companies Act, section 9). It is formed by subscribing names to a memorandum and registering, by seven or more persons for a public company, two or more for a private company, or one for a One Person Company (section 3(1)). A partnership under the Partnership Act arises from contract and is the relation between persons who share profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all (sections 4 and 5).

Partnership and an LLP

Rule. An LLP is a body corporate and a legal entity separate from its partners, with perpetual succession (LLP Act, section 3). It can sue and be sued in its own name and hold property (section 14). A partner is the agent of the LLP, not of the other partners (section 26), and is not personally liable for the LLP's obligations solely by being a partner (section 28). A partnership under the Partnership Act has the features set out in sections 4 and 5 of that Act: it arises from contract, and the business is carried on by all or any of the partners acting for all.

Example. If an LLP owes money to a supplier, the debt is the LLP's obligation, met out of its property (LLP Act, section 27(4)). A partner is not personally liable for it solely because he is a partner (section 28(1)).

Which kind of association? How to read this: start at the top. 1. Family members running a family business as such? Not partners in it (Partnership Act, section 5). 2. Persons who only share income from property they hold jointly? Not of itself partners (section 6, Explanation 1). 3. Contract to share profits of a business carried on by all or any acting for all? A partnership (section 4). 4. Incorporated under the LLP Act? An LLP: a body corporate, separate from its partners (LLP Act, section 3).

## Key Points to Remember
- Partnership arises from contract (section 5). Hindu undivided family members running the family business are not partners. Co-owners sharing income of joint property are not of themselves partners (section 6, Explanation 1). An LLP is a body corporate and a separate legal entity (LLP Act, section 3). The Partnership Act does not apply to it (LLP Act, section 4). A partner of an LLP is the agent of the LLP, not of other partners (LLP Act, section 26).
