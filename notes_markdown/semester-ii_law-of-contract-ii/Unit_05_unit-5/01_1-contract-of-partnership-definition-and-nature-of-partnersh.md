# 1. Contract of Partnership - Definition and nature of partnership
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5

## In One Minute
Section 4 of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932 defines "partnership" as the relation between persons who have agreed to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all. The persons are individually "partners", together "a firm", and the name under which they carry on business is the "firm name". The relation arises from contract and not from status (section 5). The Indian Contract Act, 1872 continues to apply to firms where it is not inconsistent with the Act (section 3). Key points for the answer: the elements of the definition and the rule in section 5.

## Meaning and Definition
The definition (section 4)

Rule. A partnership has these elements, each drawn from the words of section 4.

It is a relation between persons who have agreed. There must be an agreement between persons. Section 4 says "persons" and states no number. The agreement is to share the profits of a business. The business is carried on by all or any of them acting for all.

"Business" includes every trade, occupation and profession (section 2(b)).

Example. Anu and Bala agree to run a bakery together and to share the profits equally. Either may act for both in running it. This fits the definition in section 4.

Partner, firm and firm name (section 4)

Rule. Persons who have entered into partnership are individually "partners" and collectively "a firm". The name under which their business is carried on is the "firm name".

Partnership arises from contract, not status (section 5)

Rule. The relation of partnership arises from contract and not from status. Members of a Hindu undivided family carrying on a family business as such, and a Burmese Buddhist husband and wife carrying on business as such, are not partners in that business.

Example. Brothers who are members of a Hindu undivided family run the family shop as such. They are not partners merely because of the family relationship (section 5).

Definitions used in the Act (section 2) and application of the Contract Act (section 3)

Rule. "Act of a firm", "business", "prescribed" and "third party" are defined in section 2. Words used but not defined in this Act and defined in the Contract Act have the meaning given there (section 2(e)). The unrepealed provisions of the Contract Act continue to apply to firms, so far as they are not inconsistent with the Act (section 3).

Is there a partnership? How to read this: start at the top. 1. Have persons agreed (a contract, not status: section 5)? If not, no partnership. 2. Is the agreement to share the profits of a business (section 2(b) and section 4)? If not, no partnership. 3. Is the business carried on by all or any of them acting for all? If yes, it is a partnership under section 4. The persons are partners and the group is a firm.

## Key Points to Remember
- Partnership: relation between persons who have agreed to share profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all (section 4). Persons are partners, collectively a firm, and the business name is the firm name (section 4). Business includes every trade, occupation and profession (section 2(b)). Partnership arises from contract, not status. A Hindu undivided family running a family business is not a partnership (section 5). The Contract Act applies unless inconsistent (section 3).
