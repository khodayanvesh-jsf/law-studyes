# 3. Test of partnership
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/test-of-partnership/

## In One Minute
Section 6 of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932 gives the test. In deciding whether a group of persons is or is not a firm, or whether a person is or is not a partner in a firm, regard shall be had to the real relation between the parties, as shown by all relevant facts taken together. The section adds two explanations. Sharing profits or gross returns from property by persons with a joint or common interest in the property does not of itself make them partners. Receiving a share of the profits of a business, or a payment varying with profits, does not of itself make a person a partner, and four cases are named. Key points for the answer: the real relation, all the facts together, and the explanations.

## Meaning and Definition
The test: the real relation (section 6)

Rule. To decide whether a group is a firm, or whether a person is a partner, look at the real relation between the parties, as shown by all relevant facts taken together.

Example. Two persons sign a paper calling themselves "partners", but one only works for a fixed wage and has no say in the business. Section 6 asks for the real relation shown by all the facts, not the name used in the paper.

Joint or common property (Explanation 1)

Rule. Persons holding a joint or common interest in property who share the profits or gross returns from it are not, for that reason alone, partners.

Example. Three heirs own a house together and share its rent. Sharing the rent does not of itself make them partners.

Profit-linked receipts (Explanation 2)

Rule. Receiving a share of profits, or a payment that depends on or varies with profits, does not of itself make a person a partner. This is said in particular of four receivers.

A lender of money to persons engaged or about to engage in a business. A servant or agent, as remuneration. The widow or child of a deceased partner, as annuity. A previous owner or part owner, as consideration for the sale of the goodwill or share.

Example. Noor lends money to a shop and is repaid with a share of the shop's profits. This receipt alone does not make her a partner (Explanation 2(a)). Other facts may still show a partnership.

Is X a partner? How to read this: start at the top. 1. Look at all the relevant facts together (section 6). The real relation between the parties decides, not the label. 2. Does X only share profits or gross returns from property in which he holds a joint or common interest (Explanation 1)? That alone does not make him a partner. 3. Does X only receive a share of profits or a profit-linked payment, for example as lender, servant or agent, widow or child of a deceased partner, or previous owner (Explanation 2)? That alone does not make him a partner. Other facts may.

## Key Points to Remember
- Test: the real relation between the parties, shown by all relevant facts taken together (section 6). Sharing profits or gross returns from jointly held property does not of itself make partners (Explanation 1). Receipt of a share of profits does not of itself make a partner (Explanation 2). Four named cases: lender, servant or agent, widow or child of a deceased partner, previous owner or part owner on sale of goodwill.
