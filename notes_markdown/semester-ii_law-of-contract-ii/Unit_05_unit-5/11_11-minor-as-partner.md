# 11. Minor as partner
**Subject:** Law of Contracts – II (Semester II) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-ii/minor-as-partner/

## In One Minute
Under section 30 of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932, a person who is a minor according to the law to which he is subject may not be a partner in a firm. With the consent of all the partners for the time being, he may be admitted to the benefits of partnership (section 30(1)). His share is liable for the acts of the firm, but he is not personally liable (section 30(3)). Within six months of attaining majority, or of learning he had been admitted, whichever is later, he may give public notice that he has elected to become or not to become a partner. If he gives no notice, he becomes a partner when the six months expire (section 30(5)).

## Meaning and Definition
Admission to the benefits (section 30(1) and (2))

Rule. A minor may not be a partner. With the consent of all the partners for the time being, he may be admitted to the benefits of partnership. He has a right to the share of property and profits that is agreed, and may have access to, inspect and copy the firm's accounts.

Example. Vimal, aged 16, is admitted by all the partners of a firm to the benefits of the partnership, with a 10 per cent share of profits. He is not a partner. He is entitled to the agreed share and may inspect the accounts (section 30(1) and (2)).

Liability while a minor (section 30(3))

Rule. The minor's share is liable for the acts of the firm. He is not personally liable for any such act.

No suit for accounts, except on severing (section 30(4))

Rule. The minor may not sue the partners for an account or payment of his share, except when severing his connection with the firm. His share is then valued, as far as possible, by the rules in section 48. The partners acting together, or any partner entitled to dissolve the firm on notice, may elect in the suit to dissolve the firm. The Court then proceeds as in a suit for dissolution and settlement of accounts.

Election on attaining majority (section 30(5) and (6))

Rule. Within six months of attaining majority, or of learning that he had been admitted, whichever is later, the person may give public notice that he has elected to become, or not to become, a partner. If he gives no notice, he becomes a partner on expiry of the six months. A person who asserts that he did not know of the admission until a date after those six months must prove it.

If he becomes a partner (section 30(7))

Rule. His rights and liabilities as a minor continue up to the date he becomes a partner. He also becomes personally liable to third parties for all acts of the firm done since he was admitted to the benefits. His share in the property and profits is the share he had as a minor.

If he elects not to become a partner (section 30(8))

Rule. His rights and liabilities remain those of a minor up to the date of public notice. His share is not liable for acts of the firm done after the notice. He may sue the partners for his share of the property and profits as under sub-section (4). Section 28 (holding out) is not affected (section 30(9)).

The minor's position over time How to read this: start at the top. 1. Admitted to the benefits with the consent of all the partners (section 30(1)). Not a partner. Share liable for the firm's acts, but no personal liability (section 30(3)). 2. Within six months of majority or of knowledge, whichever is later: public notice. Elects to become a partner, or not (section 30(5)). 3. No notice given. He becomes a partner on expiry of the six months (section 30(5) proviso). 4. Becomes a partner / elects not to. Personally liable for acts since admission (section 30(7)) / share not liable for acts after the notice (section 30(8)).

## Key Points to Remember
- A minor may not be a partner but may be admitted to the benefits with all the partners' consent (section 30(1)). His share is liable for the firm's acts, but he is not personally liable (section 30(3)). Six months from majority or knowledge, whichever is later, to elect by public notice (section 30(5)). No notice: he becomes a partner on expiry of six months. If he becomes a partner: personally liable for acts since admission (section 30(7)). If he elects not to: his share is not liable for later acts (section 30(8)).
