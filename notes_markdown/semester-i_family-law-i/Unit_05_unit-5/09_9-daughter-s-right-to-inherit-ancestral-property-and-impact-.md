# 9. Daughter’s right to inherit ancestral property and impact of recent changes in law
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/daughters-right-to-inherit-ancestral-property/

## In One Minute
The rule. Since 9 September 2005, Section 6 of the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 makes the daughter of a coparcener a coparcener by birth. She has the same rights and liabilities as a son. Father need not be alive. In Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma (2020), a three-Judge Bench held that her father need not have been alive on that date. Saved partitions. The right does not reopen a partition made before 20 December 2004 by a registered deed or a court decree. An oral partition is accepted only in exceptional cases. The cases. Prakash v. Phulavati (2015) had required a living father; Danamma (2018) gave daughters a share though the father died in 2001. Vineeta Sharma settled the conflict.

## Legal Provision
- Section 6(1) (as substituted by Act 39 of 2005). Devolution of interest in coparcenary property. ―On and from the commencement of the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005, in a Joint Hindu family governed by the Mitakshara law, the daughter of a coparcener shall,―(a) by birth become a coparcener in her own right in the same manner as the son; (b) have the same rights in the coparcenary property as she would have had if she had been a son; (c) be subject to the same liabilities in respect of the said coparcenary property as that of a son, and any reference to a Hindu Mitakshara coparcener shall be deemed to include a reference to a daughter of a coparcener: Provided that nothing contained in this sub-section shall affect or invalidate any disposition or alienation including any partition or testamentary disposition of property which had taken place before the 20th day of December, 2004.
- Section 6(5). ―Nothing contained in this section shall apply to a partition, which has been effected before the 20th day of December, 2004. Explanation.―For the purposes of this section "partition" means any partition made by execution of a deed of partition duly registered under the Registration Act, 1908 (16 of 1908) or partition effected by a decree of a court.
- Other provisions used in this note:
- Section 6(1): from the commencement of the 2005 Amendment, in a Joint Hindu family governed by the Mitakshara law, the daughter of a coparcener becomes a coparcener by birth in her own right, in the same manner as the son. She has the same rights and the same liabilities. Any reference to a Mitakshara coparcener includes the daughter of a coparcener. Proviso to Section 6(1): it saves any disposition, alienation, partition or testamentary disposition made before 20 December 2004. Section 6(2): property she gets under Section 6(1) is held with the incidents of coparcenary ownership. She can dispose of it by will . Section 6(3): on a Hindu's death after the Amendment, his interest devolves by will or intestate succession and not by survivorship . A partition is deemed to have taken place, and the daughter gets the same share as a son. Section 6(4): after the Amendment, no court recognises a right to proceed against a son, grandson or great-grandson for his father's, grandfather's or great-grandfather's debt solely on the ground of pious obligation . The proviso keeps the creditor's right, and any alienation made for such a debt, for debts contracted before the Amendment. The Explanation limits "son", "grandson" and "great-grandson" in that proviso to those born or adopted before the Amendment. Section 6(5): the section does not apply to a partition effected before 20 December 2004. "Partition" means a partition by a registered deed or by a court decree. Explanation to Section 30: the interest of a male Hindu in Mitakshara coparcenary property is property he can dispose of by will. The 2005 Amendment made Section 30 read "disposed of by him or by her". State laws: the Act text prints Karnataka Sections 6A to 6C and Tamil Nadu Sections 29-A to 29-C. Both give daughters coparcenary rights from their own dates. Karnataka Section 6A(d) denies the clause (b) share on partition to a daughter married before the Hindu Succession (Karnataka Amendment) Act, 1990. Tamil Nadu Section 29-A(iv) excludes a daughter married before the Tamil Nadu Act. Both State Acts save partitions made before their commencement. The Maharashtra Act, 1994 and the Kerala Act, 1975 are described below from their own texts.

## Meaning and Definition
Ancestral property here means Mitakshara coparcenary property held by a joint Hindu family , which gives its members a right from birth. A coparcener has a birthright interest in it. Before 2005, only sons were coparceners by birth. Now a daughter is one too, with the same rights and liabilities, and no rule in Section 6 excludes a married daughter.

Coparcener by birth. Her right comes from birth, not from anyone's death. This is the old idea of unobstructed heritage , now applied to daughters ( Vineeta Sharma , paras 55, 63). Right to partition. A coparcener is one who can claim partition. The daughter can claim partition from 9 September 2005 (para 79). Retroactive. Section 6 is a retroactive statute : it operates from 9 September 2005, but on the basis of an earlier event, her birth (paras 55 to 57).

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 6(1): the daughter of a coparcener is a coparcener by birth, with the same rights and liabilities as a son. Vineeta Sharma (2020): the father need not be alive on 9 September 2005. The amendment is retroactive, not retrospective (paras 55-56). Saved partitions: registered deed or court decree before 20 December 2004. Oral partition only in exceptional cases. Prakash and Mangammal are overruled; Danamma is partly overruled. Preliminary decree is not a partition; daughters claim a share in pending final decree proceedings and appeals (para 129(iv)). Section 6 makes no distinction between married and unmarried daughters. Pending matters to be decided within six months, as far as possible (para 130).
