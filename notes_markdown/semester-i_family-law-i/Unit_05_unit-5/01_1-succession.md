# 1. Succession
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/succession/

## In One Minute
What it is. Succession is the passing of a dead person's property to other persons. Two ways. The owner may leave a will ; that is testamentary succession . If there is no valid will for a property, it passes by intestate succession . The Act. The Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (HSA) is a code of intestate succession among Hindus. It also keeps the right of every Hindu to make a will (Section 30). Extent. The Act extends to the whole of India (Section 1(2)). Scope of this note. The idea of succession and the basic scheme of the Act. The rules for intestate succession, heirs and wills are in their own notes.

## Legal Provision
- Section 30. Testamentary succession. ―Any Hindu may dispose of by will or other testamentary disposition any property, which is capable of being so disposed of by him or by her, in accordance with the provisions of the Indian Succession Act, 1925 (39 of 1925), or any other law for the time being in force and applicable to Hindus. Explanation.―The interest of a male Hindu in a Mitakshara coparcenary property or the interest of a member of a tarwad, tavazhi, illom, kutumba or kavaru in the property of the tarwad, tavazhi, illom, kutumba or kavaru shall, notwithstanding anything contained in this Act or in any other law for the time being in force, be deemed to be property capable of being disposed of by him or by her within the meaning of this section.
- Section 4(1). Overriding effect of Act. ―Save as otherwise expressly provided in this Act,― (a) any text, rule or interpretation of Hindu law or any custom or usage as part of that law in force immediately before the commencement of this Act shall cease to have effect with respect to any matter for which provision is made in this Act; (b) any other law in force immediately before the commencement of this Act shall cease to apply to Hindus in so far as it is inconsistent with any of the provisions contained in this Act.
- Other provisions used in this note:
- Section 1: short title, and extent to the whole of India. Section 2: application to Hindus by religion in any form, Buddhists, Jainas, Sikhs and the residuary group. Section 2(2) excludes Scheduled Tribes unless notified. Section 3: definitions, including "heir", "intestate" and "related". Section 3(2) says that, unless the context otherwise requires, words in the masculine gender do not include females. Section 5: properties to which the Act does not apply. Sections 6 and 7: devolution of a Mitakshara coparcener 's interest and of an interest in a tarwad, tavazhi, illom, kutumba or kavaru.

## Meaning and Definition
Section 3(1) defines the key words.

"Heir" (clause (f)): any person, male or female, who is entitled to succeed to the property of an intestate under this Act. "Intestate" (clause (g)): a person is deemed to die intestate in respect of property of which he or she has not made a testamentary disposition capable of taking effect. "Related" (clause (j)): related by legitimate kinship. Illegitimate children are deemed to be related to their mother and to one another, and their legitimate descendants are deemed to be related to them and to one another.

Worked example. A Hindu owner has two properties: a house worth ₹60 lakh and a deposit of ₹15 lakh. Her valid will gives the house to her niece and says nothing about the deposit. The house passes by testamentary succession (Section 30). For the deposit she is "intestate" (Section 3(1)(g)), so the ₹15 lakh passes to her heirs under the Act.

## Key Points to Remember
- Succession is the passing of a dead person's property: by will or without a will. The Act extends to the whole of India (Section 1) and applies to Hindus in the wide sense of Section 2. Section 4: the Act overrides old texts, custom and inconsistent laws. Section 5: three kinds of property are outside the Act. Section 30: a Hindu may dispose of property by will, "by him or by her" since the 2005 Amendment. Section 3: agnate, cognate, full blood, half blood, uterine blood, heir, intestate and related are defined for the whole Act. Section 6(3): after 2005 a coparcener's interest passes by will or succession, not by survivorship.
