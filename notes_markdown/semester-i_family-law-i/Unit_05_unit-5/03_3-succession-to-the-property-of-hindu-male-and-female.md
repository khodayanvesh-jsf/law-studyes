# 3. Succession to the property of Hindu Male and Female
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/succession-to-the-property-of-hindu-male-and-female/

## In One Minute
Two schemes. The Act has two separate schemes of intestate succession: one for a Hindu male and one for a Hindu female. Male. His property goes to four groups in order: class I heirs , class II heirs , agnates and cognates (Sections 8 to 13). Class I first. Class I heirs take together and shut out everyone else (Section 9). Section 10 gives their shares. Female. Her property goes to five groups in order, and the source of the property can change who takes it (Sections 15 and 16). Her ownership. Section 14 makes a female Hindu full owner of property she possesses, subject to Section 14(2). Special communities. Section 17 gives special rules for persons who followed marumakkattayam or aliyasantana law.

## Legal Provision
- Section 8. General rules of succession in the case of males. ―The property of a male Hindu dying intestate shall devolve according to the provisions of this Chapter:― (a) firstly, upon the heirs, being the relatives specified in class I of the Schedule; (b) secondly, if there is no heir of class I, then upon the heirs, being the relatives specified in class II of the Schedule; (c) thirdly, if there is no heir of any of the two classes, then upon the agnates of the deceased; and (d) lastly, if there is no agnate, then upon the cognates of the deceased.
- Section 15(1). General rules of succession in the case of female Hindus. ―The property of a female Hindu dying intestate shall devolve according to the rules set out in section 16,― (a) firstly, upon the sons and daughters (including the children of any pre-deceased son or daughter) and the husband; (b) secondly, upon the heirs of the husband; (c) thirdly, upon the mother and father; (d) fourthly, upon the heirs of the father; and (e) lastly, upon the heirs of the mother.
- Other provisions used in this note:
- Section 9: class I heirs take simultaneously and exclude all others; class II entries are preferred in order. Section 10: four rules on shares among class I heirs. Section 11: heirs in one class II entry share equally. Section 12: three rules of preference among agnates and cognates. Section 13: how degrees are counted. Section 14: property possessed by a female Hindu is held as full owner, subject to Section 14(2). Section 15(2): two exceptions by source of property. Section 16: order and manner of distribution among her heirs. Section 17: changes for marumakkattayam and aliyasantana law. The Schedule: the sixteen class I heirs and the nine class II entries.

## Meaning and Definition
An agnate is a person related to another by blood or adoption wholly through males. A cognate is related by blood or adoption but not wholly through males (Section 3(1)(a) and (c)). Class I and class II heirs are listed in the Schedule. The Schedule is explained in more detail in the note on Classes of heirs.

## Key Points to Remember
- Male: class I, class II, agnates, cognates (Section 8). Class I heirs take together and exclude all others (Section 9). Section 10: widow one share; each son, daughter and the mother one share; branches of pre-deceased children one share each. Female: five stages (Section 15). The source of property can send it back to the father's or husband's heirs (Section 15(2)). Degrees are counted inclusive of the intestate (Section 13). The Schedule has sixteen class I heirs and nine class II entries. Uterine brothers and sisters are excluded from "brother" and "sister". Before 1956, the 1929 Act added the son's daughter, daughter's daughter, sister and sister's son as Mitakshara heirs, after the father's father and before the father's brother. Section 14(1): a female Hindu holds property possessed by her as full owner. Section 14(2) saves restricted estates created by a gift, will, instrument, decree, order or award.
