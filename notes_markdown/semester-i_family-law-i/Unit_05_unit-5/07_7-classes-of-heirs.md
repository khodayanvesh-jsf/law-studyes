# 7. Classes of heirs
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/classes-of-heirs/

## In One Minute
Two classes in the Schedule. The Schedule to the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 lists the heirs of a male Hindu in two classes. Class I has sixteen heirs, such as the son, daughter, widow and mother. Class II has nine entries, beginning with the father. Order. Class I heirs take together and shut out everyone else. Class II comes next, entry by entry. After class II come agnates and then cognates (Section 8). Sharing. Class I heirs share by the four rules of Section 10. Heirs in one class II entry share equally (Section 11). Agnates and cognates are chosen by degrees (Sections 12 and 13).

## Legal Provision
- Section 9. Order of succession among heirs in the Schedule. ―Among the heirs specified in the Schedule, those in class I shall take simultaneously and to the exclusion of all other heirs; those in the first entry in class II shall be preferred to those in the second entry; those in the second entry shall be preferred to those in the third entry; and so on in succession.
- Section 12. Order of succession among agnates and cognates. ―The order of succession among agnates or cognates, as the case may be, shall be determined in accordance with the rules of preference laid down hereunder:― Rule 1.―Of two heirs, the one who has fewer or no degrees of ascent is preferred. Rule 2.―Where the number of degrees of ascent is the same or none, that heir is preferred who has fewer or no degrees of descent. Rule 3.―Where neither heir is entitled to be preferred to the other under Rule 1 or Rule 2 they take simultaneously.
- Section 13. Computation of degrees. ―(1) For the purposes of determining the order of succession among agnates or cognates, relationship shall be reckoned from the intestate to the heir in terms of degrees of ascent or degrees of descent or both, as the case may be. (2) Degrees of ascent and degrees of descent shall be computed inclusive of the intestate. (3) Every generation constitutes a degree either ascending or descending.
- Other provisions used in this note:
- Section 8: property of a male Hindu goes firstly to class I of the Schedule, secondly to class II, thirdly to agnates, lastly to cognates. Section 10: shares among class I heirs (Rules 1 to 4). Section 11: heirs in one class II entry share equally. Section 3(1)(a) and (c): definitions of agnate and cognate . Section 3(1)(e): full blood, half blood and uterine blood. Section 3(1)(f), (g) and (j): "heir" is any person, male or female, entitled to succeed to the property of an intestate under the Act. A person dies "intestate" in respect of property not disposed of by a will capable of taking effect. "Related" means related by legitimate kinship. Illegitimate children are related to their mother and to one another, and their legitimate descendants are related to them. Section 2: the Act applies to Hindus in any form, including Virashaivas, Lingayats and followers of the Brahmo, Prarthana or Arya Samaj, to Buddhists, Jainas and Sikhs, and to others who are not Muslims, Christians, Parsis or Jews unless shown not to be governed by Hindu law. It does not apply to members of a Scheduled Tribe unless the Central Government notifies otherwise (Section 2(2)). Section 4: any text, rule or interpretation of Hindu law, or custom, in force before the Act ceases to have effect on a matter the Act provides for. The Schedule: the lists of class I and class II heirs, with the Explanation on uterine brothers and sisters.

## Meaning and Definition
Class I heirs are the closest relatives: the deceased's children, widow, mother and certain later generations. Class II heirs are the next group, such as the father, brothers, sisters and some grandchildren. An agnate is related wholly through males. A cognate is related, but not wholly through males.

Heir. Any person, male or female, entitled to succeed to the property of an intestate under the Act (Section 3(1)(f)). Class. A list in the Schedule. Class I is one group; class II is nine groups called entries. Agnate and cognate. Relatives outside the Schedule, used only when the Schedule has no heir (Section 8).

## Key Points to Remember
- Class I has sixteen heirs, four of them added in 2005. Class II has nine entries. Class I heirs take together and shut out all others (Section 9). The mother is in class I; the father is in class II, Entry I. Class II: an earlier entry beats a later entry; one entry shares equally (Sections 9 and 11). Brother or sister in the Schedule does not include uterine brother or sister. Section 10: widow(s) one share; each son, daughter and the mother one share; each pre-deceased child's branch one share. Agnates before cognates; fewer degrees of ascent, then fewer degrees of descent (Sections 12, 13). Chander Sen : a son inheriting under Section 8 takes individually, not as karta.
