# 4. Dwelling House
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/dwelling-house/

## In One Minute
The old rule. Section 23 of the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 once gave a special rule for a dwelling house wholly occupied by members of the intestate's family. A female class I heir could not ask for partition of it until the male class I heirs chose to divide their shares. Right of residence. Old Section 23 also gave the female heir a right of residence in that house. A daughter had that right only if she was unmarried, deserted by or separated from her husband, or a widow. Omitted. The Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 omitted Section 23 with effect from 9 September 2005. The restriction and the right of residence are no longer in the Act. Why. Parliament omitted it "so as to remove the disability on female heirs contained in that section" (Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma, para 47).

## Legal Provision
- Section 23 as originally enacted. Special provision respecting dwelling-houses. ―Where a Hindu intestate has left surviving him or her both male and female heirs specified in class I of the Schedule and his or her property includes a dwelling-house wholly occupied by members of his or her family, then, notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, the right of any such female heir to claim partition of the dwelling-house shall not arise until the male heirs choose to divide their respective shares therein; but the female heir shall be entitled to a right of residence therein: Provided that where such female heir is a daughter, she shall be entitled to a right of residence in the dwelling-house only if she is unmarried or has been deserted by or has separated from her husband or is a widow.
- Section 23 now. [Special provision respecting dwelling-houses.]―Omitted by the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 (39 of 2005), s. 4 (w.e.f. 9-9-2005).
- Other provisions used in this note:
- Section 17(iv): for persons who would have been governed by marumakkattayam or aliyasantana law, Section 23 was to be treated as omitted even before 2005. Section 22: preferential right of class I heirs to buy a co-heir's interest; a female heir could transfer her interest in the house subject to it. Section 24 (omitted): removed by Section 5 of the same Amendment. As originally enacted, the widow of a pre-deceased son, of a pre-deceased son of a pre-deceased son, or of a brother did not inherit as such widow if she had remarried when the succession opened (Mulla, 14th ed., Section 24).

## Meaning and Definition
The Act's own text now keeps only the note that Section 23 was omitted. The original words are reproduced in Mulla, Principles of Hindu Law (14th ed.), Section 23. The Statement of Objects and Reasons of the 2005 Bill, as recorded by the Supreme Court in Vineeta Sharma (2020, para 47), describes the old section in one line: it " disentitles a female heir to ask for partition in respect of a dwelling house wholly occupied by a joint family until the male heirs choose to divide their respective shares therein ".

Dwelling-house (Mulla). The house in which the intestate and the members of the family lived as their permanent residence, and which the family continued to occupy on the death. Occupation does not mean actual or continuous residence, nor that all members must reside there. Wholly occupied. The whole house must be occupied by members of the family. A house only partly occupied, or partly or substantially let to tenants, is not covered (Mulla).

## Key Points to Remember
- Old Section 23 disentitled a female heir to ask for partition of a dwelling house wholly occupied by a joint family. It applied only where the intestate left both male and female class I heirs. The bar lasted until the male heirs chose to divide their respective shares. Old Section 23 also gave a female heir a right of residence. A daughter had it only if unmarried, deserted, separated from her husband or a widow. Parliament omitted it to remove the disability on female heirs ( Vineeta Sharma , para 47). Section 23 was omitted with effect from 9 September 2005 by Section 4 of Act 39 of 2005; Section 24 by Section 5.
