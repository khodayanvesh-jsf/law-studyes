# 5. The Hindu Succession Act, 1956 as amended by the Hindu Succession (Andhra Pradesh Amendment) Act, 1986 & the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/hindu-succession-act-1956-as-amended/

## In One Minute
Two amendments on daughters. The Hindu Succession Act, 1956 has been amended twice on the point of daughters. The Hindu Succession (Andhra Pradesh Amendment) Act, 1986 added Sections 29A to 29C for Andhra Pradesh, deemed in force from 5 September 1985. It made the daughter of a coparcener a coparcener by birth, twenty years before the central law. The central law. The Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 (Act 39 of 2005) did the same for the whole of India from 9 September 2005 by substituting a new Section 6. It also omitted Sections 4(2), 23 and 24, amended Section 30 and added four class I heirs. The settled meaning. Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma (2020) held that a daughter is a coparcener by birth, whether born before or after 2005, and that her father need not have been alive on 9 September 2005. What is saved. Dispositions, alienations, partitions and wills before 20 December 2004 are saved (Section 6(1), proviso ). Only a partition by registered deed or court decree counts (Section 6(5), Explanation).

## Legal Provision
- Section 29-A (Andhra Pradesh, Act 13 of 1986). Equal rights to daughter in coparcenary property. ―Notwithstanding anything contained in section 6 of this Act―(i) in a Joint Hindu Family governed by Mitakshara Law, the daughter of a coparcener shall by birth become a coparcener in her own right in the same manner as the son and have the same rights in the coparcenary property as she would have had if she had been a son, inclusive of the right to claim by survivorship; and shall be subject to the same liabilities and disabilities in respect thereto as the son; … (iv) Nothing in clause (ii) shall apply to a daughter married prior to or to a partition which had been effected before the commencement of the Hindu Succession (Andhra Pradesh Amendment) Act, 1986.
- Section 6(1) (as substituted by Act 39 of 2005). Devolution of interest in coparcenary property. ―On and from the commencement of the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005, in a Joint Hindu family governed by the Mitakshara law, the daughter of a coparcener shall,―(a) by birth become a coparcener in her own right in the same manner as the son; (b) have the same rights in the coparcenary property as she would have had if she had been a son; (c) be subject to the same liabilities in respect of the said coparcenary property as that of a son, and any reference to a Hindu Mitakshara coparcener shall be deemed to include a reference to a daughter of a coparcener: Provided that nothing contained in this sub-section shall affect or invalidate any disposition or alienation including any partition or testamentary disposition of property which had taken place before the 20th day of December, 2004.
- Section 6(5). ―Nothing contained in this section shall apply to a partition, which has been effected before the 20th day of December, 2004. Explanation.―For the purposes of this section "partition" means any partition made by execution of a deed of partition duly registered under the Registration Act, 1908 (16 of 1908) or partition effected by a decree of a court.
- The amending provisions used in this note:
- Andhra Pradesh Act 13 of 1986: inserted Chapter II-A (Sections 29A, 29B and 29C) after Chapter II, for Andhra Pradesh, deemed in force from 5 September 1985 (Section 1(3); Vineeta Sharma , para 52). Hindu Succession (Maharashtra Amendment) Act, 1994 (Maharashtra Act 40 of 1994): inserted a new Chapter I-A (Sections 29-A, 29-B and 29-C) after Section 29, for Maharashtra, deemed in force from 22 June 1994. Kerala Joint Hindu Family System (Abolition) Act, 1975: a separate State Act that abolishes the joint family system among Hindus in Kerala. Its text does not amend the 1956 Act. Act 39 of 2005, Section 2: omitted Section 4(2). Act 39 of 2005, Section 3: substituted a new Section 6. Act 39 of 2005, Sections 4 and 5: omitted Sections 23 and 24. Act 39 of 2005, Section 6: in Section 30, "disposed of by him" became "disposed of by him or by her". Act 39 of 2005, Section 7: added four more class I heirs to the Schedule.

## Meaning and Definition
Under the old Mitakshara rule, only sons, grandsons and great-grandsons were coparceners by birth. A daughter was not a coparcener. The two amendments changed that. Section 6 now makes the daughter of a coparcener a coparcener by birth, in her own right and in the same manner as a son.

"Daughter of a coparcener". Section 6(1) gives the status to the daughter of a coparcener in a Joint Hindu family governed by the Mitakshara law. Any reference to a Hindu Mitakshara coparcener in law now includes her. "By birth". The right comes from birth, not from inheritance. So it does not depend on the father dying after 2005 ( Vineeta Sharma , para 129(iii)). " Partition " for Section 6. Only a partition by a registered deed of partition or by a court decree (Section 6(5), Explanation).

## Key Points to Remember
- Andhra Pradesh Amendment, 1986 (Act 13 of 1986): Sections 29A to 29C, deemed in force from 5 September 1985. Other State laws before 2005: Tamil Nadu (1989), Karnataka Section 6A (1994), Maharashtra Act 40 of 1994 (Chapter I-A, Sections 29-A to 29-C, deemed in force from 22 June 1994), Kerala Act (1975, abolishes the joint family system). Act 39 of 2005: new Section 6 from 9 September 2005; Sections 4(2), 23 and 24 omitted; Section 30 amended; four class I heirs added. Cut-off for saved partitions: 20 December 2004, by registered deed or court decree (Section 6(5)). Section 6(4) ends the pious obligation for later debts. Section 6(3): on a coparcener's death after the Amendment, a deemed partition gives the daughter the same share as a son. Vineeta Sharma (2020): the daughter is a coparcener by birth; the father need not be alive.
