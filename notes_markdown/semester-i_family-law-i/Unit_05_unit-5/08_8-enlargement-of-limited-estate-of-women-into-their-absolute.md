# 8. Enlargement of limited estate of women into their absolute estate
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/enlargement-of-limited-estate-of-women/

## In One Minute
The old law. Under the old law, a Hindu woman who inherited property usually held only a limited estate . On her death it went to the reversioners , not to her own heirs. Section 14(1). The Hindu Succession Act, 1956 changed this. Any property possessed by a female Hindu, acquired before or after the Act, is held by her as full owner and not as a limited owner. Section 14(2). It is a narrow exception for grants that themselves prescribe a restricted estate . Tulasamma. In V. Tulasamma v. Sesha Reddy (1977), the Supreme Court held that the exception does not apply where the grant only gives effect to a right she already had , such as maintenance. Now. In Tej Bhan v. Ram Kishan (2024), the Supreme Court referred the interplay of Sections 14(1) and 14(2) to a larger bench.

## Legal Provision
- Section 14. Property of a female Hindu to be her absolute property. ―(1) Any property possessed by a female Hindu, whether acquired before or after the commencement of this Act, shall be held by her as full owner thereof and not as a limited owner. Explanation.―In this sub-section, "property" includes both movable and immovable property acquired by a female Hindu by inheritance or devise, or at a partition, or in lieu of maintenance or arrears of maintenance, or by gift from any person, whether a relative or not, before, at or after her marriage, or by her own skill or exertion, or by purchase or by prescription, or in any other manner whatsoever, and also any such property held by her as stridhana immediately before the commencement of this Act. (2) Nothing contained in sub-section (1) shall apply to any property acquired by way of gift or under a will or any other instrument or under a decree or order of a civil court or under an award where the terms of the gift, will or other instrument or the decree, order or award prescribe a restricted estate in such property.
- Other provisions used in this note:
- Section 15(1): when a female Hindu dies intestate, her property goes firstly to her sons and daughters (including the children of a pre-deceased son or daughter) and her husband, secondly to the husband's heirs, thirdly to her mother and father, fourthly to the father's heirs, and lastly to the mother's heirs. Section 15(2): property she inherited from her father or mother goes, if she leaves no child (or child of a pre-deceased child), to the father's heirs. Property she inherited from her husband or father-in-law goes in the same case to the husband's heirs. Kerala (Act 17 of 2016) adds property inherited from a pre-deceased son, which goes to that son's heirs. Section 30: any Hindu may dispose of property by will under the Indian Succession Act, 1925. Section 14(2) refers to a "will" as one of the instruments that may prescribe a restricted estate . Section 31 (repeal): the Hindu Women's Rights to Property Act, 1937 is repealed , without affecting rights already acquired (Mulla, 14th ed.).

## Meaning and Definition
A "limited estate" is an interest that a Hindu woman held for her own life, without full power to dispose of it. Section 14(1) turns such an interest into full ownership by force of the statute. No court order or new document is needed. Stridhan she already held is also covered by the Explanation.

Limited owner. She enjoys the whole income but cannot alienate the corpus except for legal necessity , benefit of the estate or with the reversioners' consent (Mulla, 14th ed., § 176). Full owner. She can sell, give or will the property, and on her death it goes to her own heirs under Section 15. Reversioner. The heir of the last full owner who would take on the limited heir's death. His interest is a mere chance of succession (spes successionis), not a vested interest (Mulla, § 175).

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 14(1): property possessed by a Hindu woman, acquired before or after the Act, is her full property. The Explanation covers property acquired in any manner, including stridhana. Before 1956 a widow's estate was limited: no sale of the corpus without legal necessity, benefit of the estate or reversioners' consent. The 1937 Act gave the widow a son's share as a Hindu woman's estate (Mulla, 14th ed.). Section 14(2) is a narrow exception for grants that prescribe a restricted estate. Tulasamma : a grant giving effect to a pre-existing right falls under Section 14(1). Section 14 decides the kind of ownership; Section 15 decides the heirs. "Possessed" includes constructive possession; the possession must rest on some right or title. Tej Bhan (December 2024) refers the Section 14(1) and 14(2) conflict to a larger bench.
