# 6. Notional Partition
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/notional-partition/

## In One Minute
Meaning. A notional partition is a partition the law assumes to have taken place immediately before a coparcener's death. It is used only to find out the share of the deceased in Mitakshara coparcenary property. Where the share goes. That share then goes to his heirs by succession, not by survivorship . Where the rule is. Before 2005 it was in Explanation 1 to Section 6. After 2005 it is in the Explanation to Section 6(3). What it does not do. It does not actually divide the family property, which stays joint (Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma, paras 101, 129(iv)).

## Legal Provision
- Section 6(3) (as substituted by Act 39 of 2005). ―Where a Hindu dies after the commencement of the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005, his interest in the property of a Joint Hindu family governed by the Mitakshara law, shall devolve by testamentary or intestate succession, as the case may be, under this Act and not by survivorship, and the coparcenary property shall be deemed to have been divided as if a partition had taken place and,―(a) the daughter is allotted the same share as is allotted to a son; (b) the share of the pre-deceased son or a pre-deceased daughter, as they would have got had they been alive at the time of partition, shall be allotted to the surviving child of such pre-deceased son or of such pre-deceased daughter; and (c) the share of the pre-deceased child of a pre-deceased son or of a pre-deceased daughter, as such child would have got had he or she been alive at the time of the partition, shall be allotted to the child of such pre-deceased child of the pre-deceased son or a pre-deceased daughter, as the case may be. Explanation.―For the purposes of this sub-section, the interest of a Hindu Mitakshara coparcener shall be deemed to be the share in the property that would have been allotted to him if a partition of the property had taken place immediately before his death, irrespective of whether he was entitled to claim partition or not.
- Old Section 6, Explanation 1 (as quoted in Gurupad). ―For the purposes of this section, the interest of a Hindu Mitakshara coparcener shall be deemed to be the share in the property that would have been allotted to him if a partition of the property had taken place immediately before his death, irrespective of whether he was entitled to claim partition or not.
- Other provisions used in this note:
- Old Section 6, proviso : if the deceased left a female relative in class I of the Schedule, or a male relative in that class who claims through such a female relative, his interest devolved by will or intestate succession under the Act and not by survivorship . Old Section 6, Explanation 2: a person who had separated from the coparcenary before the death, or any of his heirs, could not claim a share on intestacy in that interest. Sections 8, 9 and 10: class I heirs take together and exclude all others. The widow, each son, each daughter and the mother take one share each (Section 10, Rules 1 and 2). These sections give the heirs' shares in the deceased's interest once the assumed partition has fixed that interest. Explanation to Section 30: the interest of a male Hindu in Mitakshara coparcenary property is deemed to be property he can dispose of by will. Section 6(5) and its Explanation, 2005: Section 6(5) saves a partition made before 20 December 2004. "Partition" means a registered deed or a court decree. The assumed partition is not that kind of partition (see part 7 below).

## Meaning and Definition
Mitakshara coparcenary property is held jointly. A coparcener has no fixed share until partition; his share keeps changing with births and deaths in the family ( Vineeta Sharma , para 66). When a coparcener dies, the law needs a way to find his share. It does this by a legal fiction . It assumes that a partition took place just before his death and gives him the share he would then have received.

Assumed, not real. No deed, decree or division happens. The law only pretends one did. Just before death. The moment of the assumed partition is immediately before the death. Any coparcener. It applies whether or not he could himself claim partition, for example a minor coparcener (Explanation; Mulla, 14th ed., s. 6).

## Key Points to Remember
- A notional partition is a partition assumed just before a coparcener's death. It fixes only the deceased's share; the coparcenary is not divided. Gurupad : widow's share is 1/4 plus 1/24, which is 7/24. Section 6(3), after 2005: succession, not survivorship. Daughter gets the same share as a son (Section 6(3)(a)). It is not a Section 6(5) partition and does not end the coparcenary ( Prakash , paras 18, 22; Vineeta Sharma , para 101).
