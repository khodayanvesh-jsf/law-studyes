# 5. Monogamy
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester I) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/monogamy/

## In One Minute
One spouse at a time. Monogamy means having only one spouse at a time. Section 5(i) of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 (HMA) allows a marriage only if neither party has a spouse living at the time of the marriage. Civil result. A second marriage during the life of the first spouse is void (Sections 11 and 17). The second "spouse" gets no status as husband or wife. Criminal result. Section 17 applies the bigamy offence of Sections 494 and 495 of the Indian Penal Code (IPC). These are now Section 82 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS): up to seven years and fine, and up to ten years and fine if the first marriage was hidden from the new partner. A proper second ceremony is needed. There is bigamy only if the second marriage was " solemnized " with the essential ceremonies ( Bhaurao , 1965). Conversion is no escape. A Hindu who converts to another religion and marries again, without first ending the Hindu marriage, commits bigamy ( Sarla Mudgal , 1995; Lily Thomas , 2000). History. Before 1955 a Hindu man could marry any number of wives, except where State anti-bigamy Acts applied (Mulla). Monogamy is the outstanding feature of the HMA.

## Legal Provision
- Section 5, Hindu Marriage Act, 1955. Conditions for a Hindu marriage. A marriage may be solemnized between any two Hindus, if the following conditions are fulfilled, namely:— (i) neither party has a spouse living at the time of the marriage; …
- Section 11. Void marriages. Any marriage solemnised after the commencement of this Act shall be null and void and may, on a petition presented by either party thereto against the other party, be so declared by a decree of nullity if it contravenes any one of the conditions specified in clauses (i), (iv) and (v) of section 5.
- Section 17. Punishment of bigamy. Any marriage between two Hindus solemnized after the commencement of this Act is void if at the date of such marriage either party had a husband or wife living; and the provisions of sections 494 and 495 of the Indian Penal Code, 1860 (45 of 1860), shall apply accordingly.
- Section 82, Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023. Marrying again during lifetime of husband or wife. (1) Whoever, having a husband or wife living, marries in any case in which such marriage is void by reason of its taking place during the life of such husband or wife, shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to seven years, and shall also be liable to fine. Exception. —This sub-section does not extend to any person whose marriage with such husband or wife has been declared void by a Court of competent jurisdiction, nor to any person who contracts a marriage during the life of a former husband or wife, if such husband or wife, at the time of the subsequent marriage, shall have been continually absent from such person for the space of seven years, and shall not have been heard of by such person as being alive within that time provided the person contracting such subsequent marriage shall, before such marriage takes place, inform the person with whom such marriage is contracted of the real state of facts so far as the same are within his or her knowledge. (2) Whoever commits the offence under sub-section (1) having concealed from the person with whom the subsequent marriage is contracted, the fact of the former marriage, shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to ten years, and shall also be liable to fine.
- In simple words:
- Section 5(i): on the wedding day, neither the bride nor the bridegroom may have a living husband or wife. Section 11: a marriage that breaks clause (i) is void. Either party may ask the court for a decree of nullity against the other. Section 17: a marriage between two Hindus is void if either party had a living spouse on that date, and the IPC bigamy sections (now BNS Section 82) apply. BNS Section 82(1): marrying again in the life of a spouse, where the new marriage is void for that reason: up to seven years and fine. BNS Section 82(2): the same offence after hiding the first marriage from the new partner: up to ten years and fine.

## Meaning and Definition
Monogamy . The rule that a person may have only one spouse at a time. Bigamy. Marrying again while the first spouse is alive and the first marriage still subsists (continues). "Spouse living". A husband or wife of a marriage that still subsists on the date of the new marriage. A person whose marriage has been ended by death, by a divorce decree, or by a decree of nullity has no spouse living for Section 5(i). Void marriage . A marriage that has no legal effect from the start. A marriage in breach of Section 5(i) is void, not merely voidable.

If no, section 5(i) is satisfied. If yes, ask whether the earlier marriage had been ended by death, a divorce decree or a decree of nullity. If it had, there is no spouse living. If not, the new marriage is void under sections 11 and 17. Then ask whether the new marriage was solemnized with the essential ceremonies. If not, there is no bigamy (Bhaurao). If it was, ask whether an exception applies: an earlier marriage declared void, or seven years' absence with disclosure. If an exception applies, there is no offence. Otherwise it is bigamy under BNS section 82(1), up to seven years and fine, or section 82(2), up to ten years and fine, if the first marriage was concealed.

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 5(i): no spouse living at the time of marriage. A breach makes the marriage void (Sections 11 and 17) and punishable (Section 17; BNS Section 82). BNS Section 82: up to seven years and fine; up to ten years and fine with concealment. The second ceremony must be proved for bigamy ( Bhaurao ). Exceptions: earlier marriage declared void; spouse absent and unheard of for seven years, with disclosure. Conversion does not dissolve the first marriage ( Sarla Mudgal ; Lily Thomas ). Before 1955 a Hindu man could marry any number of wives (Mulla, Section 430), except under State Acts such as the Bombay Act, 1946 and the Madras Act, 1949. The Special Marriage Act, 1954 also bars a second marriage (Sections 4(a), 43, 44).
