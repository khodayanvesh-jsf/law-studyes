# 3. Conditions of Hindu Marriage
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester I) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/conditions-of-hindu-marriage/

## In One Minute
Section 5 of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 (HMA) says a marriage may be solemnized between any two Hindus if five conditions are met: No living spouse – neither party has a husband or wife living (clause (i)). Mental capacity – neither party is of unsound mind, mentally unfit for marriage and procreation, or subject to recurrent attacks of insanity (clause (ii)). Age – the bridegroom has completed 21 years and the bride 18 years (clause (iii)). No prohibited relationship – unless the custom of each party permits (clause (iv)). Not sapindas – unless the custom of each party permits (clause (v)). The results of a breach are not the same, and exams test this: Breach of clause (i), (iv) or (v): the marriage is void (Section 11). Breach of clause (i) is also the offence of bigamy (Section 17). Breach of clause (ii): the marriage is voidable (Section 12(1)(b)). Breach of clause (iii): the marriage stays valid under the HMA, but the person who procured it is punishable (Section 18(a)). The Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006 (PCMA) separately lets the party who was a child have it annulled (PCMA Section 3). There was once a clause (vi) (consent of the bride's guardian). It was omitted in 1978. Same gotra, same pravara and different castes are no longer bars.

## Legal Provision
- Section 5, Hindu Marriage Act, 1955. Conditions for a Hindu marriage. A marriage may be solemnized between any two Hindus, if the following conditions are fulfilled, namely:— (i) neither party has a spouse living at the time of the marriage; (ii) at the time of the marriage, neither party— (a) is incapable of giving a valid consent to it in consequence of unsoundness of mind; or (b) though capable of giving a valid consent, has been suffering from mental disorder of such a kind or to such an extent as to be unfit for marriage and the procreation of children; or (c) has been subject to recurrent attacks of insanity; (iii) the bridegroom has completed the age of twenty-one years and the bride, the age of eighteen years at the time of the marriage; (iv) the parties are not within the degrees of prohibited relationship unless the custom or usage governing each of them permits of a marriage between the two; (v) the parties are not sapindas of each other, unless the custom or usage governing each of them permits of a marriage between the two;
- Notes from the bare Act: clause (ii) was substituted by Act 68 of 1976. The words "or epilepsy" were omitted from clause (ii)(c) by Act 39 of 1999 (from 29 December 1999). In clause (iii) the ages were "eighteen" and "fifteen" until Act 2 of 1978 raised them to twenty-one and eighteen (from 1 October 1978). Clause (vi) was omitted by the same Act of 1978.
- Section 3(a). the expressions "custom" and "usage" signify any rule which, having been continuously and uniformly observed for a long time, has obtained the force of law among Hindus in any local area, tribe, community, group or family: Provided that the rule is certain and not unreasonable or opposed to public policy; and Provided further that in the case of a rule applicable only to a family it has not been discontinued by the family;
- Section 3(f). (i) "sapinda relationship" with reference to any person extends as far as the third generation (inclusive) in the line of ascent through the mother, and the fifth (inclusive) in the line of ascent through the father, the line being traced upwards in each case from the person concerned, who is to be counted as the first generation; (ii) two persons are said to be "sapindas" of each other if one is a lineal ascendant of the other within the limits of sapinda relationship, or if they have a common lineal ascendant who is within the limits of sapinda relationship with reference to each of them;
- Section 3(g). "degrees of prohibited relationship"—two persons are said to be within the "degrees of prohibited relationship"— (i) if one is a lineal ascendant of the other; or (ii) if one was the wife or husband of a lineal ascendant or descendant of the other; or (iii) if one was the wife of the brother or of the father's or mother's brother or of the grandfather's or grandmother's brother of the other; or (iv) if the two are brother and sister, uncle and niece, aunt and nephew, or children of brother and sister or of two brothers or of two sisters; Explanation. —For the purposes of clauses (f) and (g), relationship includes— (i) relationship by half or uterine blood as well as by full blood; (ii) illegitimate blood relationship as well as legitimate; (iii) relationship by adoption as well as by blood; and all terms of relationship in those clauses shall be construed accordingly.
- Section 11. Void marriages. Any marriage solemnised after the commencement of this Act shall be null and void and may, on a petition presented by either party thereto against the other party, be so declared by a decree of nullity if it contravenes any one of the conditions specified in clauses (i), (iv) and (v) of section 5.
- Section 12(1)(b). Voidable marriages. Any marriage solemnised, whether before or after the commencement of this Act, shall be voidable and may be annulled by a decree of nullity on any of the following grounds, namely:— … (b) that the marriage is in contravention of the condition specified in clause (ii) of section 5;
- Section 17. Punishment of bigamy. Any marriage between two Hindus solemnized after the commencement of this Act is void if at the date of such marriage either party had a husband or wife living; and the provisions of sections 494 and 495 of the Indian Penal Code, 1860 (45 of 1860), shall apply accordingly.
- Section 18. Punishment for contravention of certain other conditions for a Hindu marriage. Every person who procures a marriage of himself or herself to be solemnized under this Act in contravention of the conditions specified in clauses (iii), (iv), and (v) of section 5 shall be punishable— (a) in the case of contravention of the condition specified in clause (iii) of section 5, with rigorous imprisonment which may extend to two years or with fine which may extend to one lakh rupees, or with both. (b) in the case of a contravention of the condition specified in clause (iv) or clause (v) of section 5, with simple imprisonment which may extend to one month, or with fine which may extend to one thousand rupees, or with both;
- Clause (a) of Section 18 was substituted by Section 20 of the PCMA (from 30 October 2007). A former clause (c), on the guardian's consent, was omitted in 1978.
- In simple words: Section 5 lists what the two persons must satisfy on the wedding day. Sections 3(a), (f) and (g) explain the words used. Sections 11, 12, 17 and 18 say what happens if a condition is broken. The ceremonies themselves are a separate requirement (Section 7, covered in "Ceremonies and Registration").

## Meaning and Definition
The conditions of a Hindu marriage are the legal requirements that the two parties must satisfy at the time of the marriage . A marriage that satisfies them, and is solemnized with the ceremonies required by Section 7, is a valid Hindu marriage.

Three points come straight from the words of Section 5:

"between any two Hindus". Both parties must be persons to whom the Act applies (Section 2). If one party is not a Hindu, a marriage under the HMA is not possible; the parties may marry under the Special Marriage Act, 1954. "at the time of the marriage". Each condition is tested on the date of the wedding. A spouse who dies a week after a second wedding does not make that wedding valid. Not every condition has the same weight. Breach of some conditions destroys the marriage (void), breach of one makes it open to challenge (voidable), and breach of the age condition only attracts punishment.

If no, ask whether the groom is under 21 or the bride under 18; if yes, it is valid but punishable under section 18(a), and voidable at the child's option under PCMA section 3. If no, and the section 7 ceremonies were performed, the marriage is valid.

Mulla (notes to Sections 3(g) and 5) explains that clauses (iv) and (v) are based on the old principle of exogamy, and that the Act aims at "uniformity and certainty" on prohibited degrees, which had varied widely across India.

## Key Points to Remember
- Five conditions: no living spouse, mental capacity, age (21 and 18, completed), no prohibited relationship, no sapinda relationship. Breach of clauses (i), (iv), (v): void (Section 11). Clause (i) is also bigamy (Section 17; now BNS Section 82). Breach of clause (ii): voidable (Section 12(1)(b)). Breach of clause (iii): valid but punishable (Section 18(a)); voidable at the child's option under PCMA Section 3. Custom can save only clauses (iv) and (v), and must govern each party. Sapinda: five generations through the father, three through the mother, counting the person as the first. PCMA 2006: petition before completing two years of attaining majority; offences cognizable and non-bailable; CMRA 1929 repealed. Same gotra, pravara or caste is no bar (Section 5 has none; Section 29(1) saves older marriages). Yamunabai : a bigamous marriage is void, and the woman is not a wife for maintenance; Badshah : except where the man hid his first marriage.
