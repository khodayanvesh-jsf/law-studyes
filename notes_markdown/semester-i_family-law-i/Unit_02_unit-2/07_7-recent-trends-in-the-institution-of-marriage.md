# 7. Recent Trends in the institution of marriage
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester I) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/recent-trends-in-the-institution-of-marriage/

## In One Minute
The gap. The Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 (HMA) was not written with live-in couples, inter-caste elopements or same-sex couples in mind. Courts have built answers for each. Presumption. Long, open cohabitation as husband and wife raises a strong but rebuttable presumption of marriage ( Badri Prasad , 1978). Live-in relationships. A woman in a relationship in the nature of marriage gets the reliefs of the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 (DV Act), if the tests in Velusamy (2010) and Indra Sarma (2013) are met. Right to choose. Adults have a constitutional right to choose a spouse under Articles 19 and 21 ( Lata Singh , Shakti Vahini , Shafin Jahan ). Same-sex marriage. In Supriyo (2023) the Supreme Court held that there is no unqualified fundamental right to marry and left the recognition of same-sex unions to Parliament. Legislation. Many changes, from widow remarriage to the ban on child marriage, came through Acts of the legislatures ( Supriyo ).

## Legal Provision
- Section 2(f), Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005. "domestic relationship" means a relationship between two persons who live or have, at any point of time, lived together in a shared household, when they are related by consanguinity, marriage, or through a relationship in the nature of marriage, adoption or are family members living together as a joint family;
- Section 2(s). "shared household" means a household where the person aggrieved lives or at any stage has lived in a domestic relationship either singly or along with the respondent and includes such a household whether owned or tenanted either jointly by the aggrieved person and the respondent, or owned or tenanted by either of them …
- Section 17(1). Notwithstanding anything contained in any other law for the time being in force, every woman in a domestic relationship shall have the right to reside in the shared household, whether or not she has any right, title or beneficial interest in the same.

- Articles 19 and 21, Constitution. They protect the freedom of adults to choose a life partner ( Shakti Vahini ; Shafin Jahan ). DV Act, Section 2(f). A " domestic relationship " includes persons who live or have lived together in a household when related by marriage or "through a relationship in the nature of marriage ". DV Act, Section 17. A woman in a domestic relationship may live in the shared household even if she does not own it. HMA, Sections 5 and 7. They still decide who can marry under the Act and how. Special Marriage Act, 1954 (SMA). Dolly Rani (2024) notes that it is not restricted to Hindus: any man and woman, whatever their race, caste or creed, can become husband and wife under it.

## Meaning and Definition
Recent trends. The changes made by courts and legislatures to fit marriage law to present society. Presumption of marriage . The rule that a man and woman who have lived together for a long time as husband and wife are taken to be validly married unless it is disproved. It is a rebuttable presumption . Relationship in the nature of marriage. A live-in relationship akin to a common law marriage , which the DV Act protects ( Velusamy ). Live-in relationship. Living together without marriage. Not every live-in relationship is "in the nature of marriage".

## Key Points to Remember
- Long, open cohabitation raises a rebuttable presumption of marriage ( Badri Prasad ). Velusamy : four-part test for a relationship in the nature of marriage, plus a shared household. Indra Sarma : eight guidelines; a woman who knows her partner is married is outside the Act. Chanmuniya : questions on maintenance referred to a larger Bench. The right to choose a spouse is protected by Articles 19 and 21 ( Shakti Vahini ; Shafin Jahan ); inter-caste marriage is not barred ( Lata Singh ). Supriyo : no unqualified right to marry; same-sex recognition left to Parliament; a Cabinet Secretary committee to define entitlements. DV Act: right to reside, protection, residence, monetary, custody and compensation orders; "adult male" struck down from Section 2(q).
