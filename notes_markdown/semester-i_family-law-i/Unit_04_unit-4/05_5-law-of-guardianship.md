# 5. Law of Guardianship
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester I) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/law-of-guardianship/

## In One Minute
Who is a guardian. A guardian is a person who has the care of a minor's person, property or both. A Hindu minor may have a natural guardian , a guardian appointed by will, or a guardian appointed or declared by a court. Natural guardian. For a boy or an unmarried girl, the father is the natural guardian, and after him the mother. In Githa Hariharan v. Reserve Bank of India the Supreme Court read "after him" to mean "in the absence of" the father, not only after his death. Limits. The natural guardian cannot sell or mortgage the minor's immovable property without the court's permission; a sale without it is voidable. The welfare of the minor is the paramount consideration. Two laws. The Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, 1956 (HMGA) is in addition to the Guardians and Wards Act, 1890 (GWA). Guardianship is different from custody.

## Legal Provision
- Section 6, Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, 1956. Natural guardians of a Hindu minor. —The natural guardians of a Hindu minor, in respect of the minor's person as well as in respect of the minor's property (excluding his or her undivided interest in joint family property), are— (a) in the case of a boy or an unmarried girl—the father, and after him, the mother: provided that the custody of a minor who has not completed the age of five years shall ordinarily be with the mother; (b) in the case of an illegitimate boy or an illegitimate unmarried girl—the mother, and after her, the father; (c) in the case of a married girl—the husband: Provided that no person shall be entitled to act as the natural guardian of a minor under the provisions of this section— (a) if he has ceased to be a Hindu, or (b) if he has completely and finally renounced the world by becoming a hermit (vanaprastha) or an ascetic (yati or sanyasi). Explanation.—In this section, the expressions "father" and "mother" do not include a step-father and a step-mother.
- Section 13(1). In the appointment or declaration of any person as guardian of a Hindu minor by a court, the welfare of the minor shall be the paramount consideration.
- Other provisions used in this note:
- HMGA Section 2: the Act is in addition to, and not in derogation of, the GWA, except where it expressly says so. Section 4: a minor is a person under eighteen; a guardian is a person having the care of the minor's person or property or both, and includes a natural guardian, a guardian appointed by the will of the father or mother, a guardian appointed or declared by a court, and a person empowered under a Court of Wards law. Section 5: the Act overrides earlier Hindu law and inconsistent laws on matters it covers. Section 7: guardianship of an adopted minor son passes to the adoptive father and after him the adoptive mother. Section 8: powers of the natural guardian. Section 9: testamentary guardians . Sections 10 to 12: a minor cannot be guardian of property; a de facto guardian cannot deal with the minor's property; no guardian for the minor's undivided interest in joint family property. GWA: appointment, welfare test, duties, removal and end of a guardian's powers. HMA Section 26: custody, maintenance and education of children in matrimonial cases.

## Meaning and Definition
A guardian is a person in charge of a minor's care and protection. Guardianship covers the minor's person (upbringing, education, health) and the minor's property. A minor is a person who has not completed eighteen years (Section 4(a)). The 1956 Act applies in addition to the Guardians and Wards Act, 1890 (Section 2).

## Key Points to Remember
- Minor means under eighteen (Section 4(a)). Natural guardian of a boy or unmarried girl: the father, and after him the mother; "after" means "in the absence of" ( Githa Hariharan ). Custody of a child under five ordinarily with the mother. Section 8: no sale, mortgage or long lease of the minor's immovable property without court permission; breach is voidable. De facto guardian cannot deal with the minor's property (Section 11). Welfare of the minor is paramount (Section 13). Testamentary guardian: appointment fails if the father dies before the mother but revives if she appoints none; right of a guardian of a girl ends on her marriage (Section 9). Natural guardianship of an adopted minor son passes to the adoptive father, then the adoptive mother (Section 7). GWA: welfare test (Section 17); fiduciary duty (Section 20); removal (Section 39); end of powers (Section 41).
