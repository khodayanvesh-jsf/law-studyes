# 10. Debts and alienation of property
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester I) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/debts-and-alienation-of-property/

## In One Minute
Joint family property can be transferred so as to bind the family only by certain persons and on certain grounds. The manager ( Karta ) "cannot alienate or burden the estate qua manager except for purposes of necessity" ( Brij Narain v. Mangla Prasad , para 17); Mulla adds the benefit of the estate. A father has a further power. He may, by incurring a debt that is not for an immoral purpose, lay the estate open to execution, and a mortgage by him binds the estate if it is to discharge an antecedent debt (para 17). A buyer or lender from the manager must prove legal necessity, or that he made proper and honest inquiry. An alienation without necessity is voidable, not void. A coparcener cannot give away his undivided interest; whether he can sell it depends on the school. Since 1956 he can leave it by will (Section 30). Since 2005, Section 6(4) stops any claim against a son, grandson or great-grandson solely on the ground of pious obligation for debts contracted after 9 September 2005. The Hindu rule of damdupat limits the interest recoverable at one time to the amount of the principal, in the places where it applies.

## Legal Provision
- Section 6(4), Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (as substituted in 2005). After the commencement of the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 (39 of 2005), no court shall recognise any right to proceed against a son, grandson or great-grandson for the recovery of any debt due from his father, grandfather or great-grandfather solely on the ground of the pious obligation under the Hindu law, of such son, grandson or great-grandson to discharge any such debt: Provided that in the case of any debt contracted before the commencement of the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 (39 of 2005), nothing contained in this sub-section shall affect― (a) the right of any creditor to proceed against the son, grandson or great-grandson, as the case may be; or (b) any alienation made in respect of or in satisfaction of, any such debt, and any such right or alienation shall be enforceable under the rule of pious obligation in the same manner and to the same extent as it would have been enforceable as if the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 (39 of 2005) had not been enacted. Explanation.―For the purposes of clause (a), the expression "son", "grandson" or "great-grandson" shall be deemed to refer to the son, grandson or great-grandson, as the case may be, who was born or adopted prior to the commencement of the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 (39 of 2005). Section 6(1), proviso. Provided that nothing contained in this sub-section shall affect or invalidate any disposition or alienation including any partition or testamentary disposition of property which had taken place before the 20th day of December, 2004. Section 30, Explanation. The interest of a male Hindu in a Mitakshara coparcenary property or the interest of a member of a tarwad, tavazhi, illom, kutumba or kavaru in the property of the tarwad, tavazhi, illom, kutumba or kavaru shall, notwithstanding anything contained in this Act or in any other law for the time being in force, be deemed to be property capable of being disposed of by him or by her within the meaning of this section.
- In simple words
- Section 6(4). For a debt contracted after 9 September 2005, a creditor cannot proceed against the son, grandson or great-grandson merely because of the pious obligation . For an earlier debt, the creditor's old right against descendants born or adopted before that date, and any alienation made for that debt, are saved. Vineeta Sharma (para 61) adds that Section 6(4) makes a daughter liable in the same manner as a son. Section 6(1), proviso . A sale, gift, mortgage , partition or will made before 20 December 2004 is not upset by the daughter's new right. Section 30, Explanation. A coparcener can leave his undivided interest by will. The rest of this topic is judge-made Mitakshara law, as stated by the Privy Council in Brij Narain and by Mulla.

## Meaning and Definition
Alienation is a transfer of property by sale, mortgage, gift or a similar act. Joint family property may be alienated in two ways: by a person with power to bind the whole family property, or by a coparcener dealing only with his own undivided interest.

Mulla (section 255) lists who alone can alienate coparcenary property "so as to pass a good title to the alienee":

(1) the whole body of coparceners, where they are all adults; (2) the manager, to the extent mentioned in § 242; (3) the father, to the extent mentioned in § 256; (4) a sole surviving coparcener in the circumstances mentioned in § 257.

"No other coparcener is entitled to alienate coparcenary property so as to bind the other coparceners unless he is authorized by them to do so" (section 255).

An antecedent debt is a debt of the father that is "antecedent in fact as well as in time, that is to say, that the debt must be truly independent and not part of he transaction impeached" ( Brij Narain , para 17(4)).

## Key Points to Remember
- Only all adult coparceners, the manager, the father and a sole surviving coparcener can alienate so as to bind the family (Mulla, section 255). The manager may alienate only for legal necessity or benefit of the estate ( Brij Narain , para 17(1); Mulla, section 242). The buyer or lender must prove necessity or proper honest inquiry; he need not see to the use of the money (section 244). An alienation without necessity is voidable, not void (section 242(4)). The father may bind the estate for his own antecedent, non-immoral debt; antecedent means in time and in fact ( Brij Narain , para 17). Section 6(4) ends pious obligation as the sole ground for debts after 9 September 2005. A gift of an undivided interest is void; a sale for value is allowed in Bombay, Madras and Madhya Pradesh (sections 258–260). Damdupat: interest recoverable at one time cannot exceed the principal (section 596).
