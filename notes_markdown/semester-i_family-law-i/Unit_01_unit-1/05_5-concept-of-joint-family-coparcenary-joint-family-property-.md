# 5. Concept of Joint Family, Coparcenary, Joint Family Property and Coparcenary Property
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester I) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/concept-of-joint-family-and-coparcenary/

## In One Minute
A joint Hindu family is a wide group of relatives descended from a common ancestor, with their wives and unmarried daughters. Membership is a status that comes by birth, marriage or adoption. The family need not own any property. A coparcenary is a smaller body inside it. Its members, the coparceners , get an interest in the ancestral property by birth and can demand partition. Traditionally it was the holder of the property and three generations of male descendants; since 2005 daughters are coparceners too. Joint family property and coparcenary property mean the same thing: ancestral property, property bought with joint funds, and separate property thrown into the common stock. A member's own earnings are self-acquired property and stay separate. Exams ask you to keep these ideas apart, to list the incidents of a coparcenary ( Ghamandi Ram ), to explain blending ( Surjit Lal Chhabda ), and to say what changed after 1956 ( Chander Sen ) and 2005 ( Vineeta Sharma ).

## Legal Provision
- No Act defines "joint family" or " coparcenary ". Both come from the Mitakshara and from court decisions. The Hindu Succession Act changes how coparcenary property passes.
- Section 6(1), Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (as substituted in 2005). On and from the commencement of the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 (39 of 2005), in a Joint Hindu family governed by the Mitakshara law, the daughter of a coparcener shall,― (a) by birth become a coparcener in her own right the same manner as the son; (b) have the same rights in the coparcenery property as she would have had if she had been a son; (c) be subject to the same liabilities in respect of the said coparcenery property as that of a son, and any reference to a Hindu Mitakshara coparcener shall be deemed to include a reference to a daughter of a coparcener: Provided that nothing contained in this sub-section shall affect or invalidate any disposition or alienation including any partition or testamentary disposition of property which had taken place before the 20th day of December, 2004.
- Section 6(2). Any property to which a female Hindu becomes entitled by virtue of sub-section (1) shall be held by her with the incidents of coparcenary ownership and shall be regarded, notwithstanding anything contained in this Act or any other law for the time being in force, as property capable of being disposed of by her by testamentary disposition.
- Section 6(3) (first part). Where a Hindu dies after the commencement of the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 (39 of 2005), his interest in the property of a Joint Hindu family governed by the Mitakshara law, shall devolve by testamentary or intestate succession, as the case may be, under this Act and not by survivorship, ...
- Explanation to Section 30. The interest of a male Hindu in a Mitakshara coparcenary property ... shall, notwithstanding anything contained in this Act or in any other law for the time being in force, be deemed to be property capable of being disposed of by him or by her within the meaning of this section.
- Section 3, Kerala Joint Hindu Family System (Abolition) Act, 1975. Birth in family not to give rise to rights in property. On and after the commencement of this Act, no right to claim any interest in any property of an ancestor during his or her lifetime which is founded on the mere fact that the claimant was born in the family of the ancestor shall be recognised in any court.
- In simple words
- Section 6(1): in a Mitakshara joint family, a daughter of a coparcener is a coparcener by birth, with the same rights and liabilities as a son. Partitions and dispositions before 20 December 2004 are saved. Section 6(2): what she gets she holds as coparcenary property, and she can leave it by will . Section 6(3): when a coparcener dies after 9 September 2005, his interest passes by will or by succession, not by survivorship . Section 30, Explanation: a male Hindu can leave his interest in Mitakshara coparcenary property by will. Section 8: the property of a Hindu male who dies intestate (without a will) goes to his heirs under the Act. Chander Sen shows what this means for property a son inherits from his father. Kerala Act, Section 3: in Kerala no one gets a right in an ancestor's property merely by being born in the family.

## Meaning and Definition
Joint Hindu family . "A Joint Hindu family consists of all persons lineally descended from a common ancestor, and includes their wives and unmarried daughters. A daughter ceases to be a member of her father's family on marriage, and becomes a member of her husband's family" (Mulla, section 212(1)). It is a status, not a contract.

Coparcenary. "The coparcenary consists of only those persons who have taken by birth an interest in the property of the holder and who can enforce a partition whenever they like. It is a narrower body than a joint family" ( Sunil Kumar v. Ram Parkash (1988), quoted in Vineeta Sharma , para 27). Mulla describes it as "a common male ancestor with his lineal descendants in the male line within four degrees counting from and inclusive of such ancestor (or three degrees exclusive of the ancestor)" (section 214(1)). Since 2005, daughters are coparceners by birth.

Joint family property or coparcenary property. Mulla says the two terms are synonymous (section 220). It is property in which every coparcener has a joint interest and joint possession (section 221).

Separate property. Property that belongs to one member alone. It includes self-acquired property (Mulla, sections 220, 222).

The question this topic answers. Who belongs to the family, who among them has rights in the property, and which property is shared?

Why the law has this rule. "The cord that knits the members of the family is not property but the relationship of one another" ( Sunil Kumar , quoted in Vineeta Sharma , para 27). Rights in property, however, belong only to the smaller group who take by birth.

## Key Points to Remember
- A joint Hindu family is a status based on sapindaship and needs no property. A coparcenary is a creature of law and is smaller than the joint family; a stranger enters only by adoption. The test of a coparcener is the right to demand partition ( Vineeta Sharma , para 25). Since 2005 daughters are coparceners by birth (Section 6(1)). Joint family property and coparcenary property are synonyms: ancestral, joint acquisitions with its aid, income, blended. Self-acquired property stays separate unless blended by clear intention. A son who inherits from his father under Section 8 holds it as his own ( Chander Sen ). Kerala Act, 1975: the joint family system among Hindus is abolished; birthright ends and coparcenary property is held as tenancy in common.
