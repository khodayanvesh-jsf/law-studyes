# 6. Institution of Karta
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester I) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/institution-of-karta/

## In One Minute
The Karta is the manager of a joint Hindu family . By default he is the father or, in his absence, the senior-most coparcener . The office comes from law and seniority, not from a contract or an election. The Supreme Court has said that managership goes to a person by birth and is regulated by seniority ( Tribhovandas Haribhai Tamboli , 1991). A junior member can act as manager only if the Karta gives up the office or in exceptional situations. Only a coparcener can be Karta ( Seth Govindram Sugar Mills , 1966). The Karta has no larger share than any other member. His one special power is to deal with the whole family property for purposes the law accepts, such as legal necessity . If members dislike his management, their remedy is partition . Since 2005 a daughter is a coparcener by birth. In Sujata Sharma v. Manu Gupta (2015) the Delhi High Court held that the eldest coparcener, though a woman, is the Karta.

## Legal Provision
- No Act creates or defines the office of Karta . It is part of uncodified Mitakshara law. Three statutes touch it.
- Section 6(1), Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (as substituted in 2005). On and from the commencement of the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 (39 of 2005), in a Joint Hindu family governed by the Mitakshara law, the daughter of a coparcener shall,― (a) by birth become a coparcener in her own right the same manner as the son; (b) have the same rights in the coparcenery property as she would have had if she had been a son; (c) be subject to the same liabilities in respect of the said coparcenery property as that of a son, and any reference to a Hindu Mitakshara coparcener shall be deemed to include a reference to a daughter of a coparcener: ...
- Section 4(1)(a), Hindu Succession Act, 1956. Save as otherwise expressly provided in this Act,― (a) any text, rule or interpretation of Hindu law or any custom or usage as part of that law in force immediately before the commencement of this Act shall cease to have effect with respect to any matter for which provision is made in this Act;
- Section 6, Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, 1956 (opening words). The natural guardians of a Hindu minor, in respect of the minor's person as well as in respect of the minor's property (excluding his or her undivided interest in joint family property), are― ...
- Section 12, Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, 1956. Where a minor has an undivided interest in joint family property and the property is under the management of an adult member of the family, no guardian shall be appointed for the minor in respect of such undivided interest: Provided that nothing in this section shall be deemed to affect the jurisdiction of a High Court to appoint a guardian in respect of such interest.
- In simple words
- Section 6(1) makes a daughter a coparcener by birth, with the same rights and liabilities as a son. Since coparcenership is the qualification for the office, this is the basis for a woman becoming Karta. Section 4(1)(a) ends old Hindu law rules only on matters the Act provides for. The Act says nothing about who manages the family, so the classical rules on the Karta continue, read with the new Section 6. Sections 6 and 12 of the Guardianship Act keep a minor's undivided interest in joint family property out of the hands of a guardian. That interest is managed by the adult member who manages the family, which is the Karta.

## Meaning and Definition
Mulla states the rule this way (section 236):

"Property belonging to a joint family is ordinarily managed by the father or other senior member for the time being of the family. The manager of a joint family is called karta. The father is in all cases naturally, and in the case of minor sons necessarily, the manager of the joint family property."

So the Karta is the member who manages the joint family and its property and represents it in dealings with outsiders. The family does not need to appoint him. The office falls on the senior member who is a coparcener.

The Karta manages the joint family, which is wider than the coparcenary . Wives, widows and unmarried daughters are members of the family. But the person who holds the office must be a coparcener. See Diagram 1 .

If no (for example a widow or wife), the person cannot be Karta. If yes, is the person the father or the senior-most coparcener? If yes, the person is Karta by birth and seniority, presumed to be manager. If no, a junior member can act as manager only if the Karta gives up the office, in distress or calamity affecting the family in his absence, or if the father is missing or far away and not expected back.

## Key Points to Remember
- The Karta is the manager of the joint Hindu family; no Act creates the office. The father, or else the senior-most coparcener, is Karta by birth and seniority; the senior member is presumed to be manager. A junior member acts only if the Karta gives up the office or in exceptional circumstances ( Tribhovandas ). Coparcenership is a necessary qualification ( Seth Govindram Sugar Mills ). Since 2005 a daughter is a coparcener, so the eldest coparcener can be Karta though a woman ( Sujata Sharma ); a widow or wife cannot. The Karta has no larger share; he is not an agent; the members' remedy is partition. He accounts on partition only for what he received, unless there is fraud or misappropriation. He cannot alienate except for necessity ( Brij Narain ); he does not manage separate property or Section 8 inheritance ( Chander Sen ).
