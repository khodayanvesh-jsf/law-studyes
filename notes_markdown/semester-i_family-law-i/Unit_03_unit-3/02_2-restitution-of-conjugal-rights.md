# 2. Restitution of Conjugal Rights
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/restitution-of-conjugal-rights/

## In One Minute
The remedy. If a spouse leaves the other without reasonable excuse, the aggrieved spouse may ask the district court for a decree that the other return and live together. This is restitution of conjugal rights under Section 9 of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 (HMA). Burden. The spouse who left must prove the reasonable excuse (Explanation to Section 9). Enforcement. The court cannot force anyone to live together. A wilfully disobeyed decree is enforced only by attaching the property of the disobedient spouse (Order XXI Rule 32, Code of Civil Procedure). Road to divorce. If there is no restitution for one year or more after the decree, either spouse may seek divorce (Section 13(1A)(ii)). Valid. The Andhra Pradesh High Court struck Section 9 down in T. Sareetha (1983). The Delhi High Court upheld it in Harvinder Kaur (1983), and the Supreme Court upheld it in Saroj Rani (1984).

## Legal Provision
- Section 9, Hindu Marriage Act, 1955. Restitution of conjugal rights. When either the husband or the wife has, without reasonable excuse, withdrawn from the society of the other, the aggrieved party may apply, by petition to the district court, for restitution of conjugal rights and the court, on being satisfied of the truth of the statements made in such petition and that there is no legal ground why the application should not be granted, may decree restitution of conjugal rights accordingly. Explanation. —Where a question arises whether there has been reasonable excuse for withdrawal from the society, the burden of proving reasonable excuse shall be on the person who has withdrawn from the society.
- Section 13(1A). Either party to a marriage, whether solemnized before or after the commencement of this Act, may also present a petition for the dissolution of the marriage by a decree of divorce on the ground— … (ii) that there has been no restitution of conjugal rights as between the parties to the marriage for a period of one year or upwards after the passing of a decree for restitution of conjugal rights in a proceeding to which they were parties.
- Order XXI Rule 32(1), Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (as quoted in Saroj Rani ). Where the party against whom a decree for the specific performance of a contract, or for restitution of conjugal rights or for an injunction, has been passed, has had an opportunity of obeying the decree and has willfully failed to obey it, the decree may be enforced in the case of a decree for restitution of conjugal rights by the attachment of his property or, in the case of a decree for the specific performance of a contract, or for an injunction by his detention in the civil prison, or by the attachment of his property, or by both.
- Other provisions used in this note:
- Section 23, HMA: conditions for any relief (own wrong, collusion , delay, other legal ground) and the duty to try reconciliation. Section 23A, HMA: counter-claim on the petitioner 's adultery, cruelty or desertion. Sections 19 and 20, HMA: the court and the petition. Order XXI Rule 32(3), (4) and Rule 33, Code of Civil Procedure: sale of attached property after six months; periodical payments where the decree is against a husband. Family Courts Act, 1984, Sections 7(1) Explanation (a) and 9: a Family Court hears the petition where one exists and first tries settlement. Special Marriage Act, 1954 (SMA), Sections 22, 23(1)(b) and 27(2)(ii): the same remedy for marriages under that Act.

## Meaning and Definition
Conjugal rights are the rights of husband and wife to each other's society: living together, companionship and marital life. Saroj Rani treated this right as " inherent in the very institution of marriage itself", not a mere creation of statute.

Restitution of conjugal rights is a decree that orders a spouse who has left, without reasonable excuse , to return to the other and resume married life. It is available to the husband and the wife alike. It is the only remedy in the HMA that aims at keeping the marriage together ( Harvinder Kaur ). See Diagram 1 .

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 9: remedy when a spouse withdraws from the other's society without reasonable excuse. The burden of proving reasonable excuse lies on the spouse who left. The decree is enforced by attachment of property, not by force or arrest (Order XXI Rule 32); against a husband, also by periodical payments (Rule 33). No restitution for one year allows either party to seek divorce under Section 13(1A)(ii). Mere non-compliance is not a "wrong" under Section 23(1)(a) ( Dharmendra Kumar ). Saroj Rani : Section 9 is valid; T. Sareetha overruled. Special Marriage Act: Section 22 repeats Section 9, and Section 27(2)(ii) repeats Section 13(1A)(ii).
