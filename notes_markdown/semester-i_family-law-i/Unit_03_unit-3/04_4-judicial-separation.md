# 4. Judicial separation
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/judicial-separation/

## In One Minute
What it is. Judicial separation is a court decree that ends the duty of the spouses to live together. The marriage itself continues, so neither spouse can marry again. Grounds. Either spouse may petition on any ground on which a divorce could be sought under Section 13(1) of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 (HMA). A wife may also use her special grounds in Section 13(2) (Section 10(1)). Undoing it. The court may rescind the decree on the petition of either party if it is just and reasonable (Section 10(2)). Road to divorce. If the spouses do not resume cohabitation for one year or more after the decree, either may seek divorce (Section 13(1A)(i)). Instead of divorce. On a divorce petition, the court may grant judicial separation instead, except on conversion, renunciation of the world or presumed death (Section 13A).

## Legal Provision
- Section 10, Hindu Marriage Act, 1955. Judicial separation. (1) Either party to a marriage, whether solemnised before or after the commencement of this Act, may present a petition praying for a decree for judicial separation on any of the grounds specified in sub-section (1) of section 13, and in the case of a wife also on any of the grounds specified in sub-section (2) thereof, as grounds on which a petition for divorce might have been presented. (2) Where a decree for judicial separation has been passed, it shall no longer be obligatory for the petitioner to cohabit with the respondent, but the court may, on the application by petition of either party and on being satisfied of the truth of the statements made in such petition, rescind the decree if it considers it just and reasonable to do so.
- Section 13(1A). Either party to a marriage, whether solemnized before or after the commencement of this Act, may also present a petition for the dissolution of the marriage by a decree of divorce on the ground— (i) that there has been no resumption of cohabitation as between the parties to the marriage for a period of one year or upwards after the passing of a decree for judicial separation in a proceeding to which they were parties; …
- Section 13A. Alternate relief in divorce proceedings. In any proceeding under this Act, on a petition for dissolution of marriage by a decree of divorce, except in so far as the petition is founded on the grounds mentioned in clauses (ii), (vi) and (vii) of sub-section (1) of section 13, the court may, if it considers it just so to do having regard to the circumstances of the case, pass instead a decree for judicial separation.
- Other provisions used in this note:
- Section 13(1) and (2), HMA: the grounds (and the Explanation on desertion ). Sections 14, 15, 19, 20, 21A, 21B, 23, 24, 25, 26, HMA: one-year bar (divorce only), remarriage, procedure, conditions and ancillary orders. Section 18(2), (3), Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956 (HAMA): a wife's right to live separately with maintenance. Family Courts Act, 1984, Sections 7(1) Explanation (a) and 9. Special Marriage Act, 1954 (SMA), Sections 23, 27(1), (1A) and 27A.

## Meaning and Definition
Judicial separation is a decree that allows the spouses to live apart without ending the marriage. It gives a breathing space, which may lead to reconciliation, or to divorce if it fails. See Diagram 1 .

The decree has three effects: the petitioner need not cohabit; the marriage continues; neither spouse can remarry. From the decree there are two paths: if the parties reconcile, the court may rescind the decree under Section 10(2); if there is no cohabitation for one year or more, either party may seek divorce under Section 13(1A)(i).

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 10: the decree ends the duty to cohabit; the marriage continues. Grounds are those of divorce (Section 13(1)), and for a wife also Section 13(2). No remarriage after judicial separation. Rescission is possible under Section 10(2). No cohabitation for one year allows either party to seek divorce (Section 13(1A)(i)). Section 13A lets the court grant judicial separation instead of divorce. Special Marriage Act, 1954, Section 23: judicial separation on the grounds of Section 27 and on failure to obey a restitution decree.
