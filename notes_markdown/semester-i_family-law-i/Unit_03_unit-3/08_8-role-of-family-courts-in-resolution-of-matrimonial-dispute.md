# 8. Role of Family Courts in Resolution of matrimonial disputes
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/role-of-family-courts-in-resolution-of-matrimonial-disputes/

## In One Minute
Aim. The Family Courts Act, 1984 (FCA) provides for Family Courts "with a view to promote conciliation in, and secure speedy settlement of, disputes relating to marriage and family affairs" (long title). Who hears what. A Family Court takes over the matrimonial , family property, maintenance, legitimacy and custody suits of its area, and the maintenance cases of a Magistrate (Sections 7 and 8). Settlement first. It must first try to assist and persuade the parties to settle (Section 9). The Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 (HMA) (Section 23(2)) and Order XXXIIA of the Code of Civil Procedure (CPC) say the same for other courts. Simple procedure. Hearings in camera on request (Section 11), help of counsellors and experts (Sections 6 and 12), no lawyer as of right (Section 13), flexible evidence (Sections 14 to 16). Appeal. To the High Court within thirty days; none from a consent decree (Section 19).

## Legal Provision
- Section 9, Family Courts Act, 1984. Duty of Family Court to make efforts for settlement. (1) In every suit or proceeding, endeavour shall be made by the Family Court in the first instance, where it is possible to do so consistent with the nature and circumstances of the case, to assist and persuade the parties in arriving at a settlement in respect of the subject-matter of the suit or proceeding and for this purpose a Family Court may, subject to any rules made by the High Court, follow such procedure as it may deem fit. (2) If, in any suit or proceeding, at any stage, it appears to the Family Court that there is a reasonable possibility of a settlement between the parties, the Family Court may adjourn the proceedings for such period as it thinks fit to enable attempts to be made to effect such a settlement. (3) The power conferred by sub-section (2) shall be in addition to, and not in derogation of, any other power of the Family Court to adjourn the proceedings.
- Section 13. Right to legal representation. Notwithstanding anything contained in any law, no party to a suit or proceeding before a Family Court shall be entitled, as of right, to be represented by a legal practitioner: Provided that if the Family Court considers it necessary in the interest of justice, it may seek the assistance of a legal expert as amicus curiae.

## Meaning and Definition
A Family Court is a court established by the State Government under Section 3 of the Family Courts Act, 1984 (Section 2(d)). It is a specialised court for disputes about marriage and family affairs, built around conciliation and speed rather than a contest between lawyers. See Diagram 1 .

The district court, subordinate civil court and Magistrate cannot hear it (Section 8); it goes to the Family Court (Section 7). The Family Court first assists and persuades the parties to settle, and may adjourn for that (Section 9), with counsellors and experts (Sections 6 and 12). If they settle, a consent decree or order follows and no appeal lies (Section 19(2)). If they do not settle, the court hears the case: in camera if desired (Section 11), no lawyer as of right (Section 13), evidence not bound by the Evidence Act (Sections 14 to 16). It gives a judgment (Section 17). Appeal lies to the High Court within thirty days (Section 19).

## Key Points to Remember
- Family Courts Act, 1984: the aim is conciliation and speedy settlement of marriage and family disputes. Section 3: a Family Court for every city or town above one million people. Section 7: nullity, restitution, judicial separation, divorce, property, injunction, legitimacy, maintenance, guardianship and custody, plus Chapter IX maintenance. Section 9: the Family Court first assists the parties to settle. Section 11: in camera if the Court or either party so desires. Section 13: no lawyer as of right. Section 19: appeal to the High Court within thirty days; none from a consent decree. Section 20: overriding effect. Section 23(2) HMA: the court must try reconciliation. Order XXXIIA Rule 3: assist the parties to settle first. HMA Section 28(4): appeal within ninety days. Mediation Act, Section 7: the parties are not bound to settle.
