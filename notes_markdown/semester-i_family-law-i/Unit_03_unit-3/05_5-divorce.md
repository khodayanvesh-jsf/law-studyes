# 5. Divorce
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/divorce/

## In One Minute
What it is. Divorce ends a valid marriage by a court decree. After it, and after the appeal time, either party may marry again (Section 15). Four routes. The Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 (HMA) allows divorce on fault grounds (Section 13(1)), after a failed decree of judicial separation or restitution (Section 13(1A)), on four special grounds for a wife (Section 13(2)), and by mutual consent (Section 13B). Bars. No petition within one year of marriage without leave (Section 14). The petitioner must not take advantage of his or her own wrong, must not have condoned or connived at the fault, and must not collude or delay (Section 23). Mutual consent. Both spouses petition together after living apart for one year; the second motion comes six to eighteen months later; consent must continue till the decree ( Sureshta Devi ); the six months can be waived ( Amardeep Singh ). Irretrievable breakdown. It is not a ground in the Act. Only the Supreme Court can dissolve a marriage on it, under Article 142 ( Shilpa Sailesh , 2023).

## Legal Provision
- Section 13(1), Hindu Marriage Act, 1955. Divorce. Any marriage solemnized, whether before or after the commencement of this Act, may, on a petition presented by either the husband or the wife, be dissolved by a decree of divorce on the ground that the other party— (i) has, after the solemnization of the marriage, had voluntary sexual intercourse with any person other than his or her spouse; or (ia) has, after the solemnization of the marriage, treated the petitioner with cruelty; or (ib) has deserted the petitioner for a continuous period of not less than two years immediately preceding the presentation of the petition; or (ii) has ceased to be a Hindu by conversion to another religion; or (iii) has been incurably of unsound mind, or has been suffering continuously or intermittently from mental disorder of such a kind and to such an extent that the petitioner cannot reasonably be expected to live with the respondent. … (v) has … been suffering from venereal disease in a communicable form; or (vi) has renounced the world by entering any religious order; or (vii) has not been heard of as being alive for a period of seven years or more by those persons who would naturally have heard of it, had that party been alive.
- Section 13B(1). … a petition for dissolution of marriage by a decree of divorce may be presented to the district court by both the parties to a marriage together … on the ground that they have been living separately for a period of one year or more, that they have not been able to live together and that they have mutually agreed that the marriage should be dissolved. (2) On the motion of both the parties made not earlier than six months after the date of the presentation of the petition … and not later than eighteen months after the said date, if the petition is not withdrawn in the meantime, the court shall, on being satisfied, after hearing the parties and after making such inquiry as it thinks fit, that a marriage has been solemnised and that the averments in the petition are true, pass a decree of divorce declaring the marriage to be dissolved with effect from the date of the decree.
- Other provisions used in this note:
- HMA Section 13(1A): divorce after a failed decree of judicial separation or restitution. HMA Section 13(2): a wife's four extra grounds. HMA Sections 13A, 14, 15, 16, 21A, 23, 23A, 24 to 28: alternative relief, the one-year bar, remarriage, legitimacy of children, rival petitions, bars to relief, counter-claim, ancillary orders and appeals. Family Courts Act, 1984, Sections 7(1) Explanation (a), 9, 11 and 19. Special Marriage Act, 1954 (SMA), Sections 27, 27A, 28, 29, 30, 34 and 35. Constitution of India, Article 142(1): the Supreme Court's power to do "complete justice".

## Meaning and Definition
Divorce is the dissolution of a valid marriage by a decree of the court. It differs from nullity, which declares that no valid marriage existed or annuls a voidable one, and from judicial separation , which keeps the marriage alive.

The Act began with the fault theory of divorce : one spouse must prove a matrimonial wrong by the other. Later amendments added divorce after a failed decree (1964 and 1976) and divorce by mutual consent (1976). See Diagram 1 .

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 13(1): adultery, cruelty, desertion (two years), conversion, mental disorder, venereal disease, renunciation, presumed death. Section 13(1A): one year without cohabitation or restitution after the earlier decree; either party. Section 13(2): four grounds for a wife. Section 13B: one year apart, two motions, six to eighteen months; waivable; consent must continue. Section 14: one-year bar. Section 23: bars and reconciliation. Irretrievable breakdown: only the Supreme Court, under Article 142. Special Marriage Act, 1954: divorce in Sections 27 and 28; no conversion or renunciation ground, but imprisonment for seven years.
