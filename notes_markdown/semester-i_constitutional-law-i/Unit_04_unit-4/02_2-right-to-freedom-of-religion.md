# 2. Right to Freedom of Religion
**Subject:** Constitutional Law – I (Semester I) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/constitutional-law-i/right-to-freedom-of-religion/

## In One Minute
Articles 25 to 28 make India a State that treats all faiths alike. Article 25 protects every person's conscience and religious practice. Article 26 protects religious groups that run their own affairs. Article 27 stops a tax for any one religion. Article 28 limits religious teaching in State-linked schools. Exams ask about the limits on Article 25, the test for an essential religious practice, who is a denomination, and the four kinds of institution under Article 28. A student made to attend prayer is a common problem question.

## Legal Provision
- Article 25(1): all persons are equally entitled to freedom of conscience and the right freely to profess, practise and propagate religion. This is subject to public order, morality and health and to the other provisions of Part III. Article 25(2): nothing in Article 25 affects the operation of any existing law. Nor does it prevent the State from making a law regulating any economic, financial, political or other secular activity linked with religious practice. It may also make a law for social welfare and reform, or for throwing open Hindu religious institutions of a public character to all classes and sections of Hindus. Explanation I: wearing and carrying kirpans is part of the profession of the Sikh religion. Explanation II: "Hindus" in clause (2)(b) includes persons professing the Sikh, Jaina or Buddhist religion. Article 26: every religious denomination or any section of it has four rights, subject to public order, morality and health. It may establish and maintain institutions for religious and charitable purposes. It may manage its own affairs in matters of religion. It may own and acquire property. It may administer that property in accordance with law. Article 27: no person shall be compelled to pay any tax whose proceeds are specifically spent on the promotion or maintenance of any particular religion or religious denomination. Article 28: clause (1) bars religious instruction in an institution wholly maintained out of State funds. Clause (2) excepts an institution administered by the State but established under an endowment or trust that requires such instruction. Clause (3) bars compelling anyone in a recognised or aided institution to take part in religious instruction or worship without consent, or the guardian's consent for a minor.

## Meaning and Definition
The Constitution does not define "religion". The Court has read it widely to cover belief and the acts that belong to it. The four Articles together express secularism : the State has no religion of its own and treats all faiths alike. Each Article protects a different holder. Article 25 protects the individual. Article 26 protects a religious denomination . Article 27 protects the taxpayer. Article 28 protects the student.

All four rights are limited. Articles 25 and 26 are subject to public order, morality and health. Article 25 is also subject to the other rights in Part III. Article 26 is not.

Can religious instruction be given in this institution? How to read this: go down the boxes in order. A "No" answer sends you to the result beside the box. 1. Is the institution maintained, administered, recognised or aided by the State? Article 28 No: Article 28 does not apply. A private institution may give religious instruction. 2. Is it wholly maintained out of State funds? Article 28(1) No: instruction may be given, but no one can be made to take part without consent (the guardian's, for a minor). Article 28(3). 3. Is it administered by the State and established under an endowment or trust that requires religious instruction? Article 28(2) No: no religious instruction may be given. Article 28(1). Yes: Article 28(2) lets the institution give religious instruction.

## Key Points to Remember
- Article 25(1): all persons; conscience, profess, practise, propagate; subject to public order, morality, health and Part III. Propagate is not convert (Stainislaus, 1977). Shirur Mutt (1954): the State may regulate only secular activity; the religion's doctrines decide what is essential. Article 26 belongs to a denomination: belief system, common organisation, distinctive name. Article 27 bars a compulsory tax spent on one particular religion. Article 28: no instruction in wholly State-funded schools; consent in aided or recognised schools. Bijoe Emmanuel (1986): you cannot be forced to sing against your belief. Secularism is part of the basic structure (Kesavananda Bharati, paras 316 and 620; Coelho, 2007).
