## In One Minute
The Indian Constitution is written, very detailed and partly rigid. It sets up a federal system with a strong Centre, a parliamentary government, an independent judiciary and a list of rights that courts enforce. Exams ask for the salient features in two ways. One asks you to list and explain them. The other asks whether India is federal or unitary. Learn every feature with its Article.

## Legal Provision
- Preamble: India is a "SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC". The words "SOCIALIST" and "SECULAR" and the phrase "unity and integrity" came from the 42nd Amendment of 1976 (in force from 3 January 1977). Article 1(1): "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States." Article 13: a law inconsistent with Part III (Fundamental Rights) is void to the extent of the inconsistency. Articles 32 and 226: the Supreme Court and the High Courts can issue writs to enforce rights. Article 37: the Directive Principles in Part IV are not enforceable by any court, but are "fundamental in the governance of the country". Article 51A: the Fundamental Duties of citizens, inserted by the 42nd Amendment. Articles 74 and 75(3): a Council of Ministers aids and advises the President and is collectively responsible to the House of the People. Article 246, Article 248 and the Seventh Schedule: the Union, State and Concurrent Lists divide legislative power. Parliament has the residuary power, which is the power over matters not in the State or Concurrent List. Article 326: elections to the House of the People and State Assemblies are on adult suffrage . The voting age is eighteen, fixed by the 61st Amendment (in force from 28 March 1989). Article 368: the power and procedure to amend the Constitution. Articles 352, 356 and 360: proclamations of Emergency, failure of constitutional machinery in a State, and financial emergency.

## Meaning and Definition
The nature of a Constitution is its overall character: how it is made, how it can be changed and how it divides power. The salient features are the standout features that give the Constitution that character. Together they answer the question "What kind of Constitution is this?"

## Key Points to Remember
- Written Constitution adopted on 26 November 1949: 395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules at the start. Federal in form with a unitary bias, called quasi-federal: Articles 1, 3, 248, 249, 312, 352, 356. Parliamentary: Council of Ministers collectively responsible to the House of the People (Article 75(3)). Fundamental Rights are enforceable (Articles 32, 226). Directive Principles are not (Article 37). Duties are in Article 51A. Voting age eighteen under Article 326 since the 61st Amendment (1989). Three ways to amend: simple majority, special majority, special majority plus State ratification. Basic structure: Kesavananda Bharati (1973); Minerva Mills struck down Article 368(4) and (5).
