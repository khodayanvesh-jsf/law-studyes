# 3. Union and its Territories
**Subject:** Constitutional Law – I (Semester I) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/constitutional-law-i/union-and-its-territories/

## In One Minute
Articles 1 to 4 say what India is, what its territory includes and how Parliament can change the map. Parliament can admit new territory, form new States and alter State boundaries and names by an ordinary law. The affected State is heard, but it cannot say no. Exams ask about the words "Union of States", the difference between Article 2 and Article 3, and why handing over territory to another country is different. Union territories (Part VIII) are also asked.

## Legal Provision
- Article 1: India, that is Bharat, is a Union of States. Its territory is the States, the Union territories in the First Schedule and territories acquired later. Article 2: Parliament may by law admit into the Union, or establish, new States on such terms and conditions as it thinks fit. Article 3: Parliament may by law form new States and alter the area, boundaries or name of a State. The proviso needs the President's recommendation and a reference to the State Legislature. Article 4: a law under Article 2 or 3 amends the First and Fourth Schedules. Under Article 4(2) it is not an amendment for Article 368. Articles 239 to 241: Union territories. Article 143: the President may refer a question to the Supreme Court.

## Meaning and Definition
The Union is India as a whole, made up of States and Union territories.

Article 1(1) says " Union of States ", not "federation". The States did not make an agreement to join, so none can secede . That is why the Constitution does not use the word "federation".

Which route does a territorial change take? How to read this: go down the boxes in order. A "No" answer sends you to the result beside the box. 1. Is the territory already part of India? Articles 1(3), 2 No: Parliament may admit it or establish a new State by law, on terms it thinks fit (Article 2). 2. Does the change stay inside India? Article 3 No: handing territory to a foreign country needs a constitutional amendment under Article 368, not Article 3 (Berubari). 3. Does the Bill affect the area, boundaries or name of a State? Article 3 proviso No: the Bill needs only the President's recommendation. Yes: the President's recommendation is needed and he must refer the Bill to that State's Legislature for its views. Once the period has expired, Parliament may pass it by an ordinary law. It is not an amendment under Article 368 (Article 4(2)).

## Key Points to Remember
- Article 1: "Union of States"; territory = States, Union territories, acquired territory. Article 2: new territory. Article 3: change inside India. Article 3 Bill: President's recommendation; reference to the State; views not binding. Article 4(2): such a law is not an amendment under Article 368. Berubari (1960): ceding territory to a foreign country needs Article 368. Babulal Parate (1960): no second reference after amendment. Part VIII: Union territories; Delhi under Article 239AA; Puducherry under Article 239A.
