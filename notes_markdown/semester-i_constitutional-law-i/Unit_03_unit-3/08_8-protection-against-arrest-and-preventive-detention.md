# 8. Protection against Arrest and Preventive Detention
**Subject:** Constitutional Law – I (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/constitutional-law-i/protection-against-arrest-and-preventive-detention/

## In One Minute
Article 22 has two parts. A person arrested in the ordinary way must be told the grounds, may consult a lawyer, and must be produced before a magistrate within 24 hours. A person held under a preventive detention law gets a different, weaker set of safeguards. Exams ask for both sets, the Advisory Board, and the guidelines in Joginder Kumar and D.K. Basu. A foreign student arrested in India is covered by the first part.

## Legal Provision
- Article 22(1): a person arrested shall not be detained in custody without being told, as soon as may be, the grounds of arrest. He shall not be denied the right to consult and be defended by a legal practitioner of his choice. Article 22(2): an arrested person must be produced before the nearest magistrate within twenty-four hours, excluding the journey time. He cannot be held longer without the magistrate's authority. Article 22(3): clauses (1) and (2) do not apply to an enemy alien or to a person arrested or detained under a preventive detention law. Article 22(4): a preventive detention law cannot authorise detention beyond three months, unless an Advisory Board of persons who are, have been or are qualified to be High Court Judges reports sufficient cause. The exceptions are in a law made by Parliament under clause (7). Article 22(5): the authority must tell the detained person the grounds of the order as soon as may be and give the earliest chance to make a representation . Article 22(6): the authority need not disclose facts it considers against the public interest to disclose. Article 22(7): Parliament may prescribe the cases where detention beyond three months is allowed without an Advisory Board, the maximum period of detention, and the Board's procedure. Not in force: section 3 of the Constitution (Forty-fourth Amendment) Act, 1978 would substitute clause (4). The date is yet to be notified, so the original text applies. The new clause would cut the period to two months. The Board would be constituted on the recommendation of the Chief Justice of the appropriate High Court. It would have a Chairman who is a serving High Court Judge and at least two other members who are serving or retired High Court Judges. Clause (7)(a) would be omitted.

## Meaning and Definition
Article 22 covers two situations. An ordinary arrest follows an alleged offence and leads to a trial. Preventive detention is detention without trial to stop a future act. It is not a punishment for a past offence. For this reason the Constitution gives it its own rules in clauses (3) to (7).

Which safeguards of Article 22 apply? How to read this: go down the boxes in order. A "No" answer sends you to the result beside the box. 1. Is the person held under a preventive detention law? Article 22(3)(b) No: clauses (1) and (2) apply: grounds, lawyer, production before a magistrate within 24 hours. They do not apply to an enemy alien. 2. Has the detention lasted more than three months? Article 22(4) No: clause (5) applies: grounds, and the earliest chance to make a representation. Yes: an Advisory Board must report sufficient cause before the three months end, unless a law made by Parliament under clause (7) allows otherwise.

## Key Points to Remember
- Clauses (1) and (2): grounds, lawyer, magistrate in 24 hours excluding travel. Clause (3): enemy aliens and preventive detainees are outside clauses (1) and (2). Clause (4): three months, then the Advisory Board. The 44th Amendment's change is not notified. Clause (6) allows withholding facts against the public interest. Clause (7) lets Parliament make longer rules. Joginder Kumar (1994): no routine arrest; a friend or relative informed. D.K. Basu (1996): eleven binding requirements; compensation for custodial violence. A.K. Roy (1982): no absolute right to a lawyer before the Advisory Board.
