# 1. Right to Equality (Art.14-18)
**Subject:** Constitutional Law – I (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/constitutional-law-i/right-to-equality-art-14-18/

## In One Minute
Article 14 bans unequal treatment by the State. Articles 15 to 18 apply that idea to discrimination, public jobs, untouchability and titles. The State may still treat groups differently if the difference is reasonable, and it may never act on whim. Exams ask for the twin test of classification, the arbitrariness test, and the limits of reservation in jobs. Keep the five Articles apart and name the case for each rule.

## Legal Provision
- Article 14: the State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws within India. Article 15: no discrimination against a citizen on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth (clause 1). The same grounds cannot bar access to shops, restaurants, hotels, wells, tanks and roads (clause 2). Special provision is allowed for women and children (clause 3), for backward classes, Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (clauses 4 and 5), and for economically weaker sections (clause 6). Article 16: equality of opportunity in public employment (clause 1), with no discrimination on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, descent, place of birth or residence (clause 2). Only Parliament may prescribe a residence requirement (clause 3). The State may reserve posts for a backward class not adequately represented (clause 4) and for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in promotion (clause 4A). Clause (4B) carries forward unfilled reserved vacancies. Clause (5) protects laws on the religion of a religious office-holder. Clause (6) allows up to ten per cent for economically weaker sections. Article 17: "Untouchability" is abolished and its practice in any form is forbidden. Enforcing any disability arising from it is an offence punishable by law. Article 18: the State cannot confer a title, except a military or academic distinction (clause 1). A citizen cannot accept a foreign title (clause 2). A non-citizen holding office under the State needs the President's consent for a foreign title (clause 3). Any office-holder needs the President's consent for a foreign present, emolument or office (clause 4). Amendments: clause 15(4) is from the First Amendment (18 June 1951), 15(5) from the 93rd (20 January 2006), and 15(6) and 16(6) from the 103rd (14 January 2019). Clause 16(4A) is from the 77th (17 June 1995), with "consequential seniority" added by the 85th. Clause 16(4B) is from the 81st (9 June 2000).

## Meaning and Definition
The Right to Equality is the group of Articles 14 to 18 in Part III. Article 14 is the general rule. It protects every "person", so citizens, foreigners and companies are covered. It has two limbs. "Equality before law" means no one is above the law. "Equal protection of the laws" means people in like positions must be treated alike.

Articles 15 to 18 are specific applications. Article 15 protects only citizens against discrimination on five grounds. Article 16 does the same for public employment. Article 17 abolishes one social evil outright. Article 18 stops the State from creating a titled class. All five are claimed against the State , except Articles 15(2) and 17, which also reach private persons.

Does a State action pass Article 14? How to read this: go down the boxes in order. A "No" answer sends you to the result beside the box. 1. Does the law or order treat people differently, or leave the choice to an official? Article 14 No: there is no classification, but the action can still be tested for arbitrariness (see the arbitrariness test below). 2. Is the difference based on an intelligible differentia, a real basis that separates those grouped from those left out? first limb of the twin test No: the classification fails and Article 14 is breached (Anwar Ali Sarkar). 3. Does that basis have a rational link to the object of the law? second limb of the twin test No: the classification fails and Article 14 is breached. Yes to all: the classification is reasonable and valid (Ram Krishna Dalmia). Even then, an arbitrary action fails as unequal.

## Key Points to Remember
- Article 14 covers every person. Article 15 covers only citizens. Twin test: intelligible differentia and rational nexus (Dalmia, 1958). Arbitrariness test: Royappa (1974). Equality is antithetic to arbitrariness. Article 15(2) and Article 17 reach private persons. Indra Sawhney: creamy layer excluded, 50 per cent ceiling, no promotion under the original clause (4). Clause (4A) covers only Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes. Nagaraj requires quantifiable data. Clauses 15(6) and 16(6): ten per cent for economically weaker sections (103rd Amendment, 3:2 in Janhit Abhiyan). Article 18(2) is an absolute bar on a citizen accepting a foreign title.
