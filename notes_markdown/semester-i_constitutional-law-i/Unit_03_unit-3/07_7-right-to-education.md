# 7. Right to Education
**Subject:** Constitutional Law – I (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/constitutional-law-i/right-to-education/

## In One Minute
Article 21A makes free and compulsory education a Fundamental Right for every child of six to fourteen years. Before 2010 the Court had read the right out of Article 21. The Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009 now carries it out. Exams ask for the text of Article 21A, the change from Mohini Jain to Unni Krishnan, the three changes made by the 86th Amendment, and the 25 per cent rule for private schools.

## Legal Provision
- Article 21A: the State shall provide free and compulsory education to all children of the age of six to fourteen years, in such manner as the State may by law determine. It was inserted by the Constitution (Eighty-sixth Amendment) Act, 2002, section 2, in force from 1 April 2010. Article 45: as now worded, the State shall endeavour to provide early childhood care and education for all children until they complete six years. The 86th Amendment substituted this text. Article 51A(k): it is the duty of a parent or guardian to provide opportunities for education to a child or ward between the ages of six and fourteen. The 86th Amendment inserted it. Article 15(5): the State may make special provision for admission of backward classes, Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes to educational institutions, including private ones, but not minority institutions under Article 30(1). Article 41: the State shall, within the limits of its economic capacity and development, make effective provision for securing the right to work, to education and to public assistance in cases of unemployment, old age, sickness, disablement and other undeserved want. Article 46: the State shall promote with special care the educational and economic interests of the weaker sections, in particular the Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes, and protect them from social injustice and all forms of exploitation. Article 39(f): the State must direct its policy so that children get opportunities and facilities to develop in a healthy manner, in freedom and dignity. Childhood and youth must be protected against exploitation and against moral and material abandonment. The 42nd Amendment (1976) substituted this clause from 3 January 1977. Article 24: no child below fourteen may be employed in a factory, a mine or other hazardous employment. Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009: the law that Article 21A contemplates.

## Meaning and Definition
The right is a claim against the State. It covers a child from six years until the child completes fourteen years. It does not cover a child below six or above fourteen. The words "in such manner as the State may by law determine" leave the details to a statute.

## Key Points to Remember
- Article 21A: free and compulsory education for ages six to fourteen (86th Amendment, in force 1 April 2010). Article 45: early childhood care below six. Article 51A(k): parent's duty. Mohini Jain (1992) tied education to Article 21. Unni Krishnan (1993) limited the free right to age fourteen. RTE Act, 2009: section 12(1)(c) reserves 25 per cent of entry-level seats in private unaided schools. Pramati (2014): the Act does not apply to minority institutions.
