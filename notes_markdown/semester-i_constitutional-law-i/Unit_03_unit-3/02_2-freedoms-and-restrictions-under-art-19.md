# 2. Freedoms and Restrictions under Art.19
**Subject:** Constitutional Law – I (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/constitutional-law-i/freedoms-and-restrictions-under-art-19/

## In One Minute
Article 19(1) gives every citizen six freedoms. Clauses (2) to (6) let the State limit each one, but only by a law, only on grounds listed for that freedom, and only if the limit is reasonable. Exams ask for the grounds in clause (2), the cases on speech and sedition, and the tests that courts use to judge a restriction. Do not borrow a ground from one clause to justify a limit under another.

## Legal Provision
- Article 19(1): all citizens have the right (a) to freedom of speech and expression, (b) to assemble peaceably and without arms, (c) to form associations or unions or co-operative societies, (d) to move freely throughout India, (e) to reside and settle in any part of India, and (g) to practise any profession or carry on any occupation, trade or business. Article 19(2): reasonable restrictions on speech in the interests of the sovereignty and integrity of India, the security of the State, friendly relations with foreign States, public order, decency or morality, or in relation to contempt of court, defamation or incitement to an offence. Article 19(3): reasonable restrictions on assembly in the interests of the sovereignty and integrity of India or public order. Article 19(4): reasonable restrictions on association in the interests of the sovereignty and integrity of India, public order or morality. Article 19(5): reasonable restrictions on movement and residence in the interests of the general public or for the protection of any Scheduled Tribe. Article 19(6): reasonable restrictions on trade and profession in the interests of the general public. A law may prescribe professional or technical qualifications. A law may also let the State, or a State-owned corporation, carry on a trade, business, industry or service, wholly or partly excluding citizens. Amendments: clauses (2) and (6) were substituted by the First Amendment (in force from 18 June 1951). "Sovereignty and integrity of India" was added to clauses (2) to (4) by the 16th Amendment (5 October 1963). The 44th Amendment omitted sub-clause (f), the right to property (20 June 1979). The 97th Amendment added "co-operative societies" (8 February 2012).

## Meaning and Definition
Article 19 follows one pattern. Clause (1) gives a freedom. A later clause lets the State limit it. Each limit must be a reasonable restriction , made by a law, and tied to the grounds listed for that freedom. The Article protects only citizens. Property is no longer a Fundamental Right, so only six freedoms remain in clause (1).

## Key Points to Remember
- Six freedoms, citizens only. Property (f) left Part III in 1979. Each freedom has its own restriction clause: (2), (3), (4), (5), (6). Romesh Thappar (1950): public order was not in the original clause (2). The First Amendment added it. The 16th Amendment (1963) added sovereignty and integrity of India. Kedar Nath Singh (1962): sedition is valid only with incitement to violence or tendency to disorder. Shreya Singhal (2015): section 66A struck down. Only incitement can be restricted. Anuradha Bhasin (2020): speech and trade over the internet are protected. Indefinite shutdown is impermissible. Bijoe Emmanuel (1986): standing in respectful silence is enough. Gopalan (1950) kept Article 19 out of preventive detention laws. Maneka Gandhi (1978) held Articles 14, 19 and 21 work together. Article 358: Article 19 is suspended only during a war or external aggression Emergency. Louis De Raedt (1991): Article 19(1)(e) is for citizens, not foreigners.
