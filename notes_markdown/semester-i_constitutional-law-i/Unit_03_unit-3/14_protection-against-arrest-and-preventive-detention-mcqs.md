## Meaning and Definition
Article 22(1) and 22(2) guarantee a person arrested under ordinary law the right to be informed of the grounds of arrest, to consult and be defended by a legal practitioner of choice, and to be produced before the nearest magistrate within:

Explanation. Article 22(2) requires production before the nearest magistrate within 24 hours of arrest, excluding travel time, and prohibits detention beyond that period without the magistrate's authority.

Article 22(3) creates an important exception, under which the safeguards in Article 22(1)-(2) do NOT apply to:

Explanation. Article 22(3) excludes enemy aliens and persons detained under preventive detention laws from the ordinary-arrest safeguards of clauses (1) and (2) — preventive detention instead has its own separate, more limited safeguards under clauses (4) to (7).

Under Article 22(4) (as it originally stands and continues to operate, since the 44th Amendment's changes to it were never brought into force), a person cannot be detained under a preventive detention law for longer than __ months without obtaining the opinion of an Advisory Board:

Explanation. As confirmed in A.K. Roy v. Union of India (1982), the 44th Amendment's changes to Article 22(4) — which would reduce this period to two months and change the Board, have never been brought into force, so the original three-month rule continues to govern preventive detention.
