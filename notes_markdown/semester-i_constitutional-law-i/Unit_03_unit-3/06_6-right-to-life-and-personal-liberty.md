# 6. Right to Life and Personal Liberty
**Subject:** Constitutional Law – I (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3

## In One Minute
Article 21 has one sentence: no person shall be deprived of life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law. In 1950 the Court read it narrowly. Since 1978 the procedure must be fair, just and reasonable, and Article 21 has grown into a source of many rights. Exams ask for the Gopalan to Maneka Gandhi change, the Golden Triangle and the rights read into Article 21. For a short answer on speedy trial, name Hussainara Khatoon.

## Legal Provision
- Article 21: no person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law. Article 359(1): during a Proclamation of Emergency, the President may suspend the right to move a court to enforce Part III rights, except those in Articles 20 and 21. Article 22(1) and (2): an arrested person must be told the grounds of arrest as soon as may be and may consult and be defended by a lawyer of his choice. He must be produced before the nearest magistrate within twenty-four hours, excluding journey time. He cannot be held longer without the magistrate's authority. Article 22(3): clauses (1) and (2) do not apply to an enemy alien or to a person arrested or detained under a preventive detention law. Article 22(4) to (7): a preventive detention law cannot allow detention beyond three months unless an Advisory Board of High Court Judges, or persons qualified to be Judges, reports sufficient cause. Parliament may prescribe cases in which this is not needed, the maximum period and the Board's procedure. The authority must communicate the grounds as soon as may be and give the earliest chance to make a representation. It need not disclose facts it considers against public interest. The Article 22 note covers these safeguards in full. Article 21A: free and compulsory education for children of six to fourteen years, inserted by the 86th Amendment (1 April 2010). K.S. Puttaswamy describes the earlier Unnikrishnan decision as holding that the right to education, though not then in Part III, was protected under Article 21.

## Meaning and Definition
Article 21 protects every person, citizen or not, against the State. "Life" and "personal liberty" are not defined. The Court gives them wide meaning case by case. The phrase " procedure established by law " asks how the State may take life or liberty. It is not the American phrase " due process ", which lets a court test the fairness of a law's procedure itself.

When can the State take life or liberty? How to read this: start at the top. Each box must be answered "yes" to move down. Red text shows where the action fails. 1. Is there a law that authorises it? No: the deprivation is invalid under Article 21. 2. Does the law prescribe a procedure? No: the deprivation is invalid under Article 21. 3. Is the procedure fair, just and reasonable, and does the law meet Articles 14 and 19? Maneka Gandhi No: the law is struck down. 4. Result The deprivation is valid.

## Key Points to Remember
- Article 21 protects every person. Text: "procedure established by law". Gopalan (1950): any valid procedure; Articles 14, 19, 21 separate. Maneka Gandhi (1978): fair, just, reasonable procedure; the Golden Triangle. Francis Coralie (1981): life means life with human dignity. Puttaswamy (2017): privacy is a Fundamental Right; proportionality test. Rights read in: speedy trial, legal aid, livelihood, clean air and water, shelter. Article 359(1) cannot suspend Articles 20 and 21.
