# 3. Protection against Ex-post facto law
**Subject:** Constitutional Law – I (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/constitutional-law-i/protection-against-ex-post-facto-law/

## In One Minute
Article 20(1) stops the State from punishing a person under a law that came after the act. It also stops a heavier punishment than the law allowed when the act was done. Exams ask for the two limbs, the fact that it covers criminal law only, and the point that only a harsher law is barred. Every person gets this protection, not only citizens.

## Legal Provision
- Article 20(1): no person shall be convicted of any offence except for violation of a law in force at the time of the commission of the act charged as an offence. Nor shall a person be subjected to a penalty greater than that which might have been inflicted under the law in force at the time of the commission of the offence. Article 359(1): during a Proclamation of Emergency, the President may suspend the right to move a court for Part III rights, except those in Articles 20 and 21. So this protection survives an emergency.

## Meaning and Definition
An ex post facto law is a law made after an act that changes the legal result of that act. Article 20(1) bars such a law only in one field: criminal liability and punishment. It rests on two ideas: nullum crimen sine lege (no crime without a law) and nulla poena sine lege (no punishment without a law).

Does Article 20(1) bar the conviction or the sentence? How to read this: start at the top. Each box must be answered "yes" to move down. Red text shows where the protection applies. 1. Is the proceeding about a criminal offence and its penalty? No: Article 20(1) does not apply. 2. Was the act an offence under a law in force when it was done? No: the conviction is barred. 3. Is the penalty greater than the law in force then allowed? Yes: the extra penalty is barred. 4. Result The conviction and the penalty stand.

## Key Points to Remember
- Article 20(1) has two limbs: conviction and penalty. The law in force on the date of the act decides. It covers criminal law only. Only a harsher retrospective law is barred. The clause speaks of a "greater" penalty. Every person is covered. It cannot be suspended in an emergency (Article 359(1)).
