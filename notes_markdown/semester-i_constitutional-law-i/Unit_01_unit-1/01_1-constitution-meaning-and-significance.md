## In One Minute
A constitution is the set of basic rules that says who governs a country, how power is shared, and what the government must never do to its people. The Indian Constitution is a written document and the highest law of the land. Exams ask for the meaning, the two senses of the word, and the functions of a constitution. In real life it explains why a court can strike down even an Act of Parliament.

## Legal Provision
- Preamble : "We, the people of India" adopted, enacted and gave to themselves this Constitution. The source of authority is the people. Article 393: the short title is "the Constitution of India". Article 394: Articles 5 to 9, 60, 324, 366, 367, 379, 380, 388, 391, 392 and 393 came into force at once. The rest came into force on 26 January 1950, called the "commencement of this Constitution". Article 245(1): Parliament and the State Legislatures make laws "subject to the provisions of this Constitution". Article 13: a law that conflicts with the Fundamental Rights is void to the extent of the conflict. Article 368(1): Parliament may amend the Constitution by the procedure in that article. Only that procedure can change it.

## Meaning and Definition
The English word comes from the Latin constituere , meaning to set up or establish.

The word is used in two senses.

Wide sense: the whole system of government of a country, meaning all the rules that set up and govern the government. Every state has a constitution in this sense, written or unwritten. Britain is the usual example. Narrow sense: a selected body of legal rules, usually collected in one document, which govern the government. India, the United States and many other countries have a constitution in this sense.

A constitution in the narrow sense is therefore the fundamental law of a state. It creates the organs of the state, divides power among them, fixes limits on that power and declares the rights of the people.

## Key Points to Remember
- A constitution is the fundamental law that sets up and limits government. Two senses: a wide sense (the whole system of government) and a narrow sense (a selected set of legal rules, usually in one document). Main functions: organise, distribute power, limit power, give continuity, state ideals. The Constitution of India was adopted on 26 November 1949 and commenced on 26 January 1950 (Article 394). It is supreme because of its source, Articles 245 and 13, and judicial review. Constitutionalism means real, enforceable limits on power.
