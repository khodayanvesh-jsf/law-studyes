# 9. Framing of Indian Constitution
**Subject:** Constitutional Law – I (Semester I) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/constitutional-law-i/framing-of-indian-constitution/

## In One Minute
The Constitution was framed by a Constituent Assembly. Its members were chosen under the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946. The Assembly first met on 9 December 1946, adopted the Constitution on 26 November 1949, and held its last session on 24 January 1950. Exams ask for the dates, the numbers, the Objectives Resolution and the committees. In real life the story explains why the Preamble begins "We, the people of India".

## Legal Provision
- Preamble : "IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION." Article 394: Article 394 itself and Articles 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 60, 324, 366, 367, 379, 380, 388, 391, 392 and 393 "shall come into force at once". The remaining provisions come into force on 26 January 1950, which the Constitution calls "the commencement of this Constitution". Article 394A: the Hindi translation is signed by members of the Constituent Assembly. Article 395: the Indian Independence Act, 1947 and the Government of India Act, 1935 are repealed, together with all enactments amending or supplementing the 1935 Act. The Abolition of Privy Council Jurisdiction Act, 1949 is not repealed.

## Meaning and Definition
The framing of the Constitution is the process by which the Constituent Assembly drafted, debated and adopted the Constitution between December 1946 and January 1950. It is the Indian people, through their representatives, giving themselves the fundamental law. It was not a law imposed by Britain.

## Key Points to Remember
- Demand: M.N. Roy (1934), Congress (1935). Strength: 389 planned (296 plus 93); 299 after partition. First sitting: 9 December 1946. President: Rajendra Prasad (11 December 1946). Objectives Resolution: moved on 13 December 1946, adopted on 22 January 1947. Adopted 26 November 1949; commenced 26 January 1950. Time taken: 2 years, 11 months and 17 days, counted by Ambedkar on 25 November 1949 from the first meeting.
