# 6. Historical Perspectives
**Subject:** Constitutional Law – I (Semester I) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/constitutional-law-i/historical-perspectives/

## In One Minute
British Parliament first regulated the East India Company in 1773, took India under the Crown in 1858, and then let Indians into the legislative councils step by step. Each step left something that the Constitution later reused. Exams ask for the Acts in order with one change each, and for the 1909 separate electorates. The Constitution rejected the last of these.

## Legal Provision
- These old Acts are not in force. The Constitution deals with their legacy in these provisions.
- Article 395: repeals the Indian Independence Act, 1947 and the Government of India Act, 1935, with the Acts that amended them. Article 325: one general electoral roll for every constituency. No person is ineligible, or may claim to be on a special roll, only on grounds of religion, race, caste or sex. This ends separate electorates. Article 326: elections to the House of the People and State Assemblies are on the basis of adult suffrage.

## Meaning and Definition
The historical perspective on the Indian Constitution is the account of the British statutes between 1773 and 1909 that shaped Indian government. In that period the Company's trading rule became rule by the Crown, and a central legislature with Indian members began to take shape.

## Key Points to Remember
- 1773: Governor-General of Bengal and Calcutta Supreme Court. 1784: Board of Control, dual control. 1833: Governor-General of India. 1858: Crown rule, Secretary of State, Council of India of fifteen (the word Viceroy is not in the Act). 1861, 1892, 1909: Indian Councils Acts; 1909 gave separate electorates to Muslims. Article 325 ends separate electorates.
