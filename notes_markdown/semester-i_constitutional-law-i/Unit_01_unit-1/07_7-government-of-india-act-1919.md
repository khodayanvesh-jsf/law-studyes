# 7. Government of India Act, 1919
**Subject:** Constitutional Law – I (Semester I) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/constitutional-law-i/government-of-india-act-1919/

## In One Minute
The Government of India Act, 1919 gave Indian ministers a share of power in the provinces for the first time. It split provincial subjects into reserved and transferred ones. This split is called dyarchy. It also created a central legislature of two houses. Exams ask for dyarchy, the two lists of subjects, the central legislature and the Act's weaknesses.

## Legal Provision
- The Act is 9 & 10 Geo. 5 c. 101 (British Parliament). The text of these provisions is on this site's Bare Act page.
- Section 1(1)(d): some provincial subjects are "transferred subjects", administered by the Governor acting with Ministers. Section 1(4): provincial subjects other than transferred ones are "reserved subjects". Section 3(1): each major province is governed, for reserved subjects, by a Governor in Council, and for transferred subjects by the Governor acting with Ministers. Section 4(3): for transferred subjects the Governor is guided by his Ministers' advice, unless he sees sufficient cause to dissent . Section 11(2)(a): the Governor can certify that expenditure on a reserved subject is essential, which overrides a vote against it. Sections 17 to 19: the Indian legislature consists of the Governor-General and two Chambers, the Council of State and the Legislative Assembly. Section 23(1)(c): rules fix who may vote and how constituencies are formed, including communal electorates.

## Meaning and Definition
The Government of India Act, 1919 was the law that carried out the Montagu-Chelmsford reforms. On 20 August 1917, the Secretary of State for India, Edwin Montagu, spoke in the House of Commons. He said British policy aimed at the gradual development of self-governing institutions. Its goal was the progressive realisation of responsible government (government answerable to an elected legislature). Montagu and the Viceroy, Lord Chelmsford, published a report in July 1918. The Act followed in 1919.

Its central device was dyarchy (double government) in the provinces.

Provincial subjects (section 1(4)) Reserved subjects Governor in Council; not answerable to the provincial legislature. For example, police and justice. Transferred subjects Governor with Ministers answerable to the elected legislature. For example, education and health.

## Key Points to Remember
- Montagu declaration: 20 August 1917; report of July 1918. Dyarchy in the provinces: reserved subjects (Governor in Council), transferred subjects (Governor with Ministers). Central legislature: Council of State and Legislative Assembly. Separate electorates extended to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians and Europeans. Review after ten years: the Simon Commission, 1927, with no Indian member. Defect: responsibility without power.
