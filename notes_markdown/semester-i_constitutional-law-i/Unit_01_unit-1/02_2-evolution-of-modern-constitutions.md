## In One Minute
The modern constitution grew over about six centuries. Written limits on kings (1215), the supremacy of Parliament (1689), the first written national constitution (1787) and the rights of the citizen (1789) are its main steps. Exams ask for these milestones with dates. They also help you see where the ideas of the Indian Constitution came from.

## Legal Provision
- No article of the Indian Constitution describes this history. The following provisions show the ideas that grew out of it.
- Preamble : secures justice, liberty, equality and fraternity to all citizens. Equality and liberty were central demands of the revolutions of the eighteenth century. Part III (Articles 12 to 35): Fundamental Rights, the Indian form of a bill of rights. Article 75(3): the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the House of the People. This is the parliamentary system developed in Britain. Article 368: the written amending procedure, a feature of rigid written constitutions.

## Meaning and Definition
The evolution of modern constitutions is the story of how states moved from rule by the will of the king to rule under a fundamental law. Thinkers such as John Locke and Jean-Jacques Rousseau argued that government rests on the consent of the governed. Rulers and ruled gradually accepted written limits on power.

A modern constitution has four marks. It is the highest law. It sets up the organs of government. It limits their powers. It protects the rights of the people.

## Key Points to Remember
- Magna Carta (1215): written limit on royal power, but not a constitution. Bill of Rights (1689): Parliament supreme over the Crown. English constitution stays uncodified. United States Constitution (1787): oldest written national constitution still in force. In force from 1789. French Declaration (1789): sovereignty of the nation, rights of the citizen. India adopted its written constitution on 26 November 1949. Marbury v. Madison (1803): early judicial review.
