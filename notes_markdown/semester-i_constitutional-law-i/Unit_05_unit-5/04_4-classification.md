## In One Minute
This note is about the classification of the Directive Principles in Part IV. Textbooks sort Articles 38 to 51 into three groups: socialistic, Gandhian and liberal-intellectual. The Constitution does not use these names. They are a teaching tool. Exams ask you to place an Article in its group, or to write a short note on one principle such as equal pay for equal work in Article 39(d). Learn the Articles in each group and say that books differ on a few.

## Legal Provision
- Article 38(1): the State shall strive to promote the welfare of the people by securing and protecting, as effectively as it may, a social order in which justice, social, economic and political, shall inform all the institutions of national life. Article 38(2): the State shall, in particular, strive to minimise inequalities in income. It shall endeavour to eliminate inequalities in status, facilities and opportunities. This applies among individuals and also among groups of people living in different areas or engaged in different vocations. The 44th Amendment (20 June 1979) numbered the old Article 38 as clause (1) and added clause (2). Article 39: the State shall direct its policy towards securing the following six goals. (a) Men and women citizens equally have the right to an adequate means of livelihood. (b) The ownership and control of the community's material resources are distributed to best serve the common good. (c) The economic system does not concentrate wealth and means of production to the common detriment. (d) Equal pay for equal work for both men and women. (e) The health and strength of workers and the tender age of children are not abused. Citizens are not forced by economic necessity into work unsuited to their age or strength. (f) Children get opportunities and facilities to develop in a healthy manner, in freedom and dignity. Childhood and youth are protected against exploitation and against moral and material abandonment. The 42nd Amendment (3 January 1977) substituted clause (f). Article 39A (42nd Amendment): the State shall secure that the legal system promotes justice on a basis of equal opportunity. It shall provide free legal aid by suitable legislation or schemes so that no citizen is denied justice because of economic or other disabilities. Article 40: the State shall take steps to organise village panchayats and give them the powers and authority needed to work as units of self-government. Article 41: within the limits of its economic capacity and development, the State shall make effective provision for the right to work, to education and to public assistance. Public assistance covers unemployment, old age, sickness, disablement and other cases of undeserved want. Article 42: the State shall make provision for just and humane conditions of work and for maternity relief. Article 43: the State shall endeavour to secure, by suitable legislation or economic organisation, work, a living wage, decent conditions of work and full enjoyment of leisure and of social and cultural opportunities for all workers. It shall in particular endeavour to promote cottage industries, on an individual or co-operative basis, in rural areas. Article 43A (42nd Amendment): the State shall take steps, by legislation or otherwise, to secure workers' participation in the management of undertakings, establishments and other organisations in any industry. Article 43B (97th Amendment, 15 February 2012): the State shall endeavour to promote voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, democratic control and professional management of co-operative societies. Article 44: the State shall endeavour to secure for citizens a uniform civil code throughout India. Article 45 (86th Amendment, in force 1 April 2010): the State shall endeavour to provide early childhood care and education for all children until they complete six years. Article 46: the State shall promote with special care the educational and economic interests of the weaker sections, in particular the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes. It shall protect them from social injustice and all forms of exploitation. Article 47: the State shall regard raising the level of nutrition and standard of living, and improving public health, as among its primary duties. It shall in particular endeavour to bring about prohibition of the consumption, except for medicinal purposes, of intoxicating drinks and of drugs injurious to health. Article 48: the State shall endeavour to organise agriculture and animal husbandry on modern and scientific lines. It shall in particular take steps to preserve and improve the breeds, and to prohibit the slaughter, of cows and calves and other milch and draught cattle. Article 48A (42nd Amendment): the State shall endeavour to protect and improve the environment and to safeguard the forests and wild life of the country. Article 49: the State is obliged to protect every monument, place or object of artistic or historic interest, declared by or under a law made by Parliament to be of national importance. The protection is from spoliation, disfigurement, destruction, removal, disposal or export. Article 50: the State shall take steps to separate the judiciary from the executive in the public services of the State. Article 51: the State shall endeavour to (a) promote international peace and security, (b) maintain just and honourable relations between nations, (c) foster respect for international law and treaty obligations in the dealings of organised peoples with one another, and (d) encourage settlement of international disputes by arbitration.

## Meaning and Definition
Classification means sorting the Articles of Part IV by the idea each serves. The Directive Principles of State Policy cover very different subjects, from wages to world peace. Textbooks group them to make them easy to learn and to answer questions on.

The three groups are a convenience. The Constitution gives no such headings, and books place a few Articles differently. Treat the groups as a memory aid and know what each Article says.

## Key Points to Remember
- The three groups are a textbook device, not constitutional text. Socialistic: Articles 38, 39, 39A, 41, 42, 43, 43A. Gandhian: Articles 40, 43B, 46, 47, 48. Liberal-intellectual: Articles 44, 45, 48A, 49, 50, 51. Article 39(d) is equal pay for equal work. Articles 39A, 43A and 48A came from the 42nd Amendment; Article 43B from the 97th.
