## In One Minute
This note is about the significance of the Fundamental Duties in Article 51A. They have no penalty, but they matter in four ways. They remind citizens that rights come with responsibilities, guide courts in reading the law, support laws that protect the environment and public property, and teach civic values. Exams ask why duties were added and what use they have if they cannot be enforced. Give the four reasons and one case for each.

## Legal Provision
- Article 51A: it shall be the duty of every citizen of India to follow the eleven duties in clauses (a) to (k). Part IVA was inserted by the 42nd Amendment (3 January 1977). Article 51A(g): to protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers and wild life, and to have compassion for living creatures. Article 51A(h): to develop the scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry and reform. Article 48A: the State shall endeavour to protect and improve the environment and to safeguard the forests and wild life of the country.

## Meaning and Definition
The significance of the Fundamental Duties lies in what they do for the constitutional scheme, not in any penalty. Part III lists what the citizen can claim. Part IVA states what the citizen owes. A reader of the Constitution sees both sides of citizenship.

The Duties are moral as much as legal. They work through teaching, through court reasoning and through the laws that Parliament makes.

## Key Points to Remember
- Four reasons: balance with rights, aid for courts, support for environment laws, civic awareness. Article 51A has no penalty. Laws give legal force. Ganga case (1988): environment lessons in schools, supported by Article 51A(g). Animal Welfare Board (2014): Article 51A(g) and (h) read into a statute. Article 21 gives the right to a clean environment, Article 48A binds the State, and Article 51A(g) binds the citizen. Bijoe Emmanuel (1986): a duty under clause (a) does not oblige anyone to sing the Anthem.
