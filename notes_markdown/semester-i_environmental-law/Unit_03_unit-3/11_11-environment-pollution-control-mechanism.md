**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3

## In One Minute
Pollution control in India rests on several authorities. Pollution Control Boards grant consent and give directions. The Central Government makes rules and gives directions under the Environment (Protection) Act. Biodiversity bodies regulate access to biological resources. The National Green Tribunal hears disputes and appeals against their orders. Each authority has its own Act and tools.

## Legal Provision
- Water Act, 1974: sections 3 and 4 (Boards), 16 and 17 (functions), 25 (consent), 33A (directions). Air Act, 1981: sections 3 and 4 (the same Boards), 21 (consent), 31A (directions). Environment (Protection) Act, 1986: section 3 (measures and authorities), section 5 (directions). Biological Diversity Act, 2002: section 8 (Authority), section 22 (State Boards), section 41 (Committees). National Green Tribunal Act, 2010: sections 14 and 16 (jurisdiction), section 22 (appeal).

## Meaning and Definition
The "pollution control mechanism" is the set of bodies and powers that prevent, control and reduce pollution. Some bodies regulate in advance, by consent and rules. Others act after a breach , by directions, penalties or a decision.
