# 5. The law relating to prevention and control of water pollution
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/law-relating-to-prevention-and-control-of-water-pollution/

## In One Minute
The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974 keeps rivers, streams, wells and sewers clean. It works through Pollution Control Boards. An industry needs the Board's consent before it discharges sewage or trade effluent. The Board can act in three ways: take emergency steps, ask a court to restrain a polluter, or give written directions of its own, including closure. Questions on this Act are common in both short and long form.

## Legal Provision
- The main sections, read from the Act as stored on this site, are these.
- Section 1(2): the Act applied at first to listed States and the Union territories. Any other State could adopt it by a resolution under Article 252(1) of the Constitution. Section 2(e): "pollution" means contamination of water, or a change in its physical, chemical or biological properties, or a discharge of sewage, trade effluent or any other substance into water. The discharge must be likely to create a nuisance. Or it must make the water harmful to public health, to domestic or industrial use, or to animals, plants and aquatic life. Sections 3 and 4: the Central Pollution Control Board and a State Pollution Control Board for each State. Section 24: no person may knowingly cause or permit poisonous, noxious or polluting matter to enter a stream, well, sewer or land. Section 25: the previous consent of the State Board is needed to set up an industry likely to discharge sewage or trade effluent. It is also needed for a new outlet and for a new discharge. Section 32: emergency measures by the State Board. Section 33: an application by the Board to a court to restrain apprehended pollution. Section 33A: written directions by the Board, including closure and stopping of electricity or water.

## Meaning and Definition
Water pollution law is regulatory law. The State sets standards and permits in advance, and a Board checks compliance. This differs from a nuisance suit, where the victim goes to court after harm has happened.

The Act covers streams, wells and sewers, and also land onto which polluting matter is discharged. An effluent is a liquid waste that flows out of a factory or a sewage works. "Trade effluent" is the effluent of a trade or industry.

Central Pollution Control Board sets national direction, advises the Central Government, coordinates State Boards State Pollution Control Board grants or refuses consent, sets effluent standards, gives directions The industry or local body that discharges: it must hold consent and obey its conditions

## Key Points to Remember
- Water Act, 1974 rests on Article 252. Sections 3 and 4: Central and State Pollution Control Boards. Section 24: ban on polluting matter. Section 25: previous consent. Section 26: existing discharges. Section 32 emergency steps, section 33 court application, section 33A written directions. Section 33A allows closure and stopping of electricity or water without a court order. Section 2(e) defines pollution by harm or likely harm, not by a fixed chemical list. 2024 Amendment (Act 5 of 2024): penalty of Rs 10,000 to Rs 15 lakh (sections 41 to 45A), adjudicating officer (45B), appeal to the NGT in 60 days with 10 per cent deposit (45C). Failure to obtain consent under section 25 or 26 is still an offence: 1 year 6 months to 6 years (section 45E).
