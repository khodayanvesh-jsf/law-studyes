# 13. National Green Tribunal-Their powers and jurisdiction
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester I) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/national-green-tribunal/

## In One Minute
The National Green Tribunal (NGT) is a special forum for environmental disputes. Its bench mixes judicial members and expert members. It hears civil cases with a substantial environmental question under seven listed Acts. It can order relief, compensation and restoration. It also hears appeals against orders such as environmental clearance. It must apply sustainable development, the precautionary principle and the polluter pays principle. An appeal from the NGT goes to the Supreme Court.

## Legal Provision
- The main sections of the National Green Tribunal Act, 2010 (Act No. 19 of 2010) are these.
- Section 3: the Central Government establishes the Tribunal by notification. Section 4: a full-time Chairperson, ten to twenty full-time Judicial Members and ten to twenty full-time Expert Members, as notified. Section 14: jurisdiction over all civil cases where a substantial question relating to environment arises from the implementation of the enactments in Schedule I. Section 15: relief, compensation and restitution . Section 16: appellate jurisdiction. Section 20: the Tribunal must apply the principles of sustainable development, the precautionary principle and the polluter pays principle. Section 22: appeal to the Supreme Court. Section 29: civil courts cannot hear matters the Tribunal decides.

## Meaning and Definition
The National Green Tribunal is a statutory body for quick and expert decisions in environmental cases. The Supreme Court had urged expert environmental bodies earlier, in A.P. Pollution Control Board v. Prof. M.V. Nayudu, where it referred technical questions to an expert authority. The Tribunal combines law and science on one bench.

Its work has three parts. It decides civil disputes (original jurisdiction), it hears appeals against certain orders, and it awards relief and restoration.

## Key Points to Remember
- NGT Act, 2010 (Act No. 19 of 2010). Expert Members equal Judicial Members on a bench. Section 14: civil cases under seven Schedule I Acts, six months. The Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972 is not in Schedule I. Section 15: relief, compensation, restitution, five years. Section 16: appeals within thirty days. Section 20: three binding principles. Section 22: appeal to the Supreme Court within ninety days.
