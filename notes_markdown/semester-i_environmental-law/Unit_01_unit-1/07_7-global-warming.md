# 7. Global Warming
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester I) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/global-warming/

## In One Minute
Global warming is the long-term rise in the Earth's average surface temperature. Gases such as carbon dioxide and methane trap heat in the air, and human activity has added a great deal of them. It is the main driver of climate change. Exams ask for causes, effects and the treaty response.

## Legal Provision
- India has no single Act on global warming. The law reaches it through these sources.
- Section 2(a), Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981: an "air pollutant" is any solid, liquid or gaseous substance (including noise) present in the atmosphere in such concentration as may be or tend to be injurious to human beings, other living creatures, plants, property or environment. Section 3(1), Environment (Protection) Act, 1986: the Central Government can take all measures it thinks necessary to protect the environment and control pollution. UN Framework Convention on Climate Change, 1992, Kyoto Protocol, 1997 and Paris Agreement: the treaties that set the global response. Article 2, Paris Agreement: the goal of holding the rise in global average temperature well below 2 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels. Efforts continue to limit it to 1.5 degrees.

## Meaning and Definition
Global warming is the long-term rise in the Earth's average surface temperature, caused by the build-up of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere. A greenhouse gas is a gas that traps heat. The Earth needs some of them to stay warm enough for life. The problem is the extra amount.

How extra greenhouse gases warm the Earth How to read this: follow the boxes from top to bottom. Each step causes the next. The last box is the result. 1. Sunlight reaches the Earth and warms the surface. 2. The surface sends heat back towards space. 3. Greenhouse gases in the air trap part of that heat. 4. Human activity adds more gases, so more heat is trapped. 5. Result The average temperature of the surface rises.

## Key Points to Remember
- Global warming: long-term rise in average surface temperature. Gases: carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, CFCs, water vapour. Causes: fossil fuels, deforestation, industry, farming, cities. Paris Agreement, Article 2: well below 2 degrees, efforts towards 1.5 degrees. No separate Indian Act. The Air Act and the 1986 Act apply to harmful gases. Kyoto Annex A: six gases (carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, HFCs, PFCs, sulphur hexafluoride). Paris Art 4(1): peaking, then balance in the second half of the century.
