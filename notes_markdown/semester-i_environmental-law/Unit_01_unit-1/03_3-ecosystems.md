## In One Minute
An ecosystem is a community of living things together with the non-living surroundings they interact with, working as one unit. A pond, a forest patch and a farm are all ecosystems. Energy flows through it in one direction, while nutrients are used again and again. Exams ask for its parts, how energy moves and why pollution upsets its balance.

## Legal Provision
- No Indian Act defines an ecosystem. Two provisions still use the idea.
- Section 2(b), Biological Diversity Act, 2002: biological diversity means the variability among living organisms from all sources and the ecological complexes of which they are part, including diversity "of eco-systems". Section 2(a), Environment (Protection) Act, 1986: environment includes the inter-relationship among water, air, land and living beings, which is what an ecosystem is made of.

## Meaning and Definition
An ecosystem is a community of interacting organisms together with their physical surroundings, functioning as a unit. The British ecologist A.G. Tansley introduced the word in 1935.

Ecosystems come in three broad kinds. Terrestrial ones are on land, such as forests and grasslands. Aquatic ones are in water, such as lakes, rivers and oceans. Artificial ones are made by people, such as farms, reservoirs and city parks.

## Key Points to Remember
- Ecosystem: living community plus non-living surroundings as one unit. Roles: producers, consumers, decomposers. Energy flow is one-way; nutrient cycling is circular. A food web joins many food chains. Homeostasis: the ecosystem's tendency to stay balanced. Section 2(b) of the 2002 Act includes diversity of ecosystems.
