# 11. Ancient Indian approach to environment
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester I) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1

## In One Minute
Long before any environmental statute, Indian tradition treated care for nature as a duty. Rivers, trees and animals were held sacred, and non-violence discouraged needless harm. This old ethic is the background to Articles 48A and 51A(g) of the Constitution. Exams ask for its sources and its link to modern law.

## Legal Provision
- The ancient approach is cultural and historical. It is not a statute. It shaped the modern text in two places.
- Article 48A, Constitution of India: the State shall endeavour to protect and improve the environment and to safeguard the forests and wild life of the country. It was inserted by the Constitution (Forty-second Amendment) Act, 1976. Article 51A(g), Constitution of India: every citizen has a duty to protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers and wild life. Every citizen must also have compassion for living creatures. The same Amendment inserted it.

## Meaning and Definition
The ancient Indian approach to the environment treats the protection of nature as a moral and religious duty ( dharma ), and not only as a practical need. Writers often call this idea dharmic ecology. It rests on respect for rivers, forests, animals and the Earth, and on restraint in using them.
