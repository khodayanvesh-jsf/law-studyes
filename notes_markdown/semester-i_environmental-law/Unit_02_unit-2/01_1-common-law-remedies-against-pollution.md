# 1. Common Law remedies against pollution
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester I) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/common-law-remedies-against-pollution/

## In One Minute
Common law remedies are the ordinary rules of tort and civil and criminal procedure. A person harmed by pollution can use them even where no pollution statute applies. The main routes are trespass, nuisance, negligence, strict and absolute liability, public nuisance proceedings and injunctions. Exams ask these as long answers. The skill is matching the facts to the right route.

## Legal Provision
- There is no single Act on common law remedies. The law comes from these sources.
- Law of torts: trespass to land, nuisance, negligence and the rule in Rylands v. Fletcher (1868). Courts developed these rules through decided cases. Section 270, Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS): defines public nuisance. It replaced section 268 of the Indian Penal Code from 1 July 2024. Section 152, Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS): lets a Magistrate pass a conditional order to remove a nuisance. It replaced section 133 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973. Section 91, Code of Civil Procedure, 1908: allows a civil suit on a public nuisance without proof of special damage. Sections 38 and 39, Specific Relief Act, 1963: allow a perpetual injunction and a mandatory injunction.

## Meaning and Definition
Common law remedies against pollution are the general legal actions open to a person who suffers harm from pollution. They are called general because they were not made for the environment. Before the Water Act, 1974 and later statutes, victims had only these remedies.

They still matter. Pollution statutes mostly regulate polluters and punish them. A common law action gives the victim a personal remedy: money, an order to stop, or both.

Choosing the common law remedy How to read this: go down the boxes in order. A "No" answer sends you to the remedy beside the box. 1. Did the polluter put matter directly on the victim's land? No: go to question 2. 2. Did a hazardous activity cause an escape? No: go to question 3. 3. Did the polluter fail to take reasonable care? No: consider nuisance if smoke, smell or noise interfered with enjoyment of land. Yes to question 1: trespass. Yes to question 2: strict or absolute liability. Yes to question 3: negligence.

## Key Points to Remember
- Common law remedies are general tort and procedure rules, usable beside pollution statutes. Trespass: direct, no damage needed. Nuisance: indirect interference. Negligence needs duty, breach, causation and damage. Rylands v. Fletcher: strict liability with exceptions. M.C. Mehta: absolute liability, no exceptions. BNS section 270, BNSS section 152 and CPC section 91 deal with public nuisance. Specific Relief Act: section 38 perpetual and section 39 mandatory injunction.
