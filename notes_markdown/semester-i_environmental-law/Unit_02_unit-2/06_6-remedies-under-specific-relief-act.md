# 6. Remedies under Specific Relief Act
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester I) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/remedies-under-specific-relief-act/

## In One Minute
Damages repay a victim for harm already done. They do not stop a polluter who keeps polluting. The Specific Relief Act, 1963 gives the remedy that does: an injunction, which is a court order to stop an act or to do an act. Know sections 36 to 42: the kinds of injunction, damages with or instead of an injunction, and the cases where an injunction is refused.

## Legal Provision
- Section 36: preventive relief is granted at the discretion of the court, by injunction, temporary or perpetual. Section 37: temporary injunctions last until a specific time or further order. They may be granted at any stage of a suit and are regulated by the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908. A perpetual injunction can be granted only by a decree made at the hearing, on the merits. Section 38: a perpetual injunction may be granted to prevent breach of an obligation in the plaintiff's favour. Where the defendant invades or threatens to invade the plaintiff's right to or enjoyment of property, it may be granted in four cases. Section 39: where it is necessary to compel certain acts, which the court can enforce, the court may grant an injunction to prevent the breach and to compel those acts. Section 40: the plaintiff may claim damages in addition to, or in place of, a perpetual or mandatory injunction. Section 41: lists the cases where an injunction cannot be granted. Section 42: an injunction may enforce a negative agreement even where the court cannot enforce the positive one.

## Meaning and Definition
An injunction is a court order that requires a person to stop an act or to do an act. Pollution is usually a continuing wrong, so the most valuable remedy is an order to stop it.

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 36: preventive relief by injunction, temporary or perpetual, at the court's discretion. Section 37: temporary injunction at any stage. Perpetual only by decree on the merits. Section 38: perpetual injunction. Section 39: mandatory injunction. Section 40: damages. Section 41: grounds for refusal. Clause (ha) was added in 2018. Section 42: injunction to enforce a negative agreement. Shelfer (1895): good working rule on damages instead of an injunction.
