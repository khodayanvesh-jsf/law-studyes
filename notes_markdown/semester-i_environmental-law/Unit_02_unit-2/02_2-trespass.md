## In One Minute
Trespass to land is a direct, unauthorised interference with a person's possession of land. It is actionable without proof of damage. In pollution cases it applies when waste is put straight onto the victim's land. Drifting smoke or noise is nuisance instead.

## Legal Provision
- Trespass is a tort developed by courts. No statute defines it. Two statutes help a victim in practice.
- Section 38, Specific Relief Act, 1963: a perpetual injunction may be granted to prevent breach of an obligation. It may also be granted where the defendant invades, or threatens to invade, the plaintiff's right to or enjoyment of property. Section 37(1), Specific Relief Act, 1963: a temporary injunction lasts until a specified time or further order. It may be granted at any stage of a suit and is regulated by the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908.

## Meaning and Definition
Trespass to land is a direct and unauthorised interference with a person's possession of land. The law protects possession , not only ownership. A tenant in possession can sue.

Pollution reaches a neighbour's land Direct Waste dumped or piped onto the land: trespass. Indirect Smoke, fumes, seepage or noise travelling to the land: nuisance.

## Key Points to Remember
- Trespass to land: direct, unauthorised interference with possession. Actionable per se: no proof of damage needed. Direct deposit of waste is trespass. Drifting smoke or seepage is nuisance. Remedies: damages, injunction, limited self-help. The injunction is the effective remedy against a continuing discharge.
