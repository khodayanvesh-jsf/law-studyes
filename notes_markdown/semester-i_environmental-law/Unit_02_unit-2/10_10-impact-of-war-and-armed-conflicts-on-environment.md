# 10. Impact of War and Armed Conflicts on Environment
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester I) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/impact-of-war-and-armed-conflicts-on-environment/

## In One Minute
War poisons land and water, burns forests and kills wildlife. The damage lasts long after the fighting and crosses borders. International law protects the environment in armed conflict through a few treaties, the rules of humanitarian law and soft law. The key test is damage that is "widespread, long-term and severe".

## Legal Provision
- ENMOD Convention, 1976: prohibits the military or any other hostile use of environmental modification techniques that have widespread, long-lasting or severe effects. Articles 35(3) and 55, Additional Protocol I to the Geneva Conventions, 1977: prohibit methods or means of warfare intended or expected to damage the natural environment. The damage must be widespread, long-term and severe. Article 8(2)(b)(iv), Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court, 1998: makes it a war crime to launch an attack knowing it will cause widespread, long-term and severe environmental damage. The damage must be clearly excessive against the military advantage expected. Principles 24 and 25, Rio Declaration, 1992: Principle 24 says warfare is inherently destructive of sustainable development, and States must respect international law protecting the environment in armed conflict. Principle 25 says peace, development and environmental protection are interdependent and indivisible. International Law Commission, Draft Principles on Protection of the Environment in Relation to Armed Conflicts, 2022: 27 draft principles covering protection before, during and after a conflict.

## Meaning and Definition
The impact of war on the environment is the harm that armed conflict does to nature. It comes from direct attack, pollution, deliberate environmental warfare or the breakdown of normal protection.

## Key Points to Remember
- War harms the environment through destruction, pollution, environmental warfare, landmines, loss of governance and displacement. ENMOD, 1976 bans hostile environmental modification. Additional Protocol I, 1977 (Articles 35(3), 55) bans widespread, long-term and severe damage. Rome Statute, 1998 (Article 8(2)(b)(iv)): clearly excessive environmental damage in an attack is a war crime. Rio Principles 24 and 25 are soft law. The ILC Draft Principles were adopted in 2022. The threshold is high and enforcement is weak.
