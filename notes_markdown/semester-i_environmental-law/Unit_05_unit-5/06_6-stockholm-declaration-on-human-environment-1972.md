# 6. Stockholm Declaration on Human Environment, 1972
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5

## In One Minute
The United Nations Conference on the Human Environment met at Stockholm from 5 to 16 June 1972. It adopted the Stockholm Declaration with 26 Principles, an Action Plan with 109 recommendations, and led to the creation of UNEP. The Declaration is not a treaty. It is soft law. Yet it is the starting point of modern international environmental law, and it is named in two Indian Acts.

## Legal Provision
- Principle 21: In accordance with the UN Charter and the principles of international law, States have the sovereign right to exploit their own resources under their own environmental policies. They have the responsibility to ensure that activities within their jurisdiction or control do not damage the environment of other States or of areas beyond national jurisdiction. Principle 22: States shall cooperate to develop the international law on liability and compensation for victims of pollution. Principle 1: Man has the fundamental right to freedom, equality and adequate conditions of life in an environment of a quality that permits a life of dignity and well-being. He bears a solemn responsibility to protect and improve the environment for present and future generations. Policies of apartheid, racial segregation, discrimination and colonial or other oppression stand condemned. Principle 2: The natural resources of the earth, including air, water, land, flora and fauna and representative samples of natural ecosystems, must be safeguarded for present and future generations through careful planning or management. Principle 6: The discharge of toxic substances, and the release of heat, beyond the capacity of the environment to render them harmless must be halted, so that serious or irreversible damage is not inflicted on ecosystems. Principle 7: States shall take all possible steps to prevent pollution of the seas by substances that create hazards to human health, harm living resources and marine life, damage amenities or interfere with other legitimate uses of the sea. Principle 26: Man and his environment must be spared the effects of nuclear weapons and all other means of mass destruction. States must strive for prompt agreement on their elimination and complete destruction. Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981, preamble: records decisions taken at the Stockholm conference of June 1972, in which India participated. Those decisions were to preserve the quality of air and control air pollution. Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, preamble: records the same Stockholm decisions on protection and improvement of the human environment and the need to implement them.

## Meaning and Definition
The Stockholm Declaration is the statement of shared principles adopted at the Stockholm conference. It has a preamble and 26 numbered Principles. As a declaration, it states aims and standards without creating legal duties by itself. It is an example of soft law , which guides conduct without binding.

## Key Points to Remember
- Stockholm, 5 to 16 June 1972: preamble and 26 Principles. Action Plan: 109 recommendations. UNEP: General Assembly resolution of 15 December 1972. Principle 21: sovereign right to use resources, with responsibility not to harm others. Soft law, but named in the Air Act and the Environment (Protection) Act.
