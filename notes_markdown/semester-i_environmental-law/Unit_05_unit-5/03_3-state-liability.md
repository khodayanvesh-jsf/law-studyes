# 3. State Liability
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/state-liability/

## In One Minute
State liability asks when a State must answer to another State for environmental harm that began on its soil. The answer has two parts: the harm must be traced to the State's own conduct, and that conduct must break an international duty. The test is not automatic. The State is judged by what it did to prevent the harm. If it took reasonable care, it is much harder to blame it. If it knew of a risk and did nothing, it is much easier.

## Legal Provision
- Trail Smelter Arbitration (1938 and 1941): Canada answered for fumes from a privately owned smelter and had to pay compensation for past damage. Stockholm Declaration, 1972, Principle 22: States are to cooperate to develop the law on liability and compensation for victims of pollution.

## Meaning and Definition
State liability here means the international responsibility of a State, described under State responsibility . It arises when two things are present.

The conduct, an act or an omission , can be attributed to the State. That conduct breaches a duty the State owes under international law, such as the no-harm rule.

## Key Points to Remember
- Two elements: attributable conduct and breach of an international duty. Standard: due diligence, not automatic strict liability. A State can answer for a private polluter's harm. Remedies: stop the wrong, then restitution, compensation or satisfaction. Climate change: causation is hard to prove, but State responsibility rules apply (ICJ advisory opinion, 2025).
