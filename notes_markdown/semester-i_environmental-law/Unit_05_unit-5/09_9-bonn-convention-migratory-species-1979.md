# 9. Bonn Convention (Migratory Species) 1979
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/bonn-convention-migratory-species-1979/

## In One Minute
The Bonn Convention is the Convention on the Conservation of Migratory Species of Wild Animals. It was adopted at Bonn on 23 June 1979 and came into force on 1 November 1983. It covers all migratory wild animals, not only birds. A species that crosses many countries is safe only if every country on its route protects it. The Convention brings those countries together.

## Legal Provision
- Convention on the Conservation of Migratory Species of Wild Animals: adopted at Bonn on 23 June 1979, in force from 1 November 1983. Appendix I: migratory species that are endangered. Range States must prohibit taking them, with narrow exceptions such as scientific purposes or traditional subsistence use. Appendix II: migratory species with an unfavourable conservation status, or that would benefit from international cooperation. Range States shall endeavour to conclude agreements (Article II(3)(c)). Constitution of India, Article 253: Parliament may legislate to implement a treaty.

## Meaning and Definition
A migratory species is one that moves across national borders in its life cycle. A range State is a country in which the species lives or which it crosses.

The treaty is often wrongly called a convention on migratory birds. Its correct title speaks of migratory species of wild animals. It reaches whales, dolphins, sea turtles, bats and land mammals as well as birds.

## Key Points to Remember
- Adopted at Bonn, 23 June 1979; in force 1 November 1983. Covers all migratory wild animals, not only birds. Appendix I: strict protection. Appendix II: cooperation through agreements. India has been a Party since 1 November 1983. Art III(5): four exceptions to the ban on taking, each precise and limited in space and time. Art I: migratory species, Range State, taking, favourable status. Art XII(3): stricter domestic laws allowed.
