# 4. Customary International Law
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/customary-international-law/

## In One Minute
A treaty binds only the States that sign it. Customary international law is different. It binds all States, because they follow a practice and believe the law requires it. In environmental law, the no-harm rule is the standard example. Indian courts also treat some environmental principles as customary law, and they can then apply them at home.

## Legal Provision
- Article 38(1)(b) of the Statute of the International Court of Justice: lists international custom as evidence of a general practice accepted as law. Article 51(c) of the Constitution of India: the State shall endeavour to foster respect for international law and treaty obligations. Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum (1996): the Supreme Court held that sustainable development is part of customary international law. It added that a customary rule not contrary to domestic law is deemed incorporated in it.

## Meaning and Definition
A rule of customary international law needs two things.

A general and consistent practice of States. A belief that the practice is required by law, called opinio juris (belief that the practice is legally required).

Practice without that belief is only habit. A belief without practice is only a wish.

## Key Points to Remember
- Custom = general State practice + opinio juris. It binds all States without a treaty. No-harm rule: customary, from Trail Smelter. Vellore: sustainable development is customary; a customary rule not contrary to domestic law is deemed incorporated.
