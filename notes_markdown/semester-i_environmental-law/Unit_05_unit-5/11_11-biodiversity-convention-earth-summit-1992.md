# 11. Biodiversity Convention (Earth Summit), 1992
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5

## In One Minute
The Convention on Biological Diversity is the main treaty on life on Earth in all its variety. Its text was adopted at Nairobi on 22 May 1992 and opened for signature at the Rio Earth Summit on 5 June 1992. It has three aims: conserve biodiversity, use it sustainably, and share its benefits fairly. The third aim answers the problem of biopiracy. India gives effect to the treaty through the Biological Diversity Act, 2002.

## Legal Provision
- Convention on Biological Diversity: adopted at Nairobi on 22 May 1992, opened for signature at Rio de Janeiro on 5 June 1992 and in force from 29 December 1993. Three objectives: conservation of biological diversity, sustainable use of its components, and fair and equitable sharing of the benefits from genetic resources. India and the Convention: India signed on 5 June 1992 and ratified on 18 February 1994. It became a Party on 19 May 1994 (CBD Secretariat list of Parties). Biological Diversity Act, 2002, preamble: records that India is a Party to the Convention, signed at Rio on 5 June 1992 and in force from 29 December 1993. The preamble adds that the Convention reaffirms the sovereign rights of States over their biological resources.

## Meaning and Definition
Biological diversity is the variety of life: the range of species, the genes within them and the ecosystems they form. The Convention covers all of it, not one type of habitat.

It was one of two binding treaties signed at the Rio Earth Summit of June 1992. The other was the Convention on climate change. Together they were signed by 153 nations, according to the Vellore judgment.

## Key Points to Remember
- Adopted 22 May 1992; opened for signature 5 June 1992; in force 29 December 1993. Three objectives: conservation, sustainable use, fair and equitable benefit sharing. States have sovereign rights over their biological resources. Cartagena (2000, in force 2003) and Nagoya (2010, in force 2014). India: Biological Diversity Act, 2002. Art 15: access needs prior informed consent and mutually agreed terms (15(5), 15(4)); Art 8(j): traditional knowledge; Art 14: impact assessment. Art 8: in-situ; Art 9: ex-situ. Art 19(3): protocol on living modified organisms. Art 37: no reservations.
