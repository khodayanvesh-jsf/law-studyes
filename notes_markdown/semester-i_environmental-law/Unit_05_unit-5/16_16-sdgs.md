## In One Minute
The Sustainable Development Goals, or SDGs, are 17 goals with 169 targets. The UN General Assembly adopted them in September 2015 as part of the 2030 Agenda. They are meant to guide action for 15 years, to 2030. They are not a treaty. They are a political commitment, so they count as soft law. Yet they shape budgets, policy and the way courts discuss sustainable development.

## Legal Provision
- General Assembly resolution 70/1, "Transforming our world: the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development": adopted on 25 September 2015 without a vote. The Goals and targets: 17 goals and 169 targets, intended to guide action over the next 15 years. NITI Aayog: the Indian body that publishes the SDG India Index, which monitors the performance of States and Union Territories on the goals.

## Meaning and Definition
The Sustainable Development Goals are a set of global goals for ending poverty, protecting the planet and building peaceful societies. They apply to all countries, rich and poor, and are linked so that progress on one helps others.

## Key Points to Remember
- Adopted 25 September 2015; General Assembly resolution 70/1. 17 goals, 169 targets, to guide action to 2030. Soft law, not a treaty. India: NITI Aayog and the SDG India Index. The 5Ps: People, Planet, Prosperity, Peace, Partnership. In effect from 1 January 2016.
