# 2. Transboundary Pollution
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester I) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5

## In One Minute
Transboundary pollution is pollution that starts in one State and harms people, land or water in another State, or in areas that belong to no State. Smoke across a border, a poisoned river and a thinning ozone layer are all examples. An Indian court cannot order a factory in another country to stop. So the answer lies in international law. Its oldest rule is the no-harm rule: a State must not let its territory be used to cause serious harm to a neighbour.

## Legal Provision
- Trail Smelter Arbitration (1938 and 1941): no State may use its territory, or let it be used, to cause serious injury by fumes in another State. The injury must be shown by clear and convincing evidence. Stockholm Declaration, 1972, Principle 21: States have the sovereign right to use their own resources. They also have a responsibility to ensure that activities within their control do not damage the environment of other States or of areas beyond national jurisdiction. Rio Declaration, 1992, Principle 2: repeats the same idea of State responsibility. Customary international law: the no-harm rule binds States even without a treaty.

## Meaning and Definition
Transboundary pollution is harm to the environment of one State, or of an area beyond any State, caused by a source in the territory or control of another State. It has two shapes.

Point-to-point harm: the source and the victim can both be named and lie fairly close. Example: smoke from a border factory, or waste dumped into a river that flows into the next country. Global harm: the pollutant mixes into the air or the sea first, and the damage appears everywhere. Examples are ozone depletion and climate change .

How a transboundary pollution claim travels How to read this: follow the boxes from top to bottom. Each step leads to the next. The last box is the usual outcome. 1. A source in State A releases fumes or waste. 2. The pollution crosses into State B. 3. State B shows serious harm with clear evidence. 4. State B claims against State A under the no-harm rule. 5. Result Talks, a treaty or arbitration decide on stopping the harm and on compensation.

## Key Points to Remember
- Transboundary pollution: harm in one State, or beyond any State, from a source in another State. Two shapes: point-to-point and global. No-harm rule: Trail Smelter (1938 and 1941); Stockholm Principle 21. The State answers for failing to control private polluters; the standard is due diligence. Global harm is handled by treaties, not by claims after the event.
