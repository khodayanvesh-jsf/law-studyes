# 1. Art. 48A and Art. 51A(g) of the Constitution of India
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester I) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/art-48a-and-art-51a-g-of-the-constitution-of-india/

## In One Minute
Two short provisions put the environment into the Constitution. Article 48A tells the State to protect and improve the environment. Article 51A(g) makes the same task a fundamental duty of every citizen. Both were inserted in 1976 and took effect on 3 January 1977. Neither gives a court power to order anything on its own. Courts use them to give content to Article 21, which can be enforced.

## Legal Provision
- Article 48A (Part IV): "The State shall endeavour to protect and improve the environment and to safeguard the forests and wild life of the country." Article 51A(g) (Part IVA): it is the duty of every citizen of India "to protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers and wild life, and to have compassion for living creatures". Article 37: the provisions of Part IV are not enforceable by any court. They are still fundamental in governing the country, and the State must apply them in making laws. Source of both: the Constitution (Forty-second Amendment) Act, 1976, sections 10 and 11, in force from 3 January 1977.

## Meaning and Definition
Article 48A is a Directive Principle of State Policy . It is an instruction to the State, meaning the governments and legislatures, to make the environment a goal of policy. Article 51A(g) is one of the Fundamental Duties . It is an instruction to each citizen.

The two provisions cover different ground. Article 48A adds forests and wild life to the State's task. Article 51A(g) adds lakes, rivers and a duty of kindness to animals.

## Key Points to Remember
- Article 48A: Part IV, binds the State, not enforceable (Article 37). Article 51A(g): Part IVA, binds the citizen, names no penalty. Both inserted by the 42nd Amendment, 1976, in force from 3 January 1977. Courts read both with Article 21. Article 51A(g) includes compassion for living creatures.
