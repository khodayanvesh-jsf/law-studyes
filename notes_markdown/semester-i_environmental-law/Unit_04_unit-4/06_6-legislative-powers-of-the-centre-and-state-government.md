# 6. Legislative powers of the Centre and State Government
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester I) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/legislative-powers-of-the-centre-and-state-government/

## In One Minute
"Environment" is not a subject in any of the three Lists of the Seventh Schedule. Environmental topics are spread across them. Forests and wild animals are on the Concurrent List. Water is on the State List. Parliament can still legislate on water by State consent (Article 252) or to carry out an international decision (Article 253). If a State law and a Central law clash on a Concurrent List subject, the Central law prevails unless the State law was reserved for the President and received assent (Article 254).

## Legal Provision
- Article 246: Parliament has exclusive power over the Union List (List I). A State Legislature has exclusive power over the State List (List II). Both can legislate on the Concurrent List (List III). Article 248: Parliament has exclusive power over any matter not in the Concurrent or State List. Entry 97 of the Union List says the same. Article 252: if the Legislatures of two or more States pass resolutions asking for it, Parliament may legislate on a State matter. Other States may adopt that Act later. Article 253: Parliament may make any law for implementing a treaty, agreement or convention, or a decision made at an international conference. Article 254: a State law repugnant to a Central law on a Concurrent List subject is void to the extent of the repugnancy. Article 254(2) saves it if it was reserved for the President and he assents . Parliament can still override it later. Relevant entries: List I entries 13, 14, 54 and 56. List II entries 5, 6, 17, 18 and 23. List III entries 17, 17A and 17B.

## Meaning and Definition
Legislative competence is the power of a legislature to make a law on a subject. A law made outside that power is invalid, whatever its aim. This is why the first question about any environmental statute is which legislature could make it.

The three Lists work as follows. The Union List is for Parliament alone. The State List is for States alone. The Concurrent List is for both.

Where environmental subjects sit How to read this: start at the top box and follow the lines to the three Lists. Each leaf names an environmental subject and its entry. Seventh Schedule Union List Entry 56: inter-State rivers. Entry 54: mines, as Parliament declares. Entries 13 and 14: conferences and treaties. State List Entry 17: water. Entry 6: public health. Entry 18: land. Entry 23: mines. Concurrent List Entry 17: cruelty to animals. Entry 17A: forests. Entry 17B: wild animals and birds.

## Key Points to Remember
- Environment is not a listed subject; its parts sit in all three Lists. Entries 17A and 17B: Concurrent List, from 3 January 1977. Water: State List entry 17; Water Act through Article 252. Article 253: laws to implement treaties and international decisions. Article 254: Central law prevails over repugnant State law, unless the President assents.
