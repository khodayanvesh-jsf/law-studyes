# 3. Right to development
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester I) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/right-to-development/

## In One Minute
The right to development comes from international documents, not from a Fundamental Right in Part III of the Constitution. The UN Declaration of 1986 calls it an inalienable human right. Indian courts do not treat development and the environment as enemies. They apply sustainable development to allow a project only with safeguards. The precautionary principle and the polluter pays principle are its essential features.

## Legal Provision
- Declaration on the Right to Development, 1986: adopted by UN General Assembly resolution 41/128 on 4 December 1986. Article 1(1) says every person and all peoples may take part in and benefit from development. Rio Declaration, 1992, Principle 3: the right to development must be fulfilled so as to meet equitably the developmental and environmental needs of present and future generations. Constitution: no Article in Part III lists a right to development. Articles 21, 48A and 51A(g) supply the environmental side of the balance. National Green Tribunal Act, 2010, section 20: the Tribunal must apply sustainable development, the precautionary principle and the polluter pays principle.

## Meaning and Definition
The right to development is the claim of people to share in economic and social progress. The 1986 Declaration treats it as a human right. It is a claim in international law, not a listed right in Indian constitutional law.

Indian courts handle the claim through sustainable development . In Vellore, the Court described it as a balancing concept between ecology and development. It also quoted the Brundtland definition: development that meets the needs of the present without harming the ability of future generations to meet theirs.
