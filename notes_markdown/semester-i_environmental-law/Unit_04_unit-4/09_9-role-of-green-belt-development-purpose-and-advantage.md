# 9. Role of green belt development purpose and advantage
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester I) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/role-of-green-belt-development/

## In One Minute
A green belt is a strip or zone of trees and plants that is planted and kept on purpose. It sits around an industry, between an industrial area and homes, or around a growing city. It traps dust, softens noise and separates land uses. A consent condition, court order or town plan usually requires it. It supports pollution control but does not replace it.

## Legal Provision
- Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, section 5: the Central Government may direct the closure, prohibition or regulation of any industry, operation or process. Water Act, 1974, section 25: previous consent of the State Board for an industry that discharges effluent. Air Act, 1981, section 21: previous consent for an industrial plant in an air pollution control area. Twelfth Schedule (Article 243W): municipal functions include urban planning (item 1), regulation of land-use (item 2) and urban forestry, protection of the environment and promotion of ecological aspects (item 8). Constitution, Articles 48A and 51A(g): the State's directive and the citizen's duty to protect the environment, the broad basis for such measures.

## Meaning and Definition
A green belt is a deliberately planted and maintained zone of trees and plants, required as a planning or regulatory condition. It differs from a park or a tree-planting drive because the law or an authority requires it for a stated purpose.

## Key Points to Remember
- A green belt is a planted zone required for a stated purpose. Advantages: filters dust, softens noise, separates land uses, gives habitat. Required by consent conditions, court orders and town planning. Taj Trapezium: NEERI's Green Belt Development Plan. It supports, but does not replace, control at the source.
