# 4. restriction on freedom of trade, profession, occupation for the protection of environment
**Subject:** Environmental Law (Semester I) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/environmental-law/restriction-on-freedom-of-trade-profession-occupation/

## In One Minute
Every citizen has a freedom to practise a profession or carry on any trade or business. This is Article 19(1)(g). The freedom is not absolute. Article 19(6) lets the State make laws that put reasonable restrictions on it in the interests of the general public. Environmental laws use this power. They require consent before a factory starts, allow closure of a polluting unit, and set clean-up duties. A restriction must be made by law and must be reasonable.

## Legal Provision
- Article 19(1)(g): all citizens have the right "to practise any profession, or to carry on any occupation, trade or business". Article 19(6): nothing in sub-clause (g) stops the State from making a law that imposes, "in the interests of the general public, reasonable restrictions" on the right. Article 19(6)(i) and (ii): the State may also make laws on professional qualifications. It may also make laws on a trade or service run by the State or a State-owned corporation. Consent: section 25 of the Water Act and section 21 of the Air Act need the previous consent of the State Board. Directions: section 5 of the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 and section 33A of the Water Act can direct the closure, prohibition or regulation of an industry.

## Meaning and Definition
A reasonable restriction is a limit on a freedom that is not excessive or arbitrary compared with the harm it prevents. Protecting the environment serves the general public. A law that controls polluting trades can therefore fall inside Article 19(6).

The public has an interest in clean air and water, which Article 21 protects. Courts weigh trade against that interest.

Is an environmental restriction on business valid? How to read this: start at the top. Each box must be answered "yes" to move down. The text beside a box shows where the restriction fails. 1. Is the restriction made by a law? Article 19(6) No: a mere executive order has no statutory backing. 2. Is it in the interests of the general public? Protection of the environment No: it fails Article 19(6). 3. Is it reasonable and not excessive for the harm it prevents? Proportionality No: the court can set aside or reduce it. 4. Result The restriction is valid.

## Key Points to Remember
- Article 19(1)(g): freedom of profession, occupation, trade, business. Article 19(6): reasonable restrictions in the interests of the general public. Tests: made by law; public interest; reasonable. Section 5 of the 1986 Act allows closure, prohibition or regulation of an industry. Alembic: closure set aside as disproportionate.
