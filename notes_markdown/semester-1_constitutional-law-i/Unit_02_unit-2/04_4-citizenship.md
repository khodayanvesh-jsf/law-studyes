# 4. Citizenship
**Subject:** Constitutional Law – I (Semester 1) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/constitutional-law-i/citizenship/

## In One Minute
Articles 5 to 11 decide who was a citizen of India when the Constitution began on 26 January 1950. They deal with people living in India, migrants from and to Pakistan, and people of Indian origin abroad. After that, Parliament makes the continuing rules through the Citizenship Act, 1955. Exams ask for each Article with its dates, the modes of gaining and losing citizenship, and the line between citizens and other persons. Learn the dates 1 March 1947 and 19 July 1948.

## Legal Provision
- Article 5: at commencement, a person with domicile in India is a citizen if he meets one test. He was born in India, or either parent was born in India, or he was ordinarily resident in India for at least five years before commencement. Article 6: a migrant to India from the territory now in Pakistan is deemed a citizen on two conditions. He, or a parent or grandparent, was born in India as defined in the Government of India Act, 1935. A migrant before 19 July 1948 must have been ordinarily resident in India since migration. A migrant on or after that date must have been registered before commencement. Article 7: a person who migrated from India to Pakistan after 1 March 1947 is not deemed a citizen. The proviso exempts one who returned under a permit for resettlement or permanent return. He is treated, for Article 6(b), as having migrated to India after 19 July 1948. Article 8: a person of Indian origin ordinarily residing outside India is deemed a citizen if registered by an Indian diplomatic or consular representative. Article 9: a person who has voluntarily acquired the citizenship of a foreign State is not a citizen under Articles 5, 6 or 8. Article 10: citizens under these Articles continue to be citizens, subject to any law of Parliament. Article 11: nothing in these Articles takes away Parliament's power to make laws on the acquisition and termination of citizenship and all other matters relating to it. Citizenship Act, 1955: sections 3 to 7 (acquisition), sections 8 to 10 (loss), section 6A (Assam). These sections are cited from the Act and not from the repo Constitution data.

## Meaning and Definition
Citizenship is the legal status of belonging to the Indian State as a full member. Part II of the Constitution fixes who were citizens on the day it began, and Article 11 leaves the rest to Parliament. India has one citizenship for the whole country, not separate State citizenships.

Some rights depend on this status. Article 19 gives its freedoms to "all citizens". Article 21 protects "no person". The difference decides which rights a foreigner can claim.

## Key Points to Remember
- Article 5: domicile plus birth, parent's birth or five years' residence. Article 6: migrants from Pakistan; the dividing date is 19 July 1948. Article 7: migrants to Pakistan after 1 March 1947 are not citizens, with a proviso for returnees. Article 8: persons of Indian origin abroad, registered by consular officers. Article 9: voluntary foreign citizenship ends citizenship; Article 11: Parliament regulates by law. Citizenship Act, 1955: sections 3 to 7 gain; sections 8 to 10 loss. Louis De Raedt (1991): domicile needs intention to settle; foreigners have Article 21 only.
