# 2. Preamble to Indian Constitution
**Subject:** Constitutional Law – I (Semester 1) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/constitutional-law-i/preamble-to-indian-constitution/

## In One Minute
The Preamble is the opening statement of the Constitution. It says who made it, what kind of State India is, and what the Constitution secures for citizens: justice, liberty, equality and fraternity. Exams ask for its text, the meaning of each key word, the 1976 changes and its legal status. The status question needs two cases: Berubari and Kesavananda Bharati.

## Legal Provision
- Preamble: "WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC and to secure to all its citizens: JUSTICE, social, economic and political; LIBERTY of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship; EQUALITY of status and of opportunity; and to promote among them all FRATERNITY assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation; IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION." 42nd Amendment, 1976 (in force from 3 January 1977): replaced "SOVEREIGN DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC" with "SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC", and "Unity of the Nation" with "unity and integrity of the Nation". Article 394: only a few Articles came into force on 26 November 1949. The rest, including the Preamble, came into force on 26 January 1950. Article 368: the amending power. The question is whether it reaches the Preamble. Articles 14 to 18, 19, 25 to 28, 38 and 39: the provisions through which the Preamble's ideals work in practice.

## Meaning and Definition
A preamble is an introductory statement of the purpose and source of a document. The Preamble to the Indian Constitution has four parts.

Source of authority: "We, the people of India". Nature of the State: sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic. Objectives: justice, liberty, equality and fraternity. Enacting clause: the date of adoption and the words "adopt, enact and give to ourselves this Constitution".

The Preamble does not give rights or powers by itself. Courts use it to understand the Articles. The flow below shows how.

## Key Points to Remember
- Source: the Objectives Resolution (moved 13 December 1946, adopted 22 January 1947). Adopted 26 November 1949; in force 26 January 1950 (Article 394). 1976 (42nd Amendment) added "socialist", "secular" and "integrity". Objectives: justice, liberty, equality, fraternity. Berubari (1960): key to the makers' minds, not a part. Kesavananda Bharati (1973): part of the Constitution. It can be amended under Article 368 within the basic structure. It guides the reading of ambiguous words and cannot confer or enlarge a power.
