# 6. Definition of State
**Subject:** Constitutional Law – I (Semester 1) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/constitutional-law-i/definition-of-state/

## In One Minute
Most Fundamental Rights are claimed against "the State". Article 12 says what the State includes, and the words "other authorities" have been argued in court for decades. A body counts as State if the Government controls it deeply, funds it and gives it a special position. Exams ask for the text of Article 12 and the chain of cases that built the test. Learn the cases in order, with the one idea each added.

## Legal Provision
- Article 12: in Part III, unless the context otherwise requires, "the State" includes the Government and Parliament of India, the Government and the Legislature of each of the States, and all local or other authorities within the territory of India or under the control of the Government of India. Article 36: in Part IV, "the State" has the same meaning as in Part III. Article 13(2): the State shall not make any law which takes away or abridges the rights in Part III. Articles 32 and 226: the Supreme Court and High Courts can issue writs to enforce Part III. Article 226 also covers "any other purpose". General Clauses Act, 1897, section 3(31): defines "local authority" as a municipal committee, district board, body of port commissioners or other authority legally entitled to, or entrusted by the Government with, the control or management of a municipal or local fund.

## Meaning and Definition
The State in Article 12 has four parts. The first two are plain: the Union Government and Parliament, and each State's Government and Legislature. The third and fourth, "local authorities" and "other authorities", need explanation.

A body that is State must obey Part III. A citizen can enforce a Fundamental Right against it under Article 32 or Article 226. A body outside Article 12 cannot be sued for a Fundamental Right breach.

## Key Points to Remember
- Article 12: Government and Parliament of India, Government and Legislature of each State, local or other authorities. Mohan Lal (1967): not limited to sovereign functions. Sukhdev Singh (1975): statutory corporations; Mathew J's instrumentality theory. R.D. Shetty (1979) and Ajay Hasia (1981): factors, weighed together. Pradeep Kumar Biswas (2002): domination or control, particular and pervasive; Sabhajit Tewary overruled. Zee Telefilms (2005): BCCI is not State; Article 226 may still apply.
