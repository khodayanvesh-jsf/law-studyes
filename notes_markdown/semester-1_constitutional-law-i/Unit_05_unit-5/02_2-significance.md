## In One Minute
This note is about the significance of the Directive Principles of State Policy in Part IV. No court enforces them directly, yet they matter in four ways. They set the goals of every government, guide the reading of Fundamental Rights, lead to new laws, and support the validity of some laws. Exams ask for the significance of the Directive Principles together with their enforceability in court. Give the four uses, then the limit set by Article 37.

## Legal Provision
- Article 37: the provisions of Part IV are not enforceable by any court. The principles are nevertheless fundamental in the governance of the country. It is the duty of the State to apply them in making laws. Article 21A: the State shall provide free and compulsory education to all children of the age of six to fourteen years, in such manner as the State may, by law, determine. The 86th Amendment inserted it, in force from 1 April 2010. Article 39A: the State shall secure that the legal system promotes justice on a basis of equal opportunity. It shall provide free legal aid so that no citizen is denied justice because of economic or other disabilities. The 42nd Amendment inserted it (3 January 1977). Part IX (Articles 243 to 243O): the Panchayats. The 73rd Amendment inserted this Part (24 April 1993). Article 40 had asked the State to organise village panchayats.

## Meaning and Definition
The Directive Principles of State Policy are significant because they state what kind of society the Constitution wants. Their value lies in guiding the State, not in giving a person a right to sue. The Supreme Court has said they carry equal weight with Part III in the scheme of the Constitution, even though the method of enforcing them differs.

The significance can be grouped under four heads. The first is that they are the standing goals of governance. The second is that courts use them to read the rights in Part III. The third is that they lead to ordinary laws that courts can enforce. The fourth is that they help to justify some laws.

## Key Points to Remember
- Four uses: goals, aid to reading rights, source of laws, support for laws. No direct enforcement by any court (Article 37). Minerva Mills: Parts III and IV are like two wheels of a chariot. Article 39A led to the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987. Article 21A and the replacement of Article 45 came with the 86th Amendment.
