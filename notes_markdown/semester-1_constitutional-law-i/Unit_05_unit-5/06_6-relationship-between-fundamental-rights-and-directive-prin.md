# 6. Relationship between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles
**Subject:** Constitutional Law – I (Semester 1) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/constitutional-law-i/relationship-between-fundamental-rights-and-directive-principles/

## In One Minute
Part III gives enforceable rights. Part IV sets goals that no court enforces. The two clashed in courts and in Parliament for thirty years. The result is a middle path: neither Part can be given absolute priority, and Article 31C protects only laws that give effect to Article 39(b) and (c). This is a favourite exam topic. Questions ask for the distinctions between the two Parts, the relationship, and the need for balance with cases. Learn the sequence of cases and Amendments with dates.

## Legal Provision
- Article 13(2): the State shall not make any law which takes away or abridges the rights conferred by Part III. Any law made in contravention is void to the extent of the contravention. Article 13(4): nothing in Article 13 applies to an amendment made under Article 368. The 24th Amendment inserted it (5 November 1971). Article 368(1) and (3): Parliament may amend any provision of the Constitution by addition, variation or repeal. Nothing in Article 13 applies to an amendment made under this Article. The 24th Amendment added both. Article 37: the provisions of Part IV are not enforceable by any court. Article 39(b) and (c): ownership and control of material resources shared to serve the common good, and no concentration of wealth and means of production to the common detriment. Article 31C: notwithstanding Article 13, no law giving effect to the policy of the State towards securing the principles in Part IV is void for conflict with Article 14 or 19. The 25th Amendment inserted it (20 April 1972). The 42nd Amendment widened it, and Minerva Mills declared that widening invalid. Article 31C (rest of the text): a law containing a declaration that it gives effect to such policy cannot be questioned in court on the ground that it does not do so. A law made by a State Legislature gets this protection only if it is reserved for the President and receives his assent. Article 300A: no person shall be deprived of property save by authority of law. The 44th Amendment inserted it (20 June 1979), after removing the right to property from Part III.

## Meaning and Definition
The relationship asks which Part wins when a right in Part III meets a goal in Part IV, and how far Parliament may change Part III to reach the goals. The law moved through three stages. First, Part III was held to prevail. Next, Parliament tried to give Part IV priority by amending the Constitution. Finally, the Court held that the balance between the Parts is part of the basic structure .

## Key Points to Remember
- Champakam (1951): Part III prevails; the First Amendment added Article 15(4). Golak Nath (1967): 6:5, Parliament cannot abridge Part III; prospective overruling. 24th and 25th Amendments (1971): Article 13(4), Article 368(3), Article 31C. Kesavananda Bharati (1973): amending power but not over the basic structure. 42nd Amendment (1977) widened Article 31C; Minerva Mills (1980) struck it down. Today Article 31C covers laws for Article 39(b) and (c) only. The balance between Parts III and IV is part of the basic structure.
