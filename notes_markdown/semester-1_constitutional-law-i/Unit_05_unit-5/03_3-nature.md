## In One Minute
This note is about the nature of the Directive Principles of State Policy in Part IV. A court cannot enforce them. They speak to the State, not to individuals. They tell the State what to achieve, and their wording ranges from firm to hopeful. Exams ask for the features of the Directive Principles and their enforceability. Name four features and show how a principle becomes enforceable through a law.

## Legal Provision
- Article 37: the provisions of Part IV are not enforceable by any court. The principles are nevertheless fundamental in the governance of the country. It is the duty of the State to apply them in making laws. Article 36: "the State" in Part IV has the same meaning as in Part III. Article 41: the State shall make effective provision for the right to work, education and public assistance, "within the limits of its economic capacity and development". Article 39A: the State "shall secure" that the legal system promotes justice on a basis of equal opportunity. Article 45: the State "shall endeavour to provide" early childhood care and education for all children until they complete the age of six years.

## Meaning and Definition
The nature of a rule is the kind of rule it is. The Directive Principles of State Policy are policy instructions to the State. They are non-justiciable . They state goals, not claims. A person cannot go to court and say that the State has broken Article 41.

Four features follow from the text. Each one answers a question an examiner may ask. Is it enforceable? To whom is it addressed? What does it ask the State to do? How does it become real?

How a Directive Principle becomes enforceable How to read this: follow the boxes from top to bottom. Each box is a step. The text beside it shows what stops or follows. 1. A principle in Part IV A court cannot enforce it (Article 37). 2. The legislature makes a law The State must apply the principle in making laws (Article 37). 3. An ordinary statute exists A person can claim the rights in the statute. 4. A court enforces the statute The principle now has legal force.

## Key Points to Remember
- Not enforceable by any court (Article 37). Addressed to the State, with "State" as in Part III (Article 36). Positive aims; the wording varies from "shall secure" to "shall endeavour". Article 41 ties its aims to economic capacity and development. A principle gains force when a statute carries it out.
