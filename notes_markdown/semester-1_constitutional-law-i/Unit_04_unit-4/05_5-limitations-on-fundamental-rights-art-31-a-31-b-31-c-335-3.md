# 5. Limitations on Fundamental Rights (Art.31-A,31-B, 31 C, 335, 358 & 359)
**Subject:** Constitutional Law – I (Semester 1) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/constitutional-law-i/limitations-on-fundamental-rights-art-31-a-31-b-31-c-335-358-359/

## In One Minute
Fundamental Rights are not absolute. Articles 31A, 31B and 31C shield certain laws, mainly land reform and social-justice laws, from challenge under some Part III rights. Article 335 asks the State to consider Scheduled Caste and Scheduled Tribe claims to jobs without harming efficiency. Articles 358 and 359 cut down rights during an Emergency. Exams ask how far each shield goes and what the courts have cut back. Name I.R. Coelho for Article 31B, Minerva Mills for Article 31C and ADM Jabalpur for Article 359.

## Legal Provision
- Article 31A(1): five kinds of law are not void for conflict with Article 14 or Article 19. They are laws for acquiring an estate, taking over property management for a limited time, amalgamating corporations, changing the rights of managing agents, directors or shareholders, and changing mining leases. A State law needs the President's assent. Article 31B: no Act in the Ninth Schedule is void because it conflicts with any Part III right. This holds despite any court judgment, subject to the power of a Legislature to amend or repeal it. Article 31C: a law giving effect to the principles in Article 39(b) or (c) is not void for conflict with Article 14 or Article 19. A State law needs the President's assent. Article 335: the claims of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes shall be considered in appointments, consistently with the maintenance of efficiency of administration. A proviso allows relaxation in qualifying marks, lower standards of evaluation and reservation in promotion. Article 358: during an Emergency for war or external aggression, Article 19 does not restrict the State's power to make laws or act. Article 359: during a Proclamation of Emergency, the President may suspend the right to move a court to enforce Part III rights named in the order. Articles 20 and 21 cannot be named. Article 352(1): an Emergency can be proclaimed if the security of India is threatened by war, external aggression or armed rebellion. Article 31C (full text): notwithstanding Article 13, no law giving effect to the State's policy of securing "all or any of the principles laid down in Part IV" is void for conflict with Article 14 or Article 19. The text also has words saying a law with a declaration that it gives effect to such policy cannot be questioned in court. A State law needs the President's assent. The Constitution's footnotes record three points. The words "all or any of the principles laid down in Part IV" were put in by section 4 of the 42nd Amendment, and the footnote says that section was declared invalid in Minerva Mills (AIR 1980 SC 1789). "Article 14 or article 19" replaced "article 14, article 19 or article 31" by the 44th Amendment. The declaration words were struck down in Kesavananda Bharati (AIR 1973 SC 1461). Article 39(b) and (c): the State must direct its policy towards securing (b) that ownership and control of the material resources of the community are so distributed as best to serve the common good, and (c) that the economic system does not result in concentration of wealth and means of production to the common detriment. Article 31 (repealed) and Article 300A: Article 31, on compulsory acquisition of property, was omitted by the 44th Amendment with effect from 20 June 1979. The same Amendment inserted Article 300A: "No person shall be deprived of his property save by authority of law." Property is therefore no longer a Fundamental Right. Article 16(4), (4A) and (4B): clause (4) lets the State reserve appointments or posts for a backward class that, in the State's opinion, is not adequately represented in the services. Clause (4A) lets the State reserve posts "in matters of promotion, with consequential seniority" for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes that are not adequately represented. It was inserted in 1995, and the words on consequential seniority were put in by the 85th Amendment (2001), operative from 17 June 1995. Clause (4B), inserted in 2000, lets the State treat unfilled reserved vacancies of a year as a separate class of vacancies for later years. That class is not counted with the vacancies of the year in which it is filled when the fifty per cent ceiling is applied. Article 352(3) to (7) (how an Emergency is proclaimed and continued): the President cannot issue a Proclamation unless the Union Cabinet's decision has been communicated to him in writing. Every Proclamation must be laid before each House of Parliament. It lapses after one month unless both Houses approve it by resolutions. An approved Proclamation lasts six months and can be continued for further periods of six months by resolutions of both Houses. Each resolution needs a majority of the total membership of the House and a two-thirds majority of members present and voting. The President must revoke the Proclamation if the House of the People passes a resolution disapproving it. One-tenth of the members of the House of the People can force a special sitting within fourteen days to consider such a resolution. Article 359(1A), (1B), (2) and (3): while an order under clause (1) is in operation, the rights named in it do not restrict the State's power to make laws or take executive action. A law so made ceases to have effect when the order ends, except for things already done. Clause (1B) says this protection does not apply to a law that lacks a recital that it relates to the Emergency, or to executive action not taken under such a law. The order may extend to the whole of India or a part. Every order must be laid before each House of Parliament as soon as may be. Article 358(2): Article 358(1) does not apply to a law that lacks a recital that it relates to the Proclamation of Emergency, or to executive action not taken under such a law. Article 33: Parliament may by law decide how far Part III rights apply to members of the Armed Forces, the forces charged with maintaining public order, persons in intelligence or counter-intelligence bureaux, and persons in telecommunication systems set up for these forces. The rights may be restricted or taken away "so as to ensure the proper discharge of their duties and the maintenance of discipline among them". The present text was substituted by the 50th Amendment (1984). Article 34: Parliament may by law indemnify any person serving the Union or a State, or any other person, for an act done to maintain or restore order in an area where martial law was in force. It may also validate any sentence, punishment, forfeiture or other act under martial law in that area. This holds despite the other provisions of Part III.

## Meaning and Definition
Each of these Articles takes a class of laws or a period of time out of the full reach of Part III. They show that the Constitution itself limits Fundamental Rights . The courts have asked a second question: can a shield be used to destroy the Constitution's basic structure?

Article Which laws are shielded From which rights 31A Estate acquisition and four other classes Articles 14 and 19 31B Acts in the Ninth Schedule All of Part III 31C Laws giving effect to Article 39(b) or (c) Articles 14 and 19

Is a Ninth Schedule law protected after I.R. Coelho? How to read this: go down the boxes in order. A "No" answer sends you to the result beside the box. 1. Was the law put in the Ninth Schedule on or after 24 April 1973? Kesavananda Bharati date No: it keeps the protection of Article 31B. 2. Does it take away or abridge a Part III right? Article 31B No: there is nothing to strike down. 3. Does that damage the basic structure, tested through Article 21 read with Articles 14 and 19? I.R. Coelho No: the law stands. Yes: the law is invalid despite the Ninth Schedule.

## Key Points to Remember
- Article 31A protects five classes of law from Articles 14 and 19. Article 31B protects Ninth Schedule laws, but those added on or after 24 April 1973 can be tested on the basic structure (I.R. Coelho, 2007). Article 31C covers only Article 39(b) and (c) laws; the 42nd Amendment's widening was struck down (Minerva Mills, 1980). Article 335: efficiency of administration; the proviso came from the 82nd Amendment. Article 358: automatic, Article 19 only, war or external aggression only. Article 359: Presidential order; never Articles 20 and 21. ADM Jabalpur (1976) is the cautionary case.
