# 8. Government of India Act 1935
**Subject:** Constitutional Law – I (Semester 1) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/constitutional-law-i/government-of-india-act-1935/

## In One Minute
The Government of India Act 1935 was the last big British constitutional law for India. It ended dyarchy in the provinces, gave provinces self-rule, and divided law-making powers into three Lists. The federation it planned never began. Many parts of the Indian Constitution trace back to it. Exams ask for its main features and what the Constitution took from it.

## Legal Provision
- The text of these provisions is on this site's Bare Act page.
- Section 5: the Governors' Provinces and the acceding Indian States were to be united in a Federation of India once enough States acceded . This federation never came into force. Section 12: the Governor-General had special responsibilities, such as preventing a grave menace to peace, safeguarding financial stability, and protecting minorities and the rights of Indian States. Section 18: a Federal Legislature of the Crown (represented by the Governor-General) and two Chambers, the Council of State and the Federal Assembly. Section 52(3): a Governor uses his individual judgment where a special responsibility is involved. Section 93: a Governor could take over the powers of the provincial government by Proclamation, with the Governor-General's concurrence , but not the powers of a High Court. Section 100: legislative power was divided between the Federal Legislature and Provincial Legislatures through three Lists: Federal, Provincial and Concurrent. Article 395, Constitution of India: repeals the Act.

## Meaning and Definition
The Government of India Act 1935 was passed by the British Parliament after a long process. The steps were the Simon Commission , the Round Table Conferences in London from 1930 to 1932, and a White Paper in 1933. It was a very long Act, and much of its machinery was later carried into the Constitution.

## Key Points to Remember
- Background: Simon Commission, Round Table Conferences (1930 to 1932), White Paper (1933). Provincial autonomy from 1937; dyarchy ended in the provinces. The federation (section 5) never began. Three Lists (section 100) became the Seventh Schedule Lists. Federal Court (1937) was the forerunner of the Supreme Court. Section 93 is the forerunner of Article 356.
