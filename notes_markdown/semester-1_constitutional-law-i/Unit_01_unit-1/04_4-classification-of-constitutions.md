## In One Minute
Constitutions are sorted on separate scales: written or unwritten, rigid or flexible, federal or unitary, parliamentary or presidential, republic or monarchy. A constitution can sit at a different point on each scale. The Indian Constitution is written, partly rigid and partly flexible, federal with a strong unitary bias , parliamentary and republican. Exams ask for this exact set, so learn the reasons.

## Legal Provision
- Article 1(1): "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States." Article 3: Parliament may by law form new States and alter the areas, boundaries or names of States. Article 4(2): a law made under Articles 2 and 3 is not an amendment of the Constitution for the purposes of Article 368. Article 368(2): an amendment needs a majority of the total membership of each House and two-thirds of the members present and voting. Some changes also need approval by the Legislatures of not less than one-half of the States. Article 246 and the Seventh Schedule: the Union List, State List and Concurrent List divide legislative power. Articles 74 and 75(3): the Council of Ministers aids and advises the President and is collectively responsible to the House of the People. Articles 52 and 54: there is a President of India, elected by an electoral college.

## Meaning and Definition
To classify constitutions is to sort them by their main features. No single scale is enough. The common scales are given below.

Written or unwritten: is the fundamental law collected in one document? Rigid or flexible: how hard is it to amend? James Bryce used these two terms. Federal or unitary: is power divided between a centre and constituent units? Parliamentary or presidential: is the executive drawn from and answerable to the legislature? Republican or monarchical: is the head of state elected or hereditary?

## Key Points to Remember
- Scales: written/unwritten, rigid/flexible, federal/unitary, parliamentary/presidential, republic/monarchy. India is written, partly rigid and partly flexible, federal with unitary bias, parliamentary and a republic. Three ways to amend: simple majority outside Article 368, special majority, special majority plus State approval. Article 1 says "Union of States": no State may leave. Unitary features: Articles 3, 155, 249, 312, 352, 356. West Bengal v. Union of India (1963): not a federation by agreement.
