# 5. Privilege against Self-incrimination
**Subject:** Constitutional Law – I (Semester 1) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/constitutional-law-i/privilege-against-self-incrimination/

## In One Minute
Article 20(3) says no person accused of an offence shall be compelled to be a witness against himself. The Supreme Court reads compelled testimony as protected, and it treated the results of involuntary narcoanalysis, polygraph and brain mapping tests as testimonial in Selvi. Exams ask for the elements of the clause, M.P. Sharma and Selvi, and the statutory rules on specimens and confessions. Keep testimony and physical evidence apart.

## Legal Provision
- Article 20(3): no person accused of any offence shall be compelled to be a witness against himself. Article 359(1): during a Proclamation of Emergency, the right to move a court to enforce Part III rights may be suspended, except those in Articles 20 and 21. Section 180(2), Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023: a person questioned by the police must answer truly, except questions whose answers would tend to expose him to a criminal charge, a penalty or a forfeiture. Section 351(2) and (3), Sanhita: when the Court examines an accused to let him explain the evidence, no oath is administered. The accused is not liable to punishment for refusing to answer or for giving false answers. Sections 22 and 23, Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023: a confession caused by inducement, threat, coercion or promise from a person in authority is irrelevant. No confession made to a police officer can be proved against the accused. A confession made in police custody can be proved only if it is made in the immediate presence of a Magistrate.

## Meaning and Definition
Self-incrimination means giving incriminating information against oneself. The maxim is nemo tenetur se ipsum accusare (no one is bound to accuse himself). Article 20(3) turns it into a Fundamental Right with four elements.

The person is accused of an offence. He is compelled , so the statement is not voluntary. He is compelled to be a witness, which the cases read as testimonial compulsion . The testimony is against himself.

## Key Points to Remember
- Article 20(3) protects a person accused of an offence from compelled testimony. A Magistrate may order specimen signatures, finger impressions, handwriting or voice samples (section 349 of the Sanhita). A confession to a police officer cannot be proved against the accused (section 23 of the Adhiniyam). Results of involuntary narcoanalysis, polygraph and brain mapping tests attract Article 20(3) (Selvi, 2010). Self-incrimination is part of personal liberty under Article 21. Every person is covered. It cannot be suspended in an emergency (Article 359(1)).
