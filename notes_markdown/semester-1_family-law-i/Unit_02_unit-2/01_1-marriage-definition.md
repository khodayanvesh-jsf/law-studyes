# 1. Marriage - Definition
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester 1) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/marriage-definition/

## In One Minute
No Act defines Hindu marriage in one sentence. The definition has to be built from the classical law and from the sections of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955. Classical law. Marriage was a sacrament , "a holy union for the performance of religious duties" (Mulla). In Tikait Mon Mohini Jemadai v. Basanta Kumar Singh (1901) the Calcutta High Court said that "though marriage under the Hindu Law is a contract, it is also a sacrament"; it is "more religious than secular in character" and "the union is indissoluble ". Under the 1955 Act. Mulla's commentary defines a Hindu marriage as "a union of one man with one woman to the exclusion of all others" made by the customary rites and ceremonies of either party. Once made, it creates "a relation and a status not imposed or defined by contract but by law". It is a sacred, ceremonial and monogamous union, which the law can now declare void, annul or dissolve. What exams ask. The sacrament and contract debate; Tikait ; the ceremonies required by Section 7 ( Bhaurao , Dolly Rani ); the contract-like features added in 1955; the eight classical forms; and the civil marriage under the Special Marriage Act, 1954.

## Legal Provision
- The Act has no definition clause for "marriage". These sections together show what a Hindu marriage is in law.
- Section 5, Hindu Marriage Act, 1955. Conditions for a Hindu marriage. A marriage may be solemnized between any two Hindus, if the following conditions are fulfilled, namely:— (i) neither party has a spouse living at the time of the marriage; (ii) at the time of the marriage, neither party— (a) is incapable of giving a valid consent to it in consequence of unsoundness of mind; or (b) though capable of giving a valid consent, has been suffering from mental disorder of such a kind or to such an extent as to be unfit for marriage and the procreation of children; or (c) has been subject to recurrent attacks of insanity; (iii) the bridegroom has completed the age of twenty-one years and the bride, the age of eighteen years at the time of the marriage; (iv) the parties are not within the degrees of prohibited relationship unless the custom or usage governing each of them permits of a marriage between the two; (v) the parties are not sapindas of each other, unless the custom or usage governing each of them permits of a marriage between the two;
- Section 7. Ceremonies for a Hindu marriage. (1) A Hindu marriage may be solemnized in accordance with the customary rites and ceremonies of either party thereto. (2) Where such rites and ceremonies include the Saptapadi (that is, the taking of seven steps by the bridegroom and the bride jointly before the sacred fire), the marriage becomes complete and binding when the seventh step is taken.
- Section 4. Overriding effect of Act. Save as otherwise expressly provided in this Act,— (a) any text rule or interpretation of Hindu law or any custom or usage as part of that law in force immediately before the commencement of this Act shall cease to have effect with respect to any matter for which provision is made in this Act; (b) any other law in force immediately before the commencement of this Act shall cease to have effect in so far as it is inconsistent with any of the provisions contained in this Act.
- Section 8(5). Notwithstanding anything contained in this section, the validity of any Hindu marriage shall in no way be affected by the omission to make the entry.
- Section 29(4). Nothing contained in this Act shall be deemed to affect the provisions contained in the Special Marriage Act, 1954 (43 of 1954) with respect to marriages between Hindus solemnized under that Act, whether before or after the commencement of this Act.
- Section 12(2) proviso, Special Marriage Act, 1954. Provided that it shall not be complete and binding on the parties unless each party says to the other in the presence of the Marriage Officer and the three witnesses and in any language understood by the parties,—"I, (A), take thee (B), to be my lawful wife (or husband)".
- In simple words
- Section 5: a Hindu marriage can be made only between two Hindus who meet the conditions: no living spouse, sound mind, the minimum ages (21 and 18), and no prohibited relationship or sapinda relationship unless a custom governing each of them allows it. (Clause (vi), on the consent of the bride's guardian, was omitted in 1978.) Section 7: the marriage is made by the customary rites and ceremonies of either the bride's side or the bridegroom's side. If those rites include the saptapadi , the marriage is complete and binding when the seventh step is taken. Section 4: on every matter the Act deals with, the old texts, rules and customs of Hindu law stop applying. Other older laws stop applying only where they are inconsistent with the Act. Section 8(5): a Hindu marriage is not invalid only because it was not registered. Section 29(4): two Hindus may still marry under the Special Marriage Act, 1954, and that Act then governs their marriage. Special Marriage Act, Section 12(2): a civil marriage is complete only when each party makes the stated declaration to the other before the Marriage Officer and three witnesses.

## Meaning and Definition
The question this topic answers. What is a Hindu marriage in law: a religious sacrament, a contract , or something else?

Classical meaning. Mulla states the law before 1955 in these words: "Marriage, according to the Hindu law, is a holy union for the performance of religious duties. It is not a contract" (Mulla, section 427). The Introductory Note to the Hindu Marriage Act in Mulla adds: "In Hindu law marriage is treated as a samskara or a sacrament. It is the last of ten sacraments, enjoined by the Hindu religion for regeneration of men and obligatory in case of every Hindu who does not desire to adopt the life of a Sanyasi."

Meaning under the Act (Mulla's definition). Mulla's commentary on Section 5 says: "A Hindu marriage under the Act, it is submitted, is not entirely or necessarily a sacrament (sanskara) but a union of one man with one woman to the exclusion of all others satisfied by solemnization of the customary rites and ceremonies of either party essential for a marriage: and directly it exists, creates a relation and a status not imposed or defined by contract but by law." The same passage says: "It is also not possible to say that the legal concept of marriage under the Act is one of contract."

A union of one man with one woman. It is between a bridegroom and a bride (Sections 5(iii) and 7(2) use these words). To the exclusion of all others. It is monogamous: neither party may have a living spouse (Section 5(i)). Made by ceremonies. It must be solemnized by the customary rites and ceremonies of either party (Section 7). Without them there is no Hindu marriage. It creates a status fixed by law. The rights and duties of husband and wife come from law, not from any agreement between them.

Why the law has this rule. Marriage decides the status of the spouses, the legitimacy of children, maintenance and succession. The law therefore fixes how a marriage is made and what it means, and does not leave this to private agreement.

## Key Points to Remember
- The Hindu Marriage Act has no definition of marriage; its meaning comes from Sections 5 and 7. Classical law: "a holy union for the performance of religious duties. It is not a contract" (Mulla, s. 427). Tikait (1901): "though ... a contract, it is also a sacrament"; "the union is indissoluble"; a pre-marriage deed varying marital rights is against public policy. Mulla's definition under the Act: a union of one man with one woman to the exclusion of all others, by the customary rites of either party, creating a status fixed by law, not by contract. Bhaurao (1965): without proper ceremonies there is no "solemnized" marriage, even in the Gandharva form. Dolly Rani (2024): no ceremony, no marriage; registration is only proof. The Act added consent, nullity, divorce, restitution, judicial separation and remarriage. Eight classical forms; only Brahma and Asura recognised before 1955; the Act names no form. Special Marriage Act, 1954: civil marriage between any two persons, by declaration before the Marriage Officer and three witnesses.
