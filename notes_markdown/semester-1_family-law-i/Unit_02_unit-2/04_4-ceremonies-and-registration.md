# 4. Ceremonies and Registration
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester 1) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/ceremonies-and-registration/

## In One Minute
Ceremonies make the marriage. Under Section 7 of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 (HMA), a Hindu marriage may be solemnized in accordance with the customary rites and ceremonies of either party. Where those rites include the saptapadi (seven steps taken jointly by the bride and bridegroom before the sacred fire), the marriage becomes complete and binding when the seventh step is taken. Registration only proves the marriage. Section 8 lets the State make rules for entering marriages in a Hindu Marriage Register "for the purpose of facilitating the proof of Hindu marriages". Failure to register does not affect validity (Section 8(5)). Registration cannot create a marriage. In Dolly Rani (2024) the Supreme Court held that a certificate or registration without the ceremonies of Section 7 has no legal consequence; the couple never became husband and wife. Proof in bigamy cases. In Bhaurao (1965) the Supreme Court held that a second marriage is "solemnized" only if performed with the proper ceremonies; without proof of the essential ceremonies there is no bigamy. Alternative. Two Hindus may instead marry, or register an earlier ceremonial marriage, under the Special Marriage Act, 1954.

## Legal Provision
- Section 7, Hindu Marriage Act, 1955. Ceremonies for a Hindu marriage. (1) A Hindu marriage may be solemnized in accordance with the customary rites and ceremonies of either party thereto. (2) Where such rites and ceremonies include the Saptapadi (that is, the taking of seven steps by the bridegroom and the bride jointly before the sacred fire), the marriage becomes complete and binding when the seventh step is taken.
- Section 8. Registration of Hindu marriages. (1) For the purpose of facilitating the proof of Hindu marriages, the State Government may make rules providing that the parties to any such marriage may have the particulars relating to their marriage entered in such manner and subject to such conditions as may be prescribed in a Hindu Marriage Register kept for the purpose. (2) Notwithstanding anything contained in sub-section (1), the State Government may, if it is of opinion that it is necessary or expedient so to do, provide that the entering of the particulars referred to in sub-section (1) shall be compulsory in the State or in any part thereof, whether in all cases or in such cases as may be specified, and where any such direction has been issued, any person contravening any rule made in this behalf shall be punishable with fine which may extend to twenty-five rupees. (3) All rules made under this section shall be laid before the State Legislature, as soon as may be, after they are made. (4) The Hindu Marriage Register shall at all reasonable times be open for inspection, and shall be admissible as evidence of the statements therein contained and certified extracts therefrom shall, on application, be given by the Registrar on payment to him of the prescribed fee. (5) Notwithstanding anything contained in this section, the validity of any Hindu marriage shall in no way be affected by the omission to make the entry.
- In simple words:
- Section 7(1): the HMA does not list the ceremonies. It accepts the customary rites of the bride's side or the bridegroom's side. Section 7(2): if those rites include the saptapadi , the marriage is complete at the seventh step, not before. Section 8(1)–(3): the State makes the rules for a Hindu Marriage Register. The rules go before the State Legislature. Section 8(2): the State may make the entry compulsory; breaking such a rule is punishable with a fine of up to ₹25 only. Section 8(4): anyone may inspect the Register; its entries are admissible in evidence; certified copies are given on payment of a fee. Section 8(5): an unregistered marriage is still valid.

## Meaning and Definition
Solemnisation . To " solemnize " a marriage is "to celebrate the marriage with proper ceremonies and in due form" ( Bhaurao , adopting the Shorter Oxford Dictionary). "Merely going through certain ceremonies with the intention that the parties be taken to be married, will not make them ceremonies prescribed by law or approved by any established custom." Customary rites and ceremonies. The rites that the custom of the bride's or the bridegroom's community treats as essential. A custom must meet Section 3(a): continuously and uniformly observed for a long time, certain, not unreasonable or opposed to public policy, and, for a family custom, not discontinued. Registration. The entry of the particulars of a marriage in the Hindu Marriage Register kept under the State's rules. It is a record that helps prove the marriage.

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 7: customary rites of either party; the Act prescribes no fixed ceremony. Saptapadi: where part of the rites, the marriage is complete at the seventh step. Essential rites in a ceremonial marriage: invocation before the sacred fire and saptapadi, unless a proved custom changes them ( Bhaurao ; Mulla s. 437). Custom must be proved by definite evidence, not by what was done at one wedding. Bigamy needs a properly solemnized second marriage; admission is not proof ( Priya Bala Ghosh ). Section 8(5): non-registration does not affect validity; fine up to ₹25 where compulsory. Registration does not create a marriage ( Dolly Rani ). Seema : marriages of all religions to be compulsorily registrable; registration has great evidentiary value. Special Marriage Act, 1954: thirty days' notice, three witnesses, conclusive certificate; an earlier marriage can be registered under Sections 15 to 18.
