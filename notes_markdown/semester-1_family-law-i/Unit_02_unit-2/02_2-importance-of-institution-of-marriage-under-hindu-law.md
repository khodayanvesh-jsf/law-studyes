# 2. Importance of institution of marriage under Hindu Law
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester 1) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2

## In One Minute
Marriage is the base of the Hindu family, and its importance is of three kinds. Religious. Hindu law treats marriage as a samskara , a sacrament. In Tikait (1901) the Calcutta High Court called it "more religious than secular in character". Social. In Sarla Mudgal (1995) the Supreme Court said: "Marriage is an institution in the maintenance of which the public at large is deeply interested. It is the foundation of the family and in turn of the society without which no civilisation can exist." Legal. Many rights flow from the status of husband and wife: maintenance, legitimacy of children, succession, a right to reside, and the matrimonial remedies. Because so much depends on it, the law protects marriage. It keeps children legitimate even when a marriage is void or annulled (Section 16). It also presumes a valid marriage where a man and a woman have lived together for a long time as husband and wife ( Badri Prasad , 1978). The presumption can be rebutted.

## Legal Provision
- Section 16, Hindu Marriage Act, 1955. Legitimacy of children of void and voidable marriages. (1) Notwithstanding that a marriage is null and void under section 11, any child of such marriage who would have been legitimate if the marriage had been valid, shall be legitimate, whether such child is born before or after the commencement of the Marriage Laws (Amendment) Act, 1976 (68 of 1976), and whether or not a decree of nullity is granted in respect of that marriage under this Act and whether or not the marriage is held to be void otherwise than on a petition under this Act. (2) Where a decree of nullity is granted in respect of a voidable marriage under section 12, any child begotten or conceived before the decree is made, who would have been the legitimate child of the parties to the marriage if at the date of the decree it had been dissolved instead of being annulled, shall be deemed to be their legitimate child notwithstanding the decree of nullity. (3) Nothing contained in sub-section (1) or sub-section (2) shall be construed as conferring upon any child of a marriage which is null and void or which is annulled by a decree of nullity under section 12, any rights in or to the property of any person, other than the parents, in any case where, but for the passing of this Act, such child would have been incapable of possessing or acquiring any such rights by reason of his not being the legitimate child of his parents.
- Section 24. Maintenance pendente lite and expenses of proceedings. Where in any proceeding under this Act it appears to the court that either the wife or the husband, as the case may be, has no independent income sufficient for her or his support and the necessary expenses of the proceeding, it may, on the application of the wife or the husband, order the respondent to pay to the petitioner the expenses of the proceeding, and monthly during the proceeding such sum as, having regard to the petitioner's own income and the income of the respondent, it may seem to the court to be reasonable.
- Section 25(1). Permanent alimony and maintenance. Any court exercising jurisdiction under this Act may, at the time of passing any decree or at any time subsequent thereto, on application made to it for the purpose by either the wife or the husband, as the case may be, order that the respondent shall pay to the applicant for her or his maintenance and support such gross sum or such monthly or periodical sum for a term not exceeding the life of the applicant as, having regard to the respondent's own income and other property, if any, the income and other property of the applicant, the conduct of the parties and other circumstances of the case, it may seem to the court to be just, and any such payment may be secured, if necessary, by a charge on the immovable property of the respondent.
- Section 8(5). Notwithstanding anything contained in this section, the validity of any Hindu marriage shall in no way be affected by the omission to make the entry.
- Section 18(1), Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956. Subject to the provisions of this section, a Hindu wife, whether married before or after the commencement of this Act, shall be entitled to be maintained by her husband during her life time.
- In simple words
- Section 16(1): a child of a void marriage is legitimate, as if the marriage had been valid. No decree of nullity is needed for this. Section 16(2): when a voidable marriage is annulled, a child begotten or conceived before the decree is legitimate. Section 16(3): such a child gets rights in the property of the parents only, not of other relatives, where the child would otherwise have had no such right. Section 24: during a case under the Act, a spouse (wife or husband) without enough income may get the expenses of the case and a monthly sum. The proviso adds that the application should, as far as possible, be decided within sixty days from service of notice. Section 25(1): when or after the court passes a decree, either spouse may get maintenance for life at most, as a gross sum or a periodical sum, which may be secured by a charge on the other's immovable property. Section 8(5): not registering a marriage does not make it invalid. HAMA Section 18(1): a Hindu wife is entitled to be maintained by her husband for her whole life.

## Meaning and Definition
Meaning. The importance of the institution of marriage is the set of religious, social and legal functions it performs for the spouses, their children and society. Mulla's Introductory Note to the Hindu Marriage Act says: "Marriage is necessarily the basis of social organisation and the foundation of important legal rights and obligations."

The question this topic answers. Why does Hindu law treat marriage as an institution to be protected, and how does it protect it?

Why marriage matters: its religious, social and legal importance. How the law protects it: by the rights it attaches to marriage, by protecting children when a marriage fails, by the presumption in favour of marriage, and by providing for proof through registration.

Why the law has this rule. Marriage is a status that others rely on: the spouses, their children, their families and the public. Mulla, explaining the meaning of status, quotes: the relation of the parties and their status "are not imposed or defined by contract or agreement but by law". If marriage could be easily denied, the wife's maintenance and the children's legitimacy and inheritance would be put at risk.

## Key Points to Remember
- Marriage has religious, social and legal importance. Tikait : "more religious than secular in character"; Mulla: the last of ten sacraments. Sarla Mudgal : "the public at large is deeply interested" in maintaining marriage. Maintenance (HMA ss. 24–25 for either spouse; HAMA s. 18 for the wife; BNSS s. 144) and succession (HSA ss. 8, 15) depend on the status of spouse. Section 16 keeps children of void and voidable marriages legitimate; their property rights are limited to the parents' property. Long open cohabitation raises a strong but rebuttable presumption of marriage; a heavy burden lies on the person who denies it ( Badri Prasad ). Registration helps to prove a marriage but does not decide validity (s. 8(5); Seema ).
