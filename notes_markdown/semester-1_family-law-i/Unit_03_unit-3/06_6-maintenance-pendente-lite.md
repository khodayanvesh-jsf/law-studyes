# 6. Maintenance pendente lite
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester 1) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/maintenance-pendente-lite/

## In One Minute
What it is. Maintenance pendente lite is interim support that the court orders while a matrimonial case is pending, together with the expenses of the case (Section 24). Who may claim. Either the wife or the husband, in any proceeding under the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 (HMA). The test. The applicant has no independent income sufficient for his or her support and the necessary expenses of the proceeding. Speed. The application is to be decided, as far as possible, within sixty days of service of notice ( proviso to Section 24). After the case. The order ends with the proceeding. At or after the decree, the court may grant permanent alimony under Section 25.

## Legal Provision
- Section 24, Hindu Marriage Act, 1955. Maintenance pendente lite and expenses of proceedings. Where in any proceeding under this Act it appears to the court that either the wife or the husband, as the case may be, has no independent income sufficient for her or his support and the necessary expenses of the proceeding, it may, on the application of the wife or the husband, order the respondent to pay to the petitioner the expenses of the proceeding, and monthly during the proceeding such sum as, having regard to the petitioner's own income and the income of the respondent, it may seem to the court to be reasonable: Provided that the application for the payment of the expenses of the proceeding and such monthly sum during the proceeding, shall, as far as possible, be disposed of within sixty days from the date of service of notice on the wife or the husband, as the case may be.
- Other provisions used in this note:
- HMA Section 25: permanent alimony and maintenance at or after the decree. HMA Section 26: custody, maintenance and education of minor children, with a sixty-day target for interim maintenance and education. HMA Sections 21B, 28 and 28A: speedy trial, appeals and enforcement. Special Marriage Act, 1954 (SMA), Sections 36, 37 and 38. Family Courts Act, 1984, Sections 7 and 9. Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS), Section 144 (earlier Section 125, Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973). Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956 (HAMA), Sections 18(1) and 23(2). Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 (PWDVA), Sections 12, 20, 23 and 26.

## Meaning and Definition
Maintenance pendente lite means maintenance while the suit is pending (" pendente lite " means "while the litigation is pending"). It lets a spouse with no means live during the case and carry on the case on fair terms. The order is temporary. See Diagram 1 .

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 24: interim maintenance and expenses during any proceeding under the Act. Wife or husband may claim; the test is no independent income sufficient. Disposal within sixty days of notice, as far as possible. Section 25: permanent alimony at or after the decree, variable under Section 25(2) and (3). BNSS Section 144 also allows interim maintenance, within sixty days as far as possible. Special Marriage Act, 1954: interim maintenance (Section 36) and permanent alimony (Section 37) are for the wife only. Domestic Violence Act, 2005, Section 20: monetary relief, including maintenance, from a Magistrate.
