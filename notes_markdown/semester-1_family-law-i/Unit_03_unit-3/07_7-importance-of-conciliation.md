# 7. importance of conciliation
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester 1) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/importance-of-conciliation/

## In One Minute
What it is. Conciliation means helping the spouses to settle their dispute and, where possible, save the marriage. Duty of the court. Before granting any relief under the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 (HMA), the court must make every endeavour to bring about reconciliation, where the nature of the case allows (Section 23(2)). It may adjourn up to fifteen days and refer the parties to a person for this (Section 23(3)). Exceptions. The duty does not apply where relief is sought on conversion, mental disorder, venereal disease, renunciation or presumed death ( proviso to Section 23(2)). Family Courts. The Family Courts Act, 1984 was passed "with a view to promote conciliation"; a Family Court must first try to assist and persuade the parties to settle (Section 9). Why it matters. Breakdown of a marriage harms children and families. The six-month wait in a mutual-consent divorce and the duty to reconcile serve the same aim: peace first, decree last.

## Legal Provision
- Section 23(2), Hindu Marriage Act, 1955. Before proceeding to grant any relief under this Act, it shall be the duty of the court in the first instance, in every case where it is possible so to do consistently with the nature and circumstances of the case, to make every endeavour to bring about reconciliation between the parties: Provided that nothing contained in this sub-section shall apply to any proceeding wherein relief is sought on any of the grounds specified in clause (ii), clause (iii), clause (iv), clause (v), clause (vi) or clause (vii) of sub-section (1) of section 13. (3) For the purpose of aiding the court in bringing about such reconciliation, the court may, if the parties so desire or if the court thinks it just and proper so to do, adjourn the proceedings for a reasonable period not exceeding fifteen days and refer the matter to any person named by the parties in this behalf or to any person nominated by the court if the parties fail to name any person, with directions to report to the court as to whether reconciliation can be and has been, effected and the court shall in disposing of the proceeding have due regard to the report.
- Section 9(1), Family Courts Act, 1984. In every suit or proceeding, endeavour shall be made by the Family Court in the first instance, where it is possible to do so consistent with the nature and circumstances of the case, to assist and persuade the parties in arriving at a settlement in respect of the subject-matter of the suit or proceeding and for this purpose a Family Court may, subject to any rules made by the High Court, follow such procedure as it may deem fit.
- Other provisions used in this note:
- HMA Section 13B(2): the second motion in a mutual-consent divorce comes six to eighteen months after the petition. Family Courts Act, 1984 (FCA): long title; Sections 4(4), 5, 6, 9(2), (3), 10(3), 11, 12, 13 and 21(2)(c). Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (CPC): Order XXXIIA, Rules 1 to 5. Mediation Act, 2023: Sections 5(1), 6(1), 7, 22 and 27, and the First Schedule. Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996: Sections 61, 74 and 75. Special Marriage Act, 1954 (SMA): Section 34(2) and (3).

## Meaning and Definition
Conciliation is a process in which the court or a neutral person helps the spouses to settle their dispute by agreement. It is not a ruling: the parties remain free to accept or reject the suggestions. In matrimonial law its first aim is reconciliation (the spouses live together again); if that fails, a fair settlement of maintenance, custody and property. See Diagram 1 .

If a Family Court hears it, the Family Court first tries to assist and persuade the parties to settle under Section 9. Then the court asks whether relief is sought on a ground in Section 13(1)(ii) to (vii). If yes, the Section 23(2) duty does not apply and the case goes to trial. If no, the court must try reconciliation; it may adjourn up to fifteen days and refer the parties to a person who reports back (Section 23(3)). If reconciliation succeeds, the case ends. If it fails, the case goes to trial and decree.

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 23(2): the court must try reconciliation before granting relief. Adjournment up to fifteen days and reference to a person (Section 23(3)). Duty excluded for conversion, mental disorder, venereal disease, renunciation and presumed death. Section 9, Family Courts Act: the Family Court must first assist and persuade the parties to settle, and may adjourn for that purpose. Amardeep Singh (para 18) lists Section 23(2) HMA and Section 9 FCA among the conciliation efforts that must fail before waiver. The Section 13B waiting period gives time to reconcile and is directory. Order XXXIIA CPC and the Mediation Act, 2023 support settlement of family disputes.
