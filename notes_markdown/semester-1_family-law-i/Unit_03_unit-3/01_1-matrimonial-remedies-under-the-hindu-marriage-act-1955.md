# 1. Matrimonial Remedies under the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester 1) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/matrimonial-remedies-under-the-hindu-marriage-act-1955/

## In One Minute
Four main remedies. The Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 (HMA) gives four main matrimonial remedies: restitution of conjugal rights (Section 9), judicial separation (Section 10), a decree of nullity (Sections 11 and 12) and divorce (Sections 13, 13A, 13B and 14). Ancillary reliefs. Alongside these, the court can order maintenance pendente lite and costs (Section 24), permanent alimony (Section 25), custody of children (Section 26) and disposal of joint property (Section 27). The court. Every petition goes to the district court (Section 19). Where a Family Court has been set up, it alone hears these cases (Family Courts Act, 1984, Sections 7 and 8). Section 23 conditions. Relief is granted only if a ground exists and the bars in Section 23(1) do not apply: own wrong, connivance or condonation , consent obtained by force or fraud, collusion , delay, or any other legal ground. Reconciliation first. The court must first try to reconcile the parties (Section 23(2)); a Family Court must first try to settle the dispute (Family Courts Act, Section 9). Appeals. An appeal under the HMA lies within ninety days (Section 28(4)). An appeal from a Family Court lies to the High Court within thirty days (Family Courts Act, Section 19(3)).

## Legal Provision
- Section 9, Hindu Marriage Act, 1955. Restitution of conjugal rights. When either the husband or the wife has, without reasonable excuse, withdrawn from the society of the other, the aggrieved party may apply, by petition to the district court, for restitution of conjugal rights and the court, on being satisfied of the truth of the statements made in such petition and that there is no legal ground why the application should not be granted, may decree restitution of conjugal rights accordingly. Explanation. —Where a question arises whether there has been reasonable excuse for withdrawal from the society, the burden of proving reasonable excuse shall be on the person who has withdrawn from the society.
- Section 10(1). Judicial separation. Either party to a marriage, whether solemnised before or after the commencement of this Act, may present a petition praying for a decree for judicial separation on any of the grounds specified in sub-section (1) of section 13, and in the case of a wife also on any of the grounds specified in sub-section (2) thereof, as grounds on which a petition for divorce might have been presented.
- Section 23(1). Decree in proceedings. In any proceeding under this Act, whether defended or not, if the court is satisfied that— (a) any of the grounds for granting relief exists and the petitioner except in cases where the relief is sought by him on the ground specified in sub-clause (a), sub-clause (b) or sub-clause (c) of clause (ii) of section 5 is not in any way taking advantage of his or her own wrong or disability for the purpose of such relief, and (b) where the ground of the petition is the ground specified … in clause (i) of sub-section (1) of section 13, the petitioner has not in any manner been accessory to or connived at or condoned the act or acts complained of, or where the ground of the petition is cruelty the petitioner has not in any manner condoned the cruelty, and (bb) when a divorce is sought on the ground of mutual consent, such consent has not been obtained by force, fraud or undue influence, and (c) the petition (not being a petition presented under section 11) is not presented or prosecuted in collusion with the respondent, and (d) there has not been any unnecessary or improper delay in instituting the proceeding, and (e) there is no other legal ground why relief should not be granted, then, and in such a case, but not otherwise, the court shall decree such relief accordingly.
- Section 23(2). Before proceeding to grant any relief under this Act, it shall be the duty of the court in the first instance, in every case where it is possible so to do consistently with the nature and circumstances of the case, to make every endeavour to bring about reconciliation between the parties: Provided that nothing contained in this sub-section shall apply to any proceeding wherein relief is sought on any of the grounds specified in clause (ii), clause (iii), clause (iv), clause (v), clause (vi) or clause (vii) of sub-section (1) of section 13.
- Section 9(1), Family Courts Act, 1984. Duty of Family Court to make efforts for settlement. In every suit or proceeding, endeavour shall be made by the Family Court in the first instance, where it is possible to do so consistent with the nature and circumstances of the case, to assist and persuade the parties in arriving at a settlement in respect of the subject-matter of the suit or proceeding …
- The other sections, in short:
- Sections 11 and 12: nullity of void and voidable marriages . Sections 13, 13A, 13B and 14: divorce, alternative relief of judicial separation, divorce by mutual consent, and no divorce petition within one year of marriage. Sections 15 and 16: remarriage after divorce, and legitimacy of children of void and voidable marriages. Section 19: the court in which the petition is presented. Sections 20, 21, 21A, 21B and 21C: contents of the petition, the Code of Civil Procedure, rival petitions, speedy trial, and documents not barred for want of stamp or registration. Section 22: proceedings in camera ; no printing or publishing. Section 23A: counter-claim by the respondent . Sections 24 to 27: maintenance pendente lite , permanent alimony, custody of children, disposal of property. Sections 28 and 28A: appeals (ninety days) and enforcement. Section 29(2) and (4): customary and special-law divorces saved; the Special Marriage Act, 1954 untouched. Family Courts Act, 1984, Sections 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 19 and 20: jurisdiction , exclusion of other courts, settlement, procedure, in camera, experts, no lawyer as of right, appeal, overriding effect. Special Marriage Act, 1954 (SMA), Sections 22 to 39A: the matching remedies for marriages under that Act.

## Meaning and Definition
A matrimonial remedy is a relief that a court gives to a spouse in respect of the marriage itself. Each remedy does one of four things to the marriage (see Diagram 1 ):

The ancillary reliefs (Sections 24 to 27) do not act on the marriage. They protect a spouse and the children while the case is pending and after the decree: money for support and costs, alimony, custody and property.

## Key Points to Remember
- Four main remedies: restitution, judicial separation, nullity, divorce. Ancillary reliefs: Sections 24 to 27. Section 23 conditions apply to every relief; the court must first try reconciliation (Section 23(2)). Standard of proof: preponderance of probabilities ( Dastane ). Proceedings are in camera (Section 22; Family Courts Act, Section 11). A Family Court hears these petitions where one exists (Family Courts Act, Sections 7 and 8), and must first try to settle (Section 9). Appeal: ninety days under the HMA (Section 28(4)); thirty days from a Family Court (Family Courts Act, Section 19(3)); none from a consent decree of a Family Court. The Special Marriage Act has matching remedies in Sections 22 to 39A.
