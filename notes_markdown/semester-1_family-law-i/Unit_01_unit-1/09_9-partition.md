# 9. Partition
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester 1) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/partition/

## In One Minute
Partition ends the joint holding of coparcenary property. It has two steps. First, a coparcener separates in status by a clear and unequivocal declaration of his intention to separate, made known to the others. This is severance of status . Later, the property is divided by metes and bounds into definite lots, by agreement, arbitration or a court decree. Only a coparcener can demand partition, and since 9 September 2005 a daughter has the same right and the same share as a son (Section 6(1); Vineeta Sharma , para 79). A declaration that never reaches the others does not sever status ( Addagada Raghavamma , 1963). A partition can be reopened for fraud or mistake, or on other permissible grounds ( Vineeta Sharma , para 125). For Section 6, "partition" means only a partition by a registered deed or by a decree of a court. A partition made before 20 December 2004 is saved (Section 6(5)). An oral partition is accepted only in exceptional cases, backed by public documents ( Vineeta Sharma , paras 127–129).

## Legal Provision
- Section 6(1), Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (as substituted in 2005). On and from the commencement of the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 (39 of 2005), in a Joint Hindu family governed by the Mitakshara law, the daughter of a coparcener shall,― (a) by birth become a coparcener in her own right the same manner as the son; (b) have the same rights in the coparcenery property as she would have had if she had been a son; (c) be subject to the same liabilities in respect of the said coparcenery property as that of a son, and any reference to a Hindu Mitakshara coparcener shall be deemed to include a reference to a daughter of a coparcener: Provided that nothing contained in this sub-section shall affect or invalidate any disposition or alienation including any partition or testamentary disposition of property which had taken place before the 20th day of December, 2004. Section 6(2). Any property to which a female Hindu becomes entitled by virtue of sub-section (1) shall be held by her with the incidents of coparcenary ownership and shall be regarded, notwithstanding anything contained in this Act or any other law for the time being in force, as property capable of being disposed of by her by testamentary disposition. Section 6(3). Where a Hindu dies after the commencement of the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 (39 of 2005), his interest in the property of a Joint Hindu family governed by the Mitakshara law, shall devolve by testamentary or intestate succession, as the case may be, under this Act and not by survivorship, and the coparcenery property shall be deemed to have been divided as if a partition had taken place and,― (a) the daughter is allotted the same share as is allotted to a son; (b) the share of the pre-deceased son or a pre-deceased daughter, as they would have got had they been alive at the time of partition, shall be allotted to the surviving child of such pre-deceased son or of such pre-deceased daughter; and (c) the share of the pre-deceased child of a pre-deceased son or of a pre-deceased daughter, as such child would have got had he or she been alive at the time of the partition, shall be allotted to the child of such pre-deceased child of the pre-deceased son or a pre-deceased daughter, as the case may be. Explanation.―For the purposes of this sub-section, the interest of a Hindu Mitakshara coparcener shall be deemed to be the share in the property that would have been allotted to him if a partition of the property had taken place immediately before his death, irrespective of whether he was entitled to claim partition or not. Section 6(5). Nothing contained in this section shall apply to a partition, which has been effected before the 20th day of December, 2004. Explanation.―For the purposes of this section "partition" means any partition made by execution of a deed of partition duly registered under the Registration Act, 1908 (16 of 1908) or partition effected by a decree of a court.
- In simple words
- Section 6(1). A daughter is a coparcener by birth, with the same rights and liabilities as a son. So she can demand partition and takes a son's share. The proviso keeps alive any partition, sale, gift or will made before 20 December 2004. Section 6(2). What a daughter gets under Section 6(1) she holds as coparcenary property, and she can leave it by will. Section 6(3). When a coparcener dies after 9 September 2005, his interest does not pass to the others by survivorship . It passes by will or by intestate succession . To find his interest, the law assumes a partition just before his death; the daughter is counted like a son, and the share of a pre-deceased son or daughter goes to that person's children. Section 6(5) and Explanation. Section 6 does not disturb a partition effected before 20 December 2004. For Section 6, only a partition by a registered deed or by a court decree counts as a "partition".

## Meaning and Definition
Mulla (section 322) gives the classical idea:

"According to the true notion of an undivided Mitakshara family, no individual member of that family, whilst it remains undivided, can predicate of the joint property, that he—that particular member—has a certain definite share, one-third or one-fourth. Partition, according to that law, consists in a numerical division of the property; in other words, it consists in defining the shares of the coparceners in the joint property; an actual division of the property by metes and bounds is not necessary."

Vineeta Sharma (para 93, quoting Shub Karan Bubna , (2009) 9 SCC 689) describes the result: "'Partition' is a redistribution or adjustment of pre-existing rights , among co-owners/coparceners, resulting in a division of lands or other properties jointly held by them into different lots or portions and delivery thereof to the respective allottees. The effect of such division is that the joint ownership is terminated and the respective shares vest in them in severalty."

So partition has two senses that students often mix up:

Severance of status. A change in legal relationship only: the member ceases to be a coparcener and becomes entitled to a definite share. "That is matter of individual volition" ( Mukund Dharman Bhoir , Privy Council, quoted in Vineeta Sharma , para 87). Division by metes and bounds. "The partition or division of the joint estate, comprising the allotment of shares, which may be effected by different methods" (same passage).

## Key Points to Remember
- Partition has two steps: severance of status, then division by metes and bounds. Severance needs a clear, unequivocal declaration made known to those affected; no deed and no assent are needed. Relation back cannot affect vested rights ( Addagada Raghavamma ). A suit for partition severs status from its filing; withdrawal before trial means no severance. Partition is complete only on a final decree and possession. Every coparcener, including a daughter since 2005, can demand partition ( Vineeta Sharma , para 79). On a coparcener's death after 2005, his interest passes by succession, not survivorship (Section 6(3)). For Section 6, partition means a registered deed or a court decree; partitions before 20 December 2004 are saved. A partition can be reopened for fraud or mistake, or on other permissible grounds (para 125).
