# 2. Scope and application of Hindu Law
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester 1) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/scope-and-application-of-hindu-law/

## In One Minute
The four Hindu Acts do not define "Hindu" in one sentence. They list who is covered. Three groups are covered: Hindus by religion in any form; Buddhists, Jainas and Sikhs; and any other person who is not a Muslim, Christian, Parsi or Jew, unless it is proved that Hindu law would not have governed that person. An Explanation adds children and converts. A child of two Hindu parents is a Hindu; a child of one Hindu parent is a Hindu if brought up in that parent's community or family; a convert or re-convert is a Hindu. Members of Scheduled Tribes are outside the Acts unless the Central Government notifies otherwise. Exams ask for the three groups, the Explanation, the Scheduled Tribe exclusion, the meaning of Hindu religion ( Yagnapurushdasji ), and the effect of conversion on a Hindu marriage ( Sarla Mudgal , Lily Thomas ).

## Legal Provision
- The same application section appears in all four Acts. It is Section 2 of the Hindu Marriage Act, the Hindu Succession Act and the Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, and Section 3 of the Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act. The Hindu Marriage Act text is quoted.
- Section 2, Hindu Marriage Act, 1955. Application of Act. (1) This Act applies— (a) to any person who is a Hindu by religion in any of its forms or developments, including a Virashaiva, a Lingayat or a follower of the Brahmo, Prarthana or Arya Samaj, (b) to any person who is a Buddhist, Jaina or Sikh by religion, and (c) to any other person domiciled in the territories to which this Act extends who is not a Muslim, Christian, Parsi or Jew by religion, unless it is proved that any such person would not have been governed by the Hindu law or by any custom or usage as part of that law in respect of any of the matters dealt with herein if this Act had not been passed. Explanation. —The following persons are Hindus, Buddhists, Jainas or Sikhs by religion, as the case may be:— (a) any child, legitimate or illegitimate, both of whose parents are Hindus, Buddhists, Jainas or Sikhs by religion; (b) any child, legitimate or illegitimate, one of whose parents is a Hindu, Buddhist, Jaina or Sikh by religion and who is brought up as a member of the tribe, community, group or family to which such parent belongs or belonged; and (c) any person who is a convert or re-convert to the Hindu, Buddhist, Jaina or Sikh religion. (2) Notwithstanding anything contained in sub-section (1), nothing contained in this Act shall apply to the members of any Scheduled Tribe within the meaning of clause (25) of article 366 of the Constitution unless the Central Government, by notification in the Official Gazette, otherwise directs. (3) The expression "Hindu" in any portion of this Act shall be construed as if it included a person who, though not a Hindu by religion, is, nevertheless, a person to whom this Act applies by virtue of the provisions contained in this section.
- Section 1(2), Hindu Marriage Act, 1955. It extends to the whole of India, and applies also to Hindus domiciled in the territories to which this Act extends who are outside the said territories.
- Explanation (bb), Section 2, Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956. any child, legitimate or illegitimate, who has been abandoned both by his father and mother or whose parentage is not known and who in either case is brought up as a Hindu, Buddhist, Jaina or Sikh;
- In simple words
- Clause (a): a Hindu by religion in any form or development, including a Virashaiva, a Lingayat, and a follower of the Brahmo, Prarthana or Arya Samaj. Clause (b): a Buddhist, Jaina or Sikh by religion. Clause (c): anyone else who is not a Muslim, Christian, Parsi or Jew. He is covered unless someone proves that, without the Act, Hindu law or Hindu custom would not have governed him. The Marriage Act and the Minority and Guardianship Act add that he must be domiciled in the territories to which the Act extends. The Succession Act and the Adoptions and Maintenance Act do not have this domicile condition. Explanation: children of two Hindu (or Buddhist, Jaina, Sikh) parents; children of one such parent brought up in that parent's tribe, community, group or family; and converts or re-converts. Sub-section (2): members of a Scheduled Tribe are outside the Act unless the Central Government notifies otherwise. Sub-section (3): wherever the Act says "Hindu", it includes a person who is not a Hindu by religion but to whom the Act applies under this section. Section 1(2): the Marriage Act covers the whole of India, and also Hindus domiciled in India who live outside it. The Minority and Guardianship Act has the same clause. Explanation (bb), Adoptions and Maintenance Act only: an abandoned child, or a child of unknown parentage, brought up as a Hindu, Buddhist, Jaina or Sikh.

## Meaning and Definition
Scope means the reach of Hindu law: to whom, and where, it applies. Application means the test a court uses to decide whether a particular person is governed by the Hindu Acts.

The Acts do not define "Hindu" in a positive sentence. They list who is covered and then use a residuary (left-over) group that works by exclusion. This keeps the law wide: anyone without another personal law of marriage and succession falls under Hindu law. See Diagram 1 .

The question this topic answers. Is this person governed by the Hindu Acts? If yes, the Hindu Marriage Act decides the validity of his marriage, the Hindu Succession Act decides who inherits from him, and so on.

Why the law has this rule. Before 1955, Hindu law applied to a person because of religion and custom. The Acts kept that wide reach, brought in Buddhists, Jainas and Sikhs, who had always been governed by Hindu law in family matters, and left out only those with their own personal laws.

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 2(1) covers Hindus by religion, Buddhists, Jainas and Sikhs, and a residuary group. The residuary group is anyone not a Muslim, Christian, Parsi or Jew, unless proved outside Hindu law or custom. The Explanation covers children of two Hindu parents, children of one Hindu parent brought up in that parent's group, and converts. Scheduled Tribes are excluded unless the Central Government notifies otherwise. Hindu religion "may broadly be described as a way of life and nothing more" ( Yagnapurushadji ). Conversion does not end a Hindu marriage; a second marriage is void ( Sarla Mudgal , Lily Thomas ). Domicile in clause (c) only in the Marriage and Minority-Guardianship Acts; clause (bb) only in the Adoptions and Maintenance Act. A migrating family carries its personal law with it unless it adopts the new law (Mulla, section 14).
