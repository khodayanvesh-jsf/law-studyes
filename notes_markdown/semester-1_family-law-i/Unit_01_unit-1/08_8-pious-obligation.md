# 8. Pious Obligation
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester 1) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/pious-obligation/

## In One Minute
Under Mitakshara law a son, grandson and great-grandson must pay the debts of the father, grandfather and great-grandfather, provided the debts were not incurred for an illegal or immoral purpose. This is the pious obligation . It is a religious duty turned into a legal liability, and it is limited to the son's interest in the joint family property; it is not a personal liability. The Privy Council in Brij Narain v. Mangla Prasad (1923) held that the obligation exists whether the father is alive or dead, and that sons cannot resist their father's creditor, or his alienation for an antecedent debt , if the debt is not tainted with immorality. Since 2005, Section 6(4) of the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 stops courts from recognising any right to proceed against a son, grandson or great-grandson solely on the ground of pious obligation. Debts contracted before 9 September 2005 are saved, for descendants born or adopted before that date. Vineeta Sharma (2020) says Section 6(4) makes a daughter liable in the same manner as a son. Exams ask for the basis of the doctrine, the avyavaharika debts the son need not pay, antecedent debt, and the effect of Section 6(4).

## Legal Provision
- Section 6(4), Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (as substituted in 2005). After the commencement of the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 (39 of 2005), no court shall recognise any right to proceed against a son, grandson or great-grandson for the recovery of any debt due from his father, grandfather or great-grandfather solely on the ground of the pious obligation under the Hindu law, of such son, grandson or great-grandson to discharge any such debt: Provided that in the case of any debt contracted before the commencement of the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 (39 of 2005), nothing contained in this sub-section shall affect― (a) the right of any creditor to proceed against the son, grandson or great-grandson, as the case may be; or (b) any alienation made in respect of or in satisfaction of, any such debt, and any such right or alienation shall be enforceable under the rule of pious obligation in the same manner and to the same extent as it would have been enforceable as if the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 (39 of 2005) had not been enacted. Explanation.―For the purposes of clause (a), the expression "son", "grandson" or "great-grandson" shall be deemed to refer to the son, grandson or great-grandson, as the case may be, who was born or adopted prior to the commencement of the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 (39 of 2005).
- In simple words
- Main rule. After the 2005 Act came into force (9 September 2005), a creditor cannot sue a son, grandson or great-grandson for the father's or ancestor's debt on the sole ground of pious obligation . Proviso (a). For a debt contracted before that date, the creditor keeps his old right against the descendants. Proviso (b). For such an earlier debt, a sale or mortgage made for it, or in satisfaction of it, remains enforceable under the old rule. Explanation. The saving in clause (a) covers only descendants born or adopted before 9 September 2005. The change is prospective : it does not reopen old debts. Vineeta Sharma (para 61) says the proviso saves the creditor's right for debts contracted before the amendment and that the rights and liabilities under Section 6 both run from its commencement.

## Meaning and Definition
Mulla (section 290(1)) states the rule:

"Where the sons are joint with their father, and debts have been contracted by the father for his own personal benefit, the sons are liable to pay the debts provided they were not incurred for an illegal or immoral purpose. The liability to pay the debts contracted by the father, though for his own benefit, arises from an obligation of religion and piety which is placed upon the sons under the Mitakshara law to discharge the father's debts, where the debts are not tainted with immorality."

In Mulla, "sons" includes son's sons and son's son's sons. So the pious obligation is the liability of a son, grandson and great-grandson, arising from religious duty, to pay out of the joint family property the personal debts of the father, grandfather and great-grandfather that are not illegal or immoral ( vyavaharika debts). Brij Narain (para 12) calls it "the obligation of the son to discharge his father's debts, based on the doctrine of pious duty". See Diagram 1 .

## Key Points to Remember
- Pious obligation: the duty of a son, grandson and great-grandson to pay the father's, grandfather's and great-grandfather's personal debts not tainted with immorality (Mulla s. 290; Brij Narain paras 11–12). The liability is limited to the interest in the joint family property; it is not personal. It exists whether the father is alive or dead, and lasts only while the father's liability subsists. Avyavaharika debts (liquor, gambling, lust or wrath, fines, tolls and the like) are excluded; the son must prove the taint. A father may sell or mortgage the sons' interest for an antecedent debt, antecedent in fact as well as in time. Section 6(4): after 9 September 2005, no proceeding against descendants solely on this ground; pre-2005 debts are saved for descendants born or adopted before that date. A daughter is liable in the same manner as a son ( Vineeta Sharma , para 61).
