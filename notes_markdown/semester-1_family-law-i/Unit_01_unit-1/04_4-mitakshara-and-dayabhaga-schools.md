# 4. Mitakshara and Dayabhaga Schools
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester 1) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/mitakshara-and-dayabhaga-schools/

## In One Minute
The Mitakshara and the Dayabhaga are the two schools of Hindu law. They differ most on how a family holds property. Under the Mitakshara, a son gets an interest in ancestral property at birth. No one has a fixed share until partition, and on a death the interest passes to the others by survivorship . Under the Dayabhaga, a son gets nothing by birth. A coparcenary begins only on the father's death, each coparcener holds a defined share, and that share passes to his own heirs. They also differ on the basis of inheritance: nearness of blood in the Mitakshara, spiritual benefit in the Dayabhaga. The Hindu Succession Act, 1956 changed the Mitakshara side. Since 2005, Section 6 makes a daughter a coparcener by birth, replaces survivorship with succession, and ends the sons' pious obligation for new debts. Exams ask for the points of difference, the incidents of a Mitakshara coparcenary ( Ghamandi Ram ), and how Section 6 changed the law ( Gurupad , Vineeta Sharma ).

## Legal Provision
- Section 6, Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (as substituted in 2005). Devolution of interest in coparcenary property. (1) On and from the commencement of the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 (39 of 2005), in a Joint Hindu family governed by the Mitakshara law, the daughter of a coparcener shall,― (a) by birth become a coparcener in her own right the same manner as the son; (b) have the same rights in the coparcenery property as she would have had if she had been a son; (c) be subject to the same liabilities in respect of the said coparcenery property as that of a son, and any reference to a Hindu Mitakshara coparcener shall be deemed to include a reference to a daughter of a coparcener: Provided that nothing contained in this sub-section shall affect or invalidate any disposition or alienation including any partition or testamentary disposition of property which had taken place before the 20th day of December, 2004. ... (3) Where a Hindu dies after the commencement of the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 (39 of 2005), his interest in the property of a Joint Hindu family governed by the Mitakshara law, shall devolve by testamentary or intestate succession, as the case may be, under this Act and not by survivorship, and the coparcenery property shall be deemed to have been divided as if a partition had taken place and,― (a) the daughter is allotted the same share as is allotted to a son; ... Explanation. ―For the purposes of this sub-section, the interest of a Hindu Mitakshara coparcener shall be deemed to be the share in the property that would have been allotted to him if a partition of the property had taken place immediately before his death, irrespective of whether he was entitled to claim partition or not. (4) After the commencement of the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 (39 of 2005), no court shall recognise any right to proceed against a son, grandson or great-grandson for the recovery of any debt due from his father, grandfather or great-grandfather solely on the ground of the pious obligation under the Hindu law, of such son, grandson or great-grandson to discharge any such debt: [proviso saves debts contracted before the 2005 Act] (5) Nothing contained in this section shall apply to a partition, which has been effected before the 20th day of December, 2004. Explanation. ―For the purposes of this section "partition" means any partition made by execution of a deed of partition duly registered under the Registration Act, 1908 (16 of 1908) or partition effected by a decree of a court.
- Explanation to Section 30, Hindu Succession Act, 1956. The interest of a male Hindu in a Mitakshara coparcenary property or the interest of a member of a tarwad, tavazhi, illom, kutumba or kavaru in the property of the tarwad, tavazhi, illom, kutumba or kavaru shall, notwithstanding anything contained in this Act or in any other law for the time being in force, be deemed to be property capable of being disposed of by him or by her within the meaning of this section.
- Section 6, Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (as originally enacted, quoted in Gurupad ). When a male Hindu dies after the commencement of this Act, having at the time of his death an interest in a Mitakshara coparcenary property, his interest in the property shall devolve by survivorship upon the surviving members of the coparcenary and not in accordance with this Act: Provided that, if the deceased had left him surviving a female relative specified in class I of the Schedule or a male relative specified in that class who claims through such a female relative, the interest of the deceased in the Mitakshara coparcenary property shall devolve by testamentary or intestate succession, as the case may be, under this Act and not by survivorship. Explanation 1. —For the purposes of this section, the interest of a Hindu Mitakshara coparcener shall be deemed to be the share in the property that would have been allotted to him if a partition of the property had taken place immediately before his death, irrespective of whether he was entitled to claim partition or not. ...
- In simple words
- Section 6(1): in a Mitakshara joint family, a daughter is a coparcener by birth, with the same rights and liabilities as a son. Dispositions and partitions made before 20 December 2004 are not disturbed. Section 6(3): when a coparcener dies after 9 September 2005, his interest goes by will or by succession under the Act, not by survivorship . To find his interest, a partition is assumed just before his death, and the daughter gets the same share as a son. Section 6(4): for debts after 2005, no court lets a creditor proceed against a son, grandson or great-grandson only on the ground of pious obligation. Debts contracted before 2005 are saved, and for that saving "son, grandson or great-grandson" means one born or adopted before the 2005 Act (Explanation to Section 6(4)). Section 6(5): Section 6 does not touch a partition made before 20 December 2004, and "partition" means only a registered partition deed or a court decree. Section 30, Explanation: a male Hindu can dispose of his interest in Mitakshara coparcenary property by will. Section 6 as enacted in 1956: survivorship was the normal rule, but if the deceased left a female Class I heir (or a male Class I heir claiming through her), his interest passed by succession, and his share was worked out by assuming a partition just before his death.

## Meaning and Definition
Mitakshara is Vijnaneswara's running commentary on the Yajnavalkya Smriti, written in the latter part of the eleventh century. Its family law rests on the son's birthright in ancestral property. Dayabhaga is Jimutavahana's digest of all the Smriti Codes, written about the beginning of the twelfth century. Its family law rests on ownership by the father and defined shares (Mulla, section 11(3)).

"The conception of a coparcenary and of coparcenary property according to the Dayabhaga law is entirely distinct from that according to the Mitakshara law" (Mulla, section 272). See Diagram 1 .

Dayabhaga: a son gets nothing while the father lives; on the father's death the sons inherit as heirs and hold defined shares as coparceners; on a coparcener's death his share passes to his own heirs.

The question this topic answers. How does a member hold joint family property, and what happens to his interest when he or another member is born or dies?

Why the two schools differ. The Dayabhaga school did not accept the Mitakshara view that a son becomes an owner at birth. For the Dayabhaga, property passes only on the owner's death. Every other difference on the joint family follows from this.

## Key Points to Remember
- Mitakshara: right by birth, unity of ownership, fluctuating interest, survivorship (now replaced by succession). Dayabhaga: no birthright, defined shares, unity of possession but no unity of ownership, shares pass to heirs. A Dayabhaga coparcenary begins only on the father's death; there is none between father and sons. A Dayabhaga father has absolute power over ancestral property. Ghamandi Ram : six incidents; the coparcenary is a creature of law. Section 6(1): daughters are Mitakshara coparceners by birth; 6(3): succession replaces survivorship after 2005; 6(4): no pious obligation for new debts; 6(5): only a registered deed or a decree counts as partition. Gurupad : widow's 7/24 share under the old Section 6. Vineeta Sharma : father need not be alive on 9 September 2005; the test of a coparcener is the right to demand partition.
