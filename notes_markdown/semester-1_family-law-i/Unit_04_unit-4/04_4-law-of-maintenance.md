# 4. Law of Maintenance
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester 1) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/law-of-maintenance/

## In One Minute
What it is. Maintenance is the support that the law makes one person pay to another. Under Chapter III (Sections 18 to 28) of the Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956 (HAMA), a Hindu wife, a widowed daughter-in-law, children, aged or infirm parents and certain dependants of a dead person can claim it. How much. The court fixes the amount by the status of the parties, the claimant's reasonable wants and means, and the other factors in Section 23. A claimant who ceases to be a Hindu by conversion loses the claim under this Act (Section 24). Other laws. Maintenance is also given by Sections 24 and 25 of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 (in matrimonial cases), by Section 144 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS), which takes the place of Section 125 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, and by the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005. Nature. In V. Tulasamma v. Sesha Reddy the Supreme Court held that a Hindu woman's right to maintenance is a pre-existing right , not a mere gift.

## Legal Provision
- Section 3(b), Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956. "maintenance" includes― (i) in all cases, provision for food, clothing, residence, education and medical attendance and treatment; (ii) in the case of an unmarried daughter, also the reasonable expenses of and incident to her marriage;
- Section 18(1). Subject to the provisions of this section, a Hindu wife, whether married before or after the commencement of this Act, shall be entitled to be maintained by her husband during her life time.
- Section 22(1). Subject to the provisions of sub-section (2), the heirs of a deceased Hindu are bound to maintain the dependants of the deceased out of the estate inherited by them from the deceased.
- Other provisions used in this note:
- Section 18(2) and (3): the wife's right to live separately without losing maintenance, and when she loses it. Section 19: widowed daughter-in-law, from her father-in-law. Section 20: children and aged or infirm parents. Sections 21 and 22: the nine classes of dependants, and the heirs' duty. Sections 23 to 28: amount; claimant must be a Hindu; alteration; debts first; charge only if created; transfer of the estate. Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, Sections 24 and 25: maintenance pendente lite and permanent alimony . BNSS, Sections 144 to 147: maintenance of wives, children and parents by order of a Magistrate; Section 531 repeals the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973. Domestic Violence Act, 2005, Sections 2(a), 2(b), 2(f), 12, 17, 20 to 23, 26 and 36.

## Meaning and Definition
Maintenance is the provision of the basic needs of a person who cannot meet them, by a person whom the law binds to provide them. Section 3(b) says it includes food, clothing, residence, education, and medical attendance and treatment, and for an unmarried daughter also the reasonable expenses of her marriage. Under the Act it is a personal obligation of a living person (wife, widowed daughter-in-law, children, parents), and for dependants an obligation of the heirs, payable out of the estate they inherit. A dependant's claim is a charge on the estate only if one is created (Section 27).

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 3(b): food, clothing, residence, education, medical care; for an unmarried daughter, marriage expenses. Section 18: wife; may live apart for justified reasons; loses it if unchaste or she converts. Section 19: widowed daughter-in-law, from the father-in-law; ends on remarriage. Section 20: children (while minors) and aged or infirm parents; includes a childless step-mother. Sections 21 and 22: nine classes of dependants; heirs pay out of the estate, in proportion to their shares. Section 23: factors for the amount. Section 24: claimant must be a Hindu. Section 27: a dependant's claim is a charge only if created. Section 28: enforceable against a transferee with notice or a donee. Section 144 BNSS speaks of "any person having sufficient means"; a Magistrate of the first class orders a monthly allowance and may order interim maintenance. Hindu Marriage Act Sections 24 and 25: interim and permanent maintenance between spouses. Tulasamma : maintenance is a pre-existing right.
