# 7. The Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act 1956
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester 1) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4

## In One Minute
What it is. The Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, 1956 (HMGA) is one of the four Hindu Acts of 1955–56. It deals with the guardianship of Hindu minors in only 13 sections. Supplemental Act. It is in addition to the Guardians and Wards Act, 1890, and does not replace it (Section 2). Both Acts work together for a Hindu minor. Who is a minor. A person who has not completed eighteen years (Section 4(a)). Extent. It extends to the whole of India and also to Hindus domiciled in India who are outside it (Section 1(2)). Core rules. Sections 6 to 13 name the natural guardians , limit their powers over the minor's property, allow testamentary guardians , end the powers of a de facto guardian , and make the welfare of the minor the paramount consideration. Scope of this note. The scheme of the Act as a whole. Sections 6 to 13 are explained in detail in the note on the Law of Guardianship.

## Legal Provision
- Long title. An Act to amend and codify certain parts of the law relating to minority and guardianship among Hindus.
- Section 2. Act to be supplemental to Act 8 of 1890. ―The provisions of this Act shall be in addition to, and not, save as hereinafter expressly provided, in derogation of, the Guardians and Wards Act, 1890 (8 of 1890).
- Section 5. Over-riding effect of Act. ―Save as otherwise expressly provided in this Act,― (a) any text, rule or interpretation of Hindu Law or any custom or usage as part of that law in force immediately before the commencement of this Act shall cease to have effect with respect to any matter for which provision is made in this Act; (b) any other law in force immediately before the commencement of this Act shall cease to have effect in so far as it is inconsistent with any of the provisions contained in this Act.
- Other provisions used in this note:
- Section 1: short title, and extent to the whole of India, including Hindus domiciled in India who are outside it. Section 3: application to Hindus by religion in any form, Buddhists, Jains, Sikhs and the residuary group, with the Explanation on children and converts. Section 3(2) excludes Scheduled Tribes unless notified. Section 3(2A) excludes the Renoncants of Pondicherry. Section 4: definitions of "minor", "guardian" and "natural guardian". Sections 6 to 13: the working rules on guardians.

## Meaning and Definition
The Act is a codifying statute , but only in part: its long title says it codifies "certain parts" of the law on minority and guardianship among Hindus. Mulla notes that it does not codify the whole law of guardianship, and that rules on matters for which the Act makes no provision are not overridden by it.

Section 4 gives three definitions.

"Minor" (Section 4(a)): a person who has not completed the age of eighteen years. "Guardian" (Section 4(b)): a person having the care of the person of a minor, or of his property, or of both. It includes four kinds: (i) a natural guardian; (ii) a guardian appointed by the will of the minor's father or mother; (iii) a guardian appointed or declared by a court; (iv) a person empowered to act as such by or under any enactment relating to any Court of Wards. "Natural guardian" (Section 4(c)): any of the guardians mentioned in Section 6.

## Key Points to Remember
- The Act is in addition to the Guardians and Wards Act, 1890 (Section 2). A minor is a person who has not completed eighteen years (Section 4(a)). Guardian includes natural, testamentary, court-appointed and Court of Wards guardians (Section 4(b)). Section 5: overrides old texts, custom and inconsistent laws. Section 6: father then mother; mother then father for an illegitimate child; husband for a married girl; custody under five ordinarily with the mother. Section 8: no sale or mortgage of the minor's immovable property without court permission; breach is voidable. Section 11: a de facto guardian cannot deal with the minor's property. Section 13: the welfare of the minor is paramount.
