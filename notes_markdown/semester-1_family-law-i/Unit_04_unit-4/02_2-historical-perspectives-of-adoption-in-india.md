# 2. Historical perspectives of adoption in India
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester 1) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/historical-perspectives-of-adoption-in-india/

## In One Minute
Classical law. Hindu adoption came from the texts, two special works (the Dattaka Mimamsa and the Dattaka Chandrika), and custom and usage . Its objects were religious (a son to offer funeral cakes and water) and secular (an heir) (Mulla, Section 447). Only a boy could be adopted, and a woman could not adopt to herself. The 1956 Act. The Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956 (HAMA) put Hindu adoption into a statute. It ends any old text, rule, interpretation, custom or usage on a matter it covers (Section 4), allows a son or a daughter to be adopted, and saves adoptions made before it (Section 30). Other children. Court guidelines on inter-country adoption ( Laxmi Kant Pandey ) came first, then executive instructions, then the Juvenile Justice Act, which enables adoption among members of all faiths and communities ( Supriyo , Bhat J., para 25). Today. The Juvenile Justice Act, 2015, as amended in 2021, with the Adoption Regulations, 2022, governs adoption outside HAMA; the District Magistrate issues the adoption order.

## Legal Provision
- Section 4, Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956. Overriding effect of Act. Save as otherwise expressly provided in this Act,— (a) any text, rule or interpretation of Hindu law or any custom or usage as part of that law in force immediately before the commencement of this Act shall cease to have effect with respect to any matter for which provision is made in this Act; (b) any other law in force immediately before the commencement of this Act shall cease to apply to Hindus in so far as it is inconsistent with any of the provisions contained in this Act.
- Section 30. Savings. Nothing contained in this Act shall affect any adoption made before the commencement of this Act, and the validity and effect of any such adoption shall be determined as if this Act had not been passed.
- Other provisions used in this note:
- HAMA, Chapter II (Sections 5 to 17): the statutory law of Hindu adoption . HAMA Section 3(a): "custom" and "usage" mean a rule continuously and uniformly observed for a long time, which has obtained the force of law among Hindus in a local area, tribe, community, group or family. Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015 (JJ Act): Sections 2(2), 56, 63, 68(c), 101(6), (7) and 111(1). Supriyo records Section 63 (Chandrachud CJI, para 337). Bhat J. refers to Section 41 of the Juvenile Justice Act (Section 41 of the 2000 Act), which enables adoption among members of all faiths and communities (para 25). Guardians and Wards Act, 1890: Section 7.

## Meaning and Definition
The historical perspective traces how the law of adoption moved from old texts, custom and usage to a statute for Hindus, and then to a law open to members of all faiths. The classical rules are those described in Mulla's Principles of Hindu Law (14th edition); the modern rules are in HAMA and the JJ Act. See Diagram 1 .

## Key Points to Remember
- Classical objects: religious (funeral offerings) and secular (an heir) (Mulla, Section 447). Dattaka prevailed all over India; kritrima in Mithila. Classical law: only a boy, of the same caste; a woman could not adopt to herself in dattaka form. Section 4: old texts, rules, interpretations, custom and usage cease on matters the Act covers. HAMA: statutory adoption of sons and daughters; Section 12 gives full status. Section 30 saves adoptions made before the Act. Laxmi Kant Pandey guidelines on inter-country adoption (noted in Supriyo , para 25). Juvenile Justice Act: adoption among members of all faiths and communities (Bhat J., para 25). Shabnam Hashmi : the right to adopt was not raised to a fundamental right (quoted in Supriyo , para 307). Since 2021 the District Magistrate issues JJ Act adoption orders; the Adoption Regulations, 2022 are current.
