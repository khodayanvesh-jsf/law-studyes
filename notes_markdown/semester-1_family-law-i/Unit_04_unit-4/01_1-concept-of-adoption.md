# 1. Concept of Adoption
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester 1) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/concept-of-adoption/

## In One Minute
What it is. Adoption moves a child for good from the family of birth to the adoptive family. From the date of adoption the child is the child of the adoptive parents for all purposes (Section 12, Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956 (HAMA)). Four requisites. The taker has the capacity and right to take, the giver has the capacity to give, the child can be adopted, and the other conditions of Chapter II are met (Section 6). Void if the Act is broken. An adoption made in breach of Chapter II is void; it creates no new rights in the adoptive family and takes away none in the family of birth (Section 5). Final. A valid adoption cannot be cancelled, and the child cannot go back to the birth family (Section 15). No payment. Giving or taking payment for an adoption is punishable with up to six months' imprisonment, or fine, or both (Section 17).

## Legal Provision
- Section 6, Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956. Requisites of a valid adoption. No adoption shall be valid unless— (i) the person adopting has the capacity, and also the right, to take in adoption; (ii) the person giving in adoption has the capacity to do so; (iii) the person adopted is capable of being taken in adoption; and (iv) the adoption is made in compliance with the other conditions mentioned in this Chapter.
- Section 12. Effects of adoption. An adopted child shall be deemed to be the child of his or her adoptive father or mother for all purposes with effect from the date of the adoption and from such date all the ties of the child in the family of his or her birth shall be deemed to be severed and replaced by those created by the adoption in the adoptive family: Provided that— (a) the child cannot marry any person whom he or she could not have married if he or she had continued in the family of his or her birth; (b) any property which vested in the adopted child before the adoption shall continue to vest in such person subject to the obligations, if any, attaching to the ownership of such property, including the obligation to maintain relatives in the family of his or her birth; (c) the adopted child shall not divest any person of any estate which vested in him or her before the adoption.
- Chapter II of HAMA ( Adoption ) at a glance:
- Section 5: adoption only as Chapter II provides; an adoption in breach is void. Section 6: the four requisites. Sections 7 and 8: capacity of a male and a female Hindu to take in adoption, with the spouse's consent. Section 9: who may give a child in adoption. Section 10: who may be adopted. Section 11: other conditions, including actual giving and taking. Sections 12 to 17: effects, power to dispose of property, adoptive mother, no cancellation, presumption from registered documents, and prohibition of payments.
- Other provisions used in this note:
- HAMA Sections 2, 3 and 4: who is covered, definitions, overriding effect. Section 30: adoptions before the Act are saved. Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015 (JJ Act): Sections 2(2), 56, 61 and 63. Guardians and Wards Act, 1890: Sections 24 and 41 (for the contrast with guardianship).

## Meaning and Definition
HAMA does not define adoption. Read together, Sections 6 to 12 show it: adoption is the legal act by which a child is given and taken, with the intent to transfer the child from the family of birth to the adoptive family, so that the child becomes the child of the adoptive parents for all purposes. The JJ Act, which has its own adoption system, defines it in Section 2(2) as "the process through which the adopted child is permanently separated from his biological parents and becomes the lawful child of his adoptive parents with all the rights, privileges and responsibilities that are attached to a biological child".

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 5: an adoption in breach of Chapter II is void. Section 6: four requisites. Spouse's consent is needed (Sections 7 and 8), with exceptions. The child must be a Hindu, unmarried, under fifteen and not already adopted, unless custom permits. Giving and taking is essential; datta homam is not. Section 12: an adopted child is the child of the adopters for all purposes. Section 16 only presumes a registered adoption to be valid; registration is not compulsory under the Act. A widow may adopt in her own right (Section 8); the child becomes her late husband's son too ( Sitabai ). The Act does not apply to Scheduled Tribes unless notified (Section 2(2)).
