# 3. In country and inter- country adoptions
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester 1) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/in-country-and-inter-country-adoptions/

## In One Minute
Two routes. The Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956 (HAMA) covers adoption by Hindus. The Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015 (JJ Act) is a separate system for orphan, abandoned and surrendered children, open to people of every religion, with eligibility rules in Section 57 and in Regulation 5 of the Adoption Regulations, 2022. In-country and inter-country. In-country adoption places a child with parents resident in India. Inter-country adoption sends a child from India to parents living abroad; Indian, non-resident Indian and overseas citizen of India parents get priority (Section 59). Who decides. Since the 2021 amendment, the District Magistrate, not a court, issues the adoption order (Section 61). The Central Adoption Resource Authority (CARA) frames the Regulations and acts as India's Central Authority under the Hague Convention, 1993 (Section 68). History. Bhat J. notes that guidelines pioneered in Laxmi Kant Pandey v. UOI helped inter-country adoptions, and executive instructions filled the vacuum to some extent ( Supriyo , para 25).

## Legal Provision
- Section 57, Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015. Eligibility of prospective adoptive parents. (1) The prospective adoptive parents shall be physically fit, financially sound, mentally alert and highly motivated to adopt a child for providing a good upbringing to him. (2) In case of a couple, the consent of both the spouses for the adoption shall be required. (3) A single or divorced person can also adopt, subject to fulfilment of the criteria and in accordance with the provisions of adoption regulations framed by the Authority. (4) A single male is not eligible to adopt a girl child. (5) Any other criteria that may be specified in the adoption regulations framed by the Authority.
- Section 59(1). If an orphan or abandoned or surrendered child could not be placed with an Indian or non-resident Indian prospective adoptive parent despite the joint effort of the Specialised Adoption Agency and State Agency within sixty days from the date the child has been declared legally free for adoption, such child shall be free for inter-country adoption: Provided that children with physical and mental disability, siblings and children above five years of age may be given preference over other children for such inter-country adoption, in accordance with the adoption regulations, as may be framed by the Authority.
- Other provisions used in this note:
- JJ Act Section 2(49): " prospective adoptive parents" means the person or persons eligible to adopt under Section 57. Section 63: an adoption order makes the child the child of the adoptive parents ( Supriyo , Chandrachud CJI, para 337). Sections 2(3) and 68(c): the Authority is CARA, which frames the Adoption Regulations. Sections 35, 38, 56 to 62, 65 to 68, 80 and 101(6): surrender, legal freedom, the adoption scheme, the roles of the Specialised Adoption Agency and the District Magistrate (since the 2021 amendment), CARA, the offence of unlawful adoption and the appeal. Adoption Regulations, 2022 (G.S.R. 726(E), 23 September 2022): principles, eligible children, eligibility, availability, registration, referral, timelines, inter-country steps, relative and step-parent adoption, grievances, and HAMA adoptions that go abroad. Hague Convention on Intercountry Adoption, 1993: Articles 1, 2, 4 to 7, 15 to 17, 19, 21, 23, 24 and 32, summarised below.

## Meaning and Definition
Inter-country adoption is the adoption of a child from one country by parents living in another. In-country adoption is the adoption of a child within India by parents resident in India. Under the JJ Act, "adoption" is the process through which the child is permanently separated from the biological parents and becomes the lawful child of the adoptive parents, with all the rights, privileges and responsibilities of a biological child (Section 2(2)).

## Key Points to Remember
- Two routes: HAMA, 1956 and the JJ Act, 2015. Section 57: eligibility; both spouses consent; a single male cannot adopt a girl. Regulation 5(3): two years of stable marital relationship for couples; Supriyo (3:2) did not read it down. Regulation 5(4): age limits by the child's age; at least twenty-five years' gap. Indians first: sixty days for a child below five, thirty for older children and siblings (Regulation 8; Section 59(1)). The District Magistrate issues the adoption order; appeal to the Divisional Commissioner within thirty days. An adoption order makes the child the adopters' child (Section 63). Bhat J. notes the Laxmi Kant Pandey guidelines on inter-country adoption (para 25). The Authority under Section 2(3) is CARA, India's Central Authority under the Hague Convention (Section 68(d)).
