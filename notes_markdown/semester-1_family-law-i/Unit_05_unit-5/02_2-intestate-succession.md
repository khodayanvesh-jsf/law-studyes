# 2. Intestate succession
**Subject:** Family Law – I (Hindu Law) (Semester 1) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/family-law-i/intestate-succession/

## In One Minute
Meaning. A Hindu is intestate in respect of a property if he or she dies without a valid will for that property. The Hindu Succession Act, 1956 then fixes who inherits. This note. It covers the general rules that apply to every intestate estate (Sections 18 to 29). Sharing rules. Full blood is preferred to half blood (Section 18). Heirs who succeed together take per capita and as tenants-in-common (Section 19). Special cases. A child in the womb can inherit (Section 20). In simultaneous deaths the younger is presumed to have survived the elder (Section 21). Shut out. A murderer is disqualified (Section 25), and so are a convert's later-born children (Section 26). No heir. If there is no heir, the property goes to the Government ( escheat , Section 29).

## Legal Provision
- Section 19. Mode of succession of two or more heirs. ―If two or more heirs succeed together to the property of an intestate, they shall take the property,― (a) save as otherwise expressly provided in this Act, per capita and not per stirpes; and (b) as tenants-in-common and not as joint tenants.
- Section 25. Murderer disqualified. ―A person who commits murder or abets the commission of murder shall be disqualified from inheriting the property of the person murdered, or any other property in furtherance of the succession to which he or she committed or abetted the commission of the murder.
- Section 27. Succession when heir disqualified. ―If any person is disqualified from inheriting any property under this Act, it shall devolve as if such person had died before the intestate.
- Other provisions used in this note:
- Section 18: heirs by full blood are preferred to heirs by half blood, if the nature of the relationship is the same in every other respect. Section 20: a child in the womb at the death, born alive later, inherits as if born before the death. Section 21: when two persons die and it is uncertain who survived, the younger is presumed to have survived the elder. Section 22: class I heirs have a preferential right to acquire a co-heir's interest in immovable property or a business. Sections 24 to 28: disqualifications (Section 24 now omitted). Section 29: failure of heirs.

## Meaning and Definition
Under Section 3(1)(g), a person is deemed to die intestate in respect of property of which he or she has not made a testamentary disposition capable of taking effect. The rules in this note work after the heirs are known (Sections 8 to 17 and the Schedule). They fix how the heirs share, who is shut out, and what happens if no one qualifies.

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 18: full blood before half blood, if the relationship is otherwise the same. Section 19: per capita, and as tenants-in-common. Section 20: the child in the womb inherits. Section 21: the younger is presumed to survive. Section 22: class I heirs may buy out a co-heir; the highest offer wins. Section 25: murderer disqualified. Section 27: treated as dead before the intestate. Section 26: a convert's later-born children are barred unless Hindus when succession opens. Section 29: the Government takes if there is no heir. Section 28: disease, defect or deformity never disqualify. Section 24 (remarried widows) was omitted on 9 September 2005. Uterine brothers and sisters are not class II heirs as brothers and sisters.
