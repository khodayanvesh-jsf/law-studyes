# 6. Govt. Contracts etc
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester 1) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1

## In One Minute
The Union and the States can make contracts like anyone else (Article 298), and can sue and be sued on them (Article 300). The Indian Contract Act applies to these contracts. But the Constitution adds formalities in Article 299(1) : a contract made in the exercise of the executive power of the Union or a State must be expressed to be made by the President or the Governor , and executed on his behalf by such persons and in such manner as he directs or authorises . These formalities are mandatory . A contract that does not follow them is void and cannot be enforced by either side; there can be no implied contract with the Government, and no ratification or estoppel can cure the defect ( K.P. Chowdhary ; Mulamchand ). But if the Government has enjoyed the benefit of goods or work, it must pay compensation under Section 70 ( State of West Bengal v. B.K. Mondal ). Neither the President, the Governor nor the officer who signs is personally liable (Article 299(2)). When awarding contracts, the State must act fairly and non-arbitrarily (Article 14; Ramana Dayaram Shetty ; Kasturi Lal ). In exams, marks come from Article 299 quoted and broken into its three requirements, the effect of non-compliance with cases, the Section 70 remedy, and Article 14. In real life, it tells a supplier or contractor how to make sure a government order is binding.

## Legal Provision
- Constitution of India
- Article 298. Power to carry on trade, etc. The executive power of the Union and of each State shall extend to the carrying on of any trade or business and to the acquisition, holding and disposal of property and the making of contracts for any purpose: Provided that— (a) the said executive power of the Union shall, in so far as such trade or business or such purpose is not one with respect to which Parliament may make laws, be subject in each State to legislation by the State; and (b) the said executive power of each State shall, in so far as such trade or business or such purpose is not one with respect to which the State Legislature may make laws, be subject to legislation by Parliament.
- Article 299. Contracts. (1) All contracts made in the exercise of the executive power of the Union or of a State shall be expressed to be made by the President, or by the Governor of the State, as the case may be, and all such contracts and all assurances of property made in the exercise of that power shall be executed on behalf of the President or the Governor by such persons and in such manner as he may direct or authorise. (2) Neither the President nor the Governor shall be personally liable in respect of any contract or assurance made or executed for the purposes of this Constitution, or for the purposes of any enactment relating to the Government of India heretofore in force, nor shall any person making or executing any such contract or assurance on behalf of any of them be personally liable in respect thereof.
- Article 300(1). Suits and proceedings. The Government of India may sue or be sued by the name of the Union of India and the Government of a State may sue or be sued by the name of the State and may, subject to any provisions which may be made by Act of Parliament or of the Legislature of such State enacted by virtue of powers conferred by this Constitution, sue or be sued in relation to their respective affairs in the like cases as the Dominion of India and the corresponding Provinces or the corresponding Indian States might have sued or been sued if this Constitution had not been enacted.
- Indian Contract Act, 1872
- Section 70. Obligation of person enjoying benefit of non-gratuitous act. Where a person lawfully does anything for another person, or delivers anything to him, not intending to do so gratuitously, and such other person enjoys the benefit thereof, the latter is bound to make compensation to the former in respect of, or to restore, the thing so done or delivered.
- Section 65. Obligation of person who has received advantage under void agreement, or contract that becomes void. When an agreement is discovered to be void, or when a contract becomes void, any person who has received any advantage under such agreement or contract is bound to restore it, or to make compensation for it to the person from whom he received it.
- Section 230 (relevant part). In the absence of any contract to that effect, an agent cannot personally enforce contracts entered into by him on behalf of his principal, nor is he personally bound by them. … Such a contract shall be presumed to exist in the following cases:— … (3) where the principal, though disclosed, cannot be sued.
- In simple words
- Article 298: the Government can do business, deal in property and make contracts for any purpose. Article 299(1): a government contract must say it is made by the President (or the Governor) and must be signed on his behalf by an authorised officer in the authorised manner . Article 299(2): the President, the Governor and the officer who signs are not personally liable on such contracts. Article 300: the Union is sued as " Union of India ", and a State by its name (for example, "State of Andhra Pradesh"). Section 70: if you lawfully do work or deliver goods to someone, not as a gift, and he enjoys the benefit , he must pay for it or return it , even without a valid contract. Section 65: a person who received an advantage under an agreement found to be void must restore it or pay for it. Section 230: an agent is normally not personally bound, but is presumed bound where his principal cannot be sued .

## Meaning and Definition
What a government contract is

A government contract is a contract made in the exercise of the executive power of the Union or of a State, for example: buying supplies, awarding construction works, leasing government land, selling forest produce, or granting licences by auction.

Why the Constitution adds formalities

Public money is at stake. The formalities make sure that only authorised officers bind the Government, and only in the authorised manner. Certainty. It must be clear that the contract is the Government's, not the officer's private deal. Protection of the public interest. The Supreme Court has said the provisions are mandatory and "enacted in the public interest" ( Seth Bikhraj Jaipuria ).

Article 299(1) broken into parts

Requirement What it means Example "expressed to be made by the President, or by the Governor" The contract must say it is made in the name of the President or the Governor "This agreement is made by the Governor of Andhra Pradesh …" "executed on behalf of the President or the Governor" It must be signed on his behalf "For and on behalf of the Governor of Andhra Pradesh" "by such persons … as he may direct or authorise" The signing officer must be authorised (usually by a notification or rules of business) The District Collector, if authorised for such contracts "in such manner as he may direct or authorise" The authorised form must be followed A written deed in the authorised form

Because the contract must be "expressed" to be made and "executed", the Supreme Court has held that there can be no implied contract with the Government ( K.P. Chowdhary v. State of M.P. ).

Which contracts are covered

Article 299 covers contracts made in the exercise of the executive power of the Union or of a State . A company or corporation set up by the Government that contracts in its own name is a separate legal person; on the words of the Article, such a contract is not one made "in the exercise of the executive power of the Union or of a State".

Running example

The District Collector, Krishna (Andhra Pradesh), wants 500 school desks. Ravi Furniture quotes ₹2,500 a desk, total ₹12,50,000 (500 × ₹2,500). We will test three versions of the deal:

Version A: a written agreement "made by the Governor of Andhra Pradesh", signed "for and on behalf of the Governor" by the Collector, who is authorised by notification to sign such contracts. Version B: the Collector simply writes a letter in his own name: "Supply 500 desks at ₹2,500 each." Version C: the same as Version B, and Ravi Furniture delivers all 500 desks, which the schools start using.

## Key Points to Remember
- The Government can contract for any purpose (Article 298) and sue or be sued (Article 300). Article 299(1): in the name of the President or Governor, executed on his behalf, by an authorised person in the authorised manner . The formalities are mandatory ( Bikhraj Jaipuria ); there is no implied contract ( K.P. Chowdhary ). A non-complying contract is void ; no ratification or estoppel ( Mulamchand ). The Government must pay for benefits it enjoys under Section 70 ( B.K. Mondal ). No personal liability of the President, Governor or signing officer (Article 299(2)). A valid government contract is enforced on its terms ( Alopi Parshad ). The State must not act arbitrarily in awarding contracts (Article 14; Ramana Dayaram Shetty ; Kasturi Lal ).
