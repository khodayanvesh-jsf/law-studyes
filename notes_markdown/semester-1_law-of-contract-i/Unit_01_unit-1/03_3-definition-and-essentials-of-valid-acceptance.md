# 3. Definition and essentials of valid Acceptance
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester 1) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/definition-and-essentials-of-valid-acceptance/

## In One Minute
Acceptance is the "yes" that turns an offer into a promise. Under Section 2(b), when the person to whom a proposal is made signifies his assent to it, the proposal is accepted, and a proposal, when accepted, becomes a promise . To be valid, the acceptance must be absolute and unqualified , expressed in a usual and reasonable manner or in the manner the offer prescribes (Section 7), communicated to the offeror, and given by the offeree while the offer is still open . Acceptance may be by words or by conduct , including by performing the conditions of the offer (Sections 8, 9). Silence is not acceptance. In exams, marks come from the definition, Section 7 quoted, each essential with a case ( Hyde v. Wrench , Felthouse v. Bindley , Carlill , Powell v. Lee , Bhagwati Prasad Pawan Kumar ), and the counter-offer rule. In real life, it tells you whether your reply closed the deal or only opened a new round of bargaining.

## Legal Provision
- Section 2(b). When the person to whom the proposal is made signifies his assent thereto, the proposal is said to be accepted. A proposal, when accepted, becomes a promise;
- Section 7. Acceptance must be absolute. In order to convert a proposal into a promise, the acceptance must— (1) be absolute and unqualified; (2) be expressed in some usual and reasonable manner, unless the proposal prescribes the manner in which it is to be accepted. If the proposal prescribes a manner in which it is to be accepted, and the acceptance is not made in such manner, the proposer may, within a reasonable time after the acceptance is communicated to him, insist that his proposal shall be accepted in the prescribed manner, and not otherwise; but if he fails to do so, he accepts the acceptance.
- Section 8. Acceptance by performing conditions, or receiving consideration. Performance of the conditions of a proposal, or the acceptance of any consideration for a reciprocal promise which may be offered with a proposal, is an acceptance of the proposal.
- Section 9. Promises, express and implied. In so far as the proposal or acceptance of any promise is made in words, the promise is said to be express. In so far as such proposal or acceptance is made otherwise than in words, the promise is said to be implied.
- Section 3 (relevant part). The communication of proposals, the acceptance of proposals, and the revocation of proposals and acceptances, respectively, are deemed to be made by any act or omission of the party proposing, accepting or revoking by which he intends to communicate such proposal, acceptance or revocation, or which has the effect of communicating it.
- Section 6 (relevant part). A proposal is revoked— … (2) by the lapse of the time prescribed in such proposal for its acceptance, or, if no time is so prescribed, by the lapse of a reasonable time, without communication of the acceptance; (3) by the failure of the acceptor to fulfil a condition precedent to acceptance; …
- In simple words
- Section 2(b): acceptance is the offeree showing his assent . Once he does, the offer becomes a promise . Section 7(1): the "yes" must be complete . A "yes, but …" is not acceptance. Section 7(2): accept in a normal, reasonable way , or in the way the offer asks . If the offeree uses another way, the offeror must object within a reasonable time ; if he keeps quiet, the acceptance stands. Section 8: doing what the offer asks (or taking the consideration offered) is acceptance. Section 9: acceptance can be express (in words) or implied (by conduct). Section 3: acceptance is made by an act or omission meant to communicate it. Section 6(2), (3): acceptance must come before the offer lapses , and after any condition precedent is fulfilled.

## Meaning and Definition
What acceptance does

Acceptance is the second half of an agreement. Offer + acceptance = promise (Section 2(b)) = agreement (Section 2(e)). At the moment of acceptance the offeror loses the freedom to walk away, so the law insists that the acceptance be clear and complete.

Section 2(b) broken into parts

Words of Section 2(b) What it means "the person to whom the proposal is made" Only the offeree can accept (for a general offer , any member of the public who knows of it) "signifies" The assent must be shown outwardly, by words or conduct; a decision kept in one's mind is not enough "his assent thereto " Agreement to that proposal , on its terms "A proposal, when accepted, becomes a promise" The moment of acceptance is the moment of agreement

Why the law insists on these rules

If a changed reply counted as acceptance, the offeror would be bound to terms he never offered. If a secret decision counted, the offeror would never know whether he was bound. If silence counted, anyone could force a contract on another by a letter.

Running example

Meena writes to Ravi : "I will sell you my sewing machine, model XL-200, for ₹8,000 . Delivery at my house on Sunday. Reply by letter by Friday. " The letter reaches Ravi on Tuesday.

We will look at several replies Ravi might give and test each against the rules.

## Key Points to Remember
- Acceptance = signifying assent to a proposal; the proposal then becomes a promise (Section 2(b)). It must be absolute and unqualified (Section 7(1)); a changed reply is a counter-offer that kills the original offer ( Hyde v. Wrench ). It must follow the prescribed manner , or a usual and reasonable one; an offeror who does not object in reasonable time accepts the acceptance (Section 7(2)). It must be communicated ; a mental decision is not enough ( Felthouse v. Bindley ), and it must come from the offeree or his authorised agent ( Powell v. Lee ). It can be by conduct or performance (Sections 8, 9; Carlill ; Bhagwati Prasad Pawan Kumar ). It must be given while the offer is open (Section 6). Silence is not acceptance. In auctions subject to confirmation, the contract is complete only on confirmation ( Bhim Sen Walaiti Ram ).
