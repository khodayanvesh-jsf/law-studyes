# 6. Cancellation of instruments
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester 1) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/cancellation-of-instruments/

## In One Minute
A document can look valid on its face and still be void or voidable : a forged note, a deed signed under pressure. While it exists, it can be misused against you. Cancellation lets a court declare it void or voidable and order it delivered up and cancelled. The remedy is in Sections 31 to 33 of the Specific Relief Act, 1963. You must fear serious injury if the document is left outstanding. In exams, the conditions in Section 31, partial cancellation, and the duty to restore benefits earn the marks. In real life, this is how a farmer stops a deed forced out of him from being used to sell his land.

## Legal Provision
- Section 31. When cancellation may be ordered. (1) Any person against whom a written instrument is void or voidable, and who has reasonable apprehension that such instrument, if left outstanding may cause him serious injury, may sue to have it adjudged void or voidable; and the court may, in its discretion, so adjudge it and order it to be delivered up and cancelled. (2) If the instrument has been registered under the Indian Registration Act, 1908 (16 of 1908), the court shall also send a copy of its decree to the officer in whose office the instrument has been so registered; and such officer shall note on the copy of the instrument contained in his books the fact of its cancellation.
- Section 32. What instruments may be partially cancelled. Where an instrument is evidence of different rights or different obligations, the court may, in a proper case, cancel it in part and allow it to stand for the residue.
- Section 33. Power to require benefit to be restored or compensation to be made when instrument is cancelled or is successfully resisted as being void or voidable. (1) On adjudging the cancellation of an instrument, the court may require the party to whom such relief is granted, to restore, so far as may be any benefit which he may have received from the other party and to make any compensation to him which justice may require. (2) Where a defendant successfully resists any suit on the ground— (a) that the instrument sought to be enforced against him in the suit is voidable, the court may if the defendant has received any benefit under the instrument from the other party, require him to restore, so far as may be, such benefit to that party or to make compensation for it; (b) that the agreement sought to be enforced against him in the suit is void by reason of his not having been competent to contract under section 11 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 (9 of 1872), the court may, if the defendant has received any benefit under the agreement from the other party, require him to restore, so far as may be, such benefit to that party, to the extent to which he or his estate has benefited thereby.
- Related provision ( Limitation Act, 1963 , Schedule)
- Article 59. To cancel or set aside an instrument or decree or for the rescission of a contract. – Three years. – When the facts entitling the plaintiff to have the instrument or decree cancelled or set aside or the contract rescinded first become known to him.
- In simple words
- Section 31(1): any person against whom a written instrument is void or voidable, and who reasonably fears (has "reasonable apprehension") that it, if left outstanding, may cause him serious injury, may sue to have it declared void or voidable. The court may, in its discretion , do so and order it to be delivered up and cancelled. Section 31(2): if the instrument was registered under the Indian Registration Act, 1908, the court sends a copy of its decree to the registering officer, who notes the cancellation in his books. Section 32: where an instrument is evidence of different rights or obligations, the court may cancel it in part and let it stand for the rest. Section 33(1): on cancellation, the court may require the party granted relief to restore any benefit he received and to make any compensation justice requires. Section 33(2): a defendant may successfully resist a suit because the instrument is voidable, or because the agreement is void as he was not competent to contract under Section 11 of the Indian Contract Act. The court may still require him to restore a benefit he received. Article 59: the suit is to be filed within three years from when the plaintiff first knows the facts that entitle him to cancellation.

## Meaning and Definition
Cancellation means that the court declares a written instrument void or voidable and orders it to be delivered up and cancelled. An instrument is a document that records rights or duties, such as a deed, a bond or a promissory note .

Rescission and cancellation. Rescission acts on a contract. Cancellation acts on the written instrument, and it covers a void instrument too.

The question this part of the Act answers. When may a person who fears a bad document get the court to remove it?

Why the law has this rule. A void or voidable document that is left in another person's hands can later be shown to a buyer, a bank or a court as if it were valid. The law lets the person at risk act first, instead of waiting to be harmed.

A plaintiff must pass four tests.

Is there a written instrument (Section 31(1))? If not, Section 31 does not apply to an oral agreement. Is the instrument void or voidable as against the plaintiff (Section 31(1))? If not, the instrument is valid and there is nothing to cancel. Does the plaintiff reasonably apprehend serious injury if the instrument is left outstanding (Section 31(1))? If not, the suit fails. The law does not allow cancellation on a vague fear. Is the suit filed in time (Limitation Act, Article 59)? If not, the suit is barred.

If all the answers are yes, the court may, in its discretion, declare the instrument void or voidable and order it delivered up and cancelled. It may require restoration of benefits (Section 33). See Diagram 1 .

## Key Points to Remember
- Cancellation (Sections 31 to 33) deals with written instruments, void or voidable. Rescission deals with contracts. Section 31(1): reasonable apprehension of serious injury if the instrument is left outstanding. The court has a discretion. Section 31(2): registered instruments, with a note in the registry. Section 32: partial cancellation. Section 33: restore benefits and pay compensation, even by a successful defendant. Executants seek cancellation. Non-executants seek a declaration ( Suhrid Singh ). Three years from knowledge of the facts (Article 59). A Section 31 action is in personam and arbitrable ( Deccan Paper Mills ).
