# 11. Mandatory&Prohibitory injunctions
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester 1) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/mandatory-and-prohibitory-injunctions/

## In One Minute
A prohibitory injunction tells a person to stop doing something. A mandatory injunction tells him to do something, usually to undo what he has done. The first is the ordinary kind. The second is stronger, and the court grants it with more care. Section 38 of the Specific Relief Act, 1963 covers the ordinary perpetual injunction. Section 39 covers the mandatory one. In exams, "Mandatory Injunction" is a short-note question. You must give Section 39, the difference from the prohibitory kind, and the test for interim relief. In real life, a prohibitory order stops a wall going up; a mandatory order makes the builder pull it down.

## Legal Provision
- Section 38. Perpetual injunction when granted. (1) Subject to the other provisions contained in or referred to by this Chapter, a perpetual injunction may be granted to the plaintiff to prevent the breach of an obligation existing in his favour, whether expressly or by implication. (2) When any such obligation arises from contract, the court shall be guided by the rules and provisions contained in Chapter II. (3) When the defendant invades or threatens to invade the plaintiff’s right to, or enjoyment of, property, the court may grant a perpetual injunction in the following cases, namely:— (a) where the defendant is trustee of the property for the plaintiff; (b) where there exists no standard for ascertaining the actual damage caused, or likely to be caused, by the invasion; (c) where the invasion is such that compensation in money would not afford adequate relief; (d) where the injunction is necessary to prevent a multiplicity of judicial proceedings.
- Section 39. Mandatory injunctions. When, to prevent the breach of an obligation, it is necessary to compel the performance of certain acts which the court is capable of enforcing, the court may in its discretion grant an injunction to prevent the breach complained of, and also to compel performance of the requisite acts.
- Section 40. Damages in lieu of, or in addition to, injunction. (1) The plaintiff in a suit for perpetual injunction under section 38, or mandatory injunction under section 39, may claim damages either in addition to, or in substitution for, such injunction and the court may, if it thinks fit, award such damages. (2) No relief for damages shall be granted under this section unless the plaintiff has claimed such relief in his plaint: Provided that where no such damages have been claimed in the plaint, the court shall, at any stage of the proceedings, allow the plaintiff to amend the plaint on such terms as may be just for including such claim. (3) The dismissal of a suit to prevent the breach of an obligation existing in favour of the plaintiff shall bar his right to sue for damages for such breach.
- In simple words
- Section 38: a perpetual injunction may be granted to prevent the breach of an obligation existing in the plaintiff's favour, and where the defendant invades or threatens to invade the plaintiff's right to property, in the four cases listed. The Act does not use the word "prohibitory". Section 39: where, to prevent the breach of an obligation, it is necessary to compel the performance of certain acts which the court is capable of enforcing, the court may in its discretion grant an injunction to prevent the breach complained of, and also to compel performance of the requisite acts. Section 40: damages may be claimed in addition to, or in place of, an injunction under Section 38 or 39.

## Meaning and Definition
A prohibitory injunction forbids an act. A mandatory injunction compels (forces) an act.

Section 39 says the court may both "prevent the breach complained of" and "compel performance of the requisite acts". So a mandatory injunction has two parts: a restraint and a positive order.

The question this topic answers. When will the court only say "do not", and when will it go further and say "do" or "undo"?

Why the law has both. Stopping a wrong is not enough once the wrong is already in place. A wall already built across a passage keeps blocking it, even if the builder is forbidden to build more. Only an order to remove it gives real relief. But such an order can cost the defendant heavily, so the law asks for more before granting it.

## Key Points to Remember
- Prohibitory: "do not" (Section 38). Mandatory: "do" or "undo" (Section 39). Section 39 needs a necessary act that the court is capable of enforcing. Interim mandatory injunction: strong case, serious or irreparable injury, balance of convenience ( Dorab Cawasji Warden ). It is granted to restore the last non-contested status or to undo an illegal act. Damages may be claimed with or instead of the injunction (Section 40).
