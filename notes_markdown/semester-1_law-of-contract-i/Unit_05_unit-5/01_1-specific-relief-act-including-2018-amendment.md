# 1. Specific Relief Act including 2018 Amendment
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester 1) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/specific-relief-act-overview/

## In One Minute
Damages give money for a broken promise. Sometimes the injured person needs the thing itself: the plot of land, the document set right, the wrong stopped. The Specific Relief Act, 1963 gives these remedies. They are called specific relief . The Act has seven kinds of relief: recovery of possession, specific performance , rectification, rescission, cancellation, declaration and injunction . The Specific Relief (Amendment) Act, 2018 changed the Act by a large amount from 1 October 2018. Above all, specific performance of a contract is now the rule ("shall be enforced"), and no longer a matter of the court's choice. In exams, the scheme of the Act, Sections 3 and 4, and the 2018 changes earn the marks. Add the Supreme Court's answer on whether the 2018 changes apply to contracts made before 1 October 2018.

## Legal Provision
- Section 1. Short title, extent and commencement. (1) This Act may be called the Specific Relief Act, 1963. (2) It extends to the whole of India [***]. (3) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint.
- (The date appointed was 1 March 1964. The words "except the State of Jammu and Kashmir" were omitted from sub-section (2) with effect from 31 October 2019.)
- Section 2. Definitions. In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,— (a) "obligation" includes every duty enforceable by law; (b) "settlement" means an instrument (other than a will or codicil as defined by the Indian Succession Act, 1925 (39 of 1925)), whereby the destination or devolution of successive interests in movable or immovable property is disposed of or is agreed to be disposed of; (c) "trust" has the same meaning as in section 3 of the Indian Trusts Act, 1882 (2 of 1882), and includes an obligation in the nature of a trust within the meaning of Chapter IX of that Act; (d) "trustee" includes every person holding property in trust; (e) all other words and expressions used herein but not defined, and defined in the Indian Contract Act, 1872 (9 of 1872), have the meanings respectively assigned to them in that Act.
- Section 3. Savings. Except as otherwise provided herein, nothing in this Act shall be deemed— (a) to deprive any person of any right to relief, other than specific performance, which he may have under any contract; or (b) to affect the operation of the Indian Registration Act, 1908 (16 of 1908), on documents.
- Section 4. Specific relief to be granted only for enforcing individual civil rights and not for enforcing penal laws. Specific relief can be granted only for the purpose of enforcing individual civil rights and not for the mere purpose of enforcing a penal law.
- Section 10 (as substituted in 2018). Specific performance in respect of contracts. The specific performance of a contract shall be enforced by the court subject to the provisions contained in sub-section (2) of section 11, section 14 and section 16.
- Sections 5 to 42 contain the seven kinds of relief (see Diagram 1 ). Sections 43 and 44 were repealed by the Repealing and Amending Act, 1974, with effect from 20 December 1974.
- In simple words
- Section 1: the Act applies to the whole of India. It came into force on 1 March 1964. Section 2: defines obligation, settlement, trust and trustee . Words the Act does not define, but the Indian Contract Act does, carry their Contract Act meaning. Section 3: the Act does not take away any right to relief, other than specific performance , that a person has under a contract. It does not affect the Indian Registration Act, 1908. Section 4: specific relief is given only to enforce a person's own civil rights. It is never given merely to enforce a penal (criminal) law. Section 10 (new): the court shall enforce specific performance of a contract. The only exceptions are those in Section 11(2), Section 14 and Section 16.

## Meaning and Definition
Specific relief means relief in the exact form the person is entitled to. The court orders the actual thing: the land handed over, the contract performed, the document corrected, the right declared, the wrong stopped. Damages are only the money value of the loss. The Act does not define "specific relief", but its title and Section 4 show its scope.

The Act's own description. The preamble calls it "An Act to define and amend the law relating to certain kinds of specific relief." It is the second law on the subject. It replaced the Specific Relief Act, 1877, and was made after the Law Commission's Ninth Report.

Why the law has it. At common law in England, courts at first gave only damages. Courts of Equity began to order specific performance where damages would be inadequate. That is why these remedies come from equity (a branch of law based on fairness, as against strict rules) and why, before 2018, the court had a wide discretion (freedom to decide) whether to grant them. The 2018 Amendment made specific performance the rule. Many other reliefs in the Act, such as injunctions (Section 36) and declarations (Section 34), are still discretionary .

The structure. The Act has three Parts and eight Chapters.

Part I, Preliminary: Sections 1 to 4. Part II, Specific Relief: Chapters I to VI, Sections 5 to 35. Part III, Preventive Relief : Chapters VII and VIII, Sections 36 to 44 (Sections 43 and 44 repealed). The Schedule: the list of infrastructure projects, added in 2018.

## Key Points to Remember
- Specific relief gives the thing itself. Damages give its money value. Act 47 of 1963, in force from 1 March 1964. It replaced the 1877 Act. It has three Parts, eight Chapters and a Schedule. Sections 43 and 44 are repealed. Seven kinds of relief: possession, specific performance, rectification, rescission, cancellation, declaration and injunction (Sections 5 to 42). Section 2(e) borrows Contract Act meanings. Section 3 saves other remedies. Section 4 limits the Act to individual civil rights. The 2018 Amendment came into force on 1 October 2018. Section 10 now says "shall be enforced", subject to Sections 11(2), 14 and 16. The old discretion in Section 20 was replaced by substituted performance. Katta Sujatha Reddy (2022) held the amendment prospective. The 2024 review recalled that judgment without deciding the point again.
