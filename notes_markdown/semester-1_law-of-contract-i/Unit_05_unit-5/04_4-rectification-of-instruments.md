# 4. Rectification of instruments
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester 1) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/rectification-instruments/

## In One Minute
Sometimes two people truly agree on a deal, but the paper they sign says something else: a wrong figure, a missing clause, a misdescribed boundary. Rectification fixes the paper, not the deal. Section 26 allows it where fraud or a mutual mistake made the document fail to state the parties' real intention. The remedy is narrow and the court may refuse it. In exams, the grounds, the three routes and the contrast with rescission earn the marks. In real life, it saves a good bargain from a clerk's error.

## Legal Provision
- Section 26. When instrument may be rectified. (1) When, through fraud or a mutual mistake of the parties, a contract or other instrument in writing [not being the articles of association of a company to which the Companies Act, 1956 (1 of 1956), applies] does not express their real intention, then— (a) either party or his representative in interest may institute a suit to have the instrument rectified; or (b) the plaintiff may, in any suit in which any right arising under the instrument is in issue, claim in his pleading that the instrument be rectified; or (c) a defendant in any such suit as is referred to in clause (b), may, in addition to any other defence open to him, ask for rectification of the instrument. (2) If, in any suit in which a contract or other instrument is sought to be rectified under sub-section (1), the court finds that the instrument, through fraud or mistake, does not express the real intention of the parties, the court may, in its discretion, direct rectification of the instrument so as to express that intention, so far as this can be done without prejudice to rights acquired by third persons in good faith and for value. (3) A contract in writing may first be rectified, and then if the party claiming rectification has so prayed in his pleading and the court thinks fit, may be specifically enforced. (4) No relief for the rectification of an instrument shall be granted to any party under this section unless it has been specifically claimed: Provided that where a party has not claimed any such relief in his pleading, the court shall, at any stage of the proceeding, allow him to amend the pleading on such terms as may be just for including such claim.
- Related provisions (Indian Contract Act, 1872)
- Section 20. Agreement void where both parties are under mistake as to matter of fact. Where both the parties to an agreement are under a mistake as to a matter of fact essential to the agreement, the agreement is void. Explanation. —An erroneous opinion as to the value of the thing which forms the subject-matter of the agreement, is not to be deemed a mistake as to a matter of fact.
- Section 21. Effect of mistakes as to law. A contract is not voidable because it was caused by a mistake as to any law in force in India; but a mistake as to a law not in force in India has the same effect as a mistake of fact.
- Section 22. Contract caused by mistake of one party as to matter of fact. A contract is not voidable merely because it was caused by one of the parties to it being under a mistake as to a matter of fact.
- In simple words
- Section 26(1): when fraud or a mutual mistake makes a written contract or other instrument fail to state the parties' real intention, either party or his representative in interest may sue to have it corrected. The same relief may be claimed in a pending suit, or asked for by a defendant. A company's articles of association are left out. Section 26(2): the court may, in its discretion , correct the instrument to state the real intention, so far as this does not harm rights that third persons acquired in good faith and for value. Section 26(3): a written contract may first be corrected and then, if this was prayed for and the court thinks fit, specifically enforced. Section 26(4): no relief unless it is specifically claimed, but the court shall allow an amendment of the pleading at any stage on just terms. Contract Act Sections 20 to 22: these deal with mistake in the agreement itself, not in the writing.

## Meaning and Definition
Rectification means the court corrects the wording of a written instrument. An instrument is any document that records rights or duties, such as a sale agreement, a deed or a lease. The court does not make a new bargain. It records the bargain that was truly made.

The question this section answers. When may a court correct a signed document so that it says what the parties really agreed?

Why the law has this rule. The law normally treats a signed document as the final record of the deal. But if both sides agreed on one thing and the paper says another, holding them to the paper would enforce a bargain neither of them made. Rectification is a narrow exception that brings the paper back in line with the real agreement.

The court asks four questions in order.

Did the parties reach a concluded agreement with a real, shared intention (Section 26(1))? If not, there is nothing to rectify; the question is one of mistake or no contract. Does the writing fail to express that intention (Section 26(1))? If not, the document stands as written. Is the cause fraud or a mutual mistake, and not a one-sided mistake (Section 26(1))? If not, Section 26 does not apply. Can the correction be made without harming a third person who acquired rights in good faith and for value (Section 26(2))? If not, the court cannot rectify against that person.

If all the answers are yes, the court may, in its discretion, rectify the instrument. A contract so rectified can then be specifically enforced (Section 26(3)). See Diagram 1 .

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 26 corrects the document, not the deal. Grounds: fraud or a mutual mistake. A one-sided mistake is not enough. Three routes: separate suit, claim in a suit, defendant's request. The court has a discretion. It cannot prejudice third persons who acted in good faith and for value. A contract can be rectified and then specifically enforced in the same suit (Section 26(3)). Rectification must be specifically claimed, but the court shall allow an amendment (Section 26(4)). Rectification records the bargain made. It does not substitute a different bargain ( Venkatesha , 2026).
