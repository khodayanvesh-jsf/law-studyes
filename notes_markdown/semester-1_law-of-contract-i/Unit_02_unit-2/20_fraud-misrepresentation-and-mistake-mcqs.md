## Meaning and Definition
In Derry v. Peek, the House of Lords held that fraud requires:

Explanation. Derry v. Peek established that fraud requires knowing a statement is false, making it without belief in its truth, or being reckless as to whether it is true — an honest but mistaken belief is not fraud.

Mere silence about a fact that might affect the other party's willingness to contract is:

Explanation. The explanation to Section 17 makes clear that mere silence is not fraud unless the person has a duty to speak (e.g., in a fiduciary relationship) or their silence is itself equivalent to speech.

In Raffles v. Wichelhaus, the contract was held void because:

Explanation. Since two ships named Peerless existed and each party meant a different one, there was no genuine consensus on the subject matter — a bilateral mistake of fact under Section 20, making the agreement void.
