# 14. Wagering agreements
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester 1) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/wagering-agreements/

## In One Minute
A wagering agreement is a bet. Two people promise that one will pay the other, depending on how an uncertain future event turns out. Neither has any interest in the event except the stake. Section 30 makes wagers void , and no suit lies to recover anything won on a wager, or anything handed to a person to hold until the result. A wager is void but not illegal . So a deal connected with a wager, such as a partnership to make wagering contracts, is not tainted ( Gherulal Parakh ). In exams, the essentials and the contrast with a contingent contract earn the marks. In real life this explains why a court will not help you collect a bet.

## Legal Provision
- Section 30. Agreements by way of wager void. Agreements by way of wager are void; and no suit shall be brought for recovering anything alleged to be won on any wager, or entrusted to any person to abide the result of any game or other uncertain event on which any wager is made. Exception in favour of certain prizes for horse-racing. —This section shall not be deemed to render unlawful a subscription or contribution, or agreement to subscribe or contribute, made or entered into for or toward any plate, prize or sum of money, of the value or amount of five hundred rupees or upwards, to be awarded to the winner or winners of any horse-race. Section 294A of the Indian Penal Code not affected. —Nothing in this section shall be deemed to legalize any transaction connected with horse-racing, to which the provisions of section 294A of the Indian Penal Code (45 of 1860) apply.
- Indian Penal Code, 1860, Section 294A. Keeping lottery office. Whoever keeps any office or place for the purpose of drawing any lottery not being a State lottery or a lottery authorised by the State Government, shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to six months, or with fine, or with both. And whoever publishes any proposal to pay any sum, or to deliver any goods, or to do or forbear doing anything for the benefit of any person, on any event or contingency relative or applicable to the drawing of any ticket, lot, number or figure in any such lottery, shall be punished with fine which may extend to one thousand rupees.
- Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, Section 297. Keeping lottery office. (1) Whoever keeps any office or place for the purpose of drawing any lottery not being a State lottery or a lottery authorised by the State Government, shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to six months, or with fine, or with both. (2) Whoever publishes any proposal to pay any sum, or to deliver any goods, or to do or forbear from doing anything for the benefit of any person, on any event or contingency relative or applicable to the drawing of any ticket, lot, number or figure in any such lottery, shall be punished with fine which may extend to five thousand rupees.
- In simple words
- A bet is void. The winner cannot sue the loser for the winnings. Nor can anyone sue the person holding the bet money (the stakeholder) for it. A contribution of ₹500 or more towards a prize for the winner of a horse-race is not made unlawful by Section 30. But Section 30 does not make lawful a horse-racing transaction caught by the lottery offence (IPC Section 294A, now BNS Section 297).

## Meaning and Definition
The Act does not define a wager. The classic description is that of Hawkins J. in Carlill v. Carbolic Smoke Ball Co. (first instance, 1892): a wagering contract is one by which two persons, "professing to hold opposite views" about a future uncertain event, agree that one shall win from the other a sum of money or other stake depending on how the event turns out, neither of them having any other interest in the contract than the stake he will win or lose.

From this, a wager has four marks :

Mark Meaning 1. Uncertain future event The result of a match, an election or a draw (or a past event unknown to the parties) 2. Mutual chances of gain and loss Each side promises to pay if the event goes against him 3. One must win, the other lose If only one side can lose, it is not a wager 4. No interest except the stake Neither party has any interest in the event apart from the money bet

If a party has a real interest in the event, such as an owner insuring his own house against fire, it is not a wager. See Diagram 1 .

If no, not a wager. Do both sides stand to win or lose depending on that event? If no, not a wager. Does neither side have any interest in the event except the stake? If no, it may be a contingent contract (Section 31), such as insurance. If all are yes, it is a wager: void, no suit to recover winnings or a stake held by a stakeholder (Section 30); but it is not illegal, so collateral deals are not tainted (Gherulal Parakh).

## Key Points to Remember
- A wager depends on an uncertain event, with mutual win-or-lose promises and no interest except the stake. Section 30: wagers are void, and no suit lies to recover winnings or a stake held by a third person. A wager is void but not illegal; collateral transactions are not tainted ( Gherulal Parakh ). Horse-race prizes of 500 rupees or more are saved from Section 30, but not lottery transactions under IPC Section 294A (now BNS Section 297). A genuine interest in the event makes the agreement a contingent contract, not a wager. "Mere skill" means substantial degree or preponderance of skill ( K.R. Lakshmanan ).
