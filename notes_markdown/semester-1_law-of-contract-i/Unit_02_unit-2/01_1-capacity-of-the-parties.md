# 1. Capacity of the parties
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester 1) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/capacity-to-contract/

## In One Minute
Capacity means the legal power to bind yourself by a contract. Section 10 says an agreement is a contract only if it is made by "parties competent to contract". Section 11 says who is competent. A person must pass three tests : He is of the age of majority according to the law to which he is subject. He is of sound mind (tested under Section 12). He is not disqualified from contracting by any law to which he is subject. Capacity is about legal status , not intelligence or a fair price. A clever 17-year-old lacks it. An ordinary adult has it. If a party lacks capacity, there is no contract at all. The Privy Council held in Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose (1903) that a minor 's agreement is void , not merely voidable . In exams, the three tests, the Section 12 test of sound mind with its two rules and illustrations, and the reasons given in Mohori Bibee earn the marks. In real life, capacity decides whether a shop can sue a teenager for the price of a phone, or a buyer can keep land bought from a seriously ill seller.

## Legal Provision
- Section 10. What agreements are contracts. All agreements are contracts if they are made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for a lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and are not hereby expressly declared to be void. Nothing herein contained shall affect any law in force in India and not hereby expressly repealed by which any contract is required to be made in writing or in the presence of witnesses, or any law relating to the registration of documents.
- Section 11. Who are competent to contract. Every person is competent to contract who is of the age of majority according to the law to which he is subject, and who is of sound mind, and is not disqualified from contracting by any law to which he is subject.
- Section 12. What is a sound mind for the purposes of contracting. A person is said to be of sound mind for the purpose of making a contract, if, at the time when he makes it, he is capable of understanding it and of forming a rational judgment as to its effect upon his interests. A person who is usually of unsound mind, but occasionally of sound mind, may make a contract when he is of sound mind. A person who is usually of sound mind, but occasionally of unsound mind, may not make a contract when he is of unsound mind. Illustration (a): A patient in a lunatic asylum, who is at intervals of sound mind, may contract during those intervals. Illustration (b): A sane man, who is delirious from fever or who is so drunk that he cannot understand the terms of a contract, or form a rational judgment as to its effect on his interests, cannot contract whilst such delirium or drunkenness lasts.
- Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, 1956, Section 4(a). "minor" means a person who has not completed the age of eighteen years;
- Indian Majority Act, 1875, Section 3 (as the bare Act note to Section 11 points to it): every person domiciled in India attains majority on completing the age of eighteen years . Older versions of Section 3 fixed twenty-one years for a minor for whom a court had appointed a guardian, or whose property was under a Court of Wards, before he turned eighteen. Mohori Bibee (1895 facts) was decided under that 21-year rule. See the verification report on whether the 21-year rule is still in force.
- In simple words
- Section 10: capacity is one of the conditions of every contract . No capacity, no contract. Section 11: three tests. Old enough, sound mind, and no legal bar. Section 12: "sound mind" is tested at the time of the contract . Can the person (1) understand the contract, and (2) judge how it affects his interests? A person who is usually unsound may contract in a clear interval. A person who is usually sound may not contract while he is delirious or very drunk. Majority: the general age is 18 .

## Meaning and Definition
Capacity to contract (also called competence) is the legal ability of a person to make a contract that binds him. A person who has it is "competent to contract".

The law asks about the person, not the bargain. A very fair price does not cure a missing capacity. And a foolish bargain made by a competent adult is still a contract.

Who is not competent. Section 11 gives three groups:

Minors – persons below the age of majority. Persons of unsound mind – at the time of contracting (Section 12). Persons disqualified by law – adults of sound mind who are barred by a particular law (for example, an alien enemy in some situations).

"According to the law to which he is subject." Section 11 does not fix an age itself. It takes the age from the law that governs the person. For persons domiciled in India, the Indian Majority Act, 1875 fixes it. The bare Act places a note under Section 11: "See the Indian Majority Act, 1875 (9 of 1875)."

Why the law has this rule.

To protect the weak. A child or a person who cannot understand a deal could easily be exploited. To give certainty. A fixed age and a clear test let the other side know where it stands before it deals. Because a contract rests on consent. Only a person who can understand and judge can truly agree.

Capacity and free consent are different. Capacity asks who the person is. Free consent asks how his agreement was obtained ( coercion , fraud and so on). A competent adult who is cheated has capacity, but his consent is not free.

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 10 makes "parties competent to contract" a condition of every contract. Section 11 has three tests: age of majority, sound mind, no disqualification by law. The general age of majority in India is 18. Section 12: sound mind = ability to understand the contract and judge its effect on one's interests, at the time of making it. Usually unsound but sometimes sound: may contract in a lucid interval. Usually sound but sometimes unsound: may not contract while unsound. Capacity is a legal status; a fair price does not cure its absence. A minor's agreement is void ( Mohori Bibee ), and coming of age does not cure it ( Suraj Narain Dube ). Necessaries: the supplier is reimbursed from the incapable person's property only (Section 68). A Hindu natural guardian cannot bind the minor by a personal covenant and needs court permission to sell the minor's immovable property (HMGA Section 8).
