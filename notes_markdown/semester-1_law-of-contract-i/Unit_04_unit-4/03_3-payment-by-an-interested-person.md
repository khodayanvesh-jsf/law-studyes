# 3. Payment by an interested person
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester 1) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/payment-by-an-interested-person/

## In One Minute
Sometimes a person must pay another's debt to save his own interest. Section 69 gives him a right to be paid back by the person who should have paid. Two things must be present. The payer has a real interest in the payment, and the other person is bound by law to pay. The payer must pay "therefore", that is, because of that interest. In exams, the two conditions and the Act's illustration of the Bengal lease earn the marks. In real life, it protects a tenant, buyer or co-owner who pays a landlord's or seller's dues to stop a forced sale.

## Legal Provision
- Section 69. Reimbursement of person paying money due by another, in payment of which he is interested. A person who is interested in the payment of money which another is bound by law to pay, and who therefore pays it, is entitled to be reimbursed by the other. Illustration. B holds land in Bengal, on a lease granted by A, the zamindar. The revenue payable by A to the Government being in arrear, his land is advertised for sale by the Government. Under the revenue law, the consequence of such sale will be the annulment of B's lease. B, to prevent the sale and the consequent annulment of his own lease, pays to the Government the sum due from A. A is bound to make good to B the amount so paid.
- Related provisions
- Section 43 (second and third paragraphs). Each promisor may compel contribution. —Each of two or more joint promisors may compel every other joint promisor to contribute equally with himself to the performance of the promise, unless a contrary intention appears from the contract. Sharing of loss by default in contribution. —If any one of two or more joint promisors makes default in such contribution, the remaining joint promisors must bear the loss arising from such default in equal shares.
- Section 145. Implied promise to indemnify surety. In every contract of guarantee there is an implied promise by the principal debtor to indemnify the surety, and the surety is entitled to recover from the principal debtor whatever sum he has rightfully paid under the guarantee, but, no sums which he has paid wrongfully.
- Section 72, Illustration (a). A and B jointly owe 100 rupees to C, A alone pays the amount to C, and B, not knowing this fact, pays 100 rupees over again to C. C is bound to repay the amount to B.
- In simple words
- A person who is interested in the payment of money that another is bound by law to pay, and who pays it for that reason, gets the money back from the other. In the illustration, B, the lessee , pays the revenue that A, the zamindar, owed, to stop a sale that would end B's lease. A must make good to B what B paid. "In arrear" means unpaid after it fell due.

## Meaning and Definition
Section 69 is one of the five situations of quasi contract . The Act does not define "interested". It does not need to, because the section describes the whole claim: an interested person pays what another must pay by law, and the other reimburses him.

Why the law has this rule. The reason is fairness. The payer saved the debtor from the result of his own default. If the law let the debtor keep that saving for free, the debtor would be made richer at the payer's cost. This is unjust enrichment , and the law prevents it.

Not a promise. The claim does not come from any promise by the debtor. It comes from the law.

English source. The English earlier form of the rule is Exall v. Partridge (1799).

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 69 reimburses a person who pays money that another was bound by law to pay. The payer must be interested in the payment; a legal proprietary interest is not needed ( Seksaria ). The payment must be made "therefore", to protect that interest. The other person's duty may come from a statute, a contract or a decree. The right is to the sum paid and nothing more. A volunteer with no interest has no claim. The debtor's promise is not needed; the right comes from the law.
