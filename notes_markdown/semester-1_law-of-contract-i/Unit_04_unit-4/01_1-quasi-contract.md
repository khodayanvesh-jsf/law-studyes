# 1. Quasi Contract
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester 1) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/quasi-contract/

## In One Minute
A quasi contract is not a contract. It is a duty that the law puts on one person to pay or to return something, although the parties never made an agreement. The law does this so that nobody keeps a benefit at another person's cost. Sections 68 to 72 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 list the cases, in a chapter headed "Of Certain Relations Resembling Those Created by Contract". The idea behind all five sections is unjust enrichment . In exams, the meaning, the idea of unjust enrichment, the five sections and Moses v. Macferlan earn the marks. In real life, it decides who pays when work, goods or money change hands and there is no valid contract.

## Legal Provision
- Section 68. Claim for necessaries supplied to person incapable of contracting, or on his account. If a person, incapable of entering into a contract, or any one whom he is legally bound to support, is supplied by another person with necessaries suited to his condition in life, the person who has furnished such supplies is entitled to be reimbursed from the property of such incapable person.
- Section 69. Reimbursement of person paying money due by another, in payment of which he is interested. A person who is interested in the payment of money which another is bound by law to pay, and who therefore pays it, is entitled to be reimbursed by the other.
- Section 70. Obligation of person enjoying benefit of non-gratuitous act. Where a person lawfully does anything for another person, or delivers anything to him, not intending to do so gratuitously, and such other person enjoys the benefit thereof, the latter is bound to make compensation to the former in respect of, or to restore, the thing so done or delivered.
- Section 71. Responsibility of finder of goods. A person who finds goods belonging to another, and takes them into his custody, is subject to the same responsibility as a bailee.
- Section 72. Liability of person to whom money is paid, or thing delivered, by mistake or under coercion. A person to whom money has been paid, or anything delivered, by mistake or under coercion, must repay or return it.
- Related provision
- Section 73 (third paragraph). Compensation for failure to discharge obligation resembling those created by contract. When an obligation resembling those created by contract has been incurred and has not been discharged, any person injured by the failure to discharge it is entitled to receive the same compensation from the party in default, as if such person had contracted to discharge it and had broken his contract.
- In simple words
- Section 68: a person who supplies necessaries suited to the condition in life of a person unable in law to contract , or of anyone that person must support, is paid back from the property of the incapable person. Section 69: a person who is interested in the payment of money that another is bound by law to pay, and who therefore pays it, is paid back by the other. Section 70: where a person lawfully does anything for another, or delivers anything to him, not meaning to do it free of charge, and the other enjoys the benefit, the other must pay compensation or restore the thing. Section 71: a person who finds goods belonging to another and takes them into his keeping has the same responsibility as a bailee . Section 72: a person to whom money has been paid, or anything delivered, by mistake or under coercion must repay or return it. Section 73, third paragraph: when such an obligation is not discharged, the injured person gets the same compensation as if he had contracted for it and the contract had been broken.

## Meaning and Definition
The Act does not define quasi contract. It speaks only of "relations resembling those created by contract". The word "quasi" means "as if". The law treats the parties as if they had made a contract, but only to enforce a duty to pay or to restore.

Where the duty comes from. The Supreme Court explained the source of the duty in Mulamchand v. State of M.P. (1968). It is "not founded upon any contract or tort but upon a third category of law, namely, quasi-contract or restitution". In plain words, the duty comes from the law, not from the parties' promise. The same idea is called restitution : giving back a benefit received.

Why the law has it. The idea behind the chapter is unjust enrichment . The Latin maxim nemo debet locupletari ex aliena jactura means that no one should be made richer at another's loss. Contract law cannot help here, because there is no promise to enforce. The law therefore creates the duty itself. In State of West Bengal v. B.K. Mondal & Sons the Supreme Court said: "What s. 70 prevents is unjust enrichment and it applies as much to individuals as to corporations and Government."

## Key Points to Remember
- A quasi contract is a duty imposed by law, with no agreement between the parties. It rests on unjust enrichment: no one should be enriched at another's loss. Sections 68 to 72 cover necessaries, an interested payer, a non-gratuitous act, a finder and a payment by mistake or coercion. The claim is for reimbursement, compensation or restoration, not damages for breach. Section 73, third paragraph, gives the same compensation as on a breach when such an obligation is not discharged. Section 70 applies even where a Government contract is invalid for want of form ( B.K. Mondal ). The duty rests on "a third category of law, namely, quasi-contract or restitution" ( Mulamchand ). It does not apply where a valid contract governs the dispute ( MTNL v. Tata Communications ).
