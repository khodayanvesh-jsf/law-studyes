# 8. Remedies for breach of contract
**Subject:** Law of Contract – I (Semester 1) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-contract-i/remedies-for-breach-of-contract/

## In One Minute
When a party breaks a contract, the other party does not have to just accept it. The law gives him a set of remedies . He may end the contract and get back what he gave. He may claim money . He may ask the court to make the other side perform , or to stop him from doing something. The ordinary remedy is damages (Section 73). The others are rescission with restoration (Sections 39, 64, 65) and compensation (Section 75), an agreed sum (Section 74), quantum meruit , specific performance and injunction (Specific Relief Act, 1963). In exams, a clear list of remedies with the section for each, and the choice between them, earn the marks. In real life, the right choice depends on what the injured party actually wants: his money, or the thing promised.

## Legal Provision
- Section 39. When a party to a contract has refused to perform, or disabled himself from performing, his promise in its entirety, the promisee may put an end to the contract, unless he has signified, by words or conduct, his acquiescence in its continuance.
- Section 64 (part). When a person at whose option a contract is voidable rescinds it, the other party thereto need not perform any promise therein contained in which he is promisor. The party rescinding a voidable contract shall, if he have received any benefit thereunder from another party to such contract, restore such benefit, so far as may be, to the person from whom it was received.
- Section 65. When an agreement is discovered to be void, or when a contract becomes void, any person who has received any advantage under such agreement or contract is bound to restore it, or to make compensation for it to the person from whom he received it.
- Section 73 (first two paragraphs). When a contract has been broken, the party who suffers by such breach is entitled to receive, from the party who has broken the contract, compensation for any loss or damage caused to him thereby, which naturally arose in the usual course of things from such breach, or which the parties knew, when they made the contract, to be likely to result from the breach of it. Such compensation is not to be given for any remote and indirect loss or damage sustained by reason of the breach.
- Section 74 (first part). … the party complaining of the breach is entitled, whether or not actual damage or loss is proved to have been caused thereby, to receive from the party who has broken the contract reasonable compensation not exceeding the amount so named or, as the case may be, the penalty stipulated for.
- Section 75. A person who rightfully rescinds a contract is entitled to compensation for any damage which he has sustained through the non-fulfilment of the contract.
- Specific Relief Act, 1963, Section 10. The specific performance of a contract shall be enforced by the court subject to the provisions contained in sub-section (2) of section 11, section 14 and section 16.
- Specific Relief Act, Section 42 (main part). Where a contract comprises an affirmative agreement to do a certain act, coupled with a negative agreement, express or implied, not to do a certain act, the circumstance that the court is unable to compel specific performance of the affirmative agreement shall not preclude it from granting an injunction to perform the negative agreement.
- In simple words
- Section 39: if the other side refuses to perform the whole contract, you may end it, unless you have accepted that it continues. Sections 53 and 55: if one side prevents performance, or fails to perform on time where time is essential, the contract becomes voidable at the other's option, and he may claim compensation. Sections 64 and 65: when a contract is ended, each side must give back what it received. Section 73: damages for natural and foreseen loss, not remote loss. Section 74: if a sum is named for breach, reasonable compensation up to that sum. Section 75: a party who rightly ends the contract can also claim damages. Specific Relief Act: the court may order specific performance (Section 10), award compensation with or instead of it (Section 21), order refund of earnest money where it is refused (Section 22), and grant an injunction (Sections 36 to 42).

## Meaning and Definition
A remedy is the relief the law gives to a party whose right has been broken. A breach of contract happens when a party fails or refuses to perform his promise, or disables himself from performing it. A breach may be actual (on or after the due date) or anticipatory (before the due date).

Where the remedies come from. The Indian Contract Act deals with the effects of breach mainly in Sections 39, 53 to 55, 64, 65 and 73 to 75. The Specific Relief Act, 1963 adds the remedies a court gives "in kind": specific performance and injunction .

Why there are several remedies. Injured parties want different things. One wants out of the contract and his money back. One wants compensation for a loss. One wants the very thing promised. One wants the other side stopped.

## Key Points to Remember
- A breach gives the injured party a choice . It does not end the contract by itself (Section 39). Damages under Section 73 are the ordinary remedy. Rescission comes with restoration (Sections 64, 65) and compensation (Section 75). Section 74 limits compensation to the sum named; the court awards what is reasonable. Quantum meruit gives a reasonable price for work done. Specific performance is now enforced subject to Sections 11(2), 14 and 16 of the Specific Relief Act. Injunction can enforce a negative promise (Section 42). Inconsistent remedies, such as rescission and specific performance, cannot be claimed together.
