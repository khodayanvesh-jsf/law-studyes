# 20. Penalties for misleading advertisement
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester 1) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/penalties-for-misleading-advertisement/

## In One Minute
The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 deals with misleading advertisements on two levels. The Central Consumer Protection Authority can order the advertisement to stop and can impose a money penalty on the maker and the endorser. A court can also punish the manufacturer or service provider with imprisonment and fine. In exams, this topic is asked as a short note on misleading advertisements and their penalties. The usual trap is to mix the Authority's penalty under section 21 with the court's punishment under section 89.

## Legal Provision
- Section 2(28): a misleading advertisement is one that falsely describes a product or service or gives a false guarantee. It is also one that is likely to mislead consumers about nature, substance, quantity or quality, or that conveys a representation that would be an unfair trade practice. An advertisement that deliberately conceals important information is misleading too. Section 2(18): an endorsement is a message, statement, demonstration, or depiction of a name, likeness or seal that makes the consumer believe it reflects the opinion, finding or experience of the person endorsing. Section 21: the Central Authority may direct discontinuance or modification of the advertisement, impose a penalty, and bar an endorser. Section 89: a manufacturer or service provider who causes a false or misleading advertisement prejudicial to consumers is punishable with imprisonment up to two years and fine up to Rs 10 lakh. For each later offence, imprisonment up to five years and fine up to Rs 50 lakh. Section 92: a court takes cognizance of an offence under section 88 or section 89 only on a complaint by the Central Authority or an officer it authorises. Section 88: failing to comply with a direction of the Central Authority under section 20 or 21 is punishable with imprisonment up to six months, or fine up to Rs 20 lakh, or both. Section 24: a person aggrieved by an order of the Central Authority under section 20 or 21 may appeal to the National Commission within 30 days of receiving the order. Section 39(1)(l) and (n): a Commission deciding a complaint may order a corrective advertisement, at the cost of the opposite party, to neutralise a misleading advertisement. It may also order the party to stop issuing misleading advertisements. Section 93: an officer who searches or seizes under section 22 knowing there are no reasonable grounds is punishable with imprisonment up to one year, or fine up to Rs 10,000, or both.

## Meaning and Definition
A misleading advertisement is not limited to a plain lie. It includes an advertisement that is likely to mislead, one that makes a promise that would be an unfair trade practice, and one that hides important information on purpose. An endorser is the person whose name, face, message or seal is used to lend credit to the advertisement.

## Key Points to Remember
- Misleading advertisement is defined in section 2(28). Authority penalty: up to Rs 10 lakh; later up to Rs 50 lakh (section 21). Endorser bar: up to one year; later up to three years. Endorser's defence: due diligence (section 21(5)). Court punishment: up to two years and Rs 10 lakh; later up to five years and Rs 50 lakh (section 89).
