# 6. Rights of Consumers
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester 1) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/rights-of-consumers/

## In One Minute
Section 2(9) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 lists six consumer rights. They are the right to safety, to information, to access at competitive prices, to be heard, to seek redressal, and to consumer awareness. The list says "includes", so it is not closed. In exams, this topic is asked as "explain the rights of consumers", and as a problem matching facts to a right. The usual trap is to give the older list of 1986 or the American four rights as if they were the current law.

## Legal Provision
- Consumer Protection Act, 2019, section 2(9): defines consumer rights. Sections 5, 7 and 9: the Central, State and District Councils render advice on promotion and protection of consumer rights. Section 18: the Central Authority protects, promotes and enforces the rights of consumers as a class. Consumer Protection Act, 1986, section 6: the earlier list of six rights, with the right to consumer education in place of consumer awareness.

## Meaning and Definition
"Consumer rights" in section 2(9) includes the six rights set out below. The word "includes" means that other rights can also be recognised.

What a consumer right is, and why the law gives it

A consumer right is a protection the law gives to a buyer, because a buyer is usually the weaker party in the market. The seller knows the product, controls the price and writes the terms. The buyer often cannot test the goods, cannot follow the technical details and cannot bargain over a printed contract. Three weaknesses explain the six rights. The buyer is exposed to unsafe goods. The buyer lacks information. The buyer has little bargaining power .

The six rights answer these weaknesses in order. Safety, information, and choice at fair prices protect the buyer before and during the purchase. If something still goes wrong, the buyer needs a voice, a remedy, and enough knowledge to use them. Those are the rights to be heard, to seek redressal and to consumer awareness.

Rights and remedies are different things

A right says what the consumer is entitled to expect. A remedy is what the consumer can get when the right is violated. Section 2(9) only defines the rights. To enforce one, the consumer files a complaint under section 35 on one of the grounds in section 2(6), and the Commission can give relief under section 39(1). Because section 2(9) says consumer rights "includes" the six, the list is open.

## Key Points to Remember
- Six rights in section 2(9): safety, information, choice at competitive prices, to be heard, redressal, awareness. The list uses "includes", so it is open. The 1986 list had consumer education in place of awareness. The Authority protects rights as a class (section 18). Councils advise on promotion of rights.
