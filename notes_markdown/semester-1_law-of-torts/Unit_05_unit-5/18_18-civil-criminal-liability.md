# 18. Civil & Criminal liability
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester 1) | **Unit 5:** Unit 5
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/civil-and-criminal-liability/

## In One Minute
The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 works on two tracks. The civil track gives the consumer relief such as replacement, refund and compensation. The criminal track punishes the wrongdoer with imprisonment or fine for serious wrongs, such as ignoring a Commission's order, a false advertisement, or selling adulterated or spurious goods. In exams, this topic is asked as a short note or an essay comparing civil and criminal liability under the Act. The usual trap is to say that a consumer complaint can send a trader to jail. It cannot, except through section 72 for disobeying an order.

## Legal Provision
- Section 39: the District Commission, after finding a defect, deficiency or unfair trade practice proved, orders removal of the defect, replacement, refund, compensation, or other relief. Sections 82 to 87: product liability. A manufacturer , product service provider or product seller is liable for harm caused by a defective product. Section 72: failure to comply with a Commission's order is punishable with imprisonment of one month to three years, or fine of Rs 25,000 to Rs 1 lakh, or both. Section 88: failure to comply with a direction of the Central Authority under section 20 or 21 is punishable with imprisonment up to six months, or fine up to Rs 20 lakh, or both. Section 89: a false or misleading advertisement is punishable with imprisonment up to two years and fine up to Rs 10 lakh, and for each later offence imprisonment up to five years and fine up to Rs 50 lakh. Section 90: selling products containing an adulterant is punishable, with heavier punishment as the injury grows. Section 91: the same for spurious goods. Section 92: a court may take cognizance offences under sections 88 and 89 only on a complaint by the Central Authority or an officer it authorises. Section 93: an officer who makes a vexatious search or seizure is punishable with imprisonment up to one year, or fine up to Rs 10,000, or both.

## Meaning and Definition
Civil liability is a duty to repair the consumer's loss by giving relief or money. Criminal liability is a duty to answer to the State for an offence and to suffer the punishment the law lays down. One act, such as selling unsafe food, can lead to both.

## Key Points to Remember
- Civil track: section 39 relief and product liability (sections 82 to 87). Section 72: one month to three years, fine Rs 25,000 to Rs 1 lakh. Section 89: up to two years and Rs 10 lakh; later offence up to five years and Rs 50 lakh. Sections 90 and 91: punishment grows with the injury; death carries at least seven years. Section 92: cognizance of sections 88 and 89 only on the Central Authority's complaint.
