# 2. Definition of Tort
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester 1) | **Unit 1:** Unit 1
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/definition-of-tort/

## In One Minute
No single definition of tort satisfies every writer. Three are asked in exams: Salmond's, Winfield's and Fraser's. The Limitation Act, 1963 gives the only Indian statutory description. All of them say the same thing in different words. A tort is a civil wrong, the duty is fixed by law, and the usual remedy is damages that the court measures. Learn each definition word for word, because examiners mark the exact phrases.

## Legal Provision
- Section 2(m), Limitation Act, 1963: "tort" means a civil wrong which is not exclusively the breach of a contract or the breach of a trust. This is the statutory description. No other statute defines tort. The definitions of Salmond, Winfield and Fraser are the views of jurists, and courts treat them as guides.

## Meaning and Definition
A tort is a civil wrong that the law remedies mainly by an award of damages. The word came into English from French and goes back to the Latin tortum , meaning twisted or wrong.

The definitions below describe the same idea from different angles. Salmond starts from the remedy. Winfield starts from the duty. Fraser starts from the right that is infringed.

Three terms recur in all the definitions. They are explained first.

Unliquidated damages . Compensation whose amount is not fixed beforehand. The court decides it after hearing the case. Right in rem. A right good against the whole world, such as the right to bodily safety. Right in personam. A right good only against a particular person, such as the right to be paid under a contract.

## Key Points to Remember
- Section 2(m), Limitation Act, 1963: a civil wrong not exclusively a breach of contract or trust. Salmond: a civil wrong remedied by a common law action for unliquidated damages. Winfield: breach of a duty primarily fixed by law, owed towards persons generally. Fraser: infringement of a right in rem of a private individual, with compensation at the injured party's suit. The remedy is unliquidated damages, not a sum agreed in advance. The same facts can be a tort and a breach of contract. Essentials: a wrongful act or omission, a duty fixed by law, infringement of a right in rem, and a civil remedy in damages.
