## Meaning and Definition
In Wilkinson v. Downton (1897), the defendant was liable even though he:

Explanation. The defendant falsely told the claimant, as a joke, that her husband had broken both legs. She suffered a violent shock and fell seriously ill. A deliberate act calculated to cause physical harm, which does cause it, is actionable. Intention is taken to exist from the act, and the harm need not have been meant.

What must a claimant prove to succeed in a nervous shock claim?

Explanation. A recognised psychiatric illness must be proved — ordinary grief, sorrow, or temporary fright, however genuine, is not enough on its own.

A primary victim, for nervous shock purposes, is:

Explanation. A primary victim is one who was within the zone of physical danger, or reasonably feared for their own safety, distinguishing them from a secondary victim who witnesses harm to someone else.
