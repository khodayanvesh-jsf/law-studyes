# 4. Battery
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester 1) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/battery/

## In One Minute
Battery, in this note, means using force on another person's body, on purpose, without that person's consent. The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 describes the force in section 128 and the offence of criminal force in section 129. The sources used here are the two Sanhitas, the Limitation Act and two decided cases. The force can be very slight. A touch on clothes, or on something the person carries, can be enough. Study it together with assault, consent and the defences.

## Legal Provision
- No section of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 is titled battery. The nearest provisions are section 128 (Force) and section 129 (Criminal force). They show what "force" and "using force without consent" mean. Section 128, Force: a person uses force on another if he causes motion, change of motion or cessation of motion to that other. It is also force if he moves a substance so that it touches any part of that person's body, or anything the person is wearing or carrying. It is also force if the touch affects that person's sense of feeling. The three ways of using force ( proviso to section 128): by his own bodily power; by placing a substance so that motion follows without any further act by anyone; or by making an animal move, change its motion or stop. Section 129, Criminal force: intentionally using force on a person, without that person's consent, in order to commit an offence, or intending or knowing it is likely to cause injury, fear or annoyance . The illustrations are explained below. Section 131: assault or criminal force, otherwise than on grave and sudden provocation , is punished with imprisonment up to three months, or fine up to one thousand rupees, or both. Sections 114 and 115: hurt is causing bodily pain, disease or infirmity . Voluntarily causing hurt is punished with imprisonment up to one year, or fine up to ten thousand rupees, or both. Section 33: an act is not an offence if the harm it causes, or is meant or known to cause, is so slight that no person of ordinary sense and temper would complain of it. Sections 25 to 28 and 30: the exceptions on consent and good faith. They are explained under "Defences". Sections 34, 35, 37 to 40: the right of private defence. Section 19: an act done to avoid other harm, in good faith and without criminal intention, is not an offence even if it is known to be likely to cause harm. Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, section 43: how an arrest is made, including the use of force. Limitation Act, 1963, Article 113: three years for a suit that has no period given elsewhere in the Schedule, counted from when the right to sue accrues. The Sanhita provisions give a criminal case. The note below also uses them to understand what force and consent mean.

## Meaning and Definition
Battery , as used in this note, is the use of force on another person's body without that person's consent and without a lawful justification . The Sanhita has no section with this title. Its section 128 defines force, and section 129 defines criminal force.

Three ideas make up the wrong.

Force. Section 128 does not require a blow. Causing motion, a change of motion or stopping of motion to another person is force. Motion or contact. Force is either motion, change of motion or stopping of motion caused to the person, or a substance brought into contact with the other's body, or with anything the other is wearing or carrying. The boat, chariot and palanquin illustrations involve no touch at all. No consent. Section 129 speaks of force used without the person's consent. The meaning of a valid consent is in section 28.

## Key Points to Remember
- Force means motion, change of motion or stopping of motion caused to another person (section 128). Contact with clothes or something carried is enough where force is by a substance; causing motion to the person is also force. Criminal force needs intention, no consent, and a purpose or likely result of injury, fear or annoyance (section 129). Section 131 punishment: up to three months, or fine up to one thousand rupees, or both. Consent is not valid if given under fear or misconception known to the doer, by a person unable from unsoundness of mind or intoxication to understand it, or, unless the context shows otherwise, by a person under twelve (section 28). Private defence never extends to more harm than is necessary (section 37).
