## Meaning and Definition
In Bird v. Jones (1845), the court held there was no false imprisonment because:

Explanation. Since the claimant could still leave by another route, his liberty of movement was only partially, not totally, obstructed — no false imprisonment arose.

Must a claimant be aware of their confinement at the time it occurs for false imprisonment to be established?

Explanation. In Meering (1919) the claimant was held in the company's office without knowing it, and the Court of Appeal judges held that knowledge is not essential. The English view has been debated; awareness helps prove the tort, and its absence may reduce damages.

What degree of restraint on movement is required for false imprisonment?

Explanation. The restraint on movement must be complete; a reasonable alternative way out defeats the claim, however inconvenient that alternative is.
