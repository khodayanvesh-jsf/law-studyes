# 10. Trespass to land
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester 1) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/trespass-to-land/

## In One Minute
Trespass to land is the direct and unjustified entry on land in the possession of another, or staying on it, or placing something on it. It is actionable without proof of damage, because the law protects possession. In exams, this topic is asked as "define trespass to land and state its essentials, defences and remedies", and as a problem on entry by officials or a person who stays on after permission ends.

## Legal Provision
- Specific Relief Act, 1963, sections 5 and 6: recovery of possession of immovable property. Section 6 allows a summary suit within six months by a person dispossessed without consent and without due process, with no need to prove title. Specific Relief Act, 1963, sections 36 to 42: injunctions to stop a continuing or repeated trespass. Indian Easements Act, 1882, sections 4 and 15: a right of way or other easement can be a defence to trespass. It is acquired by twenty years of peaceable and open use as of right. Sections 52, 60 and 63: a licence is a right to do something on another's immovable property that would otherwise be unlawful. It is not an easement or an interest in the property. The grantor may revoke it, unless it is coupled with a transfer of property or the licensee has built a permanent work at expense. On revocation the licensee gets a reasonable time to leave and remove his goods. Specific Relief Act, 1963, section 6(2) to (4): no suit lies after six months from dispossession or against the Government. No appeal or review lies from the order. A person can still sue to establish title and recover possession. Limitation Act, 1963, Article 65: a suit for possession based on title must be filed within 12 years from the time the possession of the defendant becomes adverse. Article 64: a suit for possession based on previous possession and not on title: 12 years from the date of dispossession. Article 87: a suit for compensation for trespass upon immovable property: three years from the date of the trespass. Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, section 329: criminal trespass is entering property in another's possession with intent to commit an offence, or to intimidate, insult or annoy. Lawful entry followed by unlawful remaining with that intent is also covered. Punishment is up to three months, or fine up to five thousand rupees, or both. Section 329(2) and (4): house-trespass is criminal trespass in a dwelling, a place of worship or a place for custody of property. Punishment is up to one year, or fine up to five thousand rupees, or both. Introducing any part of the body is enough entry. The civil claim and the criminal case are separate. Articles 21 and 300A, Constitution of India: entry by State officials must have the authority of law.

## Meaning and Definition
Trespass to land is the unjustified, direct interference with land in the possession of another. The interference can be by entry, by staying after permission ends, or by placing or throwing things on the land. The wrong is to the claimant's possession. The tort is actionable per se (without proof of damage).

## Key Points to Remember
- Trespass to land is direct and unjustified interference with land in another's possession. Actionable per se. The wrong is to possession. A mistake about ownership is no defence. A licence can be withdrawn, and a visitor then becomes a trespasser. Remedies: damages, injunction, recovery of possession, mesne profits.
