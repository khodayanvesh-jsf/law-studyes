# 11. Nuisance - Public Nuisance and Private Nuisance
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester 1) | **Unit 3:** Unit 3
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/nuisance/

## In One Minute
Nuisance is an unlawful, indirect interference with a person's use or enjoyment of land, or with the comfort of the public. It is private nuisance when it affects an individual and public nuisance when it affects a class of the public. In exams, this topic is asked as "distinguish public nuisance from private nuisance" and as a problem on noise, smoke or smell from a neighbour. The usual traps are special damage in public nuisance and the fact that reasonable care is no defence.

## Legal Provision
- No Indian statute defines civil nuisance. The tort comes from English common law and is applied in India as justice, equity and good conscience. Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, section 133 (now section 152, Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023): a District Magistrate, Sub-divisional Magistrate or other empowered Executive Magistrate can make a conditional order. The grounds include an unlawful obstruction or nuisance in a public place, a trade or occupation injurious to the health or physical comfort of the community, a building or tree likely to fall, and an unfenced tank, well or excavation beside a public way. The order requires the person to remove the nuisance, or to desist or regulate the trade, within a fixed time. More grounds in section 152(1): building or disposing of a substance likely to cause fire or explosion, and a dangerous animal that should be destroyed or confined. A conditional order cannot be questioned in a Civil Court (section 152(2)). Procedure, Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, sections 153 to 161: the order is served on the person (section 153). He must obey it, or appear and show cause (section 154). If he denies a public right, the Magistrate inquires first. He stays the case if there is reliable evidence for the denial (section 156). Making the order absolute: if the person shows cause, the Magistrate takes evidence as in a summons-case. He makes the order absolute only if it is reasonable and proper (section 157). The proceedings must end within ninety days, extendable to one hundred and twenty days for recorded reasons. Enforcement: disobedience attracts the penalty in section 223 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (sections 155 and 160). The Magistrate may also have the act done and recover the cost (section 160(2)). Pending the inquiry, he may issue an injunction against imminent serious danger (section 161). Sections 162 and 163: section 162 lets a Magistrate order a person not to repeat or continue a public nuisance. Section 163 covers urgent cases of nuisance or apprehended danger (earlier section 144 of the Code of Criminal Procedure). Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, sections 270, 292 and 293: section 270 says a person commits a public nuisance by an act or illegal omission that causes common injury, danger or annoyance to the public, or to people living or occupying property nearby. It also covers what must cause injury, obstruction, danger or annoyance to those using a public right. A common nuisance is not excused because it causes some convenience or advantage. Section 292 punishes it with a fine up to Rs 1,000. Section 293 punishes repeating or continuing it after a lawful injunction, with simple imprisonment up to six months, a fine up to Rs 5,000, or both. Specific Relief Act, 1963, sections 36 to 42: injunctions against nuisance. Under section 38(3), a perpetual injunction may be granted against invasion of the right to enjoyment of property where there is no standard for ascertaining the damage, where money would not be adequate relief, or where it prevents a multiplicity of proceedings. Section 39 allows a mandatory injunction. Section 40 allows damages in addition to or instead of an injunction. Indian Easements Act, 1882, sections 4, 15 and 17: an easement (section 4) is a right of the owner or occupier of one piece of land to do, or to stop, something on another's land for the better enjoyment of his own land. Section 15 (acquisition by prescription ): a right of way or other easement becomes absolute after twenty years of peaceable and open enjoyment as of right, without interruption. The period is thirty years if the land belongs to the Government. Nuisance link: for an easement to pollute water, the twenty years start when the pollution first perceptibly prejudices the burdened land (Explanation IV). So a right to cause a private nuisance can arise by long use. Section 17 bars a right that would destroy the burdened property. Where the Easements Act does not apply: section 25 of the Limitation Act, 1963 gives the same twenty-year rule (see section 29(4) of that Act). Article 21, Constitution of India: the right to life includes a pollution-free environment. Public interest litigation is used against public nuisance.

## Meaning and Definition
Nuisance is an unlawful interference with a person's use or enjoyment of land, or of a right connected with it. The source is usually outside the claimant's land. Smoke, noise, smell, vibration and fumes are examples. Because the interference is indirect, nuisance differs from trespass. It is not actionable without damage in the way trespass is.

It rests on the maxim sic utere tuo ut alienum non laedas (use your own property so as not to harm another's). It is balanced by the principle of "give and take, live and let live". Neighbours must put up with ordinary, reasonable interference.

## Key Points to Remember
- Nuisance is indirect interference with use or enjoyment of land. Locality is no defence where property is damaged (St Helens). Public nuisance needs a class of the public. A private suit needs special damage. Reasonable care, public benefit and coming to the nuisance are not defences. Remedies: damages, injunction, abatement, Magistrate's order.
