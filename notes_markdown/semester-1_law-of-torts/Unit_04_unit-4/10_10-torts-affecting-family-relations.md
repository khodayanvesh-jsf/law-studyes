# 10. Torts affecting family relations
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester 1) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/torts-affecting-family-relations/

## In One Minute
English common law once protected the family through special actions: loss of consortium, enticement and harbouring of a spouse, criminal conversation, and seduction of a daughter or servant. They treated a spouse, child or servant as something belonging to the husband, parent or master. Modern law has abolished or abandoned them. In exams, this topic is asked as a short note on each action and on why it is obsolete. The usual trap is to treat these actions as live Indian law.

## Legal Provision
- No Indian Act creates these torts. They were part of English common law, and Indian courts did not develop them. Law Reform (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1970 and Administration of Justice Act, 1982 (England): these abolished the actions in England. Motor Vehicles Act, 1988: in a death claim, loss of consortium is a head of compensation for the family. The fixed sum is Rs 40,000, under National Insurance v. Pranay Sethi. Personal laws and Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023: family wrongs are dealt with by matrimonial law and criminal law. Articles 14, 15 and 21, Constitution of India: equality of spouses and personal dignity. The Supreme Court struck down the adultery offence in section 497 of the Indian Penal Code on this basis. Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, section 84: taking away, enticing , concealing or detaining a married woman, with intent that she may have illicit intercourse with any person, is punishable with imprisonment up to two years, or fine, or both. It corresponds to section 498 of the Indian Penal Code. Section 69 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023: sexual intercourse with a woman by deceitful means or a false promise to marry, not amounting to rape, is punishable with imprisonment up to ten years and fine. The Sanhita has no offence of adultery.

## Meaning and Definition
These were actions to protect a person's interest in his family relationships. The law treated the companionship and services of a wife, child or servant as a kind of property of the husband, parent or master, who could sue a third person who interfered.

## Key Points to Remember
- The old actions treated a spouse, child or servant as property. Only the husband could sue for consortium (Best v. Samuel Fox). Criminal conversation was a civil action, not a crime. They were abolished in England and never developed in India. In India, loss of consortium is a head of compensation (Rs 40,000, Pranay Sethi).
