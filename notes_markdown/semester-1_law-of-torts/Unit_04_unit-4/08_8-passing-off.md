# 8. Passing off
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester 1) | **Unit 4:** Unit 4
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/passing-off/

## In One Minute
Passing off is the tort of selling your goods or services as if they were another trader's. It protects the goodwill that a trader has built in his name, mark or get-up , whether or not it is registered. The claimant proves goodwill, misrepresentation and damage. In exams, this topic is asked as "explain passing off and distinguish it from trade mark infringement", and as a problem on a copied name or wrapper. The usual trap is to say that a registered mark is needed.

## Legal Provision
- Trade Marks Act, 1999, section 27(2): the Act does not affect rights of action against any person for passing off goods or services as the goods or services of another. Section 27(1): no one can sue to stop, or to recover damages for, infringement of an unregistered trade mark. Passing off is the remedy left for unregistered marks. Section 2(1)(zb): a trade mark is a mark that can be shown in a drawing and can tell one person's goods or services from another's. It may include the shape of goods, their packaging and a combination of colours. Section 2(1)(h): a mark is deceptively similar to another if it so nearly resembles it as to be likely to deceive or cause confusion. Section 29: a registered mark is infringed when a person, who is not the owner or a permitted user, uses an identical or deceptively similar mark in the course of trade. Section 29(2) covers confusion on the public's part. Section 29(3) presumes confusion where both the mark and the goods or services are identical. Section 29(4) covers a mark with a reputation in India used on dissimilar goods. Section 134: a suit for infringement of a registered mark, or about a right in it, cannot be filed in a court lower than a District Court. Under section 134(2), the District Court where the plaintiff actually and voluntarily resides, carries on business or works for gain has jurisdiction. A passing off suit over an identical or deceptively similar mark, registered or not, also cannot be filed below a District Court (section 134(1)(c)). Section 134(2) names only clauses (a) and (b), so it does not by itself cover passing off. Section 135(1): reliefs in a suit for infringement or passing off: an injunction, and at the plaintiff's option damages or an account of profits, with or without delivery up of infringing labels and marks for destruction or erasure. Section 135(2): the injunction may be granted without notice to the defendant. Interim orders may cover discovery of documents, preserving infringing goods or evidence, and restraining the defendant from dealing with assets in a way that defeats recovery of damages. Section 135(3)(c): in a passing off suit, the court will not award damages (other than nominal) or profits if the defendant proves two things. He did not know, and had no reasonable ground to believe, that the plaintiff's mark was in use when he began. He stopped using it at once on learning of the plaintiff's mark. Specific Relief Act, 1963, sections 36 to 42: injunctions. No Indian Act defines passing off. It is a common law tort developed by courts. Specific Relief Act, 1963, section 37: a temporary injunction continues until a specified time or further order and is regulated by the Code of Civil Procedure. A perpetual injunction can be granted only by a decree after hearing on the merits. Sections 38 to 41: section 38 covers perpetual injunction. Section 39 covers mandatory injunction. Section 40 allows damages in addition to, or in place of, an injunction. Section 41 lists cases where an injunction cannot be granted.

## Meaning and Definition
Passing off is the tort committed when a trader makes a misrepresentation in the course of trade. The misrepresentation leads, or is likely to lead, the public to believe that his goods or services are those of another trader, or are connected with that trader. The wrong is to the other trader's goodwill , which is the attractive force that brings in custom.

## Key Points to Remember
- Passing off protects unregistered goodwill. Registration is not needed. Classic trinity: goodwill, misrepresentation, damage. Innocent confusion is actionable. Section 27(2) of the Trade Marks Act preserves the action. Remedies: injunction, damages, account of profits, delivery up.
