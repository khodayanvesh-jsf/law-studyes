# 8. Extinction of liability
**Subject:** Law of Torts (Semester 1) | **Unit 2:** Unit 2
**Source:** https://nyayasaral.com/subjects/law-of-torts/extinction-of-liability/

## In One Minute
A person who has committed a tort does not stay liable for ever. The right to sue can end in several ways: death, waiver and acquiescence, release, accord and satisfaction, a judgment, and lapse of time under the law of limitation. This note gives the whole picture and the written rules behind each mode: the Limitation Act, 1963, the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 and the Fatal Accidents Act, 1855. Waiver and acquiescence, release, accord and satisfaction, and death each have their own notes. In exams, expect a short note listing the modes.

## Legal Provision
- Fatal Accidents Act, 1855, sections 1A and 2. If a wrongful act , neglect or default causes death, and the injured person could have sued had he lived, the party who would have been liable can still be sued, "notwithstanding the death of the person injured". The suit is for the wife, husband, parent and child. It is brought by the executor , administrator or representative of the dead person. Section 2 allows only one suit for the same subject-matter of complaint. The executor or administrator may add a claim for the pecuniary loss to the estate of the dead person. Section 11, Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 ( res judicata ). No court shall try a suit or issue if the matter directly and substantially in issue was directly and substantially in issue in a former suit. The former suit must have been between the same parties, or parties claiming under them, litigating under the same title. It must have been in a competent court, and the matter must have been heard and finally decided. Explanation IV: any matter that "might and ought to have been made ground of defence or attack" in the former suit is deemed to have been in issue. Order II, rule 2, CPC. Every suit must include the whole of the claim for the cause of action. A plaintiff who omits or intentionally gives up part of the claim cannot sue for it later. A person entitled to more than one relief who omits, except with the court's leave, to sue for all of them cannot later sue for the relief omitted. A plaintiff may give up a portion of his claim to bring the suit within a court's jurisdiction. The Explanation treats an obligation and its collateral security, and successive claims under the same obligation, as one cause of action. Order XXII, rules 1, 3 and 4, CPC. The death of a plaintiff or defendant does not make the suit abate if the right to sue survives. The court, on application, makes the legal representative a party. If no application is made within the time allowed by law, the suit abate against the dead party. The court may award the defendant costs , to be recovered from the estate of a dead plaintiff. Limitation Act, 1963, section 3(1). Subject to sections 4 to 24, every suit filed after the prescribed period "shall be dismissed, although limitation has not been set up as a defence". Section 9: once time has begun to run, no later disability or inability to sue stops it. Section 6: a minor, insane person or idiot at the time the period begins may sue within the same period after the disability ends. Section 17 applies where the suit is based on the defendant's fraud , or fraud conceals the claimant's knowledge of his right, or the suit is for relief from the consequences of a mistake. In those cases the period does not begin until the claimant discovers the fraud or mistake, or could have discovered it with reasonable diligence. Where a necessary document is fraudulently concealed, it does not begin until the claimant first has the means of producing the document or compelling its production. Section 22: in a continuing tort, a fresh period begins at every moment the tort continues. Section 23: where the act is not actionable without actual injury, the period runs from when the injury results. Schedule to the Limitation Act, Part VII (suits relating to tort). Articles 72 to 76, 82, 87, 91 and 113 give the periods listed below. Indian Contract Act, 1872, sections 44, 62 and 63 (rules of contract law that show the same ideas of release and satisfaction). Section 44: a release of one joint promisor does not discharge the others. Section 62: if the parties agree to substitute a new contract, or to rescind or alter it, the original contract "need not be performed". Section 63: a promisee may dispense with or remit performance in whole or in part, extend the time, or "accept instead of it any satisfaction which he thinks fit". Employees' Compensation Act, 1923, section 3(5). No right to compensation if the employee has already sued for damages in a civil court for the injury. No suit for damages lies if he has claimed compensation before the Commissioner, or has agreed on compensation with the employer under the Act.

## Meaning and Definition
The extinction of liability means the ending of the wrongdoer's duty to compensate for a tort, and of the claimant's right to sue.

For study, the modes can be put in two groups. In the first the claimant gives up the right: waiver, acquiescence, release, and accord and satisfaction. In the second the right is cut off by a rule of law: death, judgment, limitation and statute.

## Key Points to Remember
- Modes: death, waiver, acquiescence, release, accord and satisfaction, judgment, limitation, statute. A pending suit continues after a death if the right to sue survives (Order XXII, rule 1). The family has a claim on a death under section 1A of the Fatal Accidents Act, 1855. Section 11 CPC bars a second trial. Order II, rule 2 bars a later suit for a part or relief left out. A late suit is dismissed even if limitation is not pleaded (section 3). One year: false imprisonment (Article 73), malicious prosecution (Article 74), libel and slander (Articles 75, 76). Three years: trespass to land (Article 87) and the residuary Article 113. A continuing tort gives a fresh period at every moment (section 22).
